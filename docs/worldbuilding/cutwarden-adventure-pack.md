## Hook and established situation

At dusk Hadrin Mott hires the party to escort Sella Vey to an orchard drainage opening, witness its condition and return her assessment to Milebank. 'She looks at it. You sign what you saw. Nobody asks you to mend anything.' Sella answers: 'If it were routine, he would have sent someone who owes him.'

A real debris obstruction is backing water toward three occupied plots. Clearing it may affect salvagers below. A downstream repair narrowed their channel and has not been adequately checked. Hadrin accepted an optimistic report; Sella refuses a full opening without another observation. Available Cutwardens can clear debris or carry the warning before dark, not both. Party assistance can change that limit.

A posted promise reads: OPENING CLEARED BEFORE NIGHTFALL. PASSAGE TO FOLLOW. It bears the faction mark but no witness. Hadrin authorized the promise too early and wants wording that lets him keep it. No sabotage or secret villain is required.

Possible approaches: warn downstream before clearing; arrange a limited supervised intervention only if evidence supports it; move threatened stores to buy time; publicly postpone work; or open immediately and accept the unmeasured downstream exposure. A limited opening is not a guaranteed engineering solution. Recovery labor, salt measures, equipment or a strictly bounded work exchange can pay the party even if they reject Hadrin's preferred solution. Agree terms before departure.

End beat: Sella turns the premature notice over and writes WORK DELAYED. PEOPLE STILL PRESENT.

## Meeting Sella: read aloud, then give control back

The woman beside the winch has taken off one glove. She works a retaining pin between finger and thumb, wiping old grease onto a strip of cloth. A ring of keys hangs from her belt. Most are too large for anything here.

Behind her, someone has laid a shovel across the path. Not a barricade. Something to make an arriving person stop.

'Leave that where it is,' she says, without looking up. 'It means I've not checked the boards beyond it.'

She finds a split in the pin, catches it with her nail and sets it on a folded sheet.

'Hadrin said witnesses. How many of you can write?'

Let the party answer. She accepts an attributed mark, dictated account or second witness; literacy is not an entry fee.

If they ask what they are witnessing:

'What we find. Not what he promised.'

She shows them the unsigned notice. Water ticks through the masonry behind her.

'Three households above. A recovery crew below. They both heard we'd have this finished. Neither heard the other existed.'

She folds the paper along its old crease.

'We can go look, carry word, or carry bedding. Tell me which you're offering before I put your names down.'

### Running her response

Sella asks for an observable task, not loyalty. Honest uncertainty wins more trust than a grand promise. She will explain accessible evidence without a persuasion roll, will not certify an unobserved route and will not turn disagreement into an automatic fight. If threatened, she withdraws cooperation and alerts nearby workers where possible. Players can refuse or leave; do not narrate their agreement.

Clues available immediately: the cracked pin means this fitting needs suitable replacement before use; the incomplete notice proves premature communication, not the downstream water level; her last sheet predates the reported narrowing repair. These are three separate facts. She offers the party a copy, keeping her original.

## Downstream salvagers: the Low Rack Four

An informal four-person recovery partnership working beside a debris rack below the narrowed channel. Not thieves by default. They have agreements with two owners, disputed permission for a third cache and a foot-access forecast that predates the proposed opening. Their signed tally and numbered bundles establish claims, not universal ownership.

| Person | Work and presence | Want / vulnerability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mara Holt | Caller and tally keeper; waxed board case, repairs every numeral that smears. | Bring all four home with enough recovered goods to pay the carriers. Fears another crew will take staged bundles if they withdraw. |
| Osric Dane | Hauler; patched shoulder harness, checks each load aloud. | Recover the borrowed frame before anything else. Hides how much his shoulder hurts, but will admit it when asked privately. |
| Fenna Wale | Sorter and cloth repairer; hems tucked up, bone needles in a dry tin. | Save bedding and seed sacks before scrap metal. Owns no share of the metal and resents how the tally values it. |
| Tollen Pike | Runner and landing watcher; short coat, spare dry socks wrapped in cloth. | Be trusted with warning duty rather than always sent for scraps. Knows the checked foot return, not every bank shortcut. |

They share hauling straps, a sound carrying frame, a small handcart usable only on a confirmed suitable approach, and a slate tally. No boat or magical rescue resource appears just because the party needs one.

### First contact and bargaining

Mara asks: 'Is this a warning, or are you asking us to stop earning?' Give the exact source, proposed work and uncertainty. A credible scoped warning is enough to stop new loads while they discuss withdrawal; persuasion is needed for disputed concessions, not to make competent people recognize obvious evidence.

They can leave quickly without cargo. A loaded withdrawal needs the return checked, loads reduced or help staged. Offer a witnessed cache inventory, carrier labor, a bounded recovery priority or an agreed return observation. Do not demand abandonment of all possessions as the only compassionate outcome.

Internal dispute: Osric wants the frame first, Fenna wants the vulnerable textiles, Mara wants no bundle left unrecorded. Tollen can carry a correction if the landing is watched by someone else. Splitting duties creates a real manpower choice.

Useful evidence: their sheet names foot passage, an earlier observation and a review condition for drainage works. Osric saw boards installed at the narrowing repair but does not know its effect. Fenna noticed a new damp line at the rack; it is one observation, not a proven trend.

Persistent outcomes: goods moved remain moved; witnessed caches remain attributed; if the party supplies a watcher, remember whom and when they are relieved. Refusal creates a later meeting and accounting problem, not automatic off-screen deaths.

## Three household plots

Schematic, not a navigation certificate or engineering map:

Raised service path -> three short plot approaches -> backed-up feeder -> obstructed opening -> narrowed downstream channel -> Low Rack worksite.

An old orchard wall links parts of the plots, but no complete cart route is established. The Rootstone station lies elsewhere in the orchard; do not move it beside the quest for convenience. Ground, opening condition and weather must be established before risky actions.

| Plot | Residents and layout | Stakes and useful choices |

| The Reed Yard | Hesta and Corren Marr, adult siblings. A reed-drying lean-to, sleeping room on a low stone sill and bundled matting across a fenced yard. | Damp reaches the lowest stored reeds first. ### Escalation without a forced flood

Show a rising damp line, saturated stores, debris collecting or an increasingly difficult approach before losses. Do not automatically advance the regional clock once per plot. A single backing-up incident is one cause; distinct later failures may add consequences.

Each household can contribute something: knowledge, light carrying, temporary storage, meals from finite supplies or a witness. Do not make inability to haul heavy loads equivalent to moral failure. Negotiate the cost of borrowing goods and avoid surprise permanent debts.

## GM clock: The Orchard Loses Its Measures (six segments)

Tracks deterioration of access, references and communication, not an invisible deadline to drowning. Start0 unless established events justify1-2 and show the causes. Advance one segment for a consequential unresolved failure: a damaged reference still used unqualified, relevant works without a report, a correction missing a known expedition, denied essential inspection or a visibly blocked maintained opening. Count one incident once. Failed dice, rest and discussion alone do not tick it.

| Segment | Visible development | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two notices disagree about their reference. | Travelers request named witnesses. |
| 2 | A minor station is inaccessible or withdrawn. | Obtain a new observation or take a detour. |
| 3 | Work and warnings diverge. | Someone plans from review-due information; allow intervention. |
| 4 | A key approach fails a particular expected use. | Name what is affected: a load, salvage access or exit plan. |
| 5 | Credible new damage affects the Rootstone footing or approach. | Suspend verification pending inspection; the bronze is not automatically destroyed. |
| 6 | Local orchard access is unreliable and work is suspended. | Begin concrete recovery, alternate-exit and repair tasks; no automatic casualties. |

Remove one segment for a resolved contributing problem with a delivered or witnessed correction. Do not farm reductions by reposting the same notice. At6, establish a recovery task list rather than resetting with one roll. Forecasts change only when a specific relevant condition changes and that information becomes available. Do not choose secret changes after a successful forecast to force failure.

## Drainage canal encounters: expanded d20

Entries1-12 consolidate the previous d12. Entries13-20 continue it. Roll once per substantial canal leg, prolonged work or signaled change, not automatically alongside an orchard roll for the same trigger. Record completed work and one-off finds. Incompatible entries can be adapted or rerolled once. Establish creature position and behavior before checks; no automatic ambush or damage. No entry secretly ticks the clock without a qualifying consequence.

| d20 | Situation | Decision or evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Plank crossing removed for rotten-support replacement; timber late. | Help finish or seek a checked detour. No assumed load capacity. |
| 2 | Marked goods chest caught at a debris rack. | Record it, find owner or plan suitable recovery. |
| 3 | Runner has two corrections with near-identical route names. | Resolve recipients before guessing. |
| 4 | Greymaw follows the opposite bank beside a refuse sack. | Manage the attractant or keep distance; no creature bargaining. |
| 5 | A household moves an ill relative across the maintenance approach. | Coordinate access rather than punish slow movement. |
| 6 | New notch has no date or initials; survey assistant rests nearby. | Ask what it measures before using it. |
| 7 | Recent branch blockage came from a resident reinforcing a bank. | Establish effects before declaring sabotage. |
| 8 | Reed-jaw sign marks the usual landing; owner cannot date last use. | Observe or choose another checked landing. |
| 9 | Workers dispute an improvised retaining pin. | Obtain suitable equipment or suspend the work. |
| 10 | Fallen notice was read upside down; someone left with wrong directions. | Correct it and identify the departed recipient. |
| 11 | Loose fitting explains some culvert tapping, not all. | Observe within declared limits; no confirmed supernatural cause. |
| 12 | Two readings agree; witnesses refuse to share originals. | Arrange attributed copies rather than confiscation. |
| 13 | Low Rack bundle bears two legitimate marks: owner and commissioned carrier. | Reconcile the tally before treating either claimant as a thief. |
| 14 | A borrowed frame returns with a cracked joint nobody listed at departure. | Compare witnesses, arrange a repair and avoid invented certainty about blame. |
| 15 | Old step appears as debris shifts, but the next foothold is hidden. | Improve the observation, not the route's whole safety rating. |
| 16 | A cold courier must choose between drying a soaked correction and immediate delivery. | Provide shelter/materials or send a clearly attributed spoken warning ahead. |
| 17 | A Cutwarden wants a witness for a genuine successful repair. | Record its scope and remaining unknowns, not a blanket endorsement. |
| 18 | Fresh drag marks lead from the rack to dry staging ground. | The Low Rack crew moved goods to shelter; confirm before pursuing imaginary looters. |
| 19 | A shared warning board is full; someone proposes scraping off a withdrawn notice. | Preserve an attributed copy and sequence before making room. |
| 20 | Two households offer different dry waiting places, each fitting a different load. | Match the party's actual needs and agree access instead of selecting a universal safe spot. |
