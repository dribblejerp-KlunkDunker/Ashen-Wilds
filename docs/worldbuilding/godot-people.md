# The Godot game's people

**Status: Proposed. Godot addition, 2026-11-05.** Nothing here is canon until the owner says so.
Precedence as in `CANON.md`: the owner's decisions, then what ships in the Godot game, then the
canon sheet, then this file.

**Summary.** The people a survivor meets in the Godot build, and the people a survivor can be.
Edda in depth: her past, why the band left her tied and alive, the cudgel, her voice, her secrets,
how she could grow and how she takes to each background. The eight backgrounds as lives in the
Blackfen, each with a loss, a belief, a line for the fire and a hook for a personal quest. The band
that calls itself Ashbound: its leader, four members, where they went, how they come back. Six
future companions for Acts I to III, each hung on a system the game already has. Six people of the
Watchhouse refuge. A list of new names closes the file.

## Read this first

- **Only humans are people.** By owner decision O1 (2026-11-05, in `CANON.md`), goblins, orks, ogres, trolls and every other creature
  are animals or monsters: no language, names, faith, bargains or society. Every companion and
  named person here is human. The archive's goblin household (the Gutter Kin of Halwen House) is
  not used, and the Gutter-kin background is a human road waif with no goblin connection. That
  settles open question Q1 as option (a), without a goblin household.
- **The archive's habit holds.** Entries keep *what is known* apart from *what people say*. Nobody
  here knows what lies below the Vaults or what the orks fled. Nobody is chosen.
- **Sister files.** This follows `the-kingdom-and-the-breaking.md` (the Watch, the muster book),
  `act-one-the-outskirts.md` (the band's night in the Watchhouse, the Outside Fire, the Fen Gate),
  `items-and-texts.md` (the band's mark and tally) and `acts-two-and-three.md` (the Bell Road).
- **In-game lines** are plain ASCII, ready for banners. Edda is not the Merewick **Eda**; no one here
  is Mara Venn or Harl.

## 1. Edda

| | |
| --- | --- |
| In game | Found tied in the Watchhouse; cut free she says *"I owe you. Lead on; I'll keep your back."* Follows, holds a spot when told (F), fights with a cudgel whatever comes within reach of either of you. No pack, no stamina, 70 health. Knocked to nothing she is down, not dead, for 25 seconds; a clean rag lifts her |
| Full name | Edda Wray, *proposed* |
| Age | About thirty-three: born three years before the Breaking, with no grown memory of a summer |
| From | Stakeby, a stake-hands' hamlet on the fen margin of the Old Causeway |
| Trade | Stake-hand: drove and reset the Causeway's timber sections for the Watch, paid in salt |

### Before

Across the softest ground the Old Causeway is timber: oak stakes in rows, hurdles between them,
rubble over the hurdles. Timber rots. The families of Stakeby were paid by the Crown, and after S.3
by the unpaid Watch out of its tolls, to pull rotten stakes and drive new ones.

Edda grew up in that work. Her father drove stakes with a **beetle**, the trade's two-handed wooden
maul, until a section gave way under him in S.6 and the fen kept him. By twenty Edda knew every
timber section near the fen end by its sound under a cart, and she knew **the dry ways**: the
drowned line of older roads under the reeds, walkable at low water if you know which stakes still
stand. For nine winters she kept the fire at a passing place east of the Fen Gate, **the Cold
Passing**, where the Watch buried salt for patrols caught out in a squall. When her sister died of
the grey cough in S.17 she took in her sister's boy, **Col**, then four.

The salt stopped when the tower fell in the autumn of S.29, and Stakeby broke up. Edda set out for
the Watchhouse with three neighbours. Col did not come: in the spring he had gone with a band in
grey rags who came down the Causeway handing out food. He was sixteen, and they fed him.

### How the band took her

*What is known.* Two of her party reached the Outside Fire a week late with no boots and no packs:
grey rags had taken everything, let them go and kept Edda. Then the band took the Watchhouse by
night, and when it left, Edda was tied to the Assize Ring with a carter's hitch, a tin cup by her
foot, and above her head: *OURS. WE COME BACK AT THAW. TOUCH HER AND YOU ARE OURS TOO.* The band's
tally, trodden into the floor, reads in part: *1 woman, knows the dry ways. Osgar says leave her
tied, she keeps in the cold and fetches more than salt. Cuddy says she will be dead by thaw. Cuddy
is soft.* Then goblins came up through the floor drain.

*What Edda says,* once she trusts you, and not all at once: the band wanted the far side of the
ork-held Fen Gate, and at night, at low water, she walked them round it on stakes she had driven
herself. She asked for Col in return; the Doleman said Col could choose, and Col stayed. They made
her show them the Watch's salt at the Cold Passing. They came back the same way, took the
Watchhouse, tied her and went east again.

### Why they left her alive and tied

*What people guess.* All are told at the Outside Fire; the book will not choose.

- **A store.** Osgar's reading, from the tally: a guide to the dry ways is worth more than salt to
  anyone who wants to cross the fen at the thaw, when the floods come. Tied in the cold she keeps,
  like meat. The mark warns everyone off. They will be back for her.
- **The verdict.** Edda's reading. *Ashbound* is what the Watchhouse calls someone after it has
  stopped helping them, and she believes the band ties up those who served it and *leaves them to the
  ash*, so they can say they are not murderers. The cup says someone meant her to last.
- **Col.** The boy would not let them kill her. Leaving her where the refuge would find her let him
  believe she would live.
- **A message.** The Watchhouse once turned the band's people away (section 3). A woman tied to its
  own offenders' ring says: *this is what your door is for.*

### The cudgel

The band took every edge she had. Nobody takes a stick. Hers is oak hurdle-stake she shaped herself,
thrown into the corner where she could see it, and cut free she picks it up before she says anything.
She says a stick does not stick in a rib, needs no edge kept, and a stake-hand already knows the swing
(slow to start, slow to recover: a stake-driver's swing). The truer reason is the third thing she hides.

### How she speaks

Plain and short, with fen words: *hold* (stand fast), *soft* (ground that will not bear), *the wet*
(the fen), *a passing*. She names a debt and pays it. She does not swear by the Keeper but touches the
lintel going under one, because her mother did. She never says Col's name to a stranger, and never
calls anyone Ashbound whom she has not seen up close.

### What she wants

To pay the debt and be done: your back kept until you are safe, then she is her own again. Under
that, Col back by his own choice, which she knows is not the same thing. And the Causeway mended,
which she will not say aloud, because nobody is going to mend a road again.

### What she hides

1. **The salt.** The Cold Passing cache was the refuge's winter margin, and Joan Tallis (section 5)
   still counts it. Every day Edda eats at the fire she eats from the hole she made.
2. **Col.** One of the grey rags is her sister's boy. When the band comes back at thaw, he comes too.
3. **Why she will not carry an edge.** On the dry ways round the Fen Gate a man of the band slipped
   off a stake into the soft. She was nearest and could have held out the cudgel. She did not. Nobody
   saw. She does not know whether she froze or chose, and she will carry nothing that would make the
   next time easier.

### Across the acts

- **Act I: a debt.** She follows because she owes you and offers no past. Tells: she knows which
  timbers bear, flinches at the name of the Cold Passing, will not sit near the Assize Ring.
- **Act II: the band again.** With the Fen Gate broken, the band's trail east is her trail. The secrets
  come out by her own word if you have lifted her twice or more, otherwise by Joan's reckoning or
  Col's face. Whether she stays because she owes you or because she chooses to is up to you.
- **Act III: her own.** If the debt is called paid and she stays, she stops saying *I owe you*. Her last
  choice, about Col, is hers: bring him home, let him go, or stand between him and you.
- **Small mechanic growth.** She never gets a pack or stamina. Two levers that suit her: held, she reads
  the ground and stands a pace further out without losing her leash; and once her secrets are out she
  carries one rag of her own, used on herself if you cannot reach her in time.

### Edda and the eight backgrounds

- **Watch Deserter.** The Watch's tolls paid Stakeby, then stopped, and she knows someone ran from the
  tower. Slow to warm. *"You lot kept the road. My father died keeping it for you. Don't tell me about
  holding."* Go back to the tower and she goes with you; that is how she forgives.
- **Cinder Factor.** Carriers and stake-hands knew each other at every passing; she trusts you at once.
  *"You'll have walked my timbers. Did they hold?"*
- **Chapel Cleaner.** Wary of anything that smells of the Keeping, until she sees you sweep a corner
  without thinking. *"You tidy like somebody's coming back."*
- **Peat-cutter.** She listens to your breathing on the first long walk and says nothing. Later: *"My
  sister coughed like that for years before it was anything. Some never get worse."* She is kinder to
  you than to anyone.
- **Gutter-kin.** The band took Col by feeding him. She watches your hands near the stores, then stops.
  *"Somebody should have fed you."* She is harder on you than she means, because you are what Col
  could become.
- **Hedge-priest.** The hardest pairing; a sworn-hands oath strikes her as a luxury. *"You won't hit
  hard. Fine. I will. Somebody has to."* Keep the light lit through a sleet night for her and she
  brings you the last coal herself.
- **Fen Healer.** You looked at her wrists before you cut the rope. She lets you near her when she is
  down and nobody else. *"Your eyes are bad. Mine aren't. Walk on my side."*
- **Causeway Carter.** The same road from opposite ends: your cart is a load on her timbers, her timbers
  a hazard to your wheels, and both of you enjoy saying so. *"Your good ear's on the wrong side. Stand
  left of me."*

## 2. The eight backgrounds as people

Each card is a walk of life the Blackfen already knows. Below, one person in each: the life, this
survivor's past, what kit, trait and flaw mean in the world, a belief, a loss, a line for the fire and
a hook. The survivor is never named. The people they lost are.

### Watch Deserter

*Card:* "You held the Causeway tower until you didn't. The sword is the one you were issued." Drilled
(cheaper blocks; the old coif is in the shelter stash). Old wound (90 health). Arming sword, wooden
targe, torch, two rags.

**The life.** The Causeway Watch, left without pay or orders in S.3, kept the road twenty-six years on
tolls in salt and food; from S.12 it held only the tower and the toll house. Nothing was issued new
after S.3. A Watch sword had always been someone's first.

**The past.** Joined at sixteen, about S.12, under Captain Darby and then Serjeant **Agnes Kemble**.
Drilled on the targe because there was never enough mail. The old wound is a spear-thrust under the
left arm from a night in S.18 when starving travellers rushed the tower's stores; it closed wrong, and
a hard blow drives the breath out faster than it should. In the autumn of S.29 the orks came over the
wall under a cinder-squall, and the roster's standing order held: *Fall back to the Watchhouse. Bring
the book.* The survivor fell back. Whether ordered, or before the order, nobody else alive saw. The coif
was left in the stash on an earlier supply run.

**Believes** the Watch kept its oath after the Crown broke its own, and that this mattered; and that
they themselves broke it, maybe. **Lost** Kemble, fate unknown; **Pim Haddow**, who shared their watch
for six years, last seen on the wall with a torch; the tower.

**At the fire:** *"Twenty-six years without pay. We held it because nobody told us to stop. I'm still
waiting for somebody to tell me."*

**Hook: the last entry.** The muster book reached the Watchhouse. Its last entry gives four names as dead
and a date, in a hand nobody at the Watchhouse knows. The survivor knows it: it is Pim's. Pim lived,
brought the book, and went on. Kemble is not among the four dead. And the book that names the deserters
as faithfully as the dead does not name the survivor at all. Finding Pim, east of the Fen Gate once it
is broken, means learning why he left them out, and whether he lied to save them or to save himself.

### Cinder Factor

*Card:* "A road carrier. You have walked every embankment of the Blackfen with a load on your back."
Packhorse (26 kg; a full pack clatters less). Bad knees (sprint at 85%; a dodge costs 30). Boar spear,
long torch, two rags.

**The life.** *Proposed origin for the name:* before the Breaking the Cinder Factors moved slag-iron
cinder down from the Citadels. Now the Factor houses send carriers on foot along embankments no cart
can use, *paid on arrival, never before*, with a waybill saying what was carried, by whom, to whom.

**The past.** Carried for **Factor Lisle Gant** of Brackenreach for nineteen years. Packs so nothing moves
(soft goods round hard, iron wrapped in rag): the quiet pack. The knees went one embankment step at a
time: they can walk all day and cannot run. The spear is a carrier's pole, a staff, a pack-prop and a
point for Greymaw; the torch is long because a carrier who runs out of light on the Salt Trace drowns.

**Believes** a load is a promise: you carry it, or carry the news of why not. **Lost** **Ned Coppin**, their
carrying partner, who went into a broken crossing on the Salt Trace in S.27 under a full load. They
delivered his load with their own, were paid for both, and never told his widow the second payment was
his.

**At the fire:** *"Every embankment from here to the salt landings. Ask me about any of them except the
one."*

**Hook: the second payment.** They still carry Gant's waybill with both loads marked delivered. Ned's widow,
**Hanna Teague**, lives in Brackenreach's Upper Row. The quest is a carry: forty measures of salt earned in
the Blackfen and walked to Brackenreach with the truth. Salt is weight, and 26 kg on bad knees through
Greymaw country is the quest. Hanna may want neither the salt nor the truth.

### Chapel Cleaner

*Card:* "You swept a chapel floor for twenty years and know where everything is kept." Quiet hands
(searches a third faster; quieter steps). Untrained (attacks cost a third more and wear a weapon twice as
fast). Wooden targe, a long torch, four rags. No weapon.

**The life.** After the senior clergy sailed, many chapels ran on their cleaners, who knew the order of
service, the places of the dead and where the oil was kept. (Merewick has one; this survivor is not her.)

**The past.** Swept **Saint Ebbe's at Wick Hollow**, two days east of the Watchhouse, from S.10 to the autumn
of S.29, under **Father Anselm Crewe**, who stayed when his superiors left and then left too, one night in
S.22, with the silver lamp. The cleaner kept the chapel open seven more years. Soft shoes past sleeping
penitents made their steps quiet; opening chests as they opened the vestry press made their searches
quick. They never held a weapon: they swing with the whole body, the edge turns, the blade blunts. The
targe hung over the chapel door, an offering to the soldier-saints who held gateways; the rags are altar
cloth, boiled; the torch is chapel pitch and tallow, dipped like lamp-wicks.

**Believes** in the Keeping without the priests: a roof kept, a hearth kept, the dead named. **Lost** their
son **Wilkin**, of the flux in S.19, buried in the chapel yard; the congregation, one by one; Father Crewe,
who may be alive, which is worse.

**At the fire:** *"I know where the oil is, where the rope is, and where the dead are. I always know where
the dead are."*

**Hook: the copy.** The Watchhouse's admittance records were revised: households marked admitted, then
departed, were first written down as refused (`keeping-and-merewick.md`; the leaf in `items-and-texts.md`).
Nobody can say who revised them. The cleaner knows that the Watch lodged a fair copy of each winter's roll
with Saint Ebbe's for safe keeping, and that the copies are still in the vestry press, in the hand of
whoever kept the door each year. Bringing them back would say who changed the record. It would also mean
telling Gil Rudd (section 5) what everyone will then know.

### Peat-cutter

*Card:* "Your arms were made by the fen. The axe was for roots; it serves for other things." Strong back
(23 kg; heavy strikes +15%). Winded (80 stamina, slower to return). Broadaxe, short torch, one rag.

**The life.** Peat is the Blackfen's fuel. Gangs cut banks in the high fen in the grey thaw and stack the
turves to dry. The axe is for bog-oak, the drowned roots that stop a slane dead. A cutter's arms and back
are the fen's strongest; a cutter's chest is its worst.

**The past.** Cut the **Moss Hythe** banks north of the Watchhouse with their sister and a gang of six, most
of their life, and can bring the axe through bog-oak in one heavy blow. They are short of breath after any
long effort and slow to get it back: *the cutters' chest*, from dust, wet feet and smoke. Whether it is the
grey cough's first stage nobody at the fire will say to their face, and nobody can tell; that stage can last
years, and many never go further. The torch is short because cutters walk home before dark; one rag,
because a cutter binds a cut with peat-moss and gets on.

**Believes** in the fen, which gives and takes; they leave a turf at the edge of a cut for what lives in the
water and do not claim it does anything. **Lost** **Amis Brough**, the gang's eldest, through a skin of moss
into a sink in S.24; three of the gang to the cough. Their sister **Brede** is not lost. She will not come
in.

**At the fire:** *"Wet wood. Give me a week at Moss Hythe and you'll never burn wet wood again."*

**Hook: the last stack.** Brede lives alone in the gang's turf hut beside the stack cut in the last thaw: dry
peat enough for the hearth all winter, before any heat-stone comes down the Bell Road. She will not give it
to a refuge that argues every winter about letting coughers in, because she coughs too. The quest is the
carry (23 kg is why the cutter can do it at all) and the argument: bring Brede in, which means getting the
door opened to a cougher; or take the peat and leave her; or leave both.

### Gutter-kin

*Card:* "You grew up under the Roofed Mile taking what wasn't watched. The sword was someone else's."
Soft-footed (steps carry a third less; dodges cost 16; a little further sight). Frail (75 health; wounds
bleed half again as long). A worn arming sword, short torch, two rags.

**The life.** *Gutter-kin* is an old Causeway word for road waifs: children with no house, no kin but the
gutter, who live on what travellers do not watch. In the first winters there were many, and most died.
For years the Roofed Mile, the avalanche gallery on the Bell Road, sheltered a gang of them: a roof against
the ash, a dry floor between the piers, carts passing slowly enough to rob.

**The past.** Left on the Bell Road as an infant about S.4 and taken in by the gallery waifs. Grew up seeing
by the ash-light from the arches and walking the masonry without a sound: the sharper eye and the soft
foot. Grew up hungry, and a starved childhood leaves thin bones, soft gums and wounds that close slowly and
bleed long; fen healers know the sign. They left the Mile when the sheep-drags began across the road at
night and the oldest of the gang went up into the roof space and did not come back, before the board read
*ROOFED MILE. Occupied.* The sword came off a dead man below the gallery, nicked then and never kept since.

**Believes** nothing is given; a thing nobody watches belongs to whoever needs it; and you never sleep across
a doorway, because whoever is beyond it may need out more than you need in. **Lost** **Fesk**, the oldest of
the gang, who taught them to walk soft and went up into the roof. Nobody before that, because there was
nobody before that.

**At the fire:** *"First fire I ever sat at where nobody asked what I'd taken. Don't start now."*

**Hook: the gallery cache.** The waifs kept a cache behind a loose stone at the fourth pier: everything worth
keeping they ever took, and the scratched marks that were the only record of who had been one of them.
Fesk's is the last. In Act II, when the road north goes through the gallery, the survivor can look for the
cache and for what became of Fesk. Soft feet and sharp eyes matter more there than anywhere. The gallery's
animal stays unidentified: signs, a cache, perhaps a mark, never an answer.

### Hedge-priest

*Card:* "No chapel will have you, so the road is your chapel. You keep the light lit." Steady (50 poise;
stamina back a quarter faster). Sworn hands (no heavy blows). Wooden targe, the longest-burning torch,
three rags. No weapon.

**The life.** Hedge-priests are the Keeping's unlicensed: curates never ordained, penitents who began to
preach. They bury the dead the parishes cannot reach, witness oaths no register will hold, and keep a light
at night for whoever is on the road. The hierarchy's remnant calls them frauds.

**The past.** A curate at **Saint Leof's at Crake Ford** in S.1, whose priest had sailed. In the worst of the
first winter the curate opened the chapel to everyone on the road, as the Keeping said they must. A fever
and a grey cough went through the crowd, and half of those inside died before the thaw; the houses that
kept their doors shut mostly lived. Crake Ford barred the curate, and every chapel that heard the story did
the same. The sworn hands are an oath sworn against the ford-stone the morning they left: never again to add
to the dead by their own hand. They guard, shove and strike to stop; a heavy blow is a killing blow. The
steadiness is thirty years of vigils in the cold. The torch is the point of them: pitch, tallow and tow,
dipped and dried four times.

**Believes** the obligations hold whether or not the Keeper does; that they were right to open the door;
that the dead were real; and that all three are true at once. **Lost** Crake Ford: fifty-one names, said in
order on the longest night.

**At the fire:** *"I opened a door once and half of them died. I'd open it again. Ask me why, but you won't
like how long it takes."*

**Hook: the fire-keeper.** Ottor Blewitt, who carries the coal from hearth to Outside Fire (section 5), was ten
and inside Saint Leof's that winter; his mother and two sisters are among the fifty-one, and he knows the
hedge-priest's face. The quest is not a journey but the door: when the house is retaken, the refuge must
decide again whom to admit, it asks the only priest it has to speak, and Ottor tells everyone what happened
the last time this priest opened a door.

### Fen Healer

*Card:* "You have closed more wounds than you have made. You see poorly in the dark, and know it." Deft
(bandaging twice as fast, healing twice as much). Night-blind (sight at 60%). Boar spear, torch, five rags.

**The life.** Fen healers are not physicians and do not claim to be. They set bones, close wounds, deliver
children and tell the living which water to boil, paid in food when at all, and they hold opinions the
warning book cannot settle (ash-moths carry the spores; ash-rats spread the flux).

**The past.** Learned from **Hawise Scarth**, an old healer of the meres north of Merewick, and walked her
round for fifteen years after she died. Can bind a bleeding arm while the patient is still deciding whether
to scream. The night-blindness came on over the hungry years, and the healer knows exactly what it is: a
hunger sign, seen in a hundred patients, mended by liver, eggs and greens nobody has. The spear is a wading
pole first. Five rags because there are never enough.

**Believes** in boiled water, clean thread and the plain cause; that ash-lung is a sickness, not a sin and not
a verdict; that *Ashbound* is a word people use to stop being responsible, and is sometimes, terribly,
accurate. **Lost** Hawise; patients past counting; a daughter, **Mab**, born small in S.15, who lived four days.

**At the fire:** *"Give me your arm. No, the other one. You've been bleeding into that sleeve for an hour and
calling it sweat."*

**Hook: the two who came back.** The warning book records two people who say they came back from silence,
ash-lung's third stage. Hawise believed one of them and wrote her name in her own book: **Sib Arden**, of the
reed hamlets beyond Merewick. The quest is to find her, and the other if there is another, and hear what
they say. The answer stays uncertain: a misjudged sickness, a starving woman who was never sick, a true
recovery nobody can repeat, or a story told to get back inside a door. The healer must decide what to write.

### Causeway Carter

*Card:* "Carts and oxen, and a cart-axe for the things that come out of the reeds. One ear went to a squall."
Hardy (120 health, a worn gambeson; wounds bleed a quarter less long). One good ear (hearing at 70%).
Broadaxe, wooden targe, the gambeson, torch, two rags.

**The life.** Carters own carts and the beasts that pull them, which makes them rich by Blackfen measures and
a target on every road. Few draught beasts are left after thirty years; the carters who still have oxen are
known along the road by name, and so are the oxen.

**The past.** Hauled the Old Causeway with a two-ox cart for twenty years. Weather and work made them hard to
kill: ribs stove by a wheel, a Greymaw bite, home both times. The ear went in a cinder-squall in S.26: the
cart turned side-on to shelter the oxen and an ember the size of a thumbnail went into the left ear. It
healed shut; they turn their head to a sound, which is too slow when it comes from behind. The cart-axe cuts
a beast free of fouled harness and meets what comes out of the reeds. The gambeson is a carter's coat, worn
shiny where the harness rests when they pull the cart themselves.

**Believes** the road belongs to those who use it and the Watch was a toll with a sword; a beast that works for
you is fed and rested before you are. **Lost** their wife, **Maud Kersey**, of the grey cough in S.21; the oxen
**Patience** and **Plod**, left yoked at the Cold Passing the night the orks took the tower, when the carter
had to run. They knew Joss Sefton, who took the Watchhouse cart east that same week and did not come back.

**At the fire:** *"Say it again, on this side. No. Never mind. I'll watch your mouth. I'm good at mouths."*

**Hook: the cart at the passing.** Their cart is still at the Cold Passing (Edda's passing), east of the Fen
Gate. Oxen do not last a winter yoked in the open; the carter knows and goes anyway. The cart can be saved
with a wheel (timber, iron scrap, a day's work) and something to pull it, and Act II needs a handcart or
better to bring lime, cloth and chain down the Bell Road. The carter can pull it themselves, slowly, at a
weight no pack allows and a noise that carries twice as far. On the way they may find Joss's cart too.

## 3. The band that calls itself Ashbound

### Who they are

Most of those the Watchhouse calls Ashbound are the far-gone sick. A few are bands who **take the name**:
deserters and raiders in grey rags. This one is about a dozen strong, works in fives and sixes, carries rope,
moves at night and loots. The rags are a uniform and a lie: anyone in grey with a wrapped face looks like the
sick, nobody goes near the sick, and travellers drop their packs and run. *Wear the rags on the road. Cough
when they look* (the band's tally).

Their story about themselves is partly true. In the sleet of S.19 a party of deserters, debtors and their
families was turned away from the Watchhouse door, two of them coughing, and four died at the Outside Fire
before morning. The survivors took the word the house used as it shut the door. The Watchhouse records a
refusal that winter, without numbers.

**Their customs.** *The dole:* everything taken goes to the leader and is shared by tally, so much a head, the
old Watch way; it holds them together, and it is why they hate the Watch they copy. *The mark:* what they mean
to come back for, they mark *OURS*, a store, a cache, a person. *The knot:* a carter's hitch, tied the same way
every time. A survivor who has seen it once will know it again.

### Corwen Dole, the Doleman

About fifty. The tower's quartermaster, who handed out the dole of salt and bread until the starving rushed
the stores in S.18; he deserted that winter with three seasons of the tower's salt. Turned away in S.19, he
buried his wife beside the Outside Fire the next morning, in sight of the door. He is not a brute; he is
exact. He writes the tally in a Watch hand and believes he runs the last honest distribution in the Blackfen:
everyone fed, everyone equal. Everyone else is the cost. He let Col choose, meant it, and knew which way the
boy would choose.

*"I fed him. You'd have turned him away at a door. Tell me which of us is Ashbound."*

### Osgar, the hard one

Forties, from nobody knows where; he says the south, and laughs. The Doleman's second, who decides what a
thing is worth. He argued to leave Edda tied because a guide to the dry ways at the thaw fetches more than
salt, and he means to sell her guiding, or her, to whoever wants to cross the fen when the floods come. He is
the one who scratched the mark. He does not kill for pleasure, and does not stop for anything else.

*"Everything keeps in the cold. Even you."*

### Cuddy, the soft one

Thirty, a carter's son who ties the band's knots and coughs. He tied Edda to the ring, put the cup by her
foot, and told the Doleman she would be dead by thaw so nobody would come back for her. The band keeps him,
the one thing people remember in their favour: a cougher fed equally by tally, walking in the middle of the
line. He talks to Col more than anyone, and is the likeliest to let someone go, or to be left behind.

*"Drink it slow. It's all there is till somebody comes."*

### Wynn Carrow, the eyes

Thirties. A Watch archer who left the tower in S.25, four years before it fell, because Kemble kept the Watch
on the wall when nothing behind it was left to protect. The muster book names Wynn among the deserters, as
faithfully as it names the dead. Knew the Watch Deserter on the wall. Watches for the band from high ground
with a bow nobody else can draw.

*"You lasted four years longer than me. Did it help anyone?"*

### Col Wray, the youngest

Seventeen. Edda's sister's boy. Joined in the spring of S.30 because the band fed him and Stakeby was starving.
Told the Doleman his aunt knew the dry ways, argued for her life afterwards, and chose to stay. Not a monster
and not a victim: a boy fed for the first time by people who want something for it, who has decided he does
not mind. He has started wrapping his face like the others.

*"She'd have kept me mending a road for nobody. They gave me a share."*

### Where they are now

East of the Fen Gate, wintering at **the Drowned Lantern**, a former Causeway inn on the far reach, roofless
above and dry below. The Cold Passing salt is there, with the Watchhouse's rope and the healer's needles and
whatever they took from Stakeby's walkers. They raid the far reach and wait for the thaw, and they are
hungrier than they look: a dozen people eat a cache fast.

### How they come back

*Proposed; any of these, in any order.*

- **At thaw, as promised,** for what they marked, and to learn who touched her. In game: human enemies on the
  Ashbound body, the bestiary's *looting enemy*, who open containers, strip corpses, take from the stash and run
  with the loot rather than fight to the death.
- **Rope.** Near the band, a downed companion is not left to bleed: Cuddy's rope drags them off, and the next
  quest is getting them back (a lever on the DOWNED state).
- **The road.** With the Fen Gate open in Act II, carts run again, and the band waits for them.
- **The bargain.** Col comes to the door with an offer: the band leaves the refuge alone for a measure of its stores
  each month, a dole the other way. Gil wants to fight, Joan wants to count, Edda wants to see Col.
- **Cuddy alone,** late in the winter, coughing worse, left to the ash by the band that kept him. He knows where
  the Lantern's door is.

## 4. Future companions

Six further candidates beside those `acts-two-and-three.md` offers (Noll Fen, Vey Sorn, Perrin Holt, Brida
Coyle). Each is human, adds one lever to Edda's companion code, and has a reason to go.

### Dace Ormer, the eel-boy (Act I)

**Who.** Fifteen, an eel-trapper's son whose father did not come back from the traps in the autumn. Small, quick,
starving. **Met** on a ledge in the Old Quarry, living on rainwater, having learned to keep utterly still when the
troll comes. He has been watching the survivor do the same, or fail to. **Recruited** by coming into the quarry
and leaving without being pounded: he follows at a distance and comes to the Outside Fire on the second night if
there is food at the edge of the light.

**Mechanic hook: ears and eyes.** While he is near, unseen noises raise their ripples from further out and he
calls the direction in a banner (*"Behind you, left."*). He weighs nothing and fights nothing: he stays out of
reach, throws stones that can interrupt a windup, runs when hurt and comes back when it is quiet. He can cross
the troll's ground without drawing it, because he knows how to be still.

**Leaves** if anyone goes down beside you and you do not lift them. His father was left at the traps by the men
he went out with.

### Alys Cope, the stone-porter (Act II)

**Who.** Forty-five, a porter of Rook's Stair who has carried stone and lime up and down its stairs for thirty
years. Broad, silent, very tired. **Met** at the foot of the Stair, where a blocked road is starving a settlement
whose stone cannot reach the Bell Road. **Recruited** by clearing it (timber and a day's work): the Stair sends her
with its first load to whoever will pay salt.

**Mechanic hook: carry weight.** Part of your pack goes on her back (proposed: up to 15 kg, chosen in the pack
window), keeping you under your limit, quieter and faster. She is the slowest companion in the game: outrun her
and she drops back and rejoins rather than run. Each rest at a fire costs a salt ration, or she carries less the
next day.

**Leaves** at the first way underground. Her father died in a collapse in the Stair's cuts when she was fifteen,
and she will not go below ground. At the Citadels' mine roads she sets your load down at the mouth, square and
tidy, and walks home. She tells you she will before she does.

### Ivo Pargeter, the lime-man (Act II)

**Who.** Fifty, a lime-burner of the Split Kiln, brother of the worker killed by an untrained correction there,
whose tools nobody has touched since. **Met** at the Kiln, short of fuel since the Roofed Mile detour doubled what
carriers charge. **Recruited** once the Kiln has fired again, however the survivor paid for it: he comes away with
lime for the Watchhouse dead and stays because the road has more dead than the Kiln can reach.

**Mechanic hook: lime.** He spreads lime without being asked: a spore cloud he walks into he whitens and kills,
slower than a torch and without the fire; corpse-caps he limes on sight; and since the gallery animal drew back
coughing from lime in the air, he is the companion who can make the Roofed Mile passable, once, for a moment. The
cost is time: he stops to lime every body you leave, a channel of several seconds that will not hurry, while
whatever killed it may still be near.

**Leaves** if you walk on three times while he is liming. *"Lime the dead or meet them. I've met them."*

### Avice Stoke, the penitent (Act II)

**Who.** Thirty, a penitent of the Corpseway: one of those who believe survivors were left behind to repay a
debt, who carry the sick to Saint Orra's Steps and keep the warnings cut on the stone benches. Gentle, tireless,
a little frightening. **Met** at a resting bench beside a stretcher whose other bearer went ahead and did not
come back. **Recruited** by carrying the other end to the Steps; then she says the debt is yours now and comes to
see it paid.

**Mechanic hook: bandages.** Avice binds, with your rags. She lifts a downed companion in half the time and heals
them more, and binds you if you stand still near her. She also binds anyone down near a fight, strangers
included, and spends your rags doing it.

**Leaves** over the door: turn away anyone who asks for help (a cougher at the Watchhouse, a captive you do not cut
free, a wounded stranger you pass) and she is gone the next day, back to the Corpseway to carry them herself. She
is also the companion who will not let anyone she has saved leave, which the refuge will learn.

### Aldo Flitch and Smut (Act II)

**Who.** Sixty, a kennel-man who kept a fled lord's hounds and loosed them when the lord sailed; some of the
Greymaw on the roads are their pups' pups. He kept one bitch, Smut, old now, nearly blind, and still the best nose
in the fen. **Met** on the farmland below the Last Gardens, walking behind a Greymaw pack. **Recruited** by killing
no Greymaw while he can see you, or by putting him down when he comes at you and lifting him after. Either way he
comes to the fire because Smut will not leave it.

**Mechanic hook: the nose.** While Smut follows, hidden stores show themselves, and an Ashbound in the reeds or a
band waiting on a verge is marked before it rises. The cost is noise: she barks at what she smells, which tells it
where you are, and at night at nothing. Her bark has a reach in metres like your footsteps, and a troll hears it.

**Leaves** when Smut dies, to walk behind the Greymaw again. Or over a question: people say he is the one who
whistles to the Greymaw. He says he is not. The book does not settle it; accuse him and he goes.

### Gise Marrable, the lamp-woman (Act III)

**Who.** Sixty-four, a lamp-woman of Greystair, who carried the miners' lamps in the deep workings before the
Breaking. Her husband **Oswy** was a hewer on the Black Seam, in the Slagcrown's workings when the Vaults were
opened. She came down the Bell Road in S.1 and has gone back up it alone. **Met** at Coldbarrow, under the gate
that reads *TAKE NOTHING YOU CANNOT CARRY WHEN THE CART BREAKS*, with a pack she cannot carry further.
**Recruited** by carrying it through the gate; she comes anyway, because you are going where she is going.

**Mechanic hook: heat.** She carries an old Greystair **heat-stone** in a felt bag. Fired in a hearth it holds its
heat for hours: she can relight your torch from it once per firing, and near her the cold weathers bite less
(proposed: Hoarfrost and Sleet slow you less, and in Sleet the torch burns at its ordinary rate). Once the stone
is cold, the cold slows her badly.

**Leaves** by going down. She wants to reach where Oswy was and does not expect to come up. At the mouth of the
deepest road she gives you the stone and goes on alone unless you stop her. Alys Cope, if still with you, sets
down her load at the same mouth for the opposite reason. Neither knows what is below. Nobody in this file does.

## 5. The Watchhouse's people

The refuge's people, at the Outside Fire while the band's leavings and the goblins hold the house, and inside
again once it is retaken. The house that argued every winter about whom to put outside is now outside itself.
About a score in all; these six have parts to play, and all of them have an opinion about the door.

**Margery Reeve, keeper of the warning book.** Sixty. Wrote passes at the toll house before the Watch pulled back, and
has kept the warning book (`bestiary.md`) since it was begun in S.12; she saved it from the band by sitting on it in
the dark. She holds everyone to its habit (say what happened, say what you don't know, write down the dead) and wants
every torn page back. *"Did you see it, or did somebody tell you? Both are worth writing. They're not the same."*

**Ottor Blewitt, the fire-keeper.** Forty. Banks the hearth, keeps the last coal, rations the fuel, and every dusk carried
a coal from the inside hearth to the Outside Fire so the turned-away and the admitted burned the same fire. Lost his
mother and two sisters at Saint Leof's in S.1 (the Hedge-priest's hook). Kind to everyone but the one person he will
not forgive. *"Fire's not safety. Fire's fuel. Bring me fuel and I'll keep you safe."*

**Gil Rudd, the door-warden.** Sixty-two. A Watchman who lost a leg below the knee in S.14 and was sent back to the
refuge, where he has kept the door sixteen years. He was on it in S.19 when the deserters' party was sent to the Outside
Fire, and he said the word. He calls the Watch Deserter a deserter to their face, and has sat at the Outside Fire a
month without saying what it is like. *"Thirty beds, forty people, one door. You decide, then. Go on."*

**Joan Tallis, keeper of the stores.** Fifty. Writes every measure in and out. When the band came she hid the house's salt
(their tally: *Salt none, they hid it*) and will not say where until the house is retaken. Her reckoning still counts
the Cold Passing salt, which Edda gave away. She will find out. Not cruel, only exact: when salt runs out in deep winter,
exactness is how people die fairly. She and the Doleman would understand each other. *"Every measure's somebody. Tell
me who you want to go without."*

**Cress, the rag-girl.** Ten. Boils the refuge's rags; every clean rag a survivor carries passed through her hands. Her
mother set out a day ahead of her last winter and never arrived, and Cress asks every newcomer whether they saw a woman
with red thread at her cuff. (Merewick's register buries such a woman beside a sluice; this file does not say she is the
same, and Cress has never heard of Merewick.) *"That rag's mine. I boiled it. Bring it back when you're done bleeding on
it."*

**Lew Ashby, the cougher at the edge.** Thirty-five, a thatcher with the grey cough, first stage. The refuge would not let
him sleep inside and would not send him away, so he slept in the lean-to and chopped wood for Ottor. Now everyone sleeps
outside, and he still keeps three paces from the fire. When the house is retaken, the argument starts again with him.
*"I don't need in. I need the axe back by morning, that's all."*
