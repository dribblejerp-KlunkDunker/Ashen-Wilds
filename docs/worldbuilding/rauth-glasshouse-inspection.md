# What remained beneath the bench

## Document status

Project: Ashen Wilds. Full proposed scene preserved from the 2026-11-01 conversation. Rauth inspects the glasshouse after [Hesh’s challenge](hesh-challenges-oren.md), in parallel with the departing convoy described in [the aftermath](hesh-challenge-aftermath.md). This is narrative fiction, not implemented inspection logic or real-world hazard-clearance guidance. Existing documents, assets, and development checkpoints are unchanged by this addition. Revised 2026-11-05 for owner decision O1 (only humans are people): the Gutter Kin are a human household of road waifs. The original is in git history at commit fd60061.

Rauth waited until Moth crossed the court.

She was the last. She kept looking back, one hand resting on the bundle against her chest.

He stood aside as she passed.

She gave him more room than he needed.

From the loading platform came the creak of the Grey Lash’s cart. Someone asked for a binding to be checked. Oren was speaking to Elsin.

Lenna approached Rauth.

“The range.”

“I know.”

“Let them finish leaving.”

“They’ve left it.”

He went through the broken opening.

---

Without the hanging cloth, the glasshouse looked smaller.

Rauth could see from the breached wall to the rear workbench. The roof frames divided the grey sky into narrow shapes. Water dripped from one surviving pane.

He stopped just inside.

There were six hollows beneath the bench.

Not beds. Flattened cloth, dry leaves, pieces of board. One place had been raised with slate. Another had a little shelf built from a crate end.

The air smelled of wet soil and old cooking.

He kept his scarf over his mouth. In the hall they said the glasshouse lot carried the cough.

He had heard no coughing.

He listened.

The cart creaked again outside.

Nothing moved beneath the bench.

Rauth put his shield against the wall where he could reach it. He kept the spear.

---

The nearest planting bed had been cleared.

A few objects remained on its edge: a cracked cup, a bent strip of iron, three nails too corroded to trust.

He picked up the cup.

There was a repair hole beside the handle. Someone had started another and broken the rim.

He put it down.

Against the rear wall stood a shallow tray beneath a dark patch of stone.

Water fell into it.

A length of cloth that had once guided the drip was gone. The bare fastening points showed pale against the wall.

Rauth looked toward the court.

He could still see the bundle.

Then the cart moved beyond the opening.

---

He began at the outer edge.

Loose glass lay among the old beds. Some pieces had been pushed into narrow piles. Other sections were almost bare.

He did not step onto the bare sections simply because they were clear.

At the first trench cover, he crouched.

One corner had been lifted and set back badly. A scrap of grey cloth protruded beneath it.

He caught himself reaching for it.

Then he looked farther along.

A piece of thin cord ran behind the broken frame beside the cover. It disappeared beneath a board weighted with rubbish.

Rauth stayed still.

“Lenna.”

She appeared at the entrance.

“What?”

“Keep back.”

He followed the line with his eyes.

The grey cloth was not attached to it. It had merely caught beneath the slab.

The cord was darker, nearly the color of the soil.

Lenna looked down.

“They said that passage was clear.”

“The departure passage.”

He pointed toward a different strip of paving.

“That one.”

She followed the route out to the court.

Then looked back at his boots.

“You’re off it.”

“I noticed.”

---

Elsin arrived carrying her list.

Rauth raised a hand before she entered.

“Stay there.”

She stopped, annoyed.

“The account says the placed hazards were identified.”

“Along the route,” Lenna said.

Elsin checked the sheet.

Rauth waited while she found the words.

“Oh.”

He could have enjoyed that more.

Instead, he looked beneath the board again.

There was no point touching something merely to discover what would happen.

“Get the serjeant.”

---

Oren came without his helmet.

He looked through the opening, then at the cord.

“Is it theirs?”

Rauth stared at him.

“The cord,” Oren said. “Was it placed by them?”

“It didn’t grow.”

“Do you know what it does?”

“No.”

Oren looked at the narrow clear route, the disturbed trench covers, the bench.

“Can the range be entered safely?”

“That route can.”

“And the rest?”

“Not until it’s checked.”

Elsin shifted the list against her arm.

“They should have disclosed—”

“They disclosed a way out,” Lenna said.

Oren held out his hand.

Elsin gave him the paper.

He read the relevant passage himself.

Rauth watched his face.

“Well?”

Oren returned the sheet.

“We inspect before anyone works here.”

“They knew they were leaving it.”

“Yes.”

“They could have told us.”

“Yes.”

Rauth waited for more.

Oren looked at him.

“That does not make the whole room safe to search.”

---

Perrin brought a lantern from the hall.

There was enough daylight overhead, but the recess beneath the workbench remained dark.

Rauth took the lantern and set it on sound paving.

“Where’s your knife?” he asked.

Perrin’s hand went to his belt.

“Leave it there.”

“I wasn’t—”

“I know. Don’t come in after something drops.”

Perrin looked at the cord.

Then nodded.

It was the first instruction Rauth had given him that morning which required no explanation afterward.

---

They worked from what they could see.

Lenna marked the checked paving with pieces of broken tile. Rauth examined each section before anyone crossed it.

The loose board concealed more rubbish than danger, but he did not know that until he had established what lay around it.

The trench itself was another matter.

One cover had been cracked through and laid so neatly that the fracture scarcely showed from above.

Beneath it, the old masonry dropped farther than Rauth expected.

He held the lantern low.

Water reflected the flame.

Something pale lay at the bottom.

He adjusted the angle.

A strip of fabric.

Beside it, a small bone.

“Anything useful?” Elsin asked.

“Not yet.”

She wrote something.

“Don’t enter it as empty,” he said.

“I haven’t.”

He glanced at her.

She turned the sheet so he could see.

*Range not cleared for work.*

He nodded once.

---

Beneath the bench, the driest sleeping place held the mark of a body that had scarcely moved.

A folded rag remained in one corner.

Rauth reached with the spear shaft and drew it into the light.

It was tied around a flat piece of wood worn smooth in the middle.

He considered it.

Then looked at the raised sleeping place and the slate packed beneath it.

“Was that for the injured one?” Perrin asked from the entrance.

“Probably.”

“What is it?”

Rauth set it on the bench.

“Something to lean on.”

Perrin looked toward the path.

“They might need it.”

“They’ve managed.”

“They haven’t gone far.”

Rauth’s mouth tightened.

For a moment, he seemed about to tell Perrin to mind his animals.

Instead, he looked toward Oren.

The serjeant had heard.

“Leave it on the bench,” Oren said. “If they return for it, it remains with their bedding.”

“They’re not returning,” Rauth said.

“Then it remains there.”

No one touched it again.

---

The broken cup was joined by other things.

A strip of leather too short to bind anything. A wooden toggle. A little pouch containing grit and fragments Rauth could not identify.

None was worth taking into the hall.

Elsin stopped adding separate entries.

She wrote *domestic remnants*.

Rauth watched her do it.

He thought of Moth holding the bundle with both arms.

“Put down what’s here,” he said.

Elsin looked up.

“What?”

“Not that.”

He pointed at the phrase.

She waited.

“A cup. Some bedding. That board.”

“There may be more.”

“Then leave room.”

For several seconds, she regarded him.

Then she drew a line through the words.

---

By the time they reached the far end, the glasshouse was colder.

Removing the screens had opened it to the wind. Their lantern flame leaned beneath its cover.

Oren stood in the checked entrance and surveyed the range.

“Could this be made into a workroom?”

“Parts,” Rauth said.

“How much timber?”

“Ask someone who repairs roofs.”

“The rear wall?”

“Still standing.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“It’s what I know.”

Oren let the answer pass.

A movement of air lifted a scrap beneath the bench.

Rauth turned toward it immediately.

Only cloth.

He disliked that Lenna had seen him turn.

She said nothing.

---

At last, Oren asked how many people would be needed to keep the range secure.

Rauth looked at the broken outer wall, the gaps behind the beds, the low opening through which someone thin could disappear.

“One here.”

“And at night?”

“One awake here.”

Oren glanced toward the house.

The entrance hall still needed a watch.

The animals still needed tending.

Their supplies remained behind a door that could not watch itself.

“If we close the breaches?”

“We’ll need material.”

“If we block access until then?”

“Then nobody uses it.”

Oren looked over the range once more.

“Block it.”

---

They used a fallen frame and the heaviest loose pieces of timber they could move without disturbing the roof.

It was not a defense against a determined intruder.

It was a visible warning to their own people.

Elsin wanted a notice.

Rauth found a piece of pale board.

She wrote:

> **UNCHECKED**
> NO ENTRY WITHOUT LEAVE

He looked at the final line.

“Without leave from who?”

“The household.”

He tapped the first word.

“If you give Perrin leave, does that make the floor sound?”

Elsin’s pen stopped.

Oren, standing behind her, said, “Change it.”

She crossed out the second line.

Beneath the first, she wrote:

> KEEP TO MARKED PAVING

---

When they finished, Lenna went back toward the hall.

Perrin returned to the animals.

Elsin carried away the account.

Rauth remained at the opening.

The six sleeping places were visible beyond the barrier.

Their owners had held this room with cloth, rubbish, narrow passages, and the habit of knowing where to put their feet.

Now the Remnant possessed it.

Rauth took up his shield.

He had expected to feel less watched once the gutter lot were gone.

Before leaving, he checked beneath the bench again.
