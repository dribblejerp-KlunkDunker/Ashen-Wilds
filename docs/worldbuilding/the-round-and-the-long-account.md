# The Round and The Long Account: the wider world and a co-op game in it

**Status: PROPOSED** (2026-11-11), like everything in `docs/worldbuilding/`. The ten decisions listed at the end (D1 to D10) were approved by the owner on 2026-11-11 and are recorded as O3 in `CANON.md`. Everything else here is proposed. Nothing in this file is implemented.

**Summary.** Orlen is one dead quarter of a much larger world, and that world has spent thirty years going hungry under a dimmed sun. Part One sets out that world. Part Two designs **The Long Account**: a co-op game for one to four players that crosses it, drawn like the updated Shattered Octave and as grim as Ashen Wilds.

## Status and ground rules

The decisions are approved. The detail built on them stays proposed until it is on the canon sheet.

- **It follows `CANON.md`.** Only humans are people (O1). The O2 names stand. No spells, no prophecy, and anything uncanny has, or could have, a plain cause.
- **Three doors stay shut.** Who made the Vaults, what the orks fled, and why the Unnamed rise are not settled here. This file adds evidence and guesses only.
- **One door is opened partway.** Pieces of the royal fleet are found in the south. The King is never proven. That is decision D3 at the end.
- **The look is settled.** The owner's four reference images of the Shattered Octave build show a first-person, low-resolution 3D world with painted people. The section called *The look* is written from them, and the gameplay sections were revised to match.
- **Checked against the repo.** New names were checked against `docs/worldbuilding/` at today's `main`. They are listed at the end, with the ten approved decisions.

## Part One: the wider world

### The shape of the world

Orlen is the north shore of a cold sea, and eight other lands share that sea with it. Sailors call the water **the Slate Sea**. They call the ring of coasts around it **the Round**, because a trading ship once worked the whole circuit in a single season: slag-iron south, silver and salt and oil north.

Nobody has made the Round since S.3. Each coast now knows its neighbours by rumour, and knows Orlen as a grey hatching at the top of the chart.

                 |  THE SCALD AND THE HOAR  |

 +--------------+  +----------+-----------+  +--------------+
 | THE SKERRIES +--+        ORLEN         +--+  THE WEALD   |

West:          .--------------+--------------.          East:

Schematic, not to scale. Lines join lands that touch. The Lazaret Rocks lie in the Slate Sea off the Sable Shore.

The game begins at Wexhaven and its story ends at the White Deeps. Everything between them is the year's voyage.

| Land | Where it lies | What it was | What it is in S.31 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Orlen** (outsiders say *the Smother*) | North shore | The slag-iron kingdom | Ash. The fen holding on, the Citadels empty. |
| **The Scald and the Hoar** | North of the highlands | Moor, then ice and herd country | Venting ground. Herders walking south without herds. |
| **The Skerries** | Islands west of Orlen | Whaling and sealing isles | The world's lamp oil, behind shut harbours. |
| **The Weald** | East, behind the Long Marrow range | Forest steadings: timber, pitch, charcoal | A third of it is dead and standing, waiting to burn. |
| **The Slate Sea** | The middle | The Round's road | Winter ice further south each bad year. Drifting king's ships. |
| **The Sable Shore** | South-west shore | The Compact of Quays, eleven merchant ports | Harbour chains, quarantine rocks, a market in Orlen's debts. |
| **Vauclere** | Inland, behind the Sable Shore | Wine, cloth and grain | Rye on the wine terraces. A boy called Osbert the Fifth. |
| **Sephar** | Far south-east, on the great river | A river empire with a sun faith | Low floods, a doctrine of debt, a guest who says he is a king. |
| **The White Deeps** | The Saltback mountains, between Vauclere and Sephar | Six hundred years of rock-salt mines | A dark ore under the salt floor, and a wall across it. |

**Scale is unset, as the archive requires.** As a working figure only: Wexhaven to the Sable Shore is about a week under sail in fair weather, and the old Round took a season.

### The Dim

The ash did not stay over Orlen. What fell as ash in the north arrived everywhere else as **the Dim**: a weaker sun, red at noon, and summers that end early.

- **Near Orlen** the ash itself came. The Skerries, the western Weald and the northern quays had thin grey falls from S.0 to about S.4. The quays called it *the grey rain*.
- **Further off** no ash fell, and the harvests failed anyway. Vauclere's vines soured in S.2 and never recovered. Sephar's river has flooded low every year since S.1.
- **The sea cooled.** In a bad winter the pack ice reaches the roads off Port Sable, where no chart had ever drawn it.
- **Thirty poor harvests changed who rules.** Famine in the south stopped being an event and became the economy. Every realm on the Round is now governed by whoever holds the stored grain.

Nobody outside Orlen knows the cause. They know the north went dark the same year the sun weakened, and each land has drawn its own conclusion.

| Who | What they say happened | What they do about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Compact of Quays | Orlen's mines broke into a fire under the mountains. An accident of greed. | Price it. Buy Orlen's debts cheap against the day the ash clears. |
| Vauclere | A judgment on Orlen's lords, which a rightful king in exile may yet lift. | Keep an heir at court and issue paper in his name. |
| Sephar | The north stole from the sun. A debt of light is owed. | Hold a northern guest as surety and send collectors who do not return. |
| The Skerries | The sea turned. It has turned before. | Shut the harbours and sell oil. |
| The Weald | The Long Marrow is burning inside, and the fire will come out east. | Cut firebreaks every year and watch the wind. |

**Is it thinning?** Sephar's temples have measured noon light on every clear day for thirty years, and they are the only people who have. Their two registers disagree (see *Sephar*). So the wider world has measurements, and still no one can prove it.

### Orlen's unwritten edges

The archive covers the fen, the Bell Road and the Citadels. Four parts of Orlen itself are still blank, and each one is a way out of the kingdom.

- **The Downs.** The grain country between the fen and the coast. The march-lords sailed in S.3 and their stewards did not. Each walled **grange-hold** now sows rye in the thaw mud, hires **wintermen** to guard the granary, and takes a toll on its mile of the Salt Trace. They owe levies to nobody and collect them from everybody.
- **Wexhaven and the Burned Quay.** The royal city is a burned harbour, an inlet full of drowned hulls, and an upper town that foreign crews quarry for stoves. A few hundred people live in the customs vaults. Who boils the salt east of the city is left as the archive leaves it: not said.
- **The Wrack Shore.** The west coast: cliffs, wreckers' villages and signal fires. Skerry boats put in here to trade oil for people.
- **The Marrow Passes.** The highlands run east as **the Long Marrow**. Ork packs hold the passes, which suit them: stone, one way in, a wall across a road. No cart has crossed since about S.6.

### The Scald and the Hoar

North of the Citadels the moor vents and nothing lies on it: **the Scald**. Past the Scald is ice country, **the Hoar**, and the people of the Hoar are the **Hoarfolk**: reindeer herders and seal hunters with their own tongue.

- Their herds died between S.1 and S.5, on lichen that had taken up the ash. A people who measured wealth in animals now own none.
- Some walked south across the Scald. Carriers on the upper Bell Road call them *ice-walkers*. They may explain the smoke seen at Kell Tor in S.17. They may not.
- **What they say about the ground.** In their grandmothers' time the snow never lay on the Bald Hills, north of the Slagcrown. That is long before S.0. It supports the Slagcrown families, who say the venting was coming whether anyone dug or not. It proves only that those hills were warm.

### The Skerries

Forty-odd inhabited islands west of Orlen live on whales, seals, cod and rendered oil. They have no king. Boat-owners meet once a year at **the Boat-Thing** and its rulings are the only law.

- **Oil is light.** Since S.0 every lamp on the Round burns Skerry oil or goes dark. The islands are richer than they have ever been, and they sleep with the boats drawn up.
- **The harbours shut in S.2**, a year before the quays chained theirs. The Skerries take no refugees.
- **Oil-bonds.** They do buy labour: seven years at the try-pots for passage and keep. Many bonds signed in the Long Dark are still running, because keep is charged against them.
- **The Fire Chain.** Each island keeps a beacon. A dark beacon means sickness ashore, and no boat lands there until it is lit again. Some have been dark for years.
- **The grey whales.** Flensers say some whales come ashore with grey in their lungs. In those years the islands burn the carcass and render nothing. Nobody has cut one open in front of a witness who wrote it down.

### The Weald

East of the Long Marrow is forest, eight days deep on foot. The Weald has no lord. Free steadings answer to **the Moot of Posts**, and each steading keeps a **name-post** carved with its dead.

- **The Standing Dead.** The ash killed the western third. The trunks still stand, grey and barkless, with nothing growing under them. It is the largest pile of dry fuel on the Round, and it has not burned yet.
- **The Swathe.** Every steading on the dead edge cuts its firebreak each year. A steading that skips its Swathe endangers everyone downwind, and its neighbours burn it out before the forest can.
- **The Brand-wind.** A dry east wind in autumn. When it blows, Weald law forbids any open flame outdoors, including a traveller's torch.
- **The Deer Yards.** Whole herds died together in the first winters and were never limed. Those hollows still bloom.

### The Slate Sea

The sea is a region of its own, and the least governed one.

- **Nobody sails north on purpose** except the plate-reavers, in the thaw, for stoves.
- **Nobody sails in winter** without a slag-iron galley stove. Osbert III's fleet had them. The hulls that still carry one are worth more than their cargo.
- **The Unpaid.** Some king's ships never reached a harbour that would take them. Their crews turned to taking *the King's toll* from coasters, under royal colours. Their children crew them now.
- **The Dead Calm at sea.** The water goes flat as a floor and sound carries for miles. Sailors wrap the ship's bell and wait.
- **The Follower.** See *Beasts*.

### The Sable Shore and the Compact of Quays

Eleven walled ports on the south-west shore bought Orlen's slag-iron for two centuries and sold it silver, salt, wine and cloth. Each port is ruled by its counting-houses. Together they are **the Compact of Quays**, and the chief of them is **Port Sable**.

- **The Chain Years (S.3 to S.6).** When the first king's ships arrived with coughing passengers, the ports raised their harbour chains. Ships starved at anchor in sight of the bread ovens. The Compact calls it prudence, and by its own faith it broke no promise, because it had made none.
- **The Forty.** Since S.6 any northern hull must lie forty days at **the Lazaret Rocks**, three bare islands off Port Sable with old hulks moored as wards. Keepers posted there are seldom relieved. Some hulks have not been opened in years.
- **Ash-paper.** Orlen's royal receipts, charters, assessments and title deeds trade at **the Exchange** for about a hundredth of face. The buyers are betting on a restored Orlen, or on pressing the claims against whichever court wins.
- **The Collectors.** The men who reach the fen with certified southern copies are, on this proposal, agents of those houses. They will not say where the south is because their south is a counting-house.
- **Greys.** Orlen's refugees and their children. They hold no guild rights, live outside the walls in **the Limewalks**, and do the work the quays will not: salt-pans, lime kilns, dead-carts.
- **Plate-reavers.** Licensed crews sail north each thaw to strip slag-iron stoves from dead houses. One Greystair plate buys a house in Port Sable.
- **Bloom Quarters.** In southern warmth the corpse-caps fruit in days. Two ports have each walled off a district and given it up.

### Vauclere

Behind the Sable Shore lies a kingdom of river valleys, terraces and cloth towns. Queen **Ysabeau** rules it from **Saint-Aumere**.

- **The Sour Years.** The vines failed in S.2. Rye grows on the wine terraces now, badly. Vauclere has put down three peasant risings, which it numbers as the First, Second and Third Hunger.
- **Osbert, called the Fifth.** A man of nineteen at her court, said to be Aldric IV's grandson, born in exile. Nobody has shown the marriage that would make him so.
- **Paper in his name.** Her chancery appoints priests to Orlen's parishes and reasserts the old assessments under his seal. On this proposal, the priests who reach the fen claiming southern appointment come from here.
- **The Keeping in Exile.** Most of the senior clergy who sailed in S.3 landed in Vauclere, with the archive ships. They hold Orlen's registers: thirty years of who owed what to whom, kept by the people who left.
- **The treasury ships** made Vauclere too. Ysabeau's father took them in for their cargo. Nobody at court will say what remains of it.

### Sephar

At the far south-east the sea narrows and a great river comes down to it. Sephar is canals, mud-brick cities and the faith of **the Unconquered Day**.

- **An injury to a god.** The Dim was not weather to Sephar. Its sun was lessened in the sight of everyone, and its river has failed to flood properly ever since.
- **The Debt of Light.** The doctrine now taught: the sun was robbed, the robber is the north, and the debt will be collected.
- **The Embassy of Lamps (S.9).** Six ships sailed north to demand restitution. None came back. Nobody on any coast knows what became of them.
- **The two registers.** The temple at **Qesh** and the temple at **Marrah** have each measured noon light through smoked glass on every clear day since S.1. Qesh shows slow brightening since S.24. Marrah shows none. Each accuses the other of heresy, and nobody has ever carried one temple's glass to the other.
- **The Guest.** An old northerner lives in the Garden of Hours as surety for the Debt. He says he is Aldric IV. A Port Sable factor will swear the King drowned off the Lazaret Rocks in S.3 and that this is his chaplain. He knows what a king would know. So would a chaplain.

### The White Deeps

The Saltback mountains hold the oldest rock-salt mines on the Round: white galleries six hundred years deep, under the mine town of **Halsing**.

- **The ore.** In S.27 a sump crew broke through the salt floor into dark, heavy, greasy stone. Fired, it holds heat for hours. Assayers argue over whether it is slag-iron or a poorer cousin.
- **The Deeps Company.** Chartered at Port Sable in S.29, with shares held in all three southern realms. It is the first thing the three have agreed on in thirty years.
- **The hewers.** Nobody in the south knows how to work the ore, so the Company bonds the grandchildren of Orlen's Hewers' Lodges. They know exactly what they are looking at.
- **The wall.** In the autumn of S.31 the seam runs into close-fitted stone. The rest is in *The deep places* and in the story.

### Past the edges

Four directions lead off the chart, and each is a rumour of somewhere better.

- **West of the Skerries:** open ocean. A few whaling boats came back after years away, talking of a warm current and green land. Most did not come back.
- **East of the Weald: the Grass.** Horse-herding peoples who trade at the Weald's edge posts. Their herds are drifting west, so the winters are bad there too.
- **South of Sephar: the Red Country.** Desert and caravan cities, a year's journey by camel. Grain is said to be cheap there. No ship of the Round has gone to find out.
- **North of the Hoar:** nothing is known.

### What crossed the sea

Five things left Orlen after S.0, and the south has built thirty years of law and trade around them.

**1. The fleet.** It did not arrive anywhere as a fleet. A gale off the Lazaret Rocks in the spring of S.3 scattered it, and the pieces came ashore separately. In the Blackfen it stays true that nobody knows where the fleet went. In the south, this much is known:

| What | Where it ended | Who says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seven ships | The roads off Port Sable, at the chain. Three starved at anchor, two were sent to the Rocks, two sailed on. | The Compact's own harbour books |
| The treasury ships | Vauclere | Vauclere's court, which does not discuss the cargo |
| The archive ships and most senior clergy | Vauclere | The Keeping in Exile |
| At least one ship | Sephar | The Guest, and the Throne |
| An unknown number | Never made harbour. They became the Unpaid. | Every coaster they have stopped |
| The King's own ship | **Not settled.** Drowned off the Rocks, landed in Sephar, or neither. | Three accounts, none proven |

**2. The cough.** Ash-lung went south in the lungs of the people who fled. The quays treat it as catching and built the Forty on that belief. Fen healers trace it to the dust and the caps (`the-smothering.md` is the authority). This file does not decide who is right. The Forty stands either way, because it is feared and because it pays.

**3. Goblins.** They crossed in bilges, as rats do. Port Sable had **hold-goblins** in its drains by S.5. No ork has ever been seen south of the sea, and nothing that size stows away.

**4. Stoves.** Slag-iron was portable warmth when the sun was whole. Under the Dim, with no new iron leaving Orlen since the Citadels fell, every plate is treasure. The reavers exist because of it, and so does the Deeps Company.

**5. Lime.** *Lime the dead or meet them* went south with the refugees and became law on every coast within ten years. Lime-burning is Grey work. Factors read the lime price the way a physician reads a pulse: when it rises, a port is dying faster.

**How the world sees Orlen.** The charts hatch it grey and write nothing inside. The quays believe nobody lives north of Wexhaven, and that anyone who does is *ashbound*, a word that crossed the sea and became a slur for any northerner. Parishes that keep registers, argue over admissions and write down their dead would astonish Port Sable.

### Faiths

Each land's faith answers the same question differently: what do the living owe? That difference is why the coasts cannot understand each other's conduct.

| Faith | Where | What is owed | What it made of the Smothering |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Keeping** | Orlen; in exile at Saint-Aumere; among the Greys of the Limewalks | Obligations hold the dark apart, including ones you never chose | A covenant broken, by someone |
| **The Tally** | The Compact of Quays | Only what you contracted. A soul's account closes at death, and dying in debt is the one sin. | A loss to be priced. No promise was made to Orlen, so none was broken. |
| **The name-posts** | The Weald | The dead stay while their names are carved. A rotted post is a second death. | More names than there is oak to carve |
| **The Unconquered Day** | Sephar | Light is lent. Every harvest repays it. | A theft, and so a debt with a debtor |
| **Boat-law** | The Skerries | The sea takes a share of every catch. No priests. | The sea took a larger share |

The Keeping names the dead by speaking into the mouth. The Weald carves the name. The Tally closes the account. All three are ways of saying a person is finished with, and the Unnamed are what the fen has for people nobody finished with. This file draws no link between those customs and why the Unnamed rise.

### Tongues

- **Orlen speech and Quay speech** are cousins. A fen carter and a Port Sable porter understand each other slowly.
- **Quay-talk** is the trade pidgin of the whole Round. Every sailor has it.
- **Vauclere's court tongue** and **Sepharine** are foreign to both, and are learned.
- **The Hoarfolk** share no words with anyone south of the Scald.
- **Hewers' hand.** Miners sign in workings too loud or too foul to speak in. It is close kin to the listeners' hand-cant. Refugee hewers carried it south, and the Greys now use it as a language the quays cannot overhear.

### The deep places: evidence only

The Vaults under the Slagcrown may not be the only fitted stone in the world. There are five reports, every one has a plain reading, and none settles who built anything.

| Place | What is reported | The plain reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hob's Height and Kell Tor** | Galleries walled up and never reopened (archive) | Bad ground, sealed by sensible miners |
| **The White Deeps** | Close-jointed stone across the ore seam. A warm draught. Lamps that lean toward the crack. | Cooling rock joints itself into blocks. The sea cliffs at one of the quays show the same thing in daylight. |
| **The Sunk Stair, outermost Skerry** | Ledges like steps going down under the tideline in a sea cave | Wave-cut benches |
| **The Bald Hills, the Hoar** | Warm ground, and rings of stones the Hoarfolk say they did not raise | Frost sorts stones into rings without help |
| **Sephar's oldest temple** | Foundations set on older courses nobody can date | Masons reuse what is there |

**What the wider world adds to the shut questions.** Nothing here is an answer.

- **Who made the Vaults.** If the White Deeps wall is the same work, the makers were not only an older people of Orlen. If it is natural jointing, perhaps the first wall was too. Each report strengthens both readings at once.
- **What the orks fled.** No ork lives south of the sea, so the south has nothing to watch. The Company says no beasts came up at Halsing because nothing is there. The hewers say nothing came up at the Slagcrown either, until the wall was broken.
- **The Tenant.** Southern miners have no such word. Halsing's salt-cutters do keep one old custom: the last gallery of every level is left uncut, and they call it *the landlord's share*. Nobody remembers why.
- **The steps.** Witnesses at the White Deeps disagree about the steps exactly as the Slagcrown's did. Too shallow, say some. Too tall, say others. Frightened people in lamplight, says the surveyor.

## Part Two: The Long Account

### The game in one paragraph

One to four players are a small carrying company. In the thaw of S.31 they take eleven thousand unpaid royal receipts, and one old woman who was inside the Vaults, out of Wexhaven and around the Slate Sea. They are looking for anyone who will answer for the Crown. They find a south that is about to break its own wall. The game ends at the next thaw, back where it started, with the company writing down what was paid.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Kind of game | First-person, real-time co-op survival and expedition |
| Players | 1 to 4, friends-scale, no player-versus-player. Approved at up to four (D7). |
| A session | 60 to 120 minutes: one landing and its hearth |
| The whole | One in-world year. Roughly 25 to 35 hours, untested. |
| Camera | First person, as in the Shattered Octave build |
| Relation to Ashen Wilds | Same world, the year after. A separate game that reuses its systems. It needs nothing from Acts II and III. |

### Pillars

1. **The company is one body.** Light, noise, weight and the hearth are shared. No player can be safe alone, and no player can be careless alone.
2. **Every promise is written down with a name on it.** The shared ledger, **the Account**, is the quest log, the reputation system and the ending.
3. **You do not get stronger. The book gets longer.** There are no levels. Progress is gear, knowledge written in the warning book, and people who trust you.
4. **The world is bigger than you can save.** The season moves whether you are finished or not. You will leave places worse than you wanted to.
5. **Nothing uncanny is confirmed.** The worst thing in the game is never on screen, and the book is allowed to say *we do not know*.
6. **Only people are people.** Beasts have temperaments and counters. Every decision that matters has a human on each side of it.

### Structure: the Chart, the Ground, the Hearth

The game has three layers, and each one is frightening in a different way.

| Layer | What players do | Time passes in | Where the dread comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Chart** | Plot a leg across a hand-inked sea chart or road map. Pay for it in days, food, fuel and oil. | Days | Arithmetic. The food does not reach the next port unless someone is left behind. |
| **The Ground** | Land. Do the work in a handmade first-person level, 20 to 60 minutes. Carry out what you can. | Minutes, in real time | Light, noise, weight, and what you cannot see |
| **The Hearth** | Mend, cook, tend lungs, name the dead, write the book, argue over who comes aboard | An evening | Reading the Account, and seeing whose name is on what |

**The hearth moves.** At sea it is the company's coaster, the *Small Mercy*, and the slag-iron galley stove that lets her sail in the cold. On the road it is a handcart. In winter it is whatever roof the company has bargained for.

**The loop:**

1. At the hearth, read the Account: what is owed, to whom, by when.
2. On the Chart, choose a leg the company can afford.
3. Sail or haul. Weather fronts move across the Chart, and events on the way play out as short scenes.
4. Land, and do the work.
5. Back at the hearth, the dead are named, the book is written, and each promise is marked kept or broken.
6. The season advances.

**Kinds of landing.** Salvage (take something heavy out). Passage (get past people who would stop you). Delivery (present a receipt and see what happens). Escort (bring a person through). Witness (go and look, come back, and write it down so that two people have signed it).

**The year.** As the south counts it: the thaw is four weeks, the short dim summer ten, the harvest four, and winter is the rest. Orlen itself still gets only the thaw. The company must winter somewhere, and where it winters decides how it enters the last act.

**The Reckoner.** One player holds the tiller on the Chart. The role passes to the next player at each landfall. Anyone may object to a course, and an objection is written in the book with their name. It does nothing else.

### The company

The company is a roster of six to ten named people, and each player walks one of them at a time. The rest are aboard, tending the stove or lying in the sick berth.

**A person is four things.** A **background** (starting gear, one knack, one permanent flaw). A **dependant**: someone who is owed if this person dies. **Lungs**. And a **word**: the record of every promise they have made.

**There are no classes.** What a person does on a landing is decided by what they carry. Whoever holds the lantern cannot hold a shield.

**Backgrounds.** The eight from Ashen Wilds carry over unchanged. Eight more come from the wider world:

| Background | Knack | Flaw |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plate-reaver, turned** | Strips a stove in half the time. Knows what is worth the weight. | A known face. Wexhaven's people and the Greys start hostile. |
| **Lazaret keeper** | Reads lungs at a glance: the company sees exposure as numbers. | Will not leave the sick. Refuses an order to abandon one. |
| **Tallier's clerk** | Reads any paper. Spots a forged seal. | A weak back: carries 4 kg less. |
| **Runaway oil-bond** | Rows, swims, never fouls a line. | Still owned. Skerry boats will demand them back. |
| **Post-carver of the Weald** | Reads timber: sees a collapse coming early. Carves a name in a minute. | Will not burn a body that has not been named. |
| **Hewer's grandchild** | Full hewers' hand, including with hewers the company meets. Reads bad air. | The cough has already started. |
| **King's-ship deserter** | Handles the *Small Mercy* in weather: Chart legs cost less. | The Unpaid hang deserters. |
| **Register novice of Qesh** | Measures light. Reads tomorrow's weather half a day early. | Night-blind: helpless without a lamp. |

**Death is permanent.** A downed person who is not bandaged in time dies. Their player takes the next person on the roster at the hearth. The dead person's dependant goes into the Account as a debt, and the body becomes a problem (see *The dead*).

**Recruits.** People ask to come aboard at almost every landing. Berths and food are finite. The admissions argument from the Watchhouse comes with the company, and now the players have it with each other.

**Solo play.** Other roster members follow as companions, on Ashen Wilds' follow and hold orders.

### Co-op systems

Every system from Ashen Wilds is kept, and each is changed so that one player's choice lands on the others.

| System | Kept from Ashen Wilds | What co-op changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Light** | The torch costs you your shield and burns fuel | Somebody has to carry it. Their light reveals for everyone near them. |
| **Sight** | You see what is in front of you and lit. The map is charcoal memory. | Nobody sees behind themselves. A partner beyond your light is a dark painted figure. |
| **Noise** | The hearing table: walk 6 m, sprint 14 m, clang 16 m, halved through stone | The company is as loud as its loudest member. A cough is a noise. |
| **Weight** | 18 kg, then Burdened, then Overburdened | Two-person loads. The handcart. Carrying the wounded. |
| **Wounds** | Bandages, and no healing in the field | Only a partner can bandage a downed person |
| **Lungs** | Spores, masks, fire and wind | Exposure is per person and never goes down |
| **The dead** | Lime, fire, the spoken name | A fallen partner's body is the company's to deal with |
| **The book** | The warning book | Two witnesses make an entry. One makes a rumour. |

**Two-person loads.** The most valuable things in the game cannot be carried alone: a stove plate (about 38 kg), a barrel of receipts (30 kg), a stretcher, a strongbox. Both carriers lose their weapon hands and move at the slower one's pace. One walks forward and the other backward, so between them they see both ways. Everyone else is the escort. Most landings come down to this walk back.

**Hewers' hand.** A wheel of twelve signs is the silent way to talk: *stop, down, I hear, below, how many, leave it, carry, light, sick, my word, no, come*. Signs make no noise. To read one you must be facing the signer, and they must be lit. A hewer's grandchild gets the full language.

**Open ears (optional, off by default).** During a Dead Calm the game listens to the players' microphones and treats voices as noise in the world. With it off, voice chat is simply muffled to proximity. This is decision D9.

**Maps are compared.** Each player's charcoal map is their own. Two players who stand together can *compare*, and their maps merge. A player who went off alone comes back knowing things the others do not.

**Wounds.** A downed person bleeds on a clock. A partner's bandage stops it. After that they are walking wounded (no sprint, no heavy blow) or they are carried. They mend only at the hearth, over days on the Chart.

**Lungs.** The four stages are the fen's: the grey cough, the crust, silence, the bloom. At the cough, the person makes noise they cannot control. At the crust, stamina returns slowly. At silence, the person can only sign. Before the bloom, the company must decide where they are left.

**The dead.** A body can be limed, burned, or left. It can be named: a partner kneels and speaks the name. A body left unlimed fruits, and that landing carries a spore patch on the Chart for the rest of the game. A person who dies far gone and unlimed may be met there again.

### The Account

The Account is one book that every player can open, and it has three kinds of page.

- **Owed to us.** Wages, salvage shares, favours.
- **Owed by us.** Every promise, with the name of the person who made it. Every dependant of every dead member.
- **The receipts.** Eleven thousand of them, in barrels, as cargo with weight.

**Giving your word.** In any conversation a player can hold a button to give their word. The promise is entered under that person's name, not the company's. The other players then choose to **stand surety** (their name goes under it) or **stand apart**.

**Kept or broken.** At the next hearth each promise that has come due is marked. A person who breaks their word is **forsworn**, permanently, and everyone on the roster screen can see it. The community that was wronged stops dealing with that person. If others stood surety, it stops dealing with them too.

**Why it works in co-op.** Nobody votes. One player can promise something the others think is foolish, and the others decide how close to stand. The game never forces agreement, and it writes down every disagreement.

### Combat

Combat is Ashen Wilds' commitment melee, unchanged in its rules: startup, active, recovery, stamina, poise, durability, wall clangs and lodged weapons. Co-op adds formation.

- **Formation is the depth.** In a passage 1.7 m wide two people cannot both swing. The shield goes first, the spear thrusts past it (thrusts never clang), the light comes third, and the load comes last.
- **Mind your swing.** A sweeping stroke that touches a partner staggers them. It does not wound. This is a proposal, to be tuned in play.
- **Ranged stays thrown.** Stones and quicklime, as in the build. Anything more is the owner's decision.
- **Numbers do not make beasts tougher.** Four players face the same ork pack as two. What four players add is four mouths, four sets of lungs and a louder company.

### Enemies

**Beasts** follow the bestiary's rule: a temperament, and a counter that the warning book can teach. Most large encounters are a place and a condition, not a longer health bar.

**People** are different, and they are most of what the company meets south of the sea.

- They have **morale**. They yield, bargain, run, and come back with friends.
- They have **names and dependants**. Killing a named person writes a line in the Account under *owed by us*.
- They **remember**. A reaver the company spared at Wexhaven is in Port Sable in the summer.

### Multiplayer model

The model is the one `docs/PRODUCTION.md` already points at: small, cooperative, with the server owning every shared outcome.

- **Friends-scale.** One to four, matching decision P5's direction. Two is the first test, as it is now.
- **Server-owned.** Clients send intent. The server owns health, hits, loot, the Account and the Chart.
- **Hosting.** A listen server first, as in P4's temporary answer. The dedicated server role already exists for later.
- **Saving.** The company (roster, Account, book, Chart) is one versioned file on the server, on the `NetSave` pattern from P7.
- **Joining and leaving.** Players come and go at any hearth. If one leaves during a landing, their person becomes a companion on *hold*.
- **Absent players.** Their people stay aboard. They eat. They do not sicken or die while their player is away.
- **No voting, no kicking, no versus.** Disagreement is handled by the word, the surety and the Reckoner.

### The look

The look is the Shattered Octave build's, taken from the owner's four reference images: a first-person view of a low-resolution 3D world, people painted as flat figures, and two hands at the bottom of the screen.

**What each reference establishes.**

| Reference | What it shows | What this game takes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oakhaven, before and after | True geometry at low resolution: timber frames, then roofs, gables and windows | Buildings are real 3D and can be entered. Detail comes from silhouette, not from sharper textures. |
| The Whisper-Grove | A still water plane that mirrors the sky, and one huge dark shape on the skyline | Mirror water, and one unlit landmark for every land |
| Still paintings, then the same paintings walking | People are painted figures with walk cycles and soft ground shadows | Every person in the game is one of these |
| Port Cantabile | Two hands, a bottom panel with gold lettering, a compass strip, a status box, a place and weather line | The whole frame, with its contents changed |

The four reference images are the owner's and are not stored in this repository.

**From Shattered Octave to dread.** The rendering stays. What it is pointed at changes.

| In the references | In this game |
| --- | --- |
| Clear midday and long views | The Dim. A low sky the colour of slate or old brick. The far wall of haze is ash, and weather brings it closer. |
| The left hand holds a cold glowing light | The left hand holds the light: a lantern or a torch, or a shield in its place. Nothing glows that does not burn. |
| The right hand holds a weapon | The same. A wind-up lifts the weapon out of frame, so a committed blow is one you watch yourself commit to. |
| Painted people | People only. They are painted, they walk, and they face a way. |
| No beasts shown | Beasts are true 3D models put through the same low-resolution pass. They turn and take light unlike any person on screen. This is D8. |
| Mirror water | The Slate Sea in a Dead Calm, flooded holds, the fen in thaw. Still water is the one way to see behind you without turning. |
| A dark shape on the skyline | A Citadel, the Lazaret hulks, the Standing Dead. Large, far, and never lit. |

**First person does the work the vision cone did.** The isometric build had to draw what the player could not see. Here the player simply cannot see it.

- **Nothing is behind you until you turn.** A partner's lantern at your back throws your own shadow down the passage ahead.
- **A two-person load faces both ways.** One carrier walks forward and the other walks backward. Between them they see everything, and neither can fight.
- **Signs must be looked at.** Hewers' hand is made with the hands on screen. To read it you face your partner, and they must be lit.
- **Partners are paintings too.** At the edge of the light, a painted figure standing still could be anyone.
- **Painted people must be readable.** A wind-up changes the outline. Burdened changes the walk. A cough can be seen from across a room.

**The panel becomes a ledger.** It keeps the reference's frame and gold lettering, and plain ASCII text as the canon sheet requires.

| On the reference panel | In this game |
| --- | --- |
| Three colour bars | Blood, breath, load |
| The thin bar beside them | Lungs: a grey bar that only ever fills |
| The black gauge with marker lines | Noise: how far you can be heard at this moment |
| CHAR, INV, MAP, LOG | COMPANY, PACK, MAP, BOOK |
| SONG, MURMUR | SIGNS (hewers' hand) and WORD (give your word) |
| NAMES | NAMES: the roll of the company's dead, and whether each was named |
| REST, SAVE | REST, at a hearth only. Saving belongs to the server. |
| The status box: STILL, the weapon, the day and date | Stance as noise (STILL, WALKING, BURDENED), what each hand holds, the date in S-dating |
| The place and weather line | The same: *Wexhaven - Dead Calm* |
| Not present | ACCOUNT: opens the shared ledger |

No panel pauses the world. Reading the Account on a landing means standing still with your eyes down while someone else watches the door.

**The map is charcoal.** The MAP screen is drawn in charcoal as each player walks, and what they have not seen is blank paper. This is where Ashen Wilds' charcoal memory now lives.

| --- | --- | --- |

### The story

The company carries two things south: a debt nobody will pay, and a warning nobody can prove. The story is what each one costs to deliver.

**The debt.** In S.1 and S.2 the Crown took the fen's grain against paper receipts: *to be repaid in kind, by order of the King*. **Gytha Marrin** was a customs tallywoman at Wexhaven when the fleet sailed. For twenty-eight years she has taken a receipt from everyone who walked down the Salt Trace, and written them a line in exchange. She has eleven thousand. She is at the crust stage of ash-lung and cannot go herself.

**The warning.** Ebba Cray's survey went through the breach in S.0, and four came back. **Marget Sayle** is one of the four. She is sixty-two and has never described what she saw. Reavers' talk has reached her that the south is digging under its salt. She asks for passage.

Both of them have weight. The receipts are barrels in the hold. Marget cannot walk far and coughs in the cold. Keeping an old woman alive across a hungry world is the escort that runs under the whole game.

**The commission**, in Gytha's words: *Present them to whoever answers for the Crown. Bring back what is paid. If nothing is paid, bring back the name of who refused.*

### Act I: the Burned Quay

*The thaw of S.31. About six hours. The question: what does the world owe the people it shut out?*

1. **The stove.** The first landing is the drowned hull of the royal ship *Constance* in Wexhaven's inlet. The company lifts her slag-iron galley stove at low water. It teaches the two-person load, the tide as a clock, and the hulk-conger.
2. **The reavers.** Corvin Hale's crew is stripping the upper town. The company can fight them, trade with them (they sell a chart of the harbour chains), or sail in their wake and say nothing.
3. **The barrels and the witness.** Gytha's commission. Marget's request. The first admissions argument: she is one more mouth and she is sick.
4. **The crossing.** The first Dead Calm at sea. The Follower, if anyone aboard is dying. The first choice about a body.
5. **The Forty.** Every northern hull must lie forty days at the Lazaret Rocks. The company can wait and lose a third of the summer. It can buy a clean bill with a stove plate or with receipts. Or it can run the chain at night, and be outlaws on the Sable Shore from then on.

### Act II: the Round

*The short summer and the harvest. About fifteen hours, in any order. The question: who answers for a debt when the debtor has become three courts and a rumour?*

Three places answer for the Crown. Each offers something different for the receipts, and each has a hand on the White Deeps.

| Where | What is offered | What it means | Its hand on the Deeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Exchange, Port Sable** | Silver, at a hundredth. Enough grain to feed the fen parishes for one winter, delivered. | The receipts become claims the buyers will press for land. The fen is fed, and sold. | Company shares are the best-performing paper on the floor |
| **Saint-Aumere** | Osbert's seal on every receipt: payment in full, upon restoration. And a writ to carry north. | Paper for paper. The writ reasserts the old assessments on the people who are owed. | Vauclere levies the hewers |
| **The Garden of Hours** | The Guest has nothing. He offers to write *I owe this* and sign it. | It is worth nothing at the Exchange. It is the only thing Gytha asked for. If he is the King. | Sephar's priests have blessed the digging as the recovery of hidden day |

**The scene the act is built around.** The company hands the Guest one receipt. It is the one from Merewick, with *Still waiting. S.19.* written under it in charcoal. He asks whose hand that is. The game never tells the players whether he is Aldric IV.

**Side arcs**, each a landing or two:

- **The Skerries.** Buying out one oil-bond, and learning what the keep has cost.
- **The Weald.** Cutting a steading's Swathe before the Brand-wind, with no fire allowed.
- **The Unpaid.** A king's ship demands the King's toll, and has a claim on the receipts as good as anyone's.
- **The Limewalks.** The dead-cart crews stop work. The quays find out what the Greys were for.
- **The two glasses.** Carrying Qesh's smoked glass to Marrah, so the registers are compared for the first time. Half the disagreement turns out to be the instruments. The other half remains.

**The turn.** At the harvest the seam at the White Deeps meets the wall. The news reaches the company wherever it is. Marget says: *now*.

**Wintering.** The company chooses a roof: the Limewalks, the guest-house at Saint-Aumere, or the bunkhouses at Halsing. Each roof is a set of obligations, and each enters Act III through a different door.

### Act III: the eleven days

*Winter into the thaw of S.32. About eight hours. The question: what do the living owe people they will never meet?*

**The case for breaking the wall** is Ines Corrant's, and it is made in numbers. Forty thousand people in the valley towns heat with wood, and the wood went to the terraces for rye. Last winter's dead are in her ledger by parish. The ore holds heat for hours. She is not in debt and she is not proud. She has counted.

**The case against** is Edith Brode's. Unknown ground, wrong air, the right of refusal. The Company's charter recognises no such right, and her Lodge is unlawful.

**The clock.** Fire-setting begins at the thaw. It took eleven days to break the wall at the Slagcrown, and the Company's engineers have planned for the same. Each day, each person in the company can do one thing.

**Marget before the Lodge.** This is the hinge of the game. The hewers ask what she saw. She will not describe it. She says what she is willing to say, and it is short. Corrant's answer is fair: *then you have told us nothing.* The players cannot make Marget say more, and neither can the designers. A warning without proof is all the north has.

### The endings

None of them saves the world. Each one decides who pays for a winter.

| Ending | What it needs | What it costs | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The refusal** | The Lodge strikes and the town's carters and bakers stand with it. Needs Marget heard, and a spokesman who is not forsworn. | The valley winters cold, and the game gives the count. The Company evicts the hewers' families in spring. | Prove the wall mattered. Next year there is another steward. |
| **On their own hazard** | The charter amended on Orlen's old clause: volunteers, double pay, and the dependants' covenant paid in advance from the Account's silver. A gate built before the breach. | The Account is spent on strangers. The wall is opened on the twelfth day. | Show what the draught brings. The game ends on the lamps. |
| **Drown it** | Break the pumps on a Dead Calm night. The loudest work in the game. | The ore is under water for years. The hewers lose their wages and are blamed. The company is hunted in three realms and can never present the Account. | Prove anything was behind the stone |
| **Sell and sail** | Sell the receipts at the Exchange before the thaw | Grain ships go north. The fen eats for a winter, and Daw and Sturry hold its land claims. The company was not at Halsing. | Answer Gytha's question |
| **Go home** | Only the voyage back | Whatever the company was paid goes north: a signature, a sealed paper, or nothing, with lime and seed rye bought on the way. | Stop anything |

**The last scene** is the same in every ending. The Burned Quay, the thaw of S.32, and Gytha Marrin's chair, with her in it or not. The players write the Account's last line themselves, in three blanks:

*Paid: \_\_\_\_. Owing: \_\_\_\_. Refused by: \_\_\_\_.*

The King is never proven. What is under the White Deeps is never shown.

### Rules of dread

These are the rules that keep the tone, and every scene should pass all eight.

1. **The worst thing is never on screen.** The Follower is a bump on the hull. The deep is a draught.
2. **Every uncanny sign arrives with its plain cause.** The surveyor is in the room when the lamp leans.
3. **The book may say *we do not know*.** No character in the game knows more than the book.
4. **Silence is scheduled.** A Dead Calm cannot be skipped, slept through or fast-forwarded.
5. **Loss is slow and has a name.** Lungs, promises and the roster only ever get worse.
6. **No music on the Ground.**
7. **One knock, once.** No scare is ever repeated.
8. **Your partners are the uncertainty.** The dark figure at the edge of your light is probably them.

### First playable

The smallest build that proves the idea is the Wexhaven stove lift, for two players.

- One scene: the inlet, the drowned *Constance*, the quay above.
- One two-person load, carried over the existing network layer.
- One beast (the hulk-conger) and three people with morale (reavers).
- One promise given, with surety, and marked at the hearth.
- One death and one succession.

If painted people read well by one lantern in first person, the look works. If two players enjoy carrying a stove while a third watches the stairs, the game works.
