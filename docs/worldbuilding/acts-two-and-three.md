# Acts II and III: the Bell Road and the Citadels

> **Note, 2026-11-11 (owner decisions O3, O4, O5).** The isometric game this story was written for is retired. The story stands as lore: what may have happened in the winter of S.30. Its "Needs:" lines and hearing figures are notes for the retired build. The Long Account opens at the thaw that ends this story and assumes none of its choices or endings. See `CANON.md`, *Reading the older files after O3, O4 and O5*.

**Status: PROPOSED** (Godot addition, 2026-11-05). Nothing here is implemented, and nothing here is canon until the owner says so. Precedence is the one in `CANON.md`, with the owner's rule O1 above everything in this folder.

**Summary.** The whole game runs through one winter, S.30, and ends at the thaw. In Act I the survivor makes one roof hold. In Act II that roof turns out to be only as good as the road it stands on. The road the survivor opened fills up with people who left thirty years ago and now want their claims back, while the north empties of animals and people running south. In Act III the survivor walks up the Bell Road to the Highland Citadels, where everything first went wrong, and finds a place that answers nothing. There are four endings: seal the Vaults, burn them, leave them alone, or go south. None of them saves the world, and each one decides who pays for the winter.

**Owner rule O1 and what it changes.** Only humans are people. Goblins, orks, ogres, trolls and every other creature are animals or monsters: no speech, no names, no culture, no bargains. This file follows that rule wherever the archive does not:

- The **Gutter Kin** are a human household of road waifs who grew up sheltering under the Roofed Mile and chose one another as kin (the archive's revised files). Others call them *gutter*; to the Remnant they are squatters and thieves. The glasshouse clearing stays the archive's negotiated evacuation between humans. The Act II goblin nest is in Halwen's lower culvert instead.
- Saint Vey's Fold has **no ork families**. Its rear range is an **ork den**. There is no trading stone.
- The ork hold on the Old Causeway in Act I is a **pack of beasts carrying stolen arms**, not a road-clan. `CANON.md` fact 14 needs the same correction.
- "What the orks fled" stays unexplained, in the way it stays unexplained why animals flee. The orks are evidence. They are never witnesses.

**Conventions.** Every quest-giver, faction and decision-maker in this file is human. *Needs:* marks a game system that does not exist yet. Hearing figures are the Godot build's (walk 6 m, search 5 m, dodge 9 m, block 10 m, swing 12 m, sprint 14 m, clang 16 m, about half through stone). In-game text quoted here uses plain ASCII.

## 1. The shape of the whole story

**Act I: the Outskirts (early winter).** Another file in this folder covers Act I's places. In brief: the survivor reaches the ruined Wayfarer's Watchhouse at the Causeway's fen end and makes it a shelter. That means lighting the hearth and gathering what the fire and the people around it need, including the **Cast-Iron Cooking Pot** from the ogre's bone heap. It also means cutting Edda free, starting the warning book again and keeping the doorway at 1.35 m. The act closes when the survivor breaks the **ork pack** that has held the Causeway tower since the Watch lost it in autumn S.29. Its question is narrow: **what do you owe the people under your roof?**

**Act II: the Bell Road (midwinter).** With the Causeway open, carts run again, and everything that rides on them reaches the Watchhouse first. The Watchhouse needs lime for the dead, clean cloth for bandages and something to make its fuel last, so the survivor goes north up the Bell Road. There they find the Last Gardens running short of water, and Halwen House above it, divided between gardeners and the household of a southern claimant. The road's stops live off one another: the kiln, the inn, the bell-tender. At Saint Vey's Fold a priest from the south wants his hospice back. Meanwhile the animals are moving the wrong way. The ork pack in the Fold leaves its den and goes south in silence, and Coldbarrow's people come down the road asking who came with you. The act ends when letters go south, carried across the Causeway the survivor opened. Its question: **what are the people who left owed when they come back, and what do the people who stayed owe each other?**

**Act III: the Citadels (late winter into the thaw).** The survivor goes north past Coldbarrow to fallen Greystair, where a company of smelters has relit a furnace and the noise carries for miles. Past Greystair lie the venting slopes, the listeners under the hills and the empty Slagcrown. Beyond them is the mouth of the Deep-Seam Vaults: a half-built gate, a roll of miners nobody answered for, and evidence about what the orks fled that never becomes an answer. Its question: **what did the Slag-Lords owe the people they sent down, and what does anyone owe the people who will come after?**

**The theme across all three acts** is the canon one: obligation, after the people owed protection were left. Each act widens the circle. First comes one household, then a road of households that need each other and resent it, and last the realm's original debt, unpaid since S.0. Each act ends with a choice about who carries a cost. The game never lets anyone stop carrying one. Spring comes in every ending, barely, as it always does. Some people will say the ash is thinning, and no one can prove it.

## 2. Act II: the Bell Road

### The situation

The first week after the Causeway pack falls brings three things to the Watchhouse:

1. **The dead.** The fight at the tower left bodies on the causeway: orks, the Watch's own dead from last autumn and the Ashbound the pack had killed. Within days corpse-caps begin to fruit on them. Burning them would take fuel the hearth cannot spare.
2. **Traffic.** Brackenreach carters want passage north to the Split Kiln and Rook's Stair. A Remnant rider wants to go south with letters. Every one of them stops at the Watchhouse fire and asks to be let in.
3. **Heat-stones.** A Coldbarrow carter is selling **heat-stones**: fist-sized lumps of bloom-iron from Greystair. Fired at dusk, slag-iron holds heat until morning, so a stone in the hearth saves a third of a night's fuel. The carter says there will be more if the road holds. This is the player's first contact with the Bloomery Company, through its goods.

The Watchhouse needs **lime** (the Split Kiln), **clean cloth** (the Last Gardens) and **heat-stones** (from up the road). All three lie up the Bell Road.

*Needs:* dialogue with choices; a quest log that records promises in plain words; a **lime** item (spread on a body or a corpse-cap patch with E; thrown as a short white cloud); a **handcart** (a moving container, loud on stone, that cannot leave the road or pass a doorway); **human enemies** on the Ashbound body with their own temperaments.

### Main quests

#### II.1 Lime the Dead

- **Where:** the Old Causeway, the Last Gardens (on the way), the Split Kiln and the chalk cut behind it.
- **Who:** the Split Kiln's three intermarried households, who will not let anyone untrained fire the working kiln (the dead worker's tools still lie where he left them). The fuel driver who delivered sound timber over wet (side quest II.h).
- **What happens:** the kiln has lime but not enough fuel or stone, because the Roofed Mile detour has doubled what carriers charge. It will sell lime for fuel, or for stone carried down from the chalk cut. The chalk cut is a quarry-troll's ground.
- **The decision:** how to pay, and who gets the lime. There are three ways to pay. Fuel from the Watchhouse store dims the hearth for the rest of the act. Stone from the chalk cut means walking past the troll. Or the player can take the driver's wet timber off his hands, so the kiln is not cheated and he can go home. Whatever is paid, the lime covers only one site at first: either the causeway's dead or the Watchhouse's own grave ground, where Act I's Ashbound were laid.
- **Systems:** *weight*: a sack of lime is 20 kg against an 18 kg pack, so a lime run is a Burdened run that clatters and is heard 35% further. *Hearing*: the chalk-cut troll pounds wherever it heard the last sound, so the survivor carries stone in a cinder-squall, when it rests, or while the kiln is firing (see section 4). *Spores*: corpse-cap patches puff spore clouds when they are walked through, a torch burns them off at the cost of fuel, and lime clears them for good.
- **Consequences:** limed ground stays clean for the rest of the game. Ground left unlimed becomes a permanent corpse-cap field: if it is the causeway, the toll-house stretch chokes every passing cart; if it is the grave ground, the field lies by the Watchhouse door. If the player carried stone, the kiln households remember it and sell lime at the old price for the rest of the game. If the player paid in Watchhouse fuel, the Watchhouse fire burns lower until the first heat-stone comes.

#### II.2 The Cistern and the House

- **Where:** the Last Gardens (the paved cistern courtyard, the waiting gates); Halwen House (the garden office, the service passage).
- **Who:** Sella Morn (water), Nera Halwen (seed and the old plot names), Bren Tal (straps and the gate); the Garden Hands under Dema Rusk; the Halwen Remnant under Serjeant Oren Vale.
- **What happens:** the Gardens are the Watchhouse's only source of clean cloth, which means bandages. Their cistern is falling, and Sella has stopped selling. The archive's three causes all hold: a damaged feeder channel, a failed seam in the cistern lining, and one household washing cloth in secret. The player has to find the plan in the garden office (the door is swollen shut and the papers are a fused mass), follow the feeder channel through the service passage and find where the water goes. None of those three discoveries fixes the others. The Remnant has placed a man by the inspection point in the service yard.
- **The decision:** who holds the water inspection points: both groups together, the Hands, or the Remnant. A second decision is whether to name the washing household at the evening pot or settle it privately with Sella. A third is the culvert nest: blade or fire.
- **Systems:** *cramped quarters*: the service passage is narrow enough that sweeps clang. Below it, where the feeder channel runs into the old lower culvert, **goblins have nested**: they drag leather, metal and cloth into it and have choked the channel with their hoard, which is part of the water loss. The nest is cleared with thrusts in a culvert a metre wide, or burned out with lamp oil, which ruins the channel's old gaskets and sends smoke up through the service wing. *Searching* the garden office is heard 5 m away, through a wall in the Remnant's hearing. *The vision cone* in an unlit passage makes the torch a working tool, not a light to see by.
- **Consequences:** shared inspection gives the Watchhouse bandages at a fair rate, and Sella goes on resenting being watched. With the Remnant holding the points, bandages come only after the Watchhouse's name is entered on Elsin Thale's household roll (see section 5). With the Hands holding them, Dema's party grows harder on casual workers, and Noll Fen loses his place below. Whatever the outcome, something visible stays: a repaired channel, a marked water measure, or a lock on a door.

#### II.3 The Glasshouse Clearing

The archive's negotiated evacuation, used as written: `glasshouse-clearing.md`, its complications, the goods interception and the Hesh-Oren scenes.

- **Where:** Halwen's glasshouse range and maintenance trench; the chapel court; the loading platform by the carriage drive; the Old Charcoal Lodge on the lower track.
- **Who:** the **Gutter Kin**: Hesh Split-Lip, Sere (whose hip will not take the breach route), Kett, Brik, Iss and Moth. The Remnant: Oren, Rauth Kell, Lenna Marr, Elsin Thale and Perrin Holt. The Grey Lash: Marda Cale, Tern Roke and Vey Sorn. The Garden Hands, and Garran Wold, the salvage worker the Kin cut in the trench, whose boots Iss took.
- **What happens:** the Remnant wants the range. The Kin want out, but only with all six, a frame for Sere and a shelter to go to. The terms are agreed at the chapel court as the archive sets them, with Elsin reading them aloud and Hesh making her repeat them. Oren keeps the route open and limits the inspection. The Kin name their hazards and return the listed goods. The Grey Lash take all six to the lodge, not just to the court. Garran's claim is negotiated separately ("apology is not footwear"). Before anyone moves, Moth finds Kett's hidden bundle with one of Sere's tools in it.
- **The player's part:** inspect the lodge with Kett and Vey first. Recheck the route, which brings out Kett's lie about the collapsed fallback. Carry. Stand between the parties during the inspection. Keep the weather window.
- **The decision:** what to do at each of the archive's three core complications. *Garran arrives* wanting to know who settled his loss without him. *Brik challenges the load*, the hidden bundles adding up past what he agreed to carry. *Elsin finds an estate mark* on one of Sere's fittings. Then the interception at the platform: Rauth lifts Moth's bundle out of reach and calls it custody. The player can read the approved load aloud, demand a working substitute, back Marda when she stops work, or let the custody stand.
- **Systems:** *weight*: the frame, Sere's tools and the agreed load are real kilograms. Brik's limit is the pack rule applied to someone else's back. *The vision cone and hearing*: the Kin's warning lines of loose metal ring when tripped, raising ripples, and the covers in the trench give way. *Weather*: ash and wind narrow the working window, so the move has to be timed. *Companions*: Vey Sorn or Brik can walk with the survivor for the carry. *Footing*: the glass beds count as grease in sleet.
- **Consequences:** as the archive has them, success is real and not shared equally. All six reach the lodge. Garran stays angry. Brik resents Iss. Elsin keeps the estate's claim. Oren resents having had to restrain Rauth. If the custody stood, the Kin reach the lodge without Moth's rain fitting, and Moth may refuse a shelter now under-equipped. The Remnant gains the range without a battle, and the Gardens get fewer thefts and a stronger Remnant above them. Marda remembers whether the survivor kept the load honest, which decides whether the Grey Lash haul for the survivor in Act III. The Kin know the Roofed Mile from below: if they trust the survivor, Hesh describes the gallery's roof space before II.4, the way someone describes a room they lost.

#### II.4 The Third Arch

The archive's Roofed Mile story becomes a quest that follows its route.

- **Where:** the Roofed Mile; the Bell-Tender's House at the quarry junction.
- **Who:** Old Edrin, who never was a priest and keeps his bell's tongue wrapped; the warning board Annet's party sent to the Watchhouse ("ROOFED MILE. Occupied. DO NOT ENTER FOR SHELTER. A man died beyond the fifth pier. We do not know his name."); the **gallery-crawler**, an animal nobody can identify.
- **What happens:** the road north goes through the gallery. Edrin knows the sheep-track detour, and it is not safe for a loaded cart. The Split Kiln's carriers have been paying for the detour since the board went up.
- **The decision:** go through, go round, or burn it out.
  - *Through* means the authored encounter (section 4).
  - *Round* costs a day and everything that will not fit on a back.
  - *Burning out* means firing the cached carcasses between the piers with lamp oil, which the kiln and the Remnant would both pay toward. It may drive the animal onto the open road, and in hoarfrost it walks there anyway.
- **Systems:** *the vision cone*: it drops from the roof space, which is above the survivor and outside the cone, so the only warning is sound. Its claws click once on stone, and an unseen sound raises a ripple. *Noise as a lure*: striking a pier, as Annet did, draws it to the pier. *Lime*: a thrown sack makes it back off coughing. *Fire*: a pool of oil and a torch makes it cry out and withdraw. *Stone*: keeping a pier between the survivor and the animal buys time, and it waits for the survivor to step out. *Companions*: it goes for whoever is weakest and furthest from stone, which is usually the companion.
- **Consequences:** driven off, the gallery shelters travellers for the rest of the act, and the board's first line can be painted out or left. Whether it can be painted out is the player's call, and the book records who made it. Whether the animal survived stays an open question: in hoarfrost something still walks the upper road, pauses, and drags something over stone. The remains beyond the fifth pier start side quest II.g.

#### II.5 The Fold

- **Where:** Saint Vey's Fold, the walled hospice round a court.
- **Who:** **Brother Coll Hathe**, appointed by the Keeping's southern hierarchy to "recover" the hospice, with two hired escorts and a certified copy of the Fold's endowment; the human travellers who shelter in the front dormitory; Edrin's runner.
- **What happens:** for years an **ork pack** has denned in the stone rear range, behind a chapel entrance that someone blocked with dressed stone. Nobody knows who: the last hospice keepers, an early traveller, a Watch patrol. Travellers sleep in the timber-roofed front dormitory, and the orks never come into it. Animals den under stone, and timber is not stone. Whether there is more to it than that, nobody can say. Travellers keep to the front, the pack keeps to the rear, and nobody crosses the court at night. Brother Coll wants the rear range cleared, the chapel unblocked, a priest in residence and the dormitory assessed for the Keeping's dues. He asks the survivor to clear the den. The travellers ask the survivor not to, because a cleared den is a fight in the court on the night it goes wrong.
- **The decision:** clear the den for Coll; refuse, and decide whether to stop his escorts when they try it themselves; or wall the court so the pack keeps the rear and the dormitory stays usable, which kills Coll's claim to the whole hospice.
- **Systems:** *orks* in a stone range: their opening slash clangs off the walls and their overhead chop does not. A guard held too long draws the shield bash. *Armour*: the hauberk and splinted greaves exist for the chop. *Hearing*: orks have keen ears, so a den goes quiet before the survivor sees it. In a Dead Calm they go utterly silent, and with no ripples the cone is all the survivor has. *Weather*: in a cinder-squall the pack moves under the noise.
- **The evidence:** whatever the choice, the den is found half empty. The pack has been leaving at night in Dead Calms, heading **south**. The plunder pile holds things from the north: Coldbarrow chain, a Citadel tally board, a miner's lamp with its wick cut. That is all it says. Animals leave a place for reasons, and these ones cannot give them.
- **Consequences:** with the den cleared, Coll installs a priest, and travellers start paying a measure of salt to sleep under a roof they used to have free. With the court walled, Coll writes south that the Fold was refused to the Keeping, which reaches II.6. Either way, orks reappear on the Old Causeway in Act III, coming down the Bell Road, and the tower the survivor took in Act I has to be held again or given up.

#### II.6 The Report South (the act's end)

- **Where:** Halwen House, the Watchhouse, the Old Causeway's broken crossing, and Brackenreach: the broad bridge, the Upper Row behind its shopfront barricades, and the head of the Salt Trace.
- **Who:** Sabeth Rill, with her reconnaissance notes; Oren Vale, with his report; Perrin Holt, the Remnant's drover, who asks to carry the letters because his sister lives in the south; Brother Coll, with his letter; **Piers Ferrant**, who sells ladder passage and salvage rights in the Upper Row.
- **What happens:** every letter going south has to cross the Causeway, and the Causeway is open because of the survivor. The letters will decide what the south sends up the Bell Road in spring. If the player reads Lady Maudren Halwen's orders (in Oren's chest, or quoted by Sabeth), they learn why she wants the house. The Halwens' wealth was always a share of the Bell Road's traffic, and the Bell Road's traffic was slag-iron. She wants more than the house: she wants the road to the Citadels open again.
- **The decision:** what the south is told. The player can carry Sabeth's report as she wrote it, which counts the gardeners' improvements and so proves both their claim and the estate's renewed value. They can carry Oren's report, or stop the letters at the crossing, or let Perrin go alone. Reading a sealed letter is possible, and it is noticed.
- **Systems:** an *escort* across the Causeway (Perrin as a companion, on the companion system). *Greymaw* on the verges find wounds by smell, so bandaging quickly matters. Orks moving south cross the broken crossing in a Dead Calm. At Brackenreach the Upper Row pulls its ladders in at dark, so a late arrival sleeps outside.
- **Consequences**, which carry into Act III and the endings:
  - The *full report* brings a larger household in spring, with a surveyor for the upper road. Halwen becomes a service estate, and the Watchhouse is asked to register as a Crown waystation under Lady Maudren's "storage rights".
  - *Sabeth's honest report* brings a small party, and the title dispute continues.
  - If the *letters are stopped*, the Remnant is recalled for lack of news, or splits: Rauth strips the house, Lenna takes the civilians south, and Elsin's records blame the Gardens.
  - *Coll's letter* brings a second priest, or a refusal written into the southern books.
- **The act's last scene:** at the Watchhouse, the admissions argument the book has every winter, this time over refugees from Coldbarrow. Edrin's runner arrives with one sentence: "The north road is going quiet." No heat-stones have come for two weeks.

### Side quests

**II.a The Waiting Gates.** *Last Gardens.* Darnel Cross's well has failed, and travellers queue at the Gardens' gates with empty containers while Sella holds back the water. Bren wants a common guest platform that belongs to no household, and nobody will give up sleeping space for it. *Decide:* build it from Seasoned Timber, which needs carrying in at the 75% load line, and choose whose ground it stands on, or build a covered waiting shelter outside the walls instead. *Systems:* weight, hauling. *Consequence:* the platform gives Noll Fen a place that does not depend on a household's favour. The shelter keeps the gates humane and the beds closed.

**II.b Shore the Narrow Door.** *Halwen kitchen.* The pantry lintel the Hands sleep under is failing, and the sound timber lies in the Remnant's dining room under the fallen beam. *Decide:* negotiate its release, steal it, supply timber from elsewhere, or move the Hands out of the pantry. *Systems:* dragging timber from under the beam brings dust, cracking and visible movement before any collapse, and a survivor who ignores the warning is caught under it. Weight. *Consequence:* a braced door survives the clearing's complication 7, when the Hands need the same gear as the Kin, and the pantry stops being a trap.

**II.c The Missing Rope.** *Halwen kitchen.* A Remnant retainer spots his rope under Jerrit Salk's bedding during an agreed inspection and demands a search. Noll saw Jerrit bring the rope in from the front court. *Decide:* return it, pay for it without conceding the right to search, refuse the search, or tell Dema what Noll saw. *Consequence:* the boundary holds or breaks, and the Hands' trust in each other gets smaller either way.

**II.d The Upstairs Shutter.** *Halwen's upper floor.* Sabeth asks one thing: "Do not let him search the upper rooms until I have taken my things." Her notes describe which cottages are occupied and where their water comes from. *Decide:* return them to her, give them to the Gardens, or give them to Oren. *Systems:* the house's back stair and the records tower stair have a broken tread that a survivor carrying a chest cannot step over, so this is a weight check. The upper floor is a cone-and-ripple stealth room while Rauth walks the hall below. *Consequence:* the notes' contents decide what Sabeth's report can say in II.6.

**II.e The Broken Yoke.** *Halwen's carriage drive.* A Remnant pack animal lames itself on the paving. Perrin asks to borrow a Gardens cart, and its owner wants it written down that the loan creates no obligation. *Decide:* broker a bounded loan, find other hauling, or watch Rauth seize the cart. *Consequence:* without a cart Perrin cannot carry the letters in II.6, and the Remnant learns whether its word binds it when it is inconvenient.

**II.f The Split Beam.** *Darnel Cross.* The inn's main beam has split, and Ossa Denn dates the widening crack in charcoal while seven households sleep under it. *Decide:* help take the upper storey down, which yields timber but might bring the building down, or prop the beam and leave the danger in place. *Systems:* news buys a lower price here, so what the player has heard on the road becomes something to trade. *Consequence:* Darnel Cross either stays the last pack-animal stop on the road or becomes a timber yard.

**II.g The Copper Repair.** *Roofed Mile, Rook's Stair, the Split Kiln.* The man beyond the fifth pier had a pack saddle with one strap mended in copper wire. Asking along the road finds **Edwy Pole**, who carried stone to the Split Kiln from Rook's Stair, and his widow on the Stair's middle terraces. *Decide:* bring her the saddle, which is heavy, or only his name. Then decide whether the warning board's last line is corrected. *Consequence:* the warning book gets the line Annet wanted: "A man died beyond the fifth pier. His name was Edwy Pole." That is the side quest's whole reward. Saying a dead man's name is a Keeping custom, and it is what this game has instead of treasure.

**II.h The Wet Timber.** *The Split Kiln.* The driver who brought the kiln sound wood laid over wet swears he received it already bound, and he cannot afford to take it back. His child sleeps under the seat. *Decide:* who bears the loss: the kiln, the driver, or the survivor's fuel store. Or trace the load back to whoever bound it, a day's walk south. *Consequence:* the payment options in II.1 change.

**II.i The Roof Drain.** *The Bell-Tender's House.* Edrin has the tools to clear his roof drainage but can no longer climb. *Decide:* clear it, which is a climb in weather, and then ask him about the bell or not. He explains its wrapped tongue only as "noise reaches unwanted listeners". He means people and animals alike, and he does not claim to know more than that. *Consequence:* he gives the survivor the sheep-track detour and his network of runners, so news reaches the Watchhouse in Act III. He also asks whether the Watchhouse means to hang a bell. That question goes home with the survivor (section 5).

**II.j The Hanging Shed.** *Rook's Stair.* The lifting shed's machinery still works, and no resident claims to have repaired it. Someone works it at night for food left at the top. That someone is **Brida Coyle**. Five winters ago the Stair called her Ashbound and drove her out when she stopped answering. She did not die. Whether she was in the third stage of ash-lung and came back, or was starving and lost, the book cannot tell and she will not say. *Decide:* tell the Stair, bring her to the Watchhouse, or leave the arrangement as it is. *Systems:* the shed is reached by the Stair's narrow upper flights, and the rope can lift what a survivor cannot carry, which matters in Act III. *Consequence:* if she comes to the Watchhouse, the admissions argument has a face. The book records who called her Ashbound, and who stopped.

**II.k The Lower Market.** *Brackenreach.* Good wheelwright timber lies under a failed roof in the flooded Lower Market. Piers Ferrant sells access, but paying does not stop anyone else from paying too. *Systems:* harrow eels in the flooded cellars, a roof that telegraphs before it falls, and a heavy load to get back over a broken bridge. *Consequence:* a sound axle and wheel for the handcart the survivor takes north in Act III.

**II.l The Revised Register.** *Merewick and the Watchhouse.* A flood-damaged Causeway parish register turns up among the Watchhouse's salvage. One sheet lists households admitted to the waystation in S.3, later struck through as "departed". An older sheet records the same households as refused. The Merewick record-keeper can read the hand and date the ink, and she charges in salt for paper. *Decide:* copy both pages into the warning book, burn them, or find the families' descendants (two live at Merewick and one at the Nine Stakes). *Consequence:* the Watchhouse's own past becomes part of the admissions argument. The register proves the account was revised. It does not say who revised it.

### Companions in Act II

Edda stays. The companion system has room for one more at a time. Depending on the player's choices, the second can be **Noll Fen** (if the guest platform stands and the Hands have turned hard), **Vey Sorn** (if the survivor kept the Grey Lash load honest), **Kett** (if the Kin reached the lodge; quick and soft-footed, and he will leave when he decides to), **Perrin Holt** (for II.6 only) or **Brida Coyle** (if she came to the Watchhouse; she is quiet, and her companion noise is the lowest in the game). Every one of them is human, and every one has somewhere else to be.

## 3. Act III: the Citadels

### The situation

Coldbarrow's people are coming south. The heat-stones have stopped, and the coldest weeks are still ahead. The Fold's ork pack and others like it are crossing the Causeway at night. The runners say the **listeners** have come out from under the hills to beg the smelters at Greystair to stop their hammering. If the full report went south, Lady Maudren's surveyor is already on the upper road, marking the Black Seam for spring.

What sends the survivor north is plain: the fuel will not last without heat-stones, the Causeway cannot be held against animals coming down the road, and somebody ought to see why they are coming. Nothing calls the survivor north, and nothing foretells them.

### Main quests

#### III.1 Take Nothing You Cannot Carry

- **Where:** the Bent Span, Coldbarrow.
- **Who:** the household of four who keep the Bent Span with a winch, who really repair it and really overcharge; Coldbarrow's storehouse remnant; scavenging parties; Tern Roke with the Grey Lash's ropes, if Marda will lend him.
- **What happens:** a fallen tree is catching debris against one of the Span's piers, and the upstream bank is moving. Nobody has volunteered to go down and free it. The handcart has to cross the Span to reach Coldbarrow. Coldbarrow itself has a fixed anvil, a warehouse hoist, chain and iron shutters. Over its northern gate is painted SOUTH. Under the gate is painted TAKE NOTHING YOU CANNOT CARRY WHEN THE CART BREAKS.
- **The decision:** what goes north. The cart stops at Coldbarrow, because nothing on wheels gets up the upper road. The **hoist and chain** are the only tools in the north that can finish the Slagcrown's gate (III.6). The **iron shutters** would close the Watchhouse's windows against the next winter. Food and oil are what the survivor needs to live through the climb. One of the three can go by Brida's rope at Rook's Stair, if she is still working it.
- **Systems:** *weight above everything*: chain is heavy, Overburdened means no dodge, and the upper road is troll ground. *Goblins* nest in the slaughter quarter and the drains. *Weather*: a sleet-swollen stream decides whether the Span's tree can be reached today.
- **Consequences:** whatever is left at Coldbarrow belongs to whoever takes it next. Whatever is carried north decides which endings are possible.

#### III.2 The Hammers at Greystair

- **Where:** fallen Greystair. The Citadel nearest the Bell Road, it collapsed into its own workings in the first years of the Smothering. Its upper courts are now a terrace of broken stone over a hollow that breathes warm air.
- **Who:** the **Bloomery Company**: forty Coldbarrow and Dunmarrow folk led by **Tobin Crane**, who was a boy in Greystair when it fell. They work Greystair's spoil heaps for slag-iron, smelt it in a relit bloomery and hammer it into heat-stones. A listener, **Aud**, has come down to them.
- **What happens:** bloom-iron cannot be made quietly. The bellows roar, and the hammers can be heard from the venting slopes. The listeners have asked Tobin, with signs, food and finally a written plea in an old miner's hand, to stop. Tobin's answer is the Company's children. The spoil is running out, and Tobin means to follow the old drifts to the Slagcrown's Black Seam, where the ore is richer. Lady Maudren's surveyor, if he came, has offered to pay for it.
- **The decision:** silence the furnace, put the work under weather, or leave it.
  - *Silencing it:* break the bellows or persuade the Company to bank the fire. Heat-stones stop for good, the Company's families start walking south, and the Watchhouse winters colder.
  - *Working under weather:* the Company works only in cinder-squalls, when nothing is heard. Output falls by two thirds, the listeners accept it, and Tobin calls the survivor a fool and agrees.
  - *Leaving it:* the furnace night follows (section 4), and it happens again.
- **Systems:** *hearing*, with the trolls drawn by sound and the bellows usable as a lure. *Weather* as a work schedule. *Fire*: the bloomery is the only fuel store north of Coldbarrow.
- **Consequences:** this decides whether the Company's fuel and charcoal can be had for the Burn ending, and whether the listeners will meet the survivor at all.

#### III.3 The Venting Slopes

- **Where:** the slopes between Greystair and the Slagcrown. Ash vents breathe there, snow does not lie, and the ground is warm underfoot and cracked.
- **Who:** nobody living. There are miners' dead in the spore drifts, each with a brass tally tag stamped with a number and a Citadel mark. If III.2 went badly, there are Company workers far gone with ash-lung, already walking blooms.
- **What happens:** the slopes have to be crossed. The vents make their own weather: they are locked between Ashfall and Cinder-squall, and the still hollows hold spore clouds for as long as a Dead Calm would. An ogre feeds on the open ground below the vents.
- **The decision:** what to do with the tags. Carrying them adds weight, and the Slagcrown's roll can name their owners (III.5). Leaving them leaves the dead nameless.
- **Systems:** *spores and fire*: the torch burns faster on the slopes, so the fuel budget is the crossing. *The ogre*, beaten by baiting its charge into a slag heap or a vent wall. *Footing* on cinder. *Armour against heat:* mail holds heat, so a hauberk wearer's stamina returns more slowly here. *Needs:* a heat modifier on armour, a proposal.
- **Consequences:** names for the dead, or none. A crossing that cost the survivor fuel they will want later.

#### III.4 The Low Galleries

- **Where:** the old upper workings under the Slagcrown's hill: drifts, pump rooms and miners' dormitories cut into the rock, above the Vaults and not part of them.
- **Who:** the **listeners**, humans descended from miners' families, Keeping penitents and anyone else who stayed under the hills when the Citadels emptied. They keep **the Quiet**: no bells, no drums, a hand-cant instead of speech near the hills, wrapped tools, and doors hung on leather. Aud leads them, so far as anyone does.
- **What happens:** the listeners will take the survivor through the galleries to the Slagcrown's lower gate, which is the only way in that avoids the open courts, but only in silence. They do not explain the Vaults. They call what is below *the Hush*, and they avoid even that word. Asked what the orks fled, Aud signs *below* and then *we do not know*, which is the most honest answer anyone in the game gives.
- **The decision:** keep the Quiet or not. A shout, a sprint, a clang or a companion's fight noise ends the guiding: the listeners vanish into side drifts, and the survivor finds their own way. The second decision is whether to swear their terms against stone (needed for the Leave It Be ending): keep the Quiet on the road home, and wrap the Watchhouse's bell, if it has one, for as long as the survivor lives there.
- **Systems:** *the noise table is the quest*. Armour matters (mail clinks), load matters (Burdened clatters), the background matters (the Gutter-kin's soft feet, the Causeway Carter's one good ear). *Companions*: set them to HOLD at the gallery mouth, or bring only the quiet ones. *The Dead Calm* falls in the galleries' deepest stretch, and the troll pair is there (section 4).
- **Consequences:** the listeners' trust, which decides whether Leave It Be is open and whether anyone will help draw the props for the seal.

#### III.5 The Slagcrown

- **Where:** the greatest of the Citadels: Cerrin Vane's hall, the counting house, the Black Seam drift-head, and the **Weight Gate**, a slag-iron counterweighted gate across the drift, half built.
- **Who:** no one, unless the surveyor or Tobin's advance party has come ahead of the survivor.
- **What the survivor finds:**
  - **The Seam Roll.** This is the counting house's register of the crews sent down the Black Seam, with each miner's covenant: pay to their dependents if they did not come up. The last crew is the one that broke the Vaults on Cerrin Vane's order. After that page, in a dozen different hands, each entry reads "did not answer". No payment is recorded against any of them.
  - **Cerrin Vane's chair.** It is empty, with a cloak still over it. Nothing says where he went. The game does not settle it.
  - **The Weight Gate.** The masons' marks are dated by the Citadel's own year count to the first months after the Breaking. The work stopped mid-course, and the tools are still on the scaffold. Three accounts of why it was built survive, in three hands, and they do not agree: "against the heat", "against the Tenant", "against what comes up the drift". Nobody wrote down why the work stopped.
- **The decision:** what to take from the counting house. The Roll is heavy, and the brass tags from III.3 can be matched against it. Separately, if the surveyor is there, what he is told, or whether he goes home at all.
- **Consequences:** names for the tags. The surveyor's report south, the last letter of the game. The tools for the final choice.

#### III.6 The Mouth of the Vaults

- **Where:** the Black Seam drift below the Weight Gate, down to the worked chambers. The stonework there was not made by people, and who made it the game does not say.
- **What happens:** a Dead Calm settles over the mouth and does not lift. Everything the survivor does can be heard. There is no boss (section 4). There is only the choice of what to do here, and the work it takes. That work is noisy, and the noise brings whatever is near.
- **The final choices:** see *The endings* below.

### What the north shows about what the orks fled

Evidence only. Every item here has, or could have, a plain cause, and none of them adds up to an answer.

- **Dens face downhill.** Every ork den from the Fold to the Slagcrown has its one way in facing down the slope, away from the Citadels. Animals choose dens for reasons nobody else knows.
- **Walls from below.** In the drift under the Weight Gate, rough stone has been piled across a side passage from the far side, the Vault side. Claw-scored rubble, no mortar. Orks block their openings with stone, so the wall could be theirs. That would mean they shut something behind them, or shut themselves out.
- **The tally boards.** Miners' boards in the drift-head carry the last shift's marks. The final ones are cut in the listeners' hand-cant, not in numbers, as if someone wanted to count and not be heard counting.
- **"Did not answer"**, written a dozen times in the Seam Roll. The phrase is the same verdict the Watchhouse uses for the Ashbound.
- **The breath.** Warm air comes out of the drift slowly, then stops, then comes again. It could be a vent's cycle or the rock's heat. It could be anything that breathes.
- **One knock.** In the Dead Calm at the mouth, once, something knocks against stone far down, exactly as Annet heard in the Roofed Mile. It never comes again. Cooling rock knocks.
- **The animals coming down.** Packs are moving south in Dead Calms, in silence. Some of the Company say the troll-kind are gathering at Greystair because of the hammers. Some say they are coming up from somewhere. Both can be true.
- **The guesses** in `bestiary.md` all stay guesses. The game gives the player no way to choose between them, and no character who knows.

### The endings

All four end at the thaw, back at the Watchhouse, with the warning book's last entry. In every one of them spring is a few grey weeks of mud, and the ash still falls.

**Seal: finish the Weight Gate.**
- *Needs:* the Coldbarrow hoist and chain, or the Company's if it still has a furnace and a reason to help. Hands to draw the props: the listeners, if they trust the survivor, or the Company.
- *What it takes:* a night of the loudest work in the game, in a Dead Calm. Every prop that comes out is heard, and whatever is near comes.
- *What it costs:* the Black Seam is closed for good, so there will be no more heat-stones for the Watchhouse, the Company or anyone. Tobin's people walk south. The listeners' oldest dead lie in the chambers beyond the gate, and the listeners can no longer keep them. Some of them refuse to forgive the survivor for that. Lady Maudren's spring has nothing left to reopen. That protects the Gardens from her ambitions, and it also takes away the only reason the south had to keep the Bell Road repaired.
- *What it does not do:* prove a gate was ever needed. The book writes: "Sealed. We do not know against what."

**Burn: fire the drift.**
- *Needs:* the Company's charcoal and oil, the Watchhouse's lamp oil and the timbered workings at the mouth. The spore beds in the drift burn.
- *What it costs:* the winter's fuel, all of it. The smoke comes down the Bell Road for a month. The Gardens' screens grey over, the cough spreads at the Split Kiln, and the Watchhouse admits more sick travellers than it can feed. The seam itself takes the heat and holds it, as slag-iron does, so the Slagcrown's hill becomes a new venting slope that nobody will cross for a generation. The listeners take it as an alarm and leave the hills, and some of them come south to the Watchhouse.
- *What it does not do:* show whether the spores in the north thin the next season. People will argue about that for years, and the book records both sides.

**Leave it be: keep the Quiet.**
- *Needs:* the listeners' terms, sworn against stone. The Greystair furnace silenced, or working only under weather.
- *What it costs:* nothing up front, and everything for a long time after. The survivor goes home and keeps the terms: the Watchhouse's bell, if it has one, stays wrapped, there are no drums at burials, and the Dead Calm becomes a time to sit still. The surveyor's report goes south, and in some spring the south will come for the seam and the survivor will have to meet them. Nothing is solved. The orks keep coming down the road, and the Causeway tower has to be held every winter.
- *What it does not do:* settle whether silence matters. The book writes the terms down so that whoever keeps the book next can decide.

**Go south: leave the Blackfen.**
- *Needs:* only the walk back, and people willing to come.
- *What it takes:* at the Watchhouse the survivor decides who goes, and that is the last admissions argument of the game turned inside out. The road runs down the Salt Trace, past the White Trees, where cut steps in a dead trunk let someone watch both approaches. The game ends there, at the top of the steps, looking both ways.
- *What it costs:* whoever cannot travel stays. That means the old, the sick and the stubborn. Someone has to keep the Watchhouse fire, or it goes out. The survivor becomes what the Crown was to the people left behind, or something better, and the difference is exactly who they take, who they leave and what they leave them. Nobody knows where the fleet went. Nobody on the road knows what is south.
- *What it does not do:* find the ships.

How Act II shapes the endings: the full report makes Seal and Burn into acts against the south as well as against whatever is below. The Fold's outcome decides whether the Watchhouse is holding the Causeway alone. The Gardens' water decides whether there is clean cloth for the sick the smoke brings. The survivor's companions can refuse any ending. Edda can refuse to go south, Aud refuses to burn, and Tobin refuses to seal unless the survivor feeds his people. A refusal costs the survivor that companion, and the game goes on without them.

## 4. Bosses and set pieces

Every fight follows the game's rule: a creature has a temperament, and the temperament has a counter. Most of the "bosses" here are places and conditions rather than larger health bars.

### Act I (in brief; covered elsewhere)

| Encounter | Temperament | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| The ork pack at the Causeway tower | Soldier-beasts behind stolen shields: slash then chop, bash a guard held too long | The tower's cramped stair clangs their slash. Flank, and break the guard with heavy blows |
| The Blackfen Ogre at the North-West Feeding Ground (the Cooking Pot) | Juggernaut: a charge it cannot steer | Sidestep with stone behind you. It crashes and reels for 2 s |
| The Blind Quarry-Troll at the Old Quarry | Blind: pounds where it heard you | Keep still. Creep out of the ring. Hit it, then step away |
| The Spore Hollow husks | Carriers: every wound puffs spores | The torch, reach, and stepping back out of the burst |

### Act II

**The Causeway bloom (II.1).** Corpse-cap fields on the unlimed dead, and, if the player is slow, walking blooms among the Ashbound who lay down beside them. *Counter:* lime first, torch second. Never kill a walking bloom on a corpse-cap field, because the two clouds stack.

**The chalk-cut troll (II.1).** A second quarry-troll behind the Split Kiln. *Temperament:* it has lived beside the kiln for thirty years and learned to ignore the roar of a firing, the way it rests in a cinder-squall. *Counter:* carry stone while the kiln fires, when the troll's hearing is swamped. The kiln households fire only twice a week, and they fire on their schedule, not the survivor's. *Needs:* a masking noise source that lowers enemy hearing within a radius, the same rule as the weather's hearing factor.

**The culvert nest (II.2).** Eight to a dozen goblins in Halwen's lower culvert, under the service wing. *Temperament:* opportunists, worse in numbers. In a culvert a metre wide they come from both ends, and every back is turned to one of them. *Counter:* close one end first, with a door or the Hands holding it, so they all come from the front. Thrust, because sweeps clang. Or use fire, at the cost the quest describes.

**Rauth Kell, if it comes to it (II.3, II.6).** At the platform, if the custody turns to blows. The only human boss in Act II, and avoidable. *Temperament:* a door fighter with spear and battered shield, who wants a public demonstration. He fights with thrusts, which never clang, so the narrow places that punish a sweeping ork do not punish him. *Counter:* draw him out of the doorway into the open court and heavy-break his shield. Killing him ends any agreement with the Remnant, and Oren will not forgive it. *Needs:* a human enemy class.

**The gallery-crawler (II.4).** An unidentified animal in the Roofed Mile. *Temperament:* not a rusher. It waits behind stone, moves sideways to get the cart out from between itself and its prey, and drops from above. *Counter:* listen for the one click, keep a pier between you, throw lime and set oil alight. It cannot reliably be killed, only driven off, and the encounter ends when it withdraws. This is a single authored encounter, never a spawn type, as the bestiary asks.

**The Fold den (II.5).** Four to six orks in a stone range. *Temperament:* in a stone room it is a soldier's fight, and the slash clangs. In a Dead Calm the pack is utterly silent: no ripples, no footsteps, only the cone. *Counter:* fight them indoors with thrusts, and never let a Dead Calm find the survivor in the court.

**Greymaw on the Causeway (II.6).** A pack of wild dogs following the escort. *Temperament:* they track by smell and go for wounds. *Counter:* bandage as soon as you bleed, keep the escort on HOLD at the passing places, and fight with stone at your back. Who whistles to the Greymaw is left an open question.

### Act III

**The Bent Span (III.1).** An environment fight: free the debris tree on the pier with rope while the stream rises in sleet. *Counter:* timing and the weather.

**The furnace night (III.2), if the hammers did not stop.** Two quarry-trolls come out of Greystair's hollow, drawn by the hammering, and pound wherever the last blow rang. *Temperament:* blind, and they hear everything. *Counter:* the survivor has to get forty people to stand still, which no system makes easy, and then work the bellows as a lure to draw the trolls onto the slag terrace edge. Any Company worker who runs is a sound the trolls follow. *Needs:* crowds of human non-combatants who panic.

**The slope ogre (III.3).** It feeds on open cinder below the vents. *Temperament:* juggernaut, enraged below 40%. *Counter:* bait the charge into a slag heap or a vent wall. The slopes have plenty of stone, but the ash hides which heaps are stone and which are loose cinder that will not stop a charge.

**The bloom drifts (III.3).** Spore hollows where the still air holds clouds, plus walking blooms if Company workers went that way. *Counter:* fuel management, because the torch burns faster on the slopes. Clouds are burned only when the route needs it.

**The troll pair in the Low Galleries (III.4).** Two trolls in a long drift under a Dead Calm, with listeners crossing ahead of the survivor. *Temperament:* blind and acute, and everything carries ×1.6. *Counter:* walking while blocking is silent, so the survivor crosses the drift that way. Companions go on HOLD at the mouth. Mail stays off. A thrown stone can draw one of the pair away. This is the hearing system's final exam.

**The mouth (III.6). There is no boss.** Whatever is below is never shown. The last set piece is the survivor's own noise: drawing props, lighting fuel, or simply walking back up the drift in a Dead Calm. It brings the animals the survivor already knows how to beat, drawn by the survivor's own work, and some of them come up out of the drift. The fight at the end of the game is against the noise of what the survivor chose to do.

## 5. The Watchhouse across the acts

The Watchhouse is the game's one constant, and it should change visibly. The networked build's fixtures are the right model: the breach and the hearth have one-way states, a cost and server-owned outcomes. Every change below is a fixture both players see.

**Act I: a ruin becomes a shelter.** The hearth is lit and the Cooking Pot hangs over it. Edda is cut free. The warning book is opened again. The doorway stays 1.35 m because of the ogre. One room is dry, with one fire and one argument about whom to admit.

**Act II: a shelter becomes a waystation again.** The Causeway is open, so the Watchhouse fills.
- *Outside:* a lime pit and a limed grave ground (II.1), or a corpse-cap field by the door. The Roofed Mile board hangs by the entrance, corrected or not (II.g).
- *The hearth:* a heat-stone in the coals, once the first one comes. Until then, the fire burns at whatever the lime cost (II.1).
- *Supplies:* bandages from the Gardens, on whatever terms the water settled (II.2). A cart axle from Brackenreach (II.k).
- *Rooms:* a guest place or a covered waiting shelter, depending on what the survivor built at the Gardens (II.a). Possibly a cot for Brida, Garran or Noll.
- *The book:* the revised register's two pages, copied in or burned (II.l).
- *The bell question* (II.i): Edrin asks whether the Watchhouse will hang one. A bell would let the Watchhouse call people in from the fen, and it would be heard by everything else as well.
- *The south's question* (II.2, II.6): whether the Watchhouse enters its name on the Halwen household roll, or registers as a Crown waystation under Lady Maudren's rights. It once was one, and the register shows what that meant in S.3.
- *The admissions argument* gets louder with every refugee from Coldbarrow.

**Act III: the Watchhouse holds without the survivor.** Edrin's runners bring news north and south. Whoever the survivor left in charge keeps the last coal overnight. If the Fold's pack and the others come down the Causeway, the tower is held or lost, and the runners say which. The survivor comes home to a place that made its own choices while they were away. Some of those choices will be ones the survivor would not have made.

**At the thaw, by ending:**
- *Seal:* a colder Watchhouse with no heat-stones, iron shutters if they came home, and the Company's families at the door asking for the guest place.
- *Burn:* a full Watchhouse, coughing, with the smoke on the road and listeners sleeping by the hearth.
- *Leave it be:* a quiet Watchhouse with the bell wrapped, if there is one, and the book's terms copied on the first page.
- *Go south:* a Watchhouse kept by whoever stayed, with the fire lit by somebody else. The last thing the survivor sees of it is the doorway, still 1.35 m.
