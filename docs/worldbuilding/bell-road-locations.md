Structured consolidation of proposed location write-ups, not a verbatim transcript or finalized map. The road gradually loses maintained fences, roofs, and reliable shelter. [Last Gardens](the-last-gardens.md) has a separately preserved full expansion.

## The Last Gardens

Old kitchen plots behind raised stone walls, with residents sharing two heatable buildings among six cottages. Roots, cabbage, flax, screens brushed of ash, covered cistern, broth, boiled water, cloth, and strap repair. Stores for travelers are inspected after a dead dog was found inside a grain sack; motive unknown. Bare waiting places beside gates keep strangers out of beds. A falling cistern level threatens income and survival. See full document and Halwen House water-system expansion rather than treating initial suspicions as proven facts.

## Darnel Cross

Former animal-changing stop centered on an inn whose yellow-sheaf/white-horse sign has lost the horse’s head to weather. Seven households claim partitioned common-room sleeping space. Stable yard remains shared. Farrier Ossa Denn, missing three left-hand fingers, works skillfully and refuses owners holding the lead because they pull at the wrong moment. Farrier shed, well, and yard make it one of the last useful pack-animal stops.

The upper floor is nailed shut because a main beam has split, not because of a haunting. People sleep below anyway. Ossa dates the widening crack with charcoal. Dismantling the upper storey might save or destroy the building. Recent travelers provide road intelligence; bringing useful news reduces what one must pay.

## The Split Kiln

Two limekilns against a chalky cut: one split from lip to firemouth, one intermittently working. This is masonry material production, not a rival grain-preserving kiln. Fuel shed, three cottages, roofed yard, pale dust. Three intermarried households restrict firing work partly to protect trade and partly because an inexperienced correction once killed a worker and ruined a load. His tools remain untouched.

The kiln depends on stone and fuel carriers. The Roofed Mile incident raises detour costs. Encounter: a driver delivers sound fuel over concealed wet timber, insists he received it already bound, and cannot afford return; his child sleeps beneath the seat.

## The Bell-Tender’s House

Near the quarry junction, overlooks both approaches. Chapel collapsed; house remains. Old Edrin was never a priest. He inherited maintenance work from his bell-tender father. Keeps one warm room; travelers can shelter in the outer workroom with fuel.

The bell sits low between timber supports, its tongue wrapped. Edrin does not announce arrivals by ringing; he prefers a runner because noise reaches unwanted listeners. Knows culverts, route joins, and shelters. Can describe an exposed sheep-track detour around the gallery that is not safe for a loaded cart. Needs someone to clear the roof drainage channel; he has tools but cannot climb.

## Saint Vey’s Fold

Walled roadside hospice around a court, later used for sheep, now occupied in the rear range by a few displaced ork households. Chapel entrance blocked with dressed stone. Visitors cannot freely enter their shelter.

Cautious exchanges occur at an outer flat stone: iron, salt, or leather may be exchanged for repaired metal, bone work, or dry fuel. Theft could destroy this local arrangement. Human travelers still use the front dormitory with bed frames and a broken stove. The orks do not enter it; whether boundary, danger, or lack of need is unresolved. A southern religious claimant seeking recovery could imperil both occupants and human travelers without their consent.

## The Bent Span

Stone bridge over a steep stream valley; central arch settled, one parapet lost. Timbers cover damaged paving in a handcart-wide passage. Four members of one household maintain it with a winch and demand food, fuel, fittings, or labor. They really repair it and really overcharge the desperate.

Low-water descent beneath the bridge is possible, but moving cargo down and up is difficult; the best hauling ropes remain in the bridge shelter. Upstream bank movement and a debris-catching fallen tree threaten a pier. Nobody has volunteered to descend and free it.

## Coldbarrow

Former northern supply town: smithies, warehouses, lodging, slaughter yards, fortified store. A small remnant community holds a storehouse; goblins use portions of slaughter quarter and drains; temporary scavenging parties use individual buildings. No group controls the whole. A light means fuel, not safety.

Heavy useful equipment remains: fixed anvil, warehouse hoist, troughs, chain, iron shutters. Recovery requires transport and a return route, not merely winning access. Inside the northern gate: SOUTH; beneath it, TAKE NOTHING YOU CANNOT CARRY WHEN THE CART BREAKS. Beyond lies the upper Citadel road.

## Road identity

Lower stops supply water, animal care, lime, and route knowledge. North of the Roofed Mile, safe shelter is increasingly uncertain. Southward travelers ask for news; farther north, people first ask who came with them.
