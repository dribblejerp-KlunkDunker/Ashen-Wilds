# Blackfen: Old Roads and Towns

Structured consolidation of proposed geography from the 2026-11-01 conversation; not a verbatim prose archive or finalized map. Related: [Bell Road](bell-road-locations.md), [Last Gardens](the-last-gardens.md).

## Regional principle

The district was once a working landscape of grain, stone, peat, livestock, markets, and transport. Ruins retain those relationships. A town existed because a road met a river; the river changed and the bridge failed, but people still need what lies beyond. Existing regional anchors include Reedwake, Wayfarer’s Watchhouse, the Old Causeway, the Nailhouse, Bellwether Yard, the Nine Stakes, Saint Orra’s Steps, and the Highland Citadels. New placements below remain proposals.

## Four roads

### Old Causeway

Raised rubble, timber, and stone across wet ground, with a surviving toll chokepoint. Leaning distance markers, cart passing places, drainage mouths, and former inns reveal its transport history. Some sections carry wagons; others are narrow shelves. Exposure matters: travelers can be seen and cannot readily leave the road without sinking.

The Leaning Team: five horse skeletons tangled in harness below an overturned stone freight wagon. The load preserves parts of the wreck. In dry weather people strip fittings. Rising water hides the harness first and makes the wagon look empty.

### Bell Road

Northern upland approach toward country below the Citadels, named for successive chapel bells formerly heard along its length. Sheep enclosures, quarry spoil, wooded cuts, and roofless stone villages line it. Firm ground trades marsh danger for ash, wind, cold, and sparse shelter.

The Roofed Mile is an old avalanche gallery with carcasses dragged between its supports. It offers useful weather shelter but has an identifiable occupation hazard. See the separate story and location sequence.

### Salt Trace

Not one road: embankments, towpaths, shallow crossings, and boat transfers toward southern trade. Cinder Factors know different routes for different loads. The name recalls cargo feared lost from a wet pack.

The White Trees: pale dead trunks beside a collapsed former salt store and transfer landing. A meeting point. Fresh cut steps in one trunk permit observation of both approaches; someone has prepared a lookout.

### Corpseway

Former parish road toward the hospice and burial ground at Saint Orra’s Steps. Paired low stone resting benches take stretchers without placing occupants in mud. Cut names and practical warnings survive, including water beyond a wall and avoiding a lower door. Encounters can include a broken handcart, usable shelter, an account left beside a body, or someone waiting for a companion who went ahead.

## Four settlements

### Brackenreach

Former market and freight town where an upland stream became navigable to shallow boats. Warehouses, livestock square, tannery quarter, and broad bridge. A failed weir undermined lower streets; later occupants stripped the bridge for defenses.

Upper Row: inhabited behind shopfront barricades. Lower Market: flooded cellars and roofless stores. Far Bank: cut-off workshops. Useful wheelwright timber survives beneath a failed roof, with visible structural risks. Residents sell contested salvage access; payment does not guarantee exclusive possession. At night Upper Row residents pull ladders inside. Reclaiming the whole town is beyond their capacity.

### Merewick

Proposed placement on a shallow tributary near the rising edge of the fen, giving its mill moving water and nearby reed grounds. Chapel/record room, millpond, diversion channel, patched bridge, higher houses, and downstream reed plots form its center. It survives through useful production and maintenance, not fortification. Upstream runoff and debris create a new dispute: neighbors can obstruct the water even while the miller honors his bridge duties. Keep unresolved Merewick character-name conflicts separate from geography.

### Rook’s Stair

Terraced quarry settlement reached by road at the bottom and narrow stairs above. A small population holds middle terraces near a spring and repairable roofs. It trades stone, lime, salvage iron, and masonry skill. Defensibility does not solve food dependence; a blocked road can starve it.

The Hanging Shed: lifting shed projecting over the quarry, with ropes descending into fog. Machinery is serviceable; no resident claims to have repaired it. Mystery is not automatically supernatural.

### Low Harrow

Former town on reclaimed land whose drainage failed. Occupants moved to roofs and embankments, then mostly departed. Travelers now cross roof ridges, walls, upper windows, planks, and a rope fixed through a carved stone mouth. Deep water covers rooms visible when clear. The market cross protrudes above the old square; a maintained boat is tied there, but travelers do not encounter its owner. Fragile structures, water, and limited retreat make the shortcut dangerous.

## Travel relationships

Watchhouse–Brackenreach: tools, salvage, cart parts; exposed Causeway and broken crossing. Merewick–Nine Stakes: flour, reeds, bargains; visible cargo attracts claims. Bell Road–Rook’s Stair: stone, lime, repairs; cold and unreliable shelter. Salt Trace–southern routes: salt, oil, needles, news; changing crossings and route dependence. Corpseway–Steps: care and identification; sick travelers must endure the trip. Low Harrow: shorter crossing at structural and water risk.

Set exact distances only when map scale is chosen. Every route should explain its original purpose, present necessity, and what makes a return worthwhile.
