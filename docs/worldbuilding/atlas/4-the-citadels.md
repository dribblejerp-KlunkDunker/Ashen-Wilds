The five walled mining towns on the Highland scarp, where the kingdom was richest and where it broke.

## The scarp

*Region · a black escarpment running east to west for about sixty miles above the Bell Road country · held by nobody living, in most of it · danger III to V · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Five walled mining towns over their pitheads along the rim of the Highland scarp, each held by a hereditary Slag-Lord who answered to the Crown for the iron and to no one for what happened underground. Their slag-iron was portable warmth in a cold world, and only Orlen had it. It made the kingdom rich and made the kingdom depend on seams that did not last.

**The five.**

| Citadel | House | Slag-Lord at S.0 | What it made | What it is now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Slagcrown | Vane | Cerrin Vane | Deep ore, the great smelting halls, the Crown's iron | Empty, its hall laid for a meal nobody finished. The Black Seam is under it. |
| Greystair | Tarrant | Rohese Tarrant | Cast stove plates | Fallen in S.2. Its bloomery relit by Tobin Crane's Bloomery Company. |
| Dunmarrow | Corbet | Bertram Corbet | Blades, the Watch's swords and helmets | Held by orks in force (new). |
| Hob's Height | Haddon | Adam Haddon | Bells and charcoal | Walked out in S.1. Its Bell-Spire stands silent (new). |
| Kell Tor | Marne | Ysmay Marne | Deep pumps and the assay house | Mortared shut from the inside since S.1. |

**The Deep Charter (S.-196).** The Citadels hold *the deep and all within it*; the Crown holds the surface. They pay the Crown's Fifth and keep the Bell Road. Each Citadel's **Hewers' Lodge** keeps the right to refuse unsafe ground; a Slag-Lord may set it aside only by taking the ground on his own hazard. The Keeping witnesses and blesses each new level. The Charter said nothing about the deep holding anything but ore.

**How it looks.** From the Bell Road: a wall of black rock along the whole northern sky, and on its rim, spaced along sixty miles, five towns of dark stone with spoil heaps round them, each visible from the next. The Slagcrown's crown-towers in the middle, Hob's Height highest and furthest west on a needle of rock, Kell Tor furthest east on a crag above a black tarn. Under all of them, the hills are hollow.

**Old magic.** All six crafts were practised here, and the Older Work lies below. The Hewers' Lodges kept Holding and Cold light. Hob's Height cast the carrying bells. Dunmarrow's smiths marked blades. Each Citadel had a house of the Keeping.

**What hunts it.** Quarry-trolls on the high ground and in the galleries. Ork packs, most of all at Dunmarrow. Goblins in the shafts. Walking blooms on the venting slopes. An ogre feeding below the vents. This winter, everything that lives here is moving south in silence.

**Under it.** The Low Galleries, the Hewers' Road joining the five Citadels, the Eleventh Gallery, the Black Seam drift and the Deep-Seam Vaults. See **The Deep**.

## The Slagcrown

*Great complex · a walled mining city for four thousand people on the scarp's rim, with a hall cut into the hill and shafts going down eleven levels · held by nobody; an ork pack dens in the hall (new) · danger III to V · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The greatest and deepest of the five Citadels, the house of Vane: proud, crowded, in debt. Its spoil heaps ringed the pitheads like a crown. Under it, in the Eleventh Gallery, Ebba Cray's crew found the Black Seam, and in the spring of S.0 the Slagcrown broke the wall at its end.

**History.** By S.-11, when Cerrin Vane inherited, the upper levels were worked out, the middle levels thin, and the house owed the Fifth on old tonnages while feeding four thousand people. The fleet sank in S.-7; the Lord Treasurer demanded arrears and spoke of Crown stewardship. In S.-2 the Black Seam paid. In the autumn of S.-1 it ran into fitted stone. At midwinter Vane overruled the Hewers' Lodge on his own hazard, with the Keeping's treasury and Abbot Hamon Strand behind him. In the spring of S.0 the crews fire-set the wall for eleven days; on the twelfth it fell inward. In the third week the under-

priest Father Hamelin Lynde rang the alarm against orders until he was pulled off the rope, and half the Citadel came up because of it. By S.1 the Slagcrown was emptying down the Bell Road. Vane's fate is disputed.

**How it looks.** Seen from the upper road: a black city on the rim of the scarp, and round it **the crown**, nine (new) conical spoil heaps of grey slag, each with an iron winding-tower on the pithead beside it, standing against the sky like the points of a crown. Behind the city wall, roofs of dark slate climbing to the hill, and in the hill's face the great doors of the hall. Up close: everything is black, slag-iron and dark stone, furred with ash. It is warmer than it should be. The ground underfoot hums very faintly in the deep levels, and nobody knows whether that is water.

**Layout.**

1. **The crown.** The nine spoil heaps and their winding-towers, a ring round the city half a mile across. Seen from everywhere in the north.
2. **The open courts.** The wide paved yards between the heaps and the wall, where ore carts were loaded. Exposed on every side; ork packs cross them.
3. **The outer gate and barbican,** its gates open, the toll-free cart lane worn into the stone by two hundred years of iron.
4. **The warrens.** The housing for four thousand: terraces of stone rows stepping up to the hill, alleys a cart wide, doors still marked with the crews' tally numbers. Rooms left as they were: a cradle, a hewer's spare boots, a stove plate on the hearth.
5. **The house of the Keeping.** Abbot Strand's chapel, where the seam beyond the wall was blessed, and its bell, which Father Lynde rang. The bell-rope is still there, cut short.
6. **The smelting halls.** Three halls of cold furnaces the size of houses, and in the casting floor a river of slag-iron frozen mid-pour in its channel, black and glassy, stopped the day the last crew left (new).
7. **The lamp-room,** where the crews' lamps were trimmed and issued, and where Vane spoke the night before the Breaking: rows of lamps on hooks, every one numbered to a hewer.
8. **The hall.** Cut into the hill for a thousand people. Its roof is held by pillars of black slag-iron and a span no pillar could carry. A ventilation shaft brings down a single column of grey daylight onto the floor. The long tables are still laid for a meal nobody finished (new). Cerrin Vane's chair at the far end has a cloak over it. An ork pack dens behind the high table (new; the lore puts no one here).
9. **The counting house.** The weighbeams where the iron was weighed for the Fifth; the clerks' desks; the **Seam Roll**, with *did not answer* in a dozen hands and no payment against any name.
10. **The pitheads and the main shaft.** The winding-gear and the cages hanging in the shaft on slag-iron chain. The cage still runs if someone works the winch (new). It is the great shortcut: from the hall straight down to the drift-head, or back up.
11. **The lower gate.** A small door low in the hill, the listeners' way in from the Low Galleries, avoiding the open courts.
12. **The drift-head,** at the bottom of the main shaft: the miners' tally boards, whose last marks are cut in hand-cant, as if someone wanted to count and not be heard counting.
13. **The Weight Gate.** A gate across the main drift, half built, its tools still on the scaffold, with three accounts of what it was for in three hands that do not agree. Beyond it the drift goes down to the Black Seam. See **The Deep**.

**Who is here.** Nobody living, most of the time. An ork pack in the hall (new). Goblins in the upper shafts. Listeners passing through by the lower gate. In the novel's winter, perhaps Lady Maudren's surveyor, or Tobin Crane's advance party following the old drifts toward the Black Seam.

**Old magic. Holding** in the hall's span and the drift roofs. **Cold light** marks on the lintels of the old workings. **Keeping** in the chapel, whose blessing is the worst thing anyone in the north remembers a priest doing.

**Wonder and mystery.** The crown against the sky. The frozen river of iron. The column of light in the hall. The tally boards in hand-cant. The Weight Gate's three accounts. What became of Cerrin Vane: dead at the inner gate, gone down into the levels, or listed among those who reached Wexhaven.

**Dangers.** The open courts (III). The pack in the hall (III). The shafts and cages (V). Heat and bad air in the lower levels (V).

**Finds.** The Seam Roll, heavy. Lamps, numbered. Stove plates. Marked tools. The cloak on the chair.

**Hooks.** The novel's chapters 27 to 29. In The Long Account, the Slagcrown's rise is the view from the opening ledge, and Marget Sayle was one of the four who came back from the survey.

## Greystair

*Great complex · a Citadel built down a hillside in terraces (eleven of them, new), a mile from top gate to bottom, its upper courts fallen into its own workings · held by the Bloomery Company, about forty people, on its spoil heaps · danger III, IV on the furnace nights · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The Citadel most like a town: terraces stepping down a hillside, stoves in every window, the house of Tarrant that made the cast stove plates every northern house wanted. In S.1 it opened its gates to the fleeing; by S.2 it could not feed them, and it fell. In the years after, its upper courts collapsed into its own workings. Now forty people from Coldbarrow and Dunmarrow have relit its bloomery, and its furnace glow is the red light seen from the whole north.

**History.** Greystair's plates stood in every chapel, inn and hall in the north; a plate fired once in the evening keeps a room warm till morning. After the Breaking it took in more than it could feed. By spring of S.2 its terraces were full of the hungry and coughing, the refuge register had become a list of who ate, and the gates were guarded from inside as much as out. A crowd at the lower gate was refused; people with relatives in it forced the gate from within; in the confusion an ork pack came down the upper stairs. Rohese Tarrant was seen at the upper gate and not recorded after. **Tobin Crane**, who was a boy in Greystair when it fell, brought the Company back to work its spoil.

**How it looks.** From the upper road: a hillside of grey terraces like a giant's stair, roofless houses in rows on every step, the great stair itself running straight down the middle from the upper gate to the lower. At the top the upper courts are gone: a terrace of broken stone over a hollow that breathes warm air. On the spoil heaps at one side, smoke, and at night the bloomery's red glow pulsing as the bellows work. The sound of hammers carries for miles.

**Layout (top to bottom).**

1. **The upper gate,** where the pack came down and Rohese Tarrant was last seen.
2. **The upper courts,** collapsed into the workings in the first years: a terrace of broken stone where the Tarrant house stood. One wall of its plate showroom still stands at the edge, with cast plates hanging on it over the drop, each with a pattern: wheat, ships, the Keeper's hand, a stag (new).
3. **The great stair,** eleven flights (new) down the middle of the town, the top flight broken off where the courts fell.
4. **The terraces,** row on row of houses with a stove in every room, the doors marked with the refuge register's numbers.
5. **The breathing hollow,** under the broken terrace of the upper courts: the pit they fell into, open to the old workings, where warm air comes up steady day and night. Nobody sleeps near it.
6. **The register hall (new),** where the refuge register is still chained to its desk: the list of who ate, with the last pages in a hand that shakes.
7. **The lower gate,** where the crowd was refused and the gate was forced from inside.
8. **The spoil heaps,** a mountain of slag the Company works by hand for the iron left in it.
9. **The bloomery,** relit: a stone furnace, water-driven bellows, the hammer floor, racks of heat-stones glowing in rows.
10. **The Company's camp,** in the old casting sheds by the bloomery, with the children.
11. **The old drifts,** which Tobin means to follow toward the Slagcrown's Black Seam, where the ore is richer.

**Who is here. The Bloomery Company,** forty Coldbarrow and Dunmarrow folk under **Tobin Crane**, who answers the listeners' pleas with the Company's children. **Aud**, a listener, who has come down to beg them to stop. Lady Maudren's surveyor, perhaps, offering to pay for the drifts. Trolls, drawn to the hammers, or coming up anyway.

**Old magic.** Marking, faintly, in the bloom-iron: the Company has no glim-wright, but Tobin swears the old plates' patterns were more than decoration, and that the best plates stay warm a little longer than slag-iron should.

**Wonder and mystery.** The bloomery pouring at night; the children singing to the hammers, because here they are allowed to be loud. The showroom wall of plates, hanging over the drop. The breathing hollow.

**Dangers.** The noise and what it brings (IV). Furnace accidents (V). The register hall's ghosts, which are only memory.

**Finds.** Heat-stones. Plates. Bloom-iron. The refuge register.

## Dunmarrow

*Great complex · a fortress Citadel round a forge-court, with barracks for a thousand soldiers and armouries cut into the rock · held by orks in force, the largest pack anyone has counted · danger IV · the lore gives its house, lord, trade and character, the conditions at its*

*gates in S.1, the Grain March and its fall in the autumn of S.2; the ork hold as it is now, the layout and the rest are (new)*

**At a glance.** The soldier's Citadel: the largest garrison and the best forges in Orlen, where the Watch's swords and helmets were made, and the Marking of blades was a guarded craft. When its garrison was ordered south, the orks came in, and a fortress with one way in suited them exactly. It is now the greatest ork hold in the north.

**History.** House Corbet's forges worked slag-iron that holds its heat so long a smith can draw a blade from one heat, and Dunmarrow guarded the craft of quenching it. Its glim-wrights cut the marks into the best blades: cold light, a bite of cold, an edge that would not dull. Rafe's issued sword was made here. In S.1 Dunmarrow opened its gates to the fleeing and then set conditions, as all the refuge towns did. Bertram Corbet held it through the winter of S.1 with the garrison. In the summer of S.2 the Crown ordered the garrison south on the Grain March, to escort the north's requisitioned grain to Wexhaven. Corbet obeyed. Stripped of its garrison, Dunmarrow was overrun that autumn. The packs that took it never left (new); by now there are several packs, and the Marrow Passes beyond it are theirs as well.

**How it looks.** A squat black fortress on a spur of the scarp, all walls and towers and no windows, its forge-chimneys standing out of the central court like a row of black teeth. Rubble packed into every gate but one. At night, no light at all: orks need none. In the morning, nothing: orks light no fires, and the forge-chimneys have been cold since S.2.

**Layout.**

1. **The approach,** a single switchback road up the spur, overlooked by three towers.
2. **The gate,** the only opening not packed with rubble, a narrow way in the old gate's wicket.
3. **The outer ward and the barracks,** long stone halls for a thousand soldiers, now the packs' dens: bedding of stolen cloth, salt-licked walls, plunder piles.
4. **The forge-court,** the heart of the Citadel: forty forges round a paved court, the anvils still in place.
5. **The Marking Hall (new),** the glim-wrights' workshop off the forge-court: benches with their tools laid out, the walls cut with every mark the wrights knew, in rows like an alphabet, with the words for them nowhere written.
6. **The armouries,** cut into the rock behind the forge-court: racks of blades, helmets and mail, much of it still there, because orks carry what they can steal and cannot use what they cannot carry.
7. **The Corbet keep,** the lord's tower, its stair choked with rubble.
8. **The deep forges (new),** below the court, where blades were quenched in water drawn from the hill; and the shafts below them, connecting to the Hewers' Road.
9. **The postern,** a small door on the far side of the spur, walled by the garrison and never opened.

**Who is here.** Ork packs, perhaps a hundred beasts in all, drilled, silent, with old grey-jawed leaders. Nobody human. Ravens.

**Old magic.** Marking, at its source. The Marking Hall's walls are the only complete record of the marks anywhere in the world. The marks on the armoury's blades still work for anyone who knows their words.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Marking Hall's alphabet of marks. What became of the garrison after the Grain March, and whether any of it came back to find the gates packed with rubble. Whether Bertram Corbet is among the dead in the keep.

**Dangers.** The packs (IV in numbers). The single road in (V for anyone seen on it).

**Finds.** Watch swords and helmets by the hundred. Marked blades. The marks themselves, if someone could copy them out alive.

## Hob's Height and the Bell-Spire

*Great complex and spire · the smallest and highest Citadel, built up a needle of rock about 200 m tall, with its foundry at the foot and its belfry at the summit · held by nobody; one listener keeps the bells · danger III, V on the climb · the lore gives House Haddon, the bells and charcoal, the cut ropes and the walk-out; the Bell-Spire's form and contents are (new)*

**At a glance.** House Haddon's Citadel: poor, stubborn, devout, named for Hob, a miner of legend, and home of the foundry that cast every bell on the Bell Road with the carrying craft. Its town climbs a rock spire in a spiral, and at the top hangs the bell that began every message the road ever carried. It has not rung since S.1.

**History.** The bell-founders of Hob's Height cast bells so that their note would travel true, and the Bell Road's chapels were built a carrying distance apart so that a message could run from here to the fen in an afternoon. After the Breaking, the line rang the alarm. In the summer of S.1, when goblins first came up Hob's Height's shafts, the steward **Ralf Ottery** cut the bell-ropes so that panic would not stop the ore carts. By the autumn there were orks on the crag. **Adam Haddon** led his people down the Bell Road on foot and left the foundry cold. The listeners tell their oldest stories about Hob's Height.

**How it looks.** A needle of grey rock on the scarp's highest point, with the town wound round it in a spiral like the thread of a screw: houses, chapels and workshops clinging to its sides, joined by one road that circles upward seven times. At the foot, the foundry's chimneys and casting pits. At the summit, an open stone belfry with one great bell in it, and on every turn of the road below, smaller bells in frames, dozens of them, each wrapped in sacking. In wind the whole spire hums.

**Layout (foot to summit).**

1. **The foundry,** at the foot: the casting pits where bells were poured into moulds of clay and dung, the furnaces, the pattern store with the profiles of every bell the foundry ever cast, cut in wooden templates.
2. **The charcoal yards,** where the burners' clamps stood; black earth, and the burners' sheds.
3. **The Spiral,** the one road, climbing seven turns round the spire past the houses, the Keeping's house, and the Haddons' hall.
4. **The Tuning Stair (new).** Halfway up, a stair cut through the rock with bells hung in niches the whole way, from hand-bells at the bottom to bells taller than a man at the top, every one a different note. The founders tuned new bells against it. Struck in order, they would sound a scale that climbs the spire.
5. **The Haddons' hall,** small and plain for a Slag-Lord's, with Adam Haddon's chair and his Keeping books.
6. **The walled galleries.** In the spire's foot, galleries the old hewers walled up and never reopened, long before the Breaking. The goblins came up beside them, not through them.
7. **The belfry,** at the summit: the **Mother Bell (new)** , the head of the carrying line, three metres across the mouth, its tongue unwrapped and lashed to the frame with chain. The view from here is all of Orlen: the five Citadels, the Bell Road's first towers going south, and on the clearest days, very far south, a grey sheen on the sky that the old bell-founders said was the fen's water.

**Who is here. Wat Corley (new),** a listener, an old man who lives alone on the Spiral and keeps the bells wrapped, as his people swore to, climbing to the belfry every day to check the Mother Bell's chain. Goblins in the shafts below the foundry. Orks on the crag in hard winters, though there is nothing for them to eat.

**Old magic. Carrying,** at its source. The founders' pattern store holds the templates; nobody alive knows the rest of the craft. The listeners believe the ringing of S.0 and S.1 woke or called something below, and that the Mother Bell rung now would be heard the

whole length of the road and further down than anyone wants. Some say it would answer by itself if any bell in the line were rung.

**Wonder and mystery.** The spire humming in wind. The Tuning Stair's scale. The view from the belfry. What the walled galleries hold, and why the old hewers walled them.

**Dangers.** The climb in wind and ice (V). Goblins in the foundry's shafts (II, III). The bells themselves, if anyone rings one.

**Finds.** The pattern templates. Wrapped bells. Charcoal. Haddon's Keeping books.

**Hooks.** The bell rung once in the north at night (novel, chapter 15) could be from here, or from anywhere on the line.

## Kell Tor and its black tarn

*Great complex, sealed · the oldest and furthest Citadel, on a crag above a black tarn that never freezes · held by whoever is inside, if anyone · danger unknown · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** House Marne's Citadel: exact, private, respected more than liked, with the best pumps and granaries in the north and the assay house that stamped every bar of Orlen's iron with its grade. In the autumn of S.1 it mortared its gates from the inside. Its beacon burned three more nights and never again. Nobody has been inside since.

**History.** Kell Tor kept the deepest pumps and the records of the old walled galleries in its deepest levels. After the Breaking, Ysmay Marne wrote to the other Slag-Lords a letter remembered at Greystair only as short and very angry. Riders from Greystair found dressed stone across the gateway, the mortar still soft, the banner taken in. A shepherd named Wilmot saw smoke from the crag in S.17. Nobody else did.

**How it looks.** A crag of black rock rising straight out of the east end of the scarp, the Citadel's walls growing out of its top as if they were the same stone, its beacon tower on the highest point, cold. Below it, filling a hollow at the crag's foot, the **black tarn**: still black water that never freezes and always steams, and at night small pale lights that drift across it and go out. The gate is a blank wall of dressed stone.

**What is known of the inside (from the old Bell Road plans, new).** The gate and the assay house behind it, where every bar was weighed and stamped. The granaries cut into the crag, said to hold seven years of grain. The **pump houses**, where great treadwheels and rag-and-chain pumps lifted water from the deep levels into the tarn: the tarn is the

Citadel's own pumped water, warm from the deep. The beacon tower. The Marne library, with the records of every walled gallery in the five Citadels. And the deep levels, which went further down than any but the Slagcrown's.

**What people say.** They are alive, rationing years of stores. They sealed something in, because they knew what lay past their walled galleries. They went down, and the mortar was the last thing they did on the surface. They stopped the pumps and let the tarn into the deep, and sealed the gate so nobody would start them again. Or nothing remarkable: they shut out the orks, the cough came in anyway, and they died with nobody to write it down. The beasts do not go near Kell Tor, and there is a plain reason: there is nothing to eat.

**Old magic.** Unknown. The lights on the tarn are most likely marsh gas catching at warm vents.

**The atlas's rule.** Kell Tor is never entered in the novel. If The Long Account ever goes inside, that is a ruling for you to make first.

## The venting slopes and the Bloom drifts

*Open country · about twelve miles of warm hillside under the scarp between Greystair and the Slagcrown · held by nobody living · danger III to V · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Warm ground below the Citadels, where the hills breathe out through vents and no snow lies. It makes its own weather and holds spore clouds in its hollows as long as a Dead Calm would. The miners' dead lie in its drifts with brass tags, and in the last weeks of winter it flowers.

**History.** The vents opened after the Breaking, as heat rose in the lowest levels for weeks and the pumps could not keep the water down. The spore drifts gathered in the hollows below them: the **Bloom drifts**, where the spores that came up the shafts settled and the cough's first dead were left. The miners who died there wear their tally tags, each stamped with a number and a Citadel's mark.

**How it looks.** A long hillside of turf and grey stone under the black scarp, steaming. Where the vents open, the air shakes like air over a fire. In late winter: green turf when everything else is grey, primroses, coltsfoot, bees out a month early, steam curling off the stones (new). In the hollows, the drifts: grey dust lying like snow in still air, white threads in the soil, and walking blooms standing among the flowers.

**Layout.**

1. **The lower slope,** open ground where an ogre feeds.
2. **The vent line,** a row of cracks and fumaroles across the hill, warm enough to boil water in places.
3. **The flower ground (new),** the warmest turf, in bloom out of season.
4. **The Bloom drifts,** still hollows full of hanging spores, the miners' dead among them with their brass tags.
5. **The upper slope,** steep scree to the scarp's foot and the lower gate of the Slagcrown.

**Who is here.** Nobody living. The miners' dead. Walking blooms, some of them the Company's people a week ago. An ogre on the lower slope. Bees.

**Old magic.** None that anyone knows. The heat is from below, and why the hills are warm runs from the old fires to something that breathes.

**Weather.** Locked between ashfall and cinder-squall: the vents make gusts that are hot and dry one hour and still the next.

**Wonder and mystery.** A spring you can die in. The tags, each a name the Slagcrown's roll can return.

**Dangers.** Spore clouds in the hollows (V). The ogre (IV). Blooms (II). Vents underfoot (V).

**Finds.** The brass tags, heavy. Early herbs. Warmth.

**Hooks.** The novel's chapter 25.

### The ledge

*Open country · a frosted rock ledge on Orlen's highland edge, below the rim at scarp mile 46, a day and a half east of the Slagcrown · held by nobody · danger II · from the game's design*

**At a glance.** The ledge in the owner's key painting: frosted rock at the highland edge, looking west over a broken
country burning under a sun gone dark, with the Slagcrown beneath it. The Long Account opens here, in the first week
of the thaw, S.30.

**How it looks.** Frosted rock, hoarfrost underfoot. Due west, a day and a half on foot, the Slagcrown: a square
curtain with four corner towers, gate towers east and west, halls, a keep with four turrets, one great tower and two
spires, and nine spoil heaps in a ring round it. A canyon runs from under the ledge west toward it. Below, the venting
fires, and a teal hollow. The sun is coal, coin or clear by the day's number. Ash falls, and sleet comes in spells.

**Layout.**

1. **The ledge,** frosted rock at the very edge. There is no wall: you can stand at the edge and look straight down.
2. **The ramp,** the ledge's way down to the shelf.
3. **The shelf,** north of the ledge, where Marget Sayle's bothy stands. The walking bloom keeps its post here by the
   ramp's foot, its back to the way down, facing out over the drop; her door is reached by passing behind it.
4. **The teal hollow** below, where the corpse-caps grow. The path skirts it.
5. **The ledge path** down into the highland edge: the ramp's foot is where the ledge meets the rest of the zone (Z1),
   and the upper road goes on down to Coldbarrow, 18 miles and two days.

**Who is here.** The company, when the game opens: with no choices made, an old Watch deserter with a glim-marked blade
and a young causeway carter with a spear and the company's shield. The carter was born after the sun went dark and
has never seen it otherwise. One walking bloom on the shelf. **Marget Sayle**, in her bothy.

**Old magic.** Cold light beside a lantern, if anyone has the working. The deserter's blade carries a glim mark near
the guard: a cross over a stem, pale blue.

**Wonder and mystery.** The view: the coal sun, the Slagcrown beneath it, the venting fires, the teal hollow below.
Nothing is asked of anyone until each has looked at the Slagcrown.

**Dangers.** The walking bloom (II), which hunts by sound: "Walk. Do not run. There is one on the shelf, and it hears
running." The corpse-caps in the hollow: "Do not go down wind of them." Hoarfrost footing.

**Hooks.** The prologue: the view, the errand, the way down, the asking, the carry and the look back. At the top of
the ledge path Marget turns and looks at the Slagcrown once, and says nothing.

### Marget Sayle's bothy

*Small site · one low room under turf, on the shelf below the ledge · held by Marget Sayle · danger II at the door · from the game's design*

**At a glance.** Marget Sayle's home: a field-stone bothy under the ledge, in sight of the Slagcrown, with one door, a
bar for it and a banked peat fire. Gytha Marrin sends the company north to ask her to come south.

**How it looks.** Field stone laid dry, under turf and heather: peat-dark sod, heather in clumps, dead grass, a crust
of ash in the hollows, frost. The roof is hipped all round and cut short at the wall heads, its ridge low. There is no
chimney, only a rim of turf round a hole on the ridge, and the smoke out of it leaning south. One door; one small
glazed window in the south wall beside it, and none on the weather side, which is north. Inside, one low dark room;
the fire is an open hearth on the floor in a kerb of stones.

**Layout.**

1. **The door,** and its bar: heavy timber, iron at both ends, on two hooks.
2. **The room:** the hearth, and her table with a tally of days cut along its edge. She sleeps facing the door.

**Who is here.** **Marget Sayle**, sixty-two, one of the four who came back from **Ebba Cray**'s survey through the
breach in S.0; six went through. She has never described what she saw. She cannot walk far and coughs in the cold.
What she wants is to say one word to the right people: *don't*. She keeps two flasks of lamp oil, and a horn lantern
that she gives to the company the first time they speak to her.

**Dangers.** The walking bloom comes to a door. Barred, it leans on the door once and waits off it; not barred, it
follows you in. Marget: "Put the bar across. They come to a door."

**Finds.** Lamp oil. Her lantern. Peat.

**Hooks.** The asking: what the players promise her is the first entry in the Account, with a name on it. The carry:
she cannot manage the ledge path, and getting her up it is the first two-person load. The witness: whether she goes
south, and what she says at Halsing.
