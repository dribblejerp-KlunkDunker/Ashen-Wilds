The road north out of the fen, climbing through sheep country and quarry spoil to the Highland Citadels, named for the chapel bells that once carried messages its whole length.

## The road itself

*Open country and a road · about 80 miles and seven days on foot from the Watchhouse to Coldbarrow, then 16 miles and two days of climbing to Greystair · held by nobody · danger II to IV · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The upland road from the fen to the Highland Citadels: sheep enclosures, quarry spoil, wooded cuts and roofless stone villages, climbing all the way. Firm ground trades the fen's water for ash, wind, cold and sparse shelter. Southbound travellers ask for news. Further north, people first ask who came with you.

**History.** The road was built for slag-iron, and everything on it served the iron: lime for the furnaces, stone for the Citadels, charcoal, carts, inns, and the chapels whose bells carried messages from Hob's Height to the fen edge in an afternoon. After the Breaking in S.0 the chapels were meant to pass the alarm down the whole line. Some did. Some priests, told by their superiors that the Slagcrown's bell was a disorder and not an alarm, did not. In the summer of S.1, when goblins first came up Hob's Height's shafts, the steward Ralf Ottery had its bell-ropes cut so that panic would not stop the ore carts. By the autumn the Citadels were emptying down this road on foot.

**How it looks.** A pale road climbing through grey hills in long switchbacks, with drystone walls on both sides running up over the skyline, and every eight miles or so a chapel tower standing above the road like a milestone. Most of the towers are roofless now. Their bell-frames show against the sky, empty or with the bell still hanging and its tongue wrapped. North, on clear days, the Citadels' black scarp, and at night a red glow from Greystair.

**The carrying line (new).** Eleven chapel towers between the fen and the Citadels, about eight miles apart, each within the carrying distance of the next. On the scarp the line ran west through the Citadels' own belfries to Hob's Height, whose Mother Bell carried thirty miles to the Slagcrown, the longest carry the line ever had. Their bells were cast at Hob's Height with the carrying craft, so that a bell rung at one tower was heard at the next as clearly as if it hung overhead. A message ran the line by a code of strokes. Four bells are still in their frames, wrapped. Three are gone. The rest lie broken in the towers' yards. If one were rung now, nobody knows how many of the others would answer by themselves.

**Layout (south to north).** The foot of the road at the Watchhouse; the **Last Gardens** and **Halwen House**; the **Old Charcoal Lodge** off a lower track; **Darnel Cross**; the **Split Kiln**; the **Bell-Tender's House** at the quarry junction, with the branch to **Rook's Stair** and the **Drowned Measures**; the **Roofed Mile**; **Saint Vey's Fold**; the **Bent Span**; **Coldbarrow**; the **upper road** to the Citadels.

**Who is here.** Carriers paying the Roofed Mile detour. The Grey Lash, who move people for a price. The Remnant, who claim the road for Lady Maudren Halwen. Greymaw in the wooded cuts. Quarry-trolls on the high ground. Ork packs, this winter, coming south in silence.

**Old magic.** Carrying, in the chapel bells. Holding, in the Roofed Mile's arches.

**Wonder and mystery.** The towers in a line to the horizon. A bell ringing once in the north at night where nobody has rung for twenty years.

**Dangers.** Cold and exposure on the high ground (V). Greymaw (II, III). Trolls near the quarries (IV). Noise: everything north listens for bells.

## The Last Gardens

*Village · six cottages and their walled plots below Halwen House · held by the gardening households, about twenty-five people · danger I, II at the gate · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The old south gardens of the Halwen estate, still worked by the descendants of its gardeners: the only fresh food and clean cloth in the north. The place survives through attention: to water, weather, damaged leaves, missing tools, and how much a stranger can carry under a coat.

**History.** The estate family left during the Retreat and told their servants to keep the property until arrangements could be made. The first winter the gardeners took the glasshouses apart for repairs. The second they opened the estate seed room. By the fourth, the House was uninhabitable, and the gardens lasted because people were still working them. Travellers heading north asked where they could buy fresh food, and the name came from the answer.

**How it looks.** Six stone cottages behind walls raised with roof tiles, broken quernstones and pieces of carved lintel. Four have no doors and their windows packed with rubble; two are heated and lived in. Green leaves showing above the nearest beds: the first green anyone has seen in miles. A woman brushing ash from cabbage leaves with a bundle of feathers, holding each leaf from beneath so it does not tear.

**Layout.**

1. **The waiting gates.** Bare places beside each gate where strangers wait, kept bare so nobody sleeps in a bed they have not been given.
2. **The beds,** walled plots of roots, cabbage and flax, with ash-screens on frames brushed every morning.
3. **The two inhabited houses,** heated, shared by all the households.
4. **The ruined cottages,** used for storage and drying.
5. **The cistern,** covered, its level falling.
6. **The storage shed,** where travellers' stores are inspected since a dead dog was found inside a grain sack.
7. **The south wall and its apple tree (new),** trained flat against the warm stone, bearing a handful of small hard apples a year.
8. **The seed room (new as a site),** in the House's service wing, reached by the garden path: hundreds of stoppered jars with paper labels.

**Who is here. Sella Morn** (water), **Nera Halwen** (seed and the old plot names; how she came by the family's name is her own story), **Bren Tal** (straps and the gate), and the **Garden Hands** under **Dema Rusk**, who want the House back from the people who abandoned it.

**Old magic.** None. Its wonder is all work.

**Wonder and mystery.** Green over a grey wall. The seed room's labels read aloud like a roll call. Whether thirty-year-old seed will still grow; each spring Nera tries a few jars, and some come up.

**Dangers.** The Gardens themselves decide who is a thief.

**Finds.** Clean cloth, which is bandages. Broth and boiled water. Strap repair. Seed, if you are trusted.

## Halwen House

*Great complex · a two-storey country house with a downhill service wing, a chapel, a records tower and a glasshouse range · held in pieces by three factions · danger II, V in its upper floors · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The country residence of a family whose wealth came from land, storage rights and a share in the Bell Road's traffic. Its defining feature is separation: family and servants had different doors, stairs, water and rooms. Thirty years later those divisions still decide which rooms can be reached, what can be recovered, and who dies trying.

**History.** The Halwens left in stages: the youngest children first, then the treasury and archives, then the family, with promises of arrangements for the servants. Nobody came back until this winter, when Serjeant Oren Vale arrived with four retainers, five people in all, and a southern lady's orders: Lady Maudren Halwen means to have the house, and through it the road, and through the road the slag-iron.

**How it looks.** Three unequal parts on a slope: a two-storey main block with a steep roof, much of it open; a lower service wing running downhill; and a walled garden with the iron ribs of a glasshouse range along the sunny side. Beside the chapel, a narrow tower with a bell-beam and no bell. Slate still falls from the roof: some fragments on the front steps are furred with dirt, and some have clean sharp edges.

**Layout.**

1. **The carriage drive.** An avenue of pollarded trees, now a tunnel of split trunks and fallen branches, built long on purpose so guests never saw the laundry yard.
2. **The servants' path,** shorter and still used by the gardeners.
3. **The approach court and the entrance hall,** held by the Remnant. The hall's floor is a map of the estate, inlaid in stone, which shows rooms that no longer exist (new).
4. **The drawing room and the dining room.** The dining table still laid for a last meal, the silver gone and the plates left.
5. **The family passage and the upper stair,** to the **upstairs bedroom** where **Sabeth Rill** works on her reconnaissance notes.
6. **The upper floor,** mostly unclaimed, open to the sky in places. Floors that will not bear weight (V).
7. **The Chapel of the Kept Hand,** joined to the family passage by a narrow door, with its outer entrance on the paved chapel court. A carved hand closed round a flame over the door, the fingers polished by touching. Nine could sit comfortably; the household sent twenty.
8. **The records tower,** a narrow enclosed stair with one broken tread near the top, and a dry upper room of empty shelves and documents someone judged unimportant.
9. **The service door,** the narrow door between the house's two halves.
10. **The kitchen, pantry, scullery and garden office,** held by the Garden Hands. The garden office's door is swollen shut, and its plans are a fused mass of paper.
11. **The servants' stair, the laundry and the service yard,** downhill, with the water inspection point.
12. **The service passage,** under the yard, following the feeder channel.
13. **The cistern vault (new as a site),** a brick vault the size of a chapel, still black water, old coins on the floor.
14. **The lower culvert,** a metre wide, with a goblin nest at its end.
15. **The glasshouse range,** held by the **Gutter Kin**: low walls, leaning iron uprights, glass in the soil, a narrow maintenance trench along the back wall wired at ankle and throat height, their sleeping bench and sorting bed.
16. **The room nobody claims.** A small room off the family passage that every faction avoids. Its door is unlocked. Nobody will say what is in it, and the atlas leaves it for you to decide.

**Who is here.**

**The Halwen Remnant:** Serjeant **Oren Vale**, **Rauth Kell**, **Lenna Marr**, **Elsin Thale** and **Perrin Holt**, five retainers, three of them trained fighters. *Possession has been resumed.*

**The Garden Hands:** four rotating residents under **Dema Rusk**, two of them fighters. *We are taking back what we kept alive.*

**The Gutter Kin:** six road waifs, **Hesh Split-Lip**, **Sere**, **Kett**, **Brik**, **Iss** and **Moth**.

**Sabeth Rill,** upstairs, who has not approached the Gardens, and wants evidence before anyone's explanation.

**Goblins** in the lower culvert.

**Old magic. Keeping:** the chapel's covenant made instructions feel binding long after the person giving them had left. Whether it was a working or only a chaplain's skill, nobody can now say.

**Wonder and mystery.** The glass in the soil, which in a Bright Hour lights up like a field of stars. The memorial wall. The inlaid map in the hall. The room nobody claims.

**Dangers.** Falling slate and failing floors (V). The Remnant (III). The Kin's wires. The culvert nest (II, III).

**Finds.** The garden plans, if they can be separated. Wage accounts and boundary sketches in the records tower. Lady Maudren's orders in Oren's chest. Glasshouse fittings, many of them stolen already.

**Hooks.** The novel's chapters 11 to 14 and 19.

## Darnel Cross

*Inn and crossroads · 26 miles up the Bell Road, day 2 · an old animal-changing stop where a drove road crosses · held by seven households · danger I · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The last coaching stop on the road where a pack animal can still be shod: an inn at a crossroads whose sign, a yellow sheaf and a white horse, has lost the horse's head to the weather. Seven households sleep in its common room behind blanket walls, under an upper floor nailed shut because the main beam has split.

**History.** The inn served the drove road and the Bell Road together for two centuries: flocks came down from the uplands, iron went south, and both changed horses here. After S.0 the drovers stopped and the carriers dwindled; the last innkeeper left in S.6 (new). Seven households came in from the farms of the cross in the Hollow Winter and divided the common room, and nobody has left since. In S.24 the main beam split, and the farrier **Ossa Denn** began dating the crack with charcoal.

**How it looks.** A long low inn of grey stone with a moss-thick roof, the sign creaking over the cross. Inside, the common room is a maze of grey blankets on lines, each household's space marked by a pot and a bed-roll. Above, the beam: an oak trunk split along its length, propped at one end with a cart-shaft, with charcoal marks and dates climbing it like a child's height on a doorpost.

**Layout.**

1. **The crossroads and the sign,** the drove road running east to Bellwether Yard (new) and west into the uplands.
2. **The common room,** seven households behind blanket walls; one hearth, shared by turns.
3. **The nailed stair,** and above it **the upper floor**, its rooms still furnished, its floor sagging toward the split.
4. **The split beam,** with Ossa's marks, the cart-shaft prop and a second prop someone took away last winter.
5. **The stable yard,** shared; three ponies and a mule in it this winter.
6. **The farrier's shed,** Ossa's: forge, anvil, hoof-knives, a shoeing stocks.
7. **The well,** with a good stone head and a rope that is always someone else's to replace.

**Who is here.** Seven households, about thirty people (new). **Ossa Denn**, the farrier (see `../people/the-bell-road-and-the-measures.md`). Carriers changing animals. Greymaw in the birch along the drove road.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The charcoal marks climbing the beam year by year, a calendar of the building's death. The upper floor's rooms, untouched since S.6: beds made, a child's shoes under one, a coaching timetable on the wall for a road nobody coaches.

**Dangers.** The beam (it may kill the building whether it is dismantled or not). Greymaw on the drove road (II, III as a pack).

**Finds.** Shoeing and harness for any animal you bring. Horseshoe nails. In the nailed upper floor: blankets, a brass coaching horn, a locked strongbox with the inn's last takings in Crown pennies (worthless, and heavy), and the timetable. Road news, which lowers every price here.

**Hooks.** II.f The Split Beam: dismantle or prop. Who took the second prop.

## The Old Charcoal Lodge

*Shelter · 17 miles up the Bell Road, 3 miles off the lower track below Halwen · an abandoned burners' lodge in the birch woods · held by the Gutter Kin after the move · danger II · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A charcoal-burners' lodge deep in white birch, ringed by the burning floors: perfect black circles where the clamps stood, still bare of any growing thing after thirty years.

**History.** For three centuries the Citadels' furnaces ate charcoal, and the burners' lodges along the lower Bell Road made it: birch cut in winter, stacked into clamps, covered with turf and burned slow for a week. The lodge emptied in S.1, when the Citadels stopped buying. After chapter 14 of the novel and Act II of the old game, the Gutter Kin move here.

**How it looks.** White trunks in every direction, close enough to touch two at once, and then a clearing: black earth in rings, a low stone lodge with a standing rear wall and a turf roof half fallen. Inside, a dry bay and a frame-wide door.

**Layout.**

1. **The lower track** through the birch: Greymaw country.
2. **The burning floors,** nine round clearings of black earth where nothing grows, each about forty paces across.
3. **The lodge,** with its damaged outer room and the dry bay behind the standing wall.
4. **The spring,** whose flow needs checking every visit.
5. **The burners' cache** (new), under the third floor's hearth-stone: a burners' kit and a sealed jar.

**Who is here.** The Gutter Kin, five of the six, after the move. Greymaw on the track.

**Old magic.** None. The burners left a turf at each floor's edge for luck, as cutters do.

**Wonder and mystery.** The burning floors seen from the lodge roof at dusk: black holes in the white wood, like holes in the world. Why nothing has grown on them in thirty years; the burners said a burned floor rests a lifetime.

**Dangers.** Greymaw on the track (II, III).

**Finds.** Dry charcoal in the cache, enough to fire a forge for a week. A burner's long-handled rake and turf spade. The sealed jar: tallow. Birch-bark tinder, everywhere.

**Hooks.** The Kin's new home, if the player helped them reach it. The burners' cache, if the Kin have not found it first.

## The Split Kiln

*Workplace · 36 miles up the Bell Road, day 3 · two limekilns against a chalk cut · held by three intermarried households · danger IV in the cut · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The only white place in the north: two limekilns built into a chalk face, one split from lip to firemouth, one still burning white-hot at night when there is fuel, and three households white to the eyebrows with its dust.

**History.** Lime for the Citadels' mortar and the fen's fields came from here for generations. After S.0 lime became the most important thing in the north after salt, because the dead must be limed (*lime the dead or meet them*). The households restrict firing to themselves, partly to keep the trade and partly because an untrained correction once killed a worker and ruined a load; his tools still lie where he left them. The kiln depends on stone and fuel carriers, and the Roofed Mile detour has doubled what carriers charge.

**How it looks.** A white quarry face behind two squat stone towers. One is split open; you can walk into the crack and look up at the sky through it. The other roars at night, its firemouth white, and everything for a hundred paces is pale with dust. Sea-shells show in the chalk.

**Layout.**

1. **The yard and the three cottages,** with a roofed working yard between.
2. **The working kiln:** the charging platform at the top, reached by a ramp; the firemouth below.
3. **The split kiln,** cold, the crack wide enough to walk into.
4. **The fuel shed,** never full.
5. **The dead man's place** (new): the worker's tools, left where he fell beside the draw-hole, with lime thrown over them every year.
6. **The chalk cut,** a white face with ledges, a shelf where the troll rests, and a flooded sump at its foot.

**Who is here.** The kiln households. A fuel driver whose child sleeps under the seat. The **Blind Quarry-Troll** in the cut.

**Old magic.** None. The kiln-folk will not fire without singing a burning-song the whole first hour; they do not say it does anything.

**Wonder and mystery.** Sea-shells in the chalk, a hundred miles from any sea. The firemouth at night, the only white light in the north.

**Dangers.** The troll (IV), which hears the kiln and the carts. Quicklime, which burns wet skin and blinds. The split kiln's crack, which is still widening.

**Finds.** Sacks of Lime, for graves and chimneys. A lime-burner's long rake. The dead man's tools: a draw-hook and a pair of leather gauntlets, which nobody here will touch.

**Hooks.** Carry fuel for the kiln and get lime at the households' price. Bury the dead man's tools, or take them.

## The Bell-Tender's House

*Small site · 46 miles up the Bell Road, day 4 · a house and a fallen chapel at the quarry junction · held by Old Edrin · danger I · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The fifth tower of the old carrying line: a collapsed chapel and a standing house where the quarry branch leaves the road, kept by an old man who was never a priest and whose bell has not rung in years.

**History.** The chapel was built with the bell in it, one of the eleven towers that could carry a message the whole road in an afternoon. The tower fell in the first winters. **Old Edrin** inherited the bell's upkeep from his father, the bell-tender, who taught him the stroke code. Edrin keeps it low between timber supports with its tongue wrapped. He does not ring arrivals; he sends a runner, because noise reaches unwanted listeners.

**How it looks.** A stone house with a smoking chimney beside a heap of fallen chapel; the stump of the tower; a bronze bell sitting in a timber frame in the yard, its tongue bound in felt. From the house's step you can see both approaches: the road north and south, and the quarry branch going up toward Rook's Stair.

**Layout.**

1. **The collapsed chapel,** its tower a stump, its altar under the fall.
2. **The bell frame** in the yard.
3. **The warm room,** Edrin's.
4. **The outer workroom,** where travellers may shelter with their own fuel.
5. **The roof drain,** blocked: Edrin has the tools and cannot climb.
6. **The sheep-track detour** over the hill round the Roofed Mile, exposed and unsafe for a loaded cart.

**Who is here.** **Old Edrin** (see `../people/the-bell-road-and-the-measures.md`). A runner, sometimes. Travellers in the workroom.

**Old magic.** **Carrying.** Edrin's bell is a Hob's Height bell and one of the line's. Rung with the right stroke, its sound reaches the next tower about eight miles off, further in still air. Ringing costs the ringer warmth, as all small workings do; Edrin says his father came in from a long ring blue to the lips.

**Wonder and mystery.** The stroke code, which Edrin still knows and nobody else alive does. The night a bell rings once, far up the road to the north.

**Dangers.** Little here. The detour (II in weather).

**Finds.** Bell-Tongue Felt. The chapel's buried altar plate under the fall (a silver cup and paten, if anyone digs). Edrin's father's stroke-book, which Edrin will lend to someone who clears his drain.

**Hooks.** Clear the drain. Learn the code. Find out who rang the bell up the road.

## Rook's Stair: the Held Benches

*Great complex · a terraced quarry about 40 m deep with a settlement on its middle benches and galleries below · held by the Benchwardens, eighteen people · danger IV in the lower works, V everywhere at the edges · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The Citadels' great building-stone quarry: a stepped pit of grey stone cut into a hillside off the Bell Road, reached by road at the bottom and narrow stairs above. A small people holds its middle terraces near a spring, trading stone, lime, salvage iron and masonry skill. Their rule is that nobody works beneath stone another person has not inspected, and their safety marks have become a kind of property.

**History.** The quarry cut the dressed stone for the Slagcrown's halls and the Bell Road's chapels for two hundred years. It was abandoned as a working enterprise when the Citadels fell, but never emptied: cutters, haulers, their dependants and the old tally hands stayed on the benches they knew. The **Hanging Shed**, a lifting shed projecting over the quarry with ropes going down into the fog, still works, and no resident admits to having mended it.

**How it looks.** From the quarry branch: a vast grey bite out of the hill, terraces stepping down into a mist that lies in its bottom, the Hanging Shed jutting out over the drop on its beams like a heron's head. Ropes, ladders and chalk marks everywhere. The sound of water dripping into the sump. On the faces, the pale scars of cuts made two centuries ago and the dark scars of falls made last winter.

**Layout (from the lore's quarry plan, top to bottom).** Elevations: upper bench +8 m, middle bench +4 m, working floor 0 m, sump shelf -2 m.

1. **A. The upper gate.** The Benchwardens' gate, work shelter and trade point. The main exit south.
2. **B. The service cut.** A 1.35 m passage to the gate and the middle bench, too narrow for the troll's shoulders. A broken ladder overlooks the sump.
3. **C. The middle bench.** The settlement's terrace: the fresh inspection board, a rope anchor, the spring, the repairable roofs, and a failing outer edge marked with cracks. The inner lane is safe.
4. **D. The scree ramp.** A loose shortcut down to the working floor, open to view, bad footing.
5. **E. The winch.** A winch station with a frayed carrier hanging over a marked lane.
6. **F. The flooded sump.** Cold black water at the floor's east edge, with a shallow margin round it.
7. **G. The working floor.** Pillars of uncut stone and spoil heaps; a fractured overhang marked over one side.
8. **H. The lower adit.** A gallery into the hill, broad enough for the troll, bending so you cannot see along it.
9. **I. The troll shelf.** Its usual resting place.
10. **J. The lower shelter,** beyond, and the way out of the pit's bottom.
11. **The Hanging Shed,** above everything, over the drop, its ropes down into the fog.
12. **The old galleries (new).** Behind the lower adit, the first quarrymen's galleries, cut by hewers from the Citadels. Their lintels carry faded Holding marks, and they stand where the newer workings have fallen in. Nobody goes far into them.

**Who is here.**

**The Benchwardens,** eighteen cutters, haulers, dependants and tally hands. **Mara Sorn**, bench caller, assigns work and shelter and gives newcomers the worst inspected jobs. **Edda Vennick**, sounding hand, checks routes, records new cracks, and will not mark uninspected stone safe for anyone. **Tollen Rake**, stores keeper, has quietly extended debts because writing them off would show an empty store.

**Brida Coyle,** if she is still working her rope.

**The Blind Quarry-Troll,** a solitary sound-hunter, 2.6 m hunched and over a tonne, which hears a weapon swing at twelve metres and a blade on stone at sixteen.

**The gravenback (proposed in the lore),** a four-legged kill-thief with short grasping forelimbs that ranges the lower works and takes what the troll leaves.

**Old magic.** Holding, in the old galleries. The Benchwardens' chalk marks are not workings, and they say so.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Hanging Shed, which works and which nobody mends. The old galleries standing whole behind the fallen new ones. The view up from the working floor: terrace on terrace to a small grey sky.

**Dangers.** Failing edges and hanging rubble (V). The sump's cold water (V). The troll (IV). The gravenback (III). The Benchwardens' debts.

**Finds.** Dressed stone. Iron fittings. Masonry skill. Lime for the kiln. Route knowledge and inspection marks, sold.

**Hooks.** The novel's chapter 10 can move here if the kiln's troll needs a bigger stage. In The Long Account, the quarry branch leads on to the Drowned Measures.

## The Roofed Mile

*Deep place on the surface · an avalanche gallery about a mile long along a steep slope · held by the gallery-crawler · danger III, IV in its roof · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** An old avalanche gallery roofing the Bell Road where it runs under a shale slope: open arches on the valley side, thick piers carrying a roof buried under old falls. It is the best weather shelter on the road and something lives in it. The carriers have paid for the detour since Annet's party sent their board south.

**History.** The gallery was built by the Hewers' Lodge for the Citadels, to keep the iron road open when the slope came down in spring. It took a generation, and the Lodge's masons cut a Holding mark into the keystone of every arch (new). Travellers sheltered in its recesses for the best part of two hundred years and cut their names and marks on its walls. This winter Annet, Pell and young Renn came through with six sacks of lime and found drag marks, a sheep behind the third pier with its hindquarters gone, and a man beyond the fifth.

**How it looks.** From the road: a long low colonnade on the hillside, the open arches looking out over the valley like the windows of a cloister, the roof above them a slope of grey shale. Inside: almost clean ground, because the roof keeps the ash off; recesses between the piers; walls streaked with something greasy; strips of hide hanging from cracks under the roof; the wind moving across the arches with a low uneven whistle.

**Layout.**

1. **The south mouth,** where the road goes under, with drag marks crossing from the downhill verge.
2. **The arch recesses,** one between each pair of piers: old dung, a split bowl, a pack saddle with a strap mended in copper wire.
3. **The third arch,** and the sheep.
4. **The travellers' walls (new as a site).** Centuries of names, dates and marks cut into the inner wall: a boat, a hand, a list of a family's children with ages, a cart.
5. **The fifth pier,** and the man who died beyond it. *We do not know his name.*
6. **The roof-space (new).** Above the vault, under the shale, a crawlspace running the gallery's length, reached through cracks: the crawler's road, with bones packed into its corners.
7. **The middle stretch,** where the gallery curves and you cannot see either mouth.
8. **The north mouth,** and the road on to Saint Vey's Fold.
9. **The sheep-track detour,** over the slope above, exposed, not safe for a loaded cart.

**Who is here. The gallery-crawler,** an animal nobody can identify: low, long and heavy, 0.9 to 1.1 m at the shoulder and 150 to 250 kg, fast on the ground and on the walls, which drags carcasses up into the roof. Ash-moths in their thousands, which come to any flame.

**Old magic.** Holding. The keystones' marks are why the gallery still stands under a hillside of shale that has come down on it every spring for thirty years.

**Wonder and mystery.** The moths, rising to a lamp like snow falling upward. The travellers' walls. What the crawler is. Something knocking once, far inside, against stone.

**Dangers.** The crawler, from above (III, IV). A fall of shale through a damaged arch (V).

**Finds.** The pack saddle's buckles. The man beyond the fifth pier and whatever he carried. Shelter, if the crawler is gone.

## Saint Vey's Fold

*Small complex · a walled roadside hospice round a court · held by the Fold's people in the front, an ork pack in the rear · danger III · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A walled hospice on the Bell Road, later used for sheep, now shared: an ork pack dens in the stone rear range, the Fold's people live beside them behind a wall of dressed stone, and travellers sleep in the timber-roofed front dormitory, which the orks never enter.

**History.** Saint Vey kept the hospice as a shelter for drovers and pilgrims on the iron road; the Keeping endowed it, and the endowment survives in a certified copy that Brother Coll Hathe carries. After S.1 it became a sheepfold, then a ruin. For years an ork pack has denned in the rear range behind a chapel entrance that somebody blocked with dressed stone: the last hospice keepers, an early traveller or a Watch patrol, nobody knows.

**How it looks.** A long grey wall by the road with a gate in it, a cobbled court inside, a low stone range at the back with its doors packed with rubble, and a timber-roofed dormitory at the front with a broken stove. In the front dormitory's ceiling, painted planks: a summer sky, blue, with clouds, swallows and a gold sun with a face (new).

**Layout.**

1. **The gate,** and the road.
2. **The court,** which nobody crosses at night.
3. **The front dormitory,** bed frames, a broken stove, the **painted sky**.
4. **The walled chapel entrance,** dressed stone across it.
5. **The rear range,** the ork den: stone corridors, salt-licked walls, the plunder pile, and rubble packed into every opening but one.
6. **The Fold's houses,** three small stone rooms along the side wall where the Fold's people live.

**Who is here. The Fold's people,** who trade iron, salt and leather for repaired metal, bone work and dry fuel, and keep the wall mended and the court quiet after dark. **Brother Coll Hathe** with two hired escorts and the endowment. Travellers. **The pack** in the rear range.

**Old magic.** Perhaps. The orks will not go under any roofed timber, and the lore leaves the reason open: wariness, smell, or something else. Some travellers say it is the painted sky. Some say the dormitory's threshold was spoken in the old way when Saint Vey built it.

**Wonder and mystery.** The painted sky. Why the orks will not enter. Who walled the chapel.

**Dangers.** The pack (III). Coll's escorts, who will break the wall open if nobody stops them.

**Finds.** Trade with the Fold. The endowment's copy, which proves the Keeping's claim. What is in the pack's den, which came from the north.

## The Bent Span

*Small site · 76 miles up the Bell Road, day 7 · a stone bridge over a warm gorge · held by a household of four · danger II, V below · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A stone bridge over a gorge where the stream runs warm off the venting slopes and steams in the frost: its central arch settled, one parapet gone, kept open by a household of four with a winch who really repair it and really overcharge.

**History.** The bridge carried the Bell Road over the gorge for three hundred years. After S.0 the venting slopes upstream began warming the water, and the frost-and-thaw of warm water against cold stone began moving the central arch. The household came in S.11 (new), found the bridge failing, and made a living out of not letting it fail.

**How it looks.** A grey bridge with a sag in its back, timbers laid over the damaged paving in a lane just wide enough for a handcart. Below it, the gorge: walls green with moss and fern in midwinter, steam lifting off the water, small birds that live there all year. From the bridge, the warm gorge looks like a green crack in a white world.

**Layout.**

1. **The south approach.**
2. **The bridge shelter** and its winch; the best hauling ropes in the north hang here.
3. **The timbered lane** over the settled arch.
4. **The low-water way** under the bridge, for anyone who will not pay.
5. **The warm gorge,** green and steaming.
6. **The fallen tree upstream,** catching debris against one pier, and the moving bank above it.

**Who is here.** The bridge household: **Royce and Ivett Aske**, their grown son **Arn** and his wife **Gilda** (new names, checked against the lore on 2026-11-19). Birds in the gorge.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The warm gorge. Whether its warmth comes from the same fire as the venting slopes, and whether it will go cold.

**Dangers.** Fast water and a moving bank (V). The household's prices (II).

**Finds.** Hauling rope (for a price, or by theft). Fern and watercress from the gorge in midwinter, the only greens on the upper road. Driftwood caught on the fallen tree: a cart wheel, a coffin lid, a Watch targe with a name burned in it.

**Hooks.** Free the fallen tree before the pier goes. Cross by the low-water way and owe nobody.

## Coldbarrow

*Great complex · the Citadels' supply town, about half a mile across, with smithies, warehouses, slaughter yards and a fortified store · held by no single group · danger III, V in its drains · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The last town before the Citadels, built to feed and equip them. A small remnant holds the fortified storehouse; goblins hold parts of the slaughter quarter and the drains; scavenging parties come and go. No group holds the whole. A light here means fuel, not safety. Inside its northern gate is painted SOUTH, and under the gate: TAKE NOTHING YOU CANNOT CARRY WHEN THE CART BREAKS.

**History.** Coldbarrow was where the Bell Road's carts met the Citadels' needs: grain and meat up, iron down. Its smiths forged the Citadels' tools and its slaughter yards fed five Citadels' workforces. In S.1 it opened its gates to the fleeing Citadel folk, then set conditions, then emptied southward behind them. This winter its people began coming south, and Old Edrin's runner reached the Watchhouse with one sentence: *The north road is going quiet.*

**How it looks.** A grey town on a shelf of the hills, walled on its north side only, facing the Citadels as if the danger was always going to come from there. Smithy chimneys, tall warehouse gables with hoist beams, the long low roofs of the slaughter yards, and the squat fortified store in the middle like a keep. Iron shutters on half the windows.

**Layout.**

1. **The south road and the open south side,** no wall.
2. **The warehouses,** tall stone stores with hoist beams over their doors; one **warehouse hoist** still works, with its chain.
3. **The smithies' row,** a dozen forges, one with a **fixed anvil** too heavy for any cart.
4. **The slaughter quarter,** goblin ground: killing floors, hanging racks, blood channels in the floor running into the drains.
5. **The drains,** brick-vaulted, wide enough to walk stooping, running under the whole town to the stream. The goblins' road (new as a site). Whoever learns them can reach any part of the town unseen.
6. **The fortified store,** walled, iron-shuttered, with the remnant inside: troughs, stores, and the town's only safe beds.
7. **The northern gate,** and its two painted words.
8. **The upper road,** out of the gate toward the Citadels.

**Who is here. The storehouse remnant**: about fifteen people at the start of the winter, five by the time the novel reaches it in chapter 23 (both counts new). Scavenging parties from the south. Goblins in the slaughter quarter and the drains. In this winter's story, refugees heading south and ork packs coming down behind them.

**Old magic.** Marking, faintly: the best tools in the smithies' racks carry glim-wrights' marks, and nobody left alive knows their words.

**Wonder and mystery.** The blood channels in the slaughter floors, still red-brown after thirty years. The words on the gate.

**Dangers.** Goblins in numbers (III). The drains (V). Other scavengers (II, III). The gate's advice.

**Finds.** The hoist and chain (the only tools in the north that could finish the Slagcrown's gate). Iron shutters. The fixed anvil, which can be used but not taken. Marked tools. Food and oil, for the climb.

## The upper road

*Open country · 16 miles and about two days from Coldbarrow to Greystair · held by nobody* *· danger IV · lore, with new details marked*

Nothing on wheels gets up it. The road climbs in long switchbacks from Coldbarrow's shelf to the Citadels' scarp, through troll country and past the warm ground of the venting slopes. **Layout:** the switchbacks above Coldbarrow; **the Hewers' Cross (new)** , where the road crosses the hewers' old track and a stone cross stands with a cold-light mark worn into its face; the edge of the venting slopes; the view of Kell Tor's black tarn and its shut gate; the fork to fallen Greystair and its furnace glow; the last climb to the Slagcrown. **Who:** quarry-trolls on the high ground; the Bloomery Company's carriers between Greystair and Coldbarrow; listeners, who are seldom seen and see everyone. **Dangers:** trolls (IV), cold (V), and ork packs coming down. **Wonder:** the Citadels' scarp seen close for the first time, black and huge, with the Slagcrown's crown-towers on its rim.
