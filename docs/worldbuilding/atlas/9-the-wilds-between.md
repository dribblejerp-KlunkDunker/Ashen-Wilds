> large, each with lore, people and loot, and every one should leave the player in awe. These ten places fill the
> open country between the atlas's towns, roads and Citadels. All of it is **(new)**, accepted under ruling O10 in
> `../CANON.md`; the owner can strike any of it. Positions are in miles east (x) and south (y) of the Watchhouse door,
> on the same grid as the Chart of the Round (https://claude.ai/artifact/7Y4ERUJAC19mHBcDMwm19k). New names were
> checked against `docs/worldbuilding/` and the novel on 2026-11-19 with no clashes.

Every entry keeps the canon limits: only people are people (O1), small magic is small and costs warmth (O4), the
Old Crafts are dead trades' work that still acts in places and always fails somewhere, there is no prophecy, and the
three shut doors stay shut.

## The Spiral Fold

*Open country · the western uplands, about 64 miles north-west of the Watchhouse, four days off the Bell Road by the
drove road from Darnel Cross · held by one shepherd household · danger II · (new)*

**At a glance.** A sheepfold three hundred paces across, built as a spiral of drystone walls by fourteen generations of
drovers, each family adding its own ring. From the hill above it looks like a thumbprint pressed into the moor.

**History.** The drovers of the western uplands brought their flocks to winter here, and each family that wintered a
flock added a length of wall and set a ring-stone carved with its mark. The spiral grew outward for three hundred
years. After S.0 the flocks died, the families stopped coming, and the last ring was never closed. One household stayed.

**How it looks.** Grey walls curling inward over a whole hillside of short turf, the inner rings mossed and sunken,
the outer ones sharp, and at the centre a single stone hut with smoke. In fog you can walk the spiral for an hour and
never reach the middle.

**Layout.**

1. **The open end,** where the last ring stops unfinished.
2. **The rings,** each with a family's ring-stone at its gate.
3. **The lambing pens,** halfway in, roofed with turf.
4. **The centre,** a stone hut and a dew-pond.
5. **The strays' wall** (a secret), a gap in the seventh ring that lets you cut straight to the centre.

**Who is here.** **Aveline Croft**, about sixty, the last shepherd, with forty sheep and three dogs; she keeps the
ring-stones scrubbed and knows every family's mark. Greymaw on the moor. Peat-boar in the wet hollows.

**Old magic.** None. Drovers say a flock walked into the spiral at dusk cannot be scattered by wolves or Greymaw; the
truth is that walls are walls.

**Wonder and mystery.** The spiral from above at sunset, every ring-stone catching the light in turn. The ring-stone
nobody can read: the oldest, at the centre, with a mark that matches no family.

**Dangers.** Greymaw (II, III as a pack). Fog in the spiral.

**Finds.** Wool and fleeces. A wether bell cast at Hob's Height (Aveline will trade it for a ring-stone mark recut).
Mutton, in autumn. In the lambing pens, a drover's pack left in S.1: shears, a tally-stick and a purse of Crown pennies.

**Hooks.** Garth Collier of Bellwether Yard is the son of a Spiral Fold family; his ring-stone needs recutting. The
unreadable mark at the centre.

## The Hanging Tarns

*Open country and a drowned camp · a valley in the western uplands about 101 miles north-west of the Watchhouse, ten
or eleven days off the Bell Road · held by nobody · danger III, V on the ice · (new)*

**At a glance.** Seven tarns stepped down a high valley, each spilling over a stone lip into the next, frozen into a
staircase of blue ice for half the year. Under the ice of the lowest lies a prospectors' camp, drowned whole when an
old dam broke.

**History.** Prospectors from the Slagcrown worked the valley for a lesser seam in S.-20, damming the lowest tarn to
wash ore. In the thaw of S.-14 the dam broke and the lowest tarn rose over the camp in a night. The prospectors' names
are cut in the valley wall. The camp is still there, ten feet down.

**How it looks.** A valley of grey crags with seven terraces of water, each a different colour: black, slate, green,
white, blue, jade, and the last clear as glass. In hard frost you can lie on the lowest ice and see the camp below:
roofs, a sluice box, a wheelbarrow, a lantern still on its hook.

**Layout.**

1. **The valley mouth** and the prospectors' memorial wall.
2. **The seven tarns** and their spill-lips, a stair you can climb in frost.
3. **The broken dam** at the foot of the lowest tarn.
4. **The drowned camp,** under the lowest ice.
5. **The hermit's hut** above the third tarn.
6. **The crags,** where a quarry-troll lies up in summer.

**Who is here.** **Ulric Tibb**, a hermit fisher who has lived above the third tarn since S.9 and cuts holes in the ice
for char; he lost a brother in the camp before he was born, and has never gone down. A **Blind Quarry-Troll** on the
crags in summer. Harrow eels in the lower tarns.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The camp under the ice, perfectly kept. The colours of the seven tarns, which nobody has
explained. Why the lantern on its hook is still lit in some fishermen's tellings.

**Dangers.** Thin ice (V). The troll (IV in summer). Cold.

**Finds.** Under the ice (by a hole cut and a cold dive): a prospectors' assay kit, slag-iron picks, a strongbox of
ore samples and a ledger. Char from Ulric, smoked. On the memorial wall, the prospectors' names.

**Hooks.** Raise the camp's ledger: it records a lesser seam of slag-iron ore still in the valley wall. Ulric's brother.

## The Ringing Field

*Small site · the scarp foot under Hob's Height, about 104 miles north-north-west of the Watchhouse · held by nobody ·
danger II, III · (new)*

**At a glance.** A field of hundreds of bells and bell-blanks, the bell-founders' test castings and failures, half-sunk
in the turf below Hob's Height. On a windy day some of them hum.

**History.** Hob's Height cast the Bell Road's bells. Every casting was tested by ear at the foot of the scarp, and the
ones that were not good enough were rolled into the field and left. Two hundred years of failures. When Adam Haddon
led his people out in S.1, they left the field as it was.

**How it looks.** A slope of turf studded with bronze: bells as big as a cart lying on their sides, blanks like
upturned bowls, cracked bells green with age, and among them, set upright on stone stools, a few that were never
failures, waiting for towers that were never built.

**Layout.**

1. **The founders' track,** down from Hob's Height's lower gate.
2. **The failures,** spread over the slope.
3. **The waiting bells,** on their stools in a row.
4. **The testing frame,** a great oak gallows with a striking beam.
5. **The founders' shed,** roofless, with mould boxes and a pile of tin ingots.

**Who is here.** **Wat Corley**, the listener of Hob's Height, comes down some days to wrap the waiting bells' tongues,
one by one, as his people swore to. Goblins from Hob's Height's shafts. Orks on the crag in hard winters.

**Old magic.** **Carrying.** The waiting bells were cast with the founders' marks, and some still carry: struck, they are
heard at the next tower eight miles off, and further in still air. The cracked ones carry wrong, and nobody knows where
their sound goes. Striking one costs the striker warmth, as any working does.

**Wonder and mystery.** The field humming in a west wind, a low chord from two hundred years of bronze. The cracked
bells.

**Dangers.** Goblins (I, II in a group). Orks (III, in hard winters). Noise, which brings both.

**Finds.** Bell-metal (bronze, heavy, valuable in the south). Tin ingots. A small carrying bell, light enough to carry,
which Wat Corley will not let you take without a quarrel.

**Hooks.** Help Wat wrap the waiting bells before the goblins find them. Ring one to call the Bell Road, once.

## The Iron Stair

*Spire · the scarp face between Greystair and Dunmarrow, about 98 miles north of the Watchhouse · held by nobody ·
danger IV · (new)*

**At a glance.** A stair of slag-iron plates bolted down the scarp's sheer face for three hundred metres beside an ore
chute, so that miners could climb from the uplands to the pitheads without the long road round. Its landings still
carry the hewers' Holding marks, and some of them still hold.

**History.** Cut by the Hewers' Lodges for the Slag-Lords in S.-120, when the upper road was blocked by a landslip for a
decade. Thousands climbed it daily. In S.1 goblins came down the chute, and the Stair was abandoned. Two landings have
fallen since.

**How it looks.** A black zigzag of iron and rock on the face of the scarp, the chute beside it a rusted trough as wide
as a cart. From the bottom it vanishes into cloud. From the top you can see the whole uplands to the fen.

**Layout.**

1. **The Foot,** a stone gatehouse and the chute's spill-heap.
2. **The lower flights,** sound.
3. **The broken landing** at a third of the way, where a rope bridge was strung in S.0 and is still there, rotten.
4. **The marked landings,** where the Holding marks hold the rock overhead.
5. **The fallen landing,** a gap of ten metres crossed only by the chute.
6. **The Head,** a ledge with a winch-house, a stride from the pitheads.

**Who is here.** Nobody. Goblins in the chute. Ravens.

**Old magic.** **Holding**, failing. The marks on the landings hold the overhang; where a mark is worn, the rock has come
down. Nobody alive can cut a new one.

**Wonder and mystery.** The view from the Head: the whole country from the scarp to the fen, the Bell Road's towers in a
line, and on a clear night the lamp of Saint Orra's Steps, a hundred miles away.

**Dangers.** The climb (IV). The fallen landing (V). Goblins in the chute (II in a group). Wind.

**Finds.** Slag-iron plates, which are treasure in the south if you can carry one. The winch-house's tools and a coil of
chain. Miners' lamps abandoned on the landings.

**Hooks.** A shortcut to the pitheads for anyone with a head for heights. Bring a plate down.

## Hollowmere Dam

*Great complex · the eastern uplands about 53 miles north-east of the Watchhouse, four days east of the Bell Road from
the Split Kiln · held by a warden's household · danger II, V on the sluice · (new)*

**At a glance.** A stone dam forty metres high across an upland valley, built by the Crown to hold back Hollowmere and
feed the Causeway's sluices in dry summers. Behind it, a drowned village; below it, the whole north-east of the fen.

**History.** Built by Wynfrith's engineers with the Causeway (S.-140), it feeds the upland stream that becomes navigable at
Brackenreach; its sluices were opened in dry summers to keep that stream and the Causeway's fen side deep enough for
boats. Since S.0 nobody has opened them. The lake has risen two metres in thirty
years, and the dam is weeping at its foot. If it fails, the north-east fen floods to the Causeway's crown.

**How it looks.** A great grey wall across a valley, a road along its top, a sluice-house like a chapel at its centre
with a wheel two storeys high. Behind it, still water reflecting the scarp; under the water, a church tower and the
roofs of the village the lake drowned.

**Layout.**

1. **The dam road,** along the crest.
2. **The sluice-house** and its great wheel, rusted but whole.
3. **The warden's house** at the east end.
4. **The weep,** water running from the dam's foot where no water should.
5. **The drowned village of Hollow,** under the lake, its tower top showing.
6. **The spillway,** dry for thirty years, a stone channel full of bones (Greymaw use it as a den).

**Who is here.** **Jessamy Hesketh**, the dam-warden, about fifty, whose family has kept the dam for six generations; she
has the sluice-key and the old warden's book that says when to open, and is afraid to. Her two sons. Greymaw in the
spillway.

**Old magic.** **Staking**: the dam's footings were staked by Stakeby's hands in the old way, and the weep is where one
stake has failed.

**Wonder and mystery.** The drowned village's tower, with its bell still in it: on still evenings the lake moves it, and
it rings once.

**Dangers.** The sluice (V: opening it floods Low Harrow's drowned lanes further and drains the north-east meres).
Greymaw (II, III).

**Finds.** The sluice-key and the warden's book. Dry stores in the warden's house. The drowned church's plate, if
anyone dives.

**Hooks.** Open the sluice a little each week to save the dam, and decide whose ground floods. Bring a stake-hand to
re-drive the failed footing.

## The Kettle

*Small site · the Warm Valley, high in the Long Marrow about 142 miles east-north-east of the Watchhouse, nine days
from the Lower Gate of the Marrow Passes · held by nobody · danger II, IV in weather · lore names the valley; the rest (new)*

**At a glance.** One high valley where snow never lies and a hot spring steams in a stone basin that may be cut or may
be natural. Prospectors called it the Kettle. The Weald's fear that the Long Marrow is burning inside has one fact under
it here, and it proves only that one valley is warm.

**History.** Citadel prospectors found it in S.-60 and used it as a winter camp. They argued for twenty years about
whether the basin was cut. Nobody has lived here since S.4.

**How it looks.** A green bowl in a white range, the only green for a hundred miles, with a stone basin at its heart
steaming in the frost, its rim worn smooth as a cup's, and around it the prospectors' turf huts, fallen in.

**Layout.**

1. **The snow line,** where white turns green in a single step.
2. **The basin,** steaming.
3. **The prospectors' huts.**
4. **The offering ledge,** where prospectors left a stone each from home.
5. **The vent** above the basin, where the ground is too hot to stand on.

**Who is here.** Nobody. Ork packs from the Passes in winter, sometimes: they feel the cold badly.

**Old magic.** None that anyone can show. The basin's rim carries marks that might be tool marks, or might be water.

**Wonder and mystery.** Bathing in the Kettle in a blizzard. The offering ledge: a hundred stones from a hundred valleys.

**Dangers.** Weather on the way (IV). Orks (IV, in winter).

**Finds.** The prospectors' cache in the largest hut: a map of the Long Marrow's trails, a set of assay scales, a sack of
salt gone hard. Watercress in the outflow in midwinter.

**Hooks.** The map shows the prospectors' trail round the Saddle Wall of the Marrow Passes.

## The Cold Edge

*Open country · where the Scald ends and the Hoar begins, about 161 miles north of the Watchhouse, eight days across the
Scald · held by nobody · danger IV · lore names the Edge; the rest (new)*

**At a glance.** A line across the north where the venting warmth of the Scald stops and the ice begins: green moss on
one side of a stride, blue ice on the other, running east and west out of sight.

**History.** The Hoarfolk drove herds to the Edge every autumn to graze the warm moss. Their herds died between S.1 and
S.5 on lichen that had taken up the ash. The Edge is where they left the last herd: a hundred reindeer frozen where they
lay down.

**How it looks.** Steam rising off the moss, and a stride away a wall of ice a man's height, blue inside, with the frozen
herd under it like a carving. Hoarfolk cairns every mile along the line.

**Layout.**

1. **The Cairn Line's end,** where the old prospectors' track from the scarp foot reaches the Edge.
2. **The moss,** warm to the hand.
3. **The ice wall.**
4. **The frozen herd.**
5. **The Hoarfolk cairns,** each with a carved antler on top.

**Who is here.** Ice-walkers going south, sometimes: Hoarfolk who share no words with anyone south of the Scald and speak
in signs. Carriers call one of them the Salt-Woman because she trades skins for salt every year. Hoar-bears on the ice.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** Standing with one foot on warm moss and one on ice. The herd under the ice. What the cairns' antler
carvings say.

**Dangers.** Cold (IV). Hoar-bears (IV). Getting lost on the moss in steam.

**Finds.** Reindeer horn and hide from the frozen herd's edge. Skins and seal-oil lamps traded from the ice-walkers for
salt. A Hoarfolk carved antler, which the ice-walkers will want back.

**Hooks.** Trade with the Salt-Woman. Return an antler to its cairn.

## The Kings' Barrows

*Deep place · the high Downs about 70 miles south of the Watchhouse, two days west of the Salt Trace · held by a
warden family · danger III · (new)*

**At a glance.** Nine long barrows in a line along the highest down, the graves of Orlen's first kings before the Deep
Charter. One was opened by robbers in S.12 and its name-stones carried off, and since then the Unnamed walk there.

**History.** The barrows were old when Godric II was crowned. Each king's name was cut on a stone at the barrow's door.
A warden family kept them for the Crown. In S.12, a hungry year, robbers opened the fifth barrow, took its goods and
carried off its name-stones to build a grange wall. The Unnamed rise at unnamed graves, and the fifth barrow has been
walked ever since. Why a name works stays unsettled.

**How it looks.** Nine green humps each a hundred paces long, white chalk showing where the turf has slipped, a stone
door at each east end. The fifth door is broken, and the chalk round it is trodden in a ring.

**Layout.**

1. **The ridge track** along the down.
2. **The nine barrows,** each with its door and name-stone; the fifth without.
3. **The fifth barrow's passage,** forty paces into the chalk, with side chambers.
4. **The warden's cottage** at the ridge's end.
5. **The robbers' camp,** a hollow below the fifth barrow, with their leavings.

**Who is here.** **Garrick Hollin**, the last warden, about seventy, who saw the robbers and could not stop them, and
knows which grange wall the stones went into. The Unnamed at the fifth barrow.

**Old magic.** **Keeping**, failing: the name-stones were the Keeping's work, and the barrows that keep them are quiet.

**Wonder and mystery.** The barrows from the ridge track at dusk: nine dark shapes in a line, and the fifth one
walked. The fifth king's name, which nobody remembers.

**Dangers.** The Unnamed (III). The passage's roof chalk.

**Finds.** What the robbers missed in the fifth barrow's side chambers: an amber necklace, a bronze torque, a cup of
black glass. In the robbers' camp, a grange's dated receipt that names who bought the stones.

**Hooks.** Bring the name-stones back from the grange wall at Whitlow, and the fifth barrow goes quiet. Name the fifth king.

## Whitlow Grange

*Town · the largest of the grange-holds, on the Salt Trace about 100 miles south-south-east of the Watchhouse, about five
days north of Wexhaven · held by its steward · about four hundred people · danger I inside, II outside · (new)*

**At a glance.** A walled farm grown into a town round a tithe barn as big as a cathedral, which holds more grain than
anywhere north of the sea. Its steward governs in the name of a march-lord nobody has heard from in thirty years, and
its mile of the Salt Trace is the most expensive in Orlen.

**History.** The march-lord sailed in S.3. His steward did not, and has collected the lord's levies ever since, in grain,
from everybody. The wintermen who guard the barn are paid in grain; many are deserters and a few are Watch. Part of
Whitlow's east wall was built in S.12 with stones from the Kings' Barrows.

**How it looks.** A long white wall round a ring of farm buildings, cottages, a forge and a chapel, all overshadowed by
the barn: a vast timber nave on stone footings, its roof a hill of thatch, its doors big enough for a laden wagon.

**Layout.**

1. **The Trace gate** and its toll-house.
2. **The great barn,** the granary, guarded day and night.
3. **The steward's hall.**
4. **The wintermen's barracks.**
5. **The east wall,** where the barrow-stones are.
6. **The tenants' row,** where the people who work for a share live.

**Who is here.** **Ambrose Fell**, the steward, about sixty, who keeps the march-lord's seal and believes he is the only
thing between the Downs and starvation, which may be true. Wintermen. Tenants. In hard winters, people from the fen at
the gates.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The inside of the barn: a cathedral of grain, sacks to the rafters, swallows in the roof, the
smell of summer in midwinter.

**Dangers.** The wintermen (II). The toll (it takes a third of a carrier's load).

**Finds.** Grain, for a price, or for work. The march-lord's seal and Fell's levy rolls in the steward's hall. Winterman
arms. The barrow-stones in the east wall, with the robbers' receipt to prove it.

**Hooks.** Take the barrow-stones out of the wall: Fell will want something in exchange. The levy rolls name every family
in the Downs, which is what the Collectors would kill for.

## Gull's Throat

*Deep place · a sea cave on the Wrack Shore about 165 miles west of the Watchhouse, two weeks by the Downs and the
coast · held by a wrecker family · danger III, V at the tide · (new)*

**At a glance.** A sea cave so large a ship can float into it on the spring tide, and over a century the sea has packed it
with wrecks: hulls stacked on hulls up to the cave's roof, and among them a galley of Osbert III's slag-iron fleet with
its galley stove still in it.

**History.** The cove's currents carry wreckage into the cave and leave it there. The Varrow wreckers have salvaged it
for four generations. The galley, the *Royal Tern*, went into the cave in S.-50 and is too deep in the stack to reach; its
stove is the most valuable thing on the Wrack Shore, and everybody knows it.

**How it looks.** A black arch in the cliff with the sea booming into it, and inside, by a fire-cradle's light, a mountain
of ships: ribs, masts, figureheads, keels, a cathedral of wreck with gulls nesting in every crack.

**Layout.**

1. **The cliff path** down to the cove.
2. **The Varrow huts,** roofed with hull planking.
3. **The tide-mouth,** passable at low water.
4. **The stack,** climbed by rope and plank inside the cave.
5. **The *Royal Tern*,** near the stack's bottom, its stern showing at the lowest tides.
6. **The roof gallery,** a ledge where the Varrows keep what they do not show.

**Who is here.** **Ide Varrow**, the family's matriarch, about seventy, who will sell anything but the galley. Her
grandsons, who would sell the galley. Skerry boat-masters each thaw. Hoar-bears in pack-ice winters.

**Old magic.** Wind-knots hung on every hut. Nothing else.

**Wonder and mystery.** The cave at a spring tide, the whole stack groaning and shifting as the sea lifts it. Figureheads
from every coast on the Round looking down from the dark.

**Dangers.** The tide (V). The stack shifting (IV). The Varrows (III).

**Finds.** Wreck salvage of every kind: rope, canvas, chain, lamp oil, a ship's bell, a box of Sable Shore silver marks.
The *Royal Tern*'s slag-iron galley stove, if you can reach it at the lowest tide and lift it: worth a house in Port Sable.

**Hooks.** The stove. Ide's price. The grandsons' price.
