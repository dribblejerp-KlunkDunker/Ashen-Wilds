Every place in Orlen and the Round, written up in depth: its history, its look, its layout zone by zone, who lives there, and what old magic still acts in it. It is built so each place can be laid out before any art is made. Everything here is proposed until you rule on it, and it goes into the Ashen-Wilds repo once approved.

## The ruling: old magic, half-understood

You ruled on 2026-11-14 that places can be plainly magical: halls, spires and deep places built or changed by the old peoples' workings, which still act after the people who made them are dead. Nobody alive fully understands any of it.

**What this changes.** The canon sheet's rule that every strange thing has a plain cause now applies to *events*, not to *places*. A hall that should have fallen and has not, a bell that is heard twelve miles off as if it rang beside you, a line of lights that wakes under a hill: these are real workings, and the atlas says so.

**What it keeps.**

**The three shut doors stay shut.** Who made the Vaults, what the orks fled, and why the Unnamed rise get evidence and guesses, never answers.

**No beast works magic** (O1). Beasts live in magical places; they do not use them.

**No prophecy, no chosen one.** A working does not care who speaks it.

**Every working costs something:** warmth (the fen calls it *the chill*), salt, sound, or time.

**Half-understood means disputed.** Every craft below is explained three different ways by the three people who still practise it, and the atlas gives all three.

## The Old Crafts

Orlen's old magic was not one art but six trades, each kept by the people whose work needed it, each passed down by mouth and never written. The Keeping's register-keepers would not record workings, and most of the trades died with the Citadels in S.1 and S.2. What is left is in places: the things those trades made still work, and nobody can make more.

| Craft | Who kept it | What it did | What still acts | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holding | The Hewers' Lodge of the five Citadels | Marks cut into living rock so a roof would stand where it had no right to. **Three explanations of every craft.** Hewers say the marks *remind the rock*. The Keeping says the words are *debts owed and paid*. Smiths say *it is only a better way of doing the work*. The atlas never says which is right.

**How this sits with small magic (O4).** O4 governs every working a living person can do: it is small, it is spoken aloud, it takes warmth (*the chill*), a mark fades in hours, and nothing anyone alive does throws fire or moves a wall. The Old Crafts in this atlas do not break that. What still acts in places is the **dead trades' work**: Holding marks, carrying bells and stakes made by trades that died with the Citadels in S.1 and S.2, or are dying now, by methods nobody alive has whole. A living hewer can make cold light; no living hewer can hold a roof. Where the atlas shows a great working, it is always old, it is always failing somewhere, and it can never be made again. *Confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-14 as an extension of O4 under the old-magic ruling.*

## Kinds of place

The world is built at the scale of Elden Ring and Dark Souls: wide dangerous country connecting a handful of enormous places, each one a world of its own, many of them deep underground.

| Kind | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Great complexes | A single huge built place explored for hours: many levels, a looping route that opens shortcuts back to its start, a held heart at the end. | The Slagcrown, the Bell-Spire of Hob's Height (new), Saint Orra's Steps, Halwen House, Coldbarrow, Greystair |
| Deep places | Underground on a scale that should not be possible: caverns, drowned halls, galleries held up by Holding marks, roads under mountains. | The Burning Bank (new), the Low Galleries, the Hewers' Road (new), the Eleventh Gallery, the Black Seam drift, the White Deeps |
| Spires | Towers seen from far away, used to find your way. Each is a landmark before it is a place. | Hob's Height's Bell-Spire, Kell Tor's beacon, Saint Orra's tower, the Slagcrown's crown-towers, the Causeway tower |
| Cities and towns | Places where people still live, each held by a faction, with its own laws, prices and quarrels. | Dunmere, Brackenreach, Coldbarrow, Wexhaven, Port Sable, Saint-Aumere, Halsing |
| Open country | The land between, with weather, roads, beasts, ruins and small sites. | The Blackfen, the Bell Road uplands, the venting slopes, the Drowned Measures |
| Small sites | A single ruin, crossing or camp with one danger and one find. | The White Trees, the Leaning Team, the Cold Passing, the Bent Span |

**Every great complex follows five rules.**

1. **It is visible before it is reached.** Its tallest part is seen from the country around it for hours or days.
2. **Its route loops.** You fight forward and then open a door, lower a lift or drop a ladder that brings you back near the start.
3. **It has a held heart:** a chamber at the end where its story is, and usually its worst danger.
4. **It tells its own history in things,** not in speeches: rooms left mid-use, names cut in stone, the dead where they fell.
5. **Someone still lives in or near it,** and wants something from whoever comes.

## How every entry is laid out

Each place gets the same headings, so a layout can be drawn from the entry alone.

1. **The line under the name:** kind, scale, who holds it, how dangerous it is, and whether it is from the lore or **(new)** .
2. **At a glance:** what it is, in two sentences.
3. **History:** what it was built for, what happened to it, what it is now.
4. **How it looks:** materials, colours, light, sound and smell, and what you see of it from far off.
5. **Layout:** the place as numbered zones in the order you meet them, with how they connect, what is above and below, the shortcuts, and the secrets. This is the brief for the map.
6. **Who is here:** the people (named), the factions, the beasts and the dead.
7. **Old magic:** which crafts still act here, and how.
8. **Wonder and mystery:** what stops you, and what nobody can explain.
9. **Dangers.**
10. **Finds:** what is worth taking, for the novel and the game.
11. **Hooks:** where it touches the novel and The Long Account.

**Danger tiers** follow the bestiary: I (a nuisance), II (can kill you alone), III (kills a careless group), IV (leave), and V (the place itself kills: fire, water, rock, air).
