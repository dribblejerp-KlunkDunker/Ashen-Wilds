## Orlen, and the Downs

### Orlen

*A kingdom with no king · the north shore of the Slate Sea, from the Wrack Shore cliffs to the Long Marrow, about 250 miles each way · held by nobody · lore*

The slag-iron kingdom. Outsiders call it *the Smother*. Its kings granted the Deep Charter in S.-196, built the Causeway and the waystations, refitted the fleet with slag-iron stoves, and spent the Fifth faster than the Citadels could deliver it. **Aldric IV**, the Gentle King, sailed in S.3 with the court, the treasury ships, the archive ships and the senior clergy, and the harbour at Wexhaven was burned behind them. Nobody knows where he is. Thirty years on the kingdom is ash: the fen holding on, the Citadels empty, the grange-holds of the Downs taking tolls from everyone, and its debts traded for a hundredth of their face in a counting-house across the sea. **Its four parts:** the **Blackfen** and the **Bell Road** country; the **Highland Citadels** on the scarp; the **Downs**, the grain country between the fen and the coast; and the coast itself, with **Wexhaven** in the south and the **Wrack Shore** in the west. **Its edges:** the **Scald** north of the scarp, the **Long Marrow** range east.

### The Downs and the grange-holds

*Open country and fortified farms · rolling chalk grain country between the fen and the coast, about 120 miles deep and 250 long · held by the grange-holds · danger II, III at the granaries · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The kingdom's grain country. The march-lords sailed in S.3 and their stewards did not. Each walled **grange-hold** now sows rye in the thaw mud, hires **wintermen** to guard the granary, and takes a toll on its mile of the Salt Trace. They owe levies to nobody and collect them from everybody.

**How it looks.** Long bare chalk downs under the Dim, grey-green in summer and white in winter, with a walled farm on every rise: a great tithe barn, a granary on staddle stones, a tower for the watch, and a wall round all of it. Between them, open field gone back to thin grass, and the Salt Trace running south between toll-posts.

**A grange-hold, zone by zone (new, typical).** 1. The **toll-post** on the Trace, with its chain. 2. The **outer field**, sown in strips. 3. The **wall** and its gate, wide enough for a wagon. 4. The **yard** and the **tithe barn**, timber-framed, the size of a church. 5. The **granary** on staddle stones, under guard day and night. 6. The **steward's house**, with the march-lord's empty chair kept in the hall. 7. The **wintermen's barracks**. 8. The **watch tower**, from which the next grange-hold's tower can be seen.

**Who is here. The stewards**, who govern in lords' names that nobody has heard from in thirty years. **The wintermen**, hired guards paid in grain, many of them deserters, a few of them Watch. Tenants who work for a share. Greymaw in the open country, and in hard winters, people from the fen at the gates.

**Old magic.** Little. The tithe barns' great doors were blessed by the Keeping when they were raised, and stewards still have them blessed, because a barn that burns is a winter that kills.

**Wonder and mystery.** The **White Horse of the Downs (new):** a horse a quarter of a mile long cut into the turf of the highest down, down to the white chalk. Nobody knows who cut it or when. The grange-holds keep it scoured every spring, each hold doing its own stretch, though they agree on nothing else.

**Dangers.** The grange-holds' tolls and wintermen (II, III). The open downs in snow (V).

**Finds.** Rye. Wagons. Hired swords. News from the coast.

## Wexhaven, the Burned Quay and the Constance

### Wexhaven and the Burned Quay

*Burned royal city · a deep-water inlet on the south coast, scale left unset as the archive requires · a few hundred people in the customs vaults, the upper town quarried by foreign crews · danger III in the upper town, IV on the quay, V in the inlet · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The royal city, and the place the kingdom ended. In the spring of S.3 the fleet sailed from here, and the night after, the harbour burned. Now it is a black waterfront, an inlet full of drowned hulls, and an upper town that **plate-reavers** from across the sea strip for slag-iron stoves. A few hundred people live in the stone **customs vaults** under the quay. This is where the voyage of *the Round and the Long Account* begins, and where it ends.

**History.** The Crown's Fifth came down the Salt Trace to Wexhaven, and plates and bars went out of it for silver and salt. In the spring of S.3, when the inlet was clear of ice, **Aldric IV**, the court, the treasuries, archives and relics, the Keeping's southern hierarchy and the Grain March's grain sailed with the royal fleet. The proclamation read in the **harbour square** promised relief before the second harvest. The night after, the quays, warehouses, yards, every ship left in the inlet down to the fishing boats, and much of the lower city burned. Whether it was ordered is disputed four ways. The harbour-marshal **Hugh Ardern** commanded the rear guard, did not sail, and is not recorded after S.3. The salt-pans east of the city did not burn, and salt still comes up the Trace in small amounts. Who boils it is not said.

**How it looks.** A city on hills round a narrow inlet. From the Trace you see the upper town first: grey roofs with no smoke, every chimney broken open where a stove was pulled out. Below, the burned lower city runs down to a black shelf of quay, and beyond it the inlet is a forest of masts standing out of grey water at every angle.

**Layout, zone by zone.**

1. **The Trace road in**, past the last grange-hold's toll-post.
2. **The upper town**: lanes of houses gutted for their stoves, and the reavers' working camp wherever the current job is.
3. **The Long Steps (new)** : the stair down through the burned lower city to the quay. A **shortcut** opens later through a fallen warehouse's hoist-shaft.
4. **The harbour square**, where the proclamation was read, its fountain dry and full of ash.
5. **The Burned Quay**: black warehouse ribs, cranes fallen across the lanes, and **Gytha Marrin's chair** set out where she can see the inlet.
6. **The customs vaults**: stone-roofed, unburned, below the quay. The few hundred live here, and Gytha keeps the receipts here.
7. **Wat Hebble's slip (new placement)** , where the shipwright built the *Small Mercy* from wreck timber over nine years.
8. **The Breakwater (new)** : a stone arm out to a dead light-tower.
9. **The inlet of drowned hulls**, crossed hull to hull at dead low water, and the *Constance* at its deep end.

**Who is here. Gytha Marrin, the Receipt-Wife**, once a customs tallywoman, who for twenty-eight years has taken a royal grain receipt from everyone who came down the Trace and written them a line in exchange. She holds eleven thousand. She has ash-lung at the crust stage and cannot go herself; she wants an answer before she stops answering. **Wat Hebble**, shipwright, who wants his hull back in one piece. **Corvin Hale**, reaver captain,

licensed, pays his crew, thinks of himself as a salvor, and wants stoves. His crew will fight, trade (they sell a chart of the harbour chains), or let a company sail in their wake. The people of the vaults, who start hostile to reavers and anyone who looks like one.

**Old magic.** Almost none. The slag-iron in the stoves is ore that keeps heat, not a working, and that is the whole reason the city is quarried.

**Wonder and mystery.** The masts at dawn standing out of the mist like a drowned wood. The dead light-tower, whose lamp-room glass is still whole and catches the low sun so that for a minute at dusk it seems lit. The vaults, warm with a few saved stoves, and the sound of eleven thousand paper receipts being counted.

**Dangers.** Reavers (III). Fallen floors in the lower city (III). The inlet's cold water and the tide (IV). What holds the dark inside the hulls (V).

**Finds.** Salvage, stoves, rope, ship's stores. News from across the sea, via the reavers. Passage, if Wat's hull floats.

**Hooks.** Gytha's commission: *Present them to whoever answers for the Crown. Bring back what is paid. If nothing is paid, bring back the name of who refused.* The last scene of every ending is here, at her chair, with her in it or not.

### The Constance

*Drowned royal ship · one of the hulls burned at her mooring in S.3, lying in the deep end of the inlet · held by a hulk-conger · danger V · lore, with new layout marked*

**At a glance.** The first landing of the voyage. Her slag-iron **galley stove** is still aboard, and lifting it at low water is a two-person load, with the tide as the clock and a **hulk-conger** in the hold.

**Layout (new).** 1. The **exposed side**, walked like a sloping street at dead low water. 2. The **upper deck**, burned to the beams. 3. The **gun deck**, half flooded. 4. The **galley**, where the stove sits on its iron bed, too heavy for one. 5. The **hold**, under water and dark, where the conger lies. 6. The **great cabin** at the stern, with its windows staved in.

**Who is here.** The **hulk-conger** (beast; never reasoned with): a big eel, kin to the fen's harrow eels. It holds one dark space and strikes whatever enters it. How large one can get in a hull nobody opens is a question nobody wants answered.

**How to beat it.** Bait it out with meat on a line, or wait for low tide.

**Wonder and mystery.** A royal ship lying on her side in the grey water, her carved stern lettering still legible at low tide.

**Dangers.** The hulk-conger (V). The tide coming back (IV). The stove's weight on a sloping, wet deck.

**Finds.** The stove. Whatever the cabin held that the fire missed.

## The Wrack Shore, the Long Marrow and the Marrow Passes

### The Wrack Shore

*Cliff coast and wreckers' villages · Orlen's west coast, about 220 miles of cliff, facing the Skerries · held by the wrecker villages · danger III, IV in pack-ice winters · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Black cliffs, shingle coves and villages that live off what the sea throws at them. Signal fires burn on the headlands. Skerry boats put in here, the only place in Orlen they will, to trade lamp oil for people: men and women who sign **oil-bonds** for seven years at the try-pots, with keep charged against the bond.

**How it looks.** Cliffs two hundred feet high, streaked white with birds and grey with ash, broken by narrow coves where a boat can land on one tide in three. Each village is a huddle of stone huts on a cove's back slope, a winch for hauling boats, a fire-cradle on the headland above, and a midden of wreck timber.

**A wreckers' cove, zone by zone (new, typical).** 1. The **cliff path** down, with a rope for the bad stretch. 2. The **headland fire-cradle**, an iron basket on a stone tower. 3. The **wreck yard**, timber and salvage sorted into what burns and what sells. 4. The **huts**, roofed with hull planking. 5. The **bond shed** on the beach, where the Skerry boat-masters sit with their ledgers. 6. The **sea caves** behind the cove, where the villages keep what they do not show.

**Who is here.** Wrecker families, a few dozen to a cove, who say they only salvage and are believed by nobody. Skerry boat-masters each thaw, buying bonds with oil. People from the fen and the Downs who walked west to sign. In pack-ice winters, **hoar-bears** (beasts) come ashore off the ice and wait at doors as they wait at seal holes; the counter is fire and never going out alone.

**Old magic. Wind-knots** bought from Skerry boat-wives hang in every hut, each holding one breath of wind until it is untied. The fire-cradles were raised by the Crown's coast-wardens, and the oldest are slag-iron and hold a fire through the worst rain.

**Wonder and mystery.** The chain of headland fires seen from the sea at night, a line of red points up the coast. Which fires guide boats in and which draw them onto the rocks is the shore's oldest question, and each village gives a different answer about its neighbours.

**Dangers.** Wreckers (III). The cliff paths in ice (IV). Hoar-bears in a pack-ice winter (IV).

**Finds.** Lamp oil, the only plentiful light in Orlen. Wreck salvage, and sometimes a slag-iron fitting off a lost ship. Passage to the Skerries, at the price of a bond.

**Hooks.** A bond signed in the Long Dark that should have ended years ago. A fire that was lit the wrong night.

### The Long Marrow

*Mountain range · the highlands running about 80 miles east from the Citadels to the Weald's edge · held by nobody, its passes by ork packs · danger IV · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The spine of the north-east. Bare, cold, high country, cut by deep valleys, where the old Citadel prospectors went and found little. The Weald believes the Long Marrow is burning inside and that the fire will come out east, and cuts its firebreaks every year on that belief.

**How it looks.** Ridge after ridge of grey stone, white on top for most of the year, with vents smoking on the western slopes nearest the Citadels and fewer as you go east. Abandoned prospect pits and cairns mark the old trails.

**Wonder and mystery (new). The Warm Valley**: one high valley where snow never lies and a hot spring steams in a stone basin that may be cut or may be natural. Prospectors called it the Kettle. Nobody lives there now. The Weald's fear has one fact under it here, and it proves only that one valley is warm.

**Dangers.** Weather (IV). Ork packs near the passes (IV). The vents on the western slopes.

### The Marrow Passes

*Mountain road and its walls · the old carting road, about 60 miles, east through the Long Marrow to the Weald · held by ork packs since about S.6 · danger IV, V at the walls · lore, with new layout marked*

**At a glance.** The only cart road from Orlen to the Weald. Ork packs hold it, and it suits them: stone, one way in, a wall across a road. No cart has crossed since about S.6.

**Layout (new).** 1. **The Lower Gate**, a toll-house and wall across the valley mouth, built by the Crown and now an ork pack's den. 2. **The Climb**, switchbacks under cliffs, with fallen carts still in the ditches. 3. **The Saddle Wall**, a second wall at the pass's height, where a

| pack holds the parapet in silence. 4. The Weald Steps, the long descent east into forest. A secret: the old prospectors' trail round the Saddle Wall, which a carrier could find from the cairns. Who is here. Ork packs (beasts; never reasoned with), holding walls and choke points without a sound. Why none of them ever went on east into the Weald is not known. Dangers. The packs (V at the walls). Exposure on the Saddle (IV). Hooks. The Weald pays for news of the passes. A cart is said to have crossed in S.29; the Moot of Posts denies it. |
