A low basin off the Bell Road's quarry branch: drowned field walls, broken drainage works, occupied banks, and routes that change with the water.

## The basin

*Open country · a basin about twenty miles across, two days by bank and boat, reached from the Bell Road's quarry branch past Rook's Stair · held by its households, with the Silt Readers and the Cutwardens selling knowledge and work · danger II, V in the water · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A drowned country of measured fields: submerged boundary walls, broken drainage, occupied banks and roofs, and routes that change with the water and the banks. There is no basin-wide master lever. Its question is who is entitled to stay dry when protecting one bank sends water toward another.

**History.** The basin was the Crown's great drainage scheme, *the King's Measures* (new): low wet ground walled into measured fields by royal surveyors two centuries ago, drained through a system of cuts, mouths and brick culverts, and let to tenants by the measure. After S.0 nobody kept the sluices. The water came back a field at a time, and the people stayed on what was highest: banks, foundations, old survey posts, roofs. The **Silt Readers** began when an old crossing mark survived while the bank under it moved, and they started dating their observations.

**How it looks.** Horizontal distance. Dark water to the horizon broken by pale posts, drowned hedges, the tops of walls in straight lines, roofs with people on them, and ash collected in the lee corners. From a height at low water the whole old field system shows through the surface in a pattern of perfect rectangles, like a ledger ruled across the land (new).

**Route web (from the lore).** The Bell Road quarry approach; the **Dry Table**; **Milebank**, joined to **the Peg Houses**; **the Three Mouths**, joined to **the Long Cut**; **the Lower Measures**; **Far Bank Gate**.

**Who is here.**

**The Silt Readers,** twelve people at the Dry Table. *A depth mark without a date is a lie. A reading is not a promise.* **Ulla Renn** keeps the sheets; **Neris Kett** makes the field observations and records her failures; **Dain Orrel** repairs poles and escorts crossings. Their contradiction is unequal timeliness: paying clients get the freshest readings.

**The Cutwardens,** who do drainage work for hire under **Hadrin Mott**, with **Sella Vey** who will not certify a route she has not observed.

**The Low Rack Four,** a salvage partnership below the narrowed channel: **Mara Holt**, **Osric Dane**, **Fenna Wale**, **Tollen Pike**.

**The plot households: Hesta and Corren Marr** at the Reed Yard; **Widow Elsweth Pell and her son Jory** at the Three Steps; **Devren and Alis Hobb and Alis's father Noll** at the Bent Fence.

**Bank keepers** at Milebank and the Peg Houses: **Berra Tull**, **Orenna Sedge**.

**What hunts it. Reed-jaws** in the deep pockets: the lore names them and leaves them undescribed. The atlas proposes a long ridge-backed ambush animal of the reeds, seen as a head-and-ridge shape that could be floating timber, which takes from the landings (new description). Harrow eels in the flooded recesses. Greymaw on the dry approaches.

**Old magic.** Almost none, by the lore's own ruling: a shift is not a magical relocation, and nobody detects weight by magic. The oldest survey posts were staked by Stakeby's hands in the old way, and they are the ones that never lean.

**Mystery.** Tapping east of the Return Shelf: drift against submerged stone explains some of it; the rest is untraced. The Readers record its direction, timing and limits.

## The upper Measures

### The Dry Table

*The Silt Readers' house · an old weighing house on a raised foundation at the basin's north edge, a day from Rook's Stair · twelve people · danger I · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The King's weighing house, where the measured fields' harvests were weighed for rent, now the house of the Silt Readers: a long stone table that is a desk by day and a bed by night, and thirty years of dated readings in one hand after another, the only true map of a country that will not hold still.

**History.** The weighing mechanism went for scrap in the Hollow Winter (new). The Readers began when an old crossing mark survived while the bank under it moved, and someone started dating observations. They settled here because the foundation is the highest dry floor in the upper basin. Paying clients get the freshest readings: their contradiction, and they know it.

**How it looks.** A square stone house on a raised plinth with wide steps, its great doors blocked to a single small one, a long room inside lit by one upper shutter. Two boards by the door; banded poles racked along the wall like spears.

**Layout.**

1. **The steps and the plinth,** the dry ground everyone waits on.
2. **The screened entry,** where Neris leaves wet gear.
3. **The long room and the stone table,** cleared for bedding every night.
4. **The record cupboard,** oilcloth-covered.
5. **The pole rack,** banded poles in their lengths.
6. **The hearth,** where Dain keeps pole joints away from the records.
7. **The upper shutter,** where Ulla works.
8. **The boards outside:** *immediate warnings* (free) and *services and debts* (larger). **The correction board,** where every withdrawn recommendation is posted with its date and reason.

**Who is here.** The Silt Readers: **Ulla Renn**, **Neris Kett**, **Dain Orrel** and nine more. Clients waiting on the steps.

**Old magic.** None, by the Readers' own rule: *a reading is not a promise.*

**Wonder and mystery.** The archive: a country's history written in water levels. The correction board, the only place in the north where being wrong is posted as proudly as being right.

**Dangers.** None here (I).

**Finds.** Readings (sold, or free if the danger is immediate). Banded poles. Copies of old sheets that show where the Measures' roads were five, ten and thirty years ago, which is where the salvage is.

**Hooks.** Buy a reading, or earn one. Bring a dated observation and see it posted.

### Milebank and the Red Stitch

*A bank-road village · a raised road a mile long carrying a row of homes · about sixty people (new) · danger II, V at the seam · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A narrow raised road a mile long with a row of homes along its crest, their short doors facing the passage, the foundations of the old village under the water on both sides, and halfway along it the Red Stitch: a long repair seam held by salvaged boards and marked with red-brown cloth, which the whole village watches.

**History.** Milebank was the measured fields' service road; when the water rose the tenants moved onto it. The Stitch is the place where it nearly failed in S.17 (new) and was packed again by every household in turn. Established households can buy their way out of bank labour with materials; newcomers go into the water.

**How it looks.** A mile of crest crowded with low houses, woodpiles and rain-barrels kept off the shared passage; on either hand the drowned village, chimney-tops and the ridge of a church roof showing in the water. Halfway, the dark seam and the red cloths fluttering.

**Layout.**

1. **The south end and its waiting ground,** where strangers stop.
2. **The row of homes,** doors to the crest.
3. **The Red Stitch,** midway, with its tally-post of inspections.
4. **The drowned church** (new), its roof ridge a hundred paces off the bank, where the village rings no bell.
5. **The north end,** where the bank joins the Peg Houses.

**Who is here.** **Berra Tull**, who keeps the Stitch's inspection history and watches where visitors step before asking their names. The bank households. Harrow eels in the drowned houses.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The drowned village seen through clear winter water: lanes, a well-head, a cart still in a yard.

**Dangers.** The Stitch if it fails (V). Harrow eels (II).

**Finds.** Bank labour paid in food and a dry bed. From the drowned church (by boat, at low water, if you dare): its plate, its register in a tin box in the vestry, and the church's bell, still hung.

**Hooks.** Work the Stitch and earn a place. Raise the church's register for Mara Venn of Merewick, who would give a great deal for another parish's book.

### The Peg Houses and the Crooked Pair

*A post village · homes on the highest old foundations round two survey posts · about forty people (new) · danger II · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Stone rooms and timber additions on the highest foundations in the basin, joined by short walks, with flat scrap roofs and drying screens making low ragged silhouettes, and in open water between them the Crooked Pair: two survey posts once read together, one now leaning, so that the village's book and the Readers' sheet disagree and neither side is lying.

**History.** The King's surveyors set their posts here two centuries ago, and the oldest still carry their marks: a crown over a measuring rod, and the date of the first draining. The posts staked by Stakeby's hands in the old way are the ones that never lean.

**How it looks.** A cluster of patched rooftops on stone stumps, linked by plank walks, laundry and fish on screens; in the water, two pale posts, one upright and one at a drunken angle, each carved from base to top with marks.

**Layout.**

1. **The walks between foundations.**
2. **The Crooked Pair,** in open water between the houses.
3. **The local book's cupboard,** in the oldest room.
4. **The drying screens.**
5. **The King's post** (new), the oldest survey post, under a little roof of its own, where the village swears oaths.

**Who is here.** **Orenna Sedge**, broad-handed in an oversized grey coat, who wants the Readers' notation taught here so residents can keep their own records. The post households.

**Old magic.** **Staking**, worn thin: the King's post was staked with the word and has never moved.

**Wonder and mystery.** The surveyors' marks on the King's post: dates going back two hundred years, a whole kingdom's patience in one piece of oak.

**Dangers.** Reed-jaws near the landings (II, III). Arguments about which post is right.

**Finds.** The local book (not for sale; copying it is a favour). Fish. Surveyors' brass rods, salvaged from the drowned survey hut.

**Hooks.** Teach Orenna the notation. Settle the Crooked Pair.

## The lower Measures

### The Three Mouths and the King's Sluice

*Small site and deep place · three drainage openings below broken masonry, and the brick culverts behind them · held by nobody · danger II, V inside · the Mouths are lore; the culverts behind them are (new)*

**At a glance.** Three drainage openings meet below broken masonry, and behind them the King's Sluice: tall brick vaults running a mile under the basin to a pumping hall where a great treadwheel stands in black water.

**History.** The Mouths drained the measured fields to the river. One is partly useful, one holds debris, and one has been narrowed by successive local repairs; pale water stains and old tool sockets show the present arrangement was not the original one. A household's recent patch protects its own room while worsening a neighbour's route.

**How it looks.** Three black arches in a wall of broken masonry, water sliding into one, debris choking another. Behind them, at low water, a boat can go in under the basin through vault after vault, past sluice gates rusted open, the surveyors' marks every hundred paces, the sound of water everywhere.

**Layout.**

1. **The three Mouths:** the useful one, the choked one, the narrowed one.
2. **The forebay,** where debris collects.
3. **The vaults,** flooded to the crown in most places and half-full at low water.
4. **The sluice gates,** rusted open.
5. **The pumping hall,** a mile in: a great treadwheel in black water, its walkers' benches still in place.

**Who is here.** Nobody. Harrow eels in the vaults.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The pumping hall: a cathedral built to move water, silent, with the treadwheel twice the height of a house. The tapping heard east of the Return Shelf may come from here: drift knocking on the vaults, or something else.

**Dangers.** The water rising while you are inside (V). Harrow eels (II).

**Finds.** In the pumping hall's keeper's room (above the waterline): the sluice-keepers' last log, a set of sluice keys, lamp oil in sealed jars, and the King's drainage plan of the whole basin, the best salvage map anyone could own.

**Hooks.** Clear the choked Mouth and lower the water for three households, and raise it for two others.

### The Long Cut and the Four Steps

*A drainage channel · straight for miles across the lower basin · danger II, III · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A straight drainage channel that promises a simple journey and does not keep the promise: its maintenance walk survives only in sections, reeds hide the gaps, and collapsed retaining walls interrupt the banks.

**History.** The Cut was the scheme's spine, dug straight by the King's surveyors. The **Four Steps** are exposed masonry used as a local reading station, if their stability is checked; they are not a zero level for the whole basin.

**How it looks.** A ruled line of dark water through reeds to the horizon, stone walls appearing and vanishing along its sides, and at its midpoint four broad stone steps going down into the water.

**Layout.**

1. **The head,** where it leaves the Three Mouths.
2. **The maintenance walk,** in sections.
3. **The collapses,** where retaining walls have gone into the channel.
4. **The Four Steps,** midway.
5. **The tail,** where the Cut meets the river.

**Who is here.** Reed-jaws in the deep pockets. Harrow eels in the recesses. Greymaw waiting on the dry approaches.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Cut at dawn in hoarfrost, a mile of perfectly straight white ice.

**Dangers.** Reed-jaws (III). Harrow eels (II). Greymaw (II, III). Signs: disturbed reeds, a slide, scraps above the waterline.

**Finds.** Whatever reed-jaws leave at their slides: a carrying frame, a boot, a drowned pack with its load. Stone from the collapses, good for a bank.

**Hooks.** Read the Four Steps for the Readers. Find what the reed-jaws took from the landing.

### The Lower Measures and the Drowned Orchard

*Open water and fields · the basin's south-east · danger II · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A field of submerged boundary walls, ridge tops and old planting lines; at low water, from a bank, an orchard's ordered stumps show through the surface, and a few of its trees are still alive, flowering over the flood in spring.

**History.** The orchard was the measured fields' pride: apples for the Citadels' tables. A disputed masonry socket in it might restore an older reference for the whole basin.

**How it looks.** Flat water ruled by wall-tops. In spring, white blossom on black water: the only fruit trees in the basin.

**Layout.**

1. **The boundary walls,** walkable at low water if you know them.
2. **The Drowned Orchard,** stumps in rows.
3. **The living trees,** five of them (new), at the orchard's high end.
4. **The disputed socket,** a masonry footing in the orchard's middle.

**Who is here.** Nobody lives here. The plot households come for wood and, in autumn, for apples.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** Blossom over the flood. Apples in autumn, from trees standing in water.

**Dangers.** Deep water between the walls (II, V if you fall in loaded).

**Finds.** Apples in autumn. Applewood. In the socket's footing, a surveyors' brass datum plate.

**Hooks.** Settle the socket's reference. Graft the living trees somewhere dry.

### Far Bank Gate and the Return Shelf

*A gate and a waiting place · the basin's far edge · danger II · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Two gate piers above a drowned estate approach, their doors gone, and beside them the Return Shelf: a covered platform where recovered belongings wait to be claimed, and travellers wait for escorts and leave messages naming their next destination.

**History.** The gate was a manor's; the manor is under the water. The Shelf grew because people kept bringing things out of the water and leaving them where someone might look.

**How it looks.** Two tall stone piers with nothing between them, and beside them a long roofed platform hung with shoes, straps, tools and part of a carrying frame, each with a scrap of cloth tied on giving where it was found.

**Layout.**

1. **The gate piers.**
2. **The Return Shelf** and its hanging belongings.
3. **The message posts,** where notices are compared and destinations written.
4. **The drowned approach,** east of the Shelf, where people hear tapping.

**Who is here.** Travellers waiting for escorts. **Esken Vale**, some days, waiting for news of his handcart.

**Old magic.** None.

**Wonder and mystery.** The tapping east of the Shelf: drift against submerged stone explains some of it; the rest is untraced. The Readers record its direction, timing and limits.

**Dangers.** The drowned approach (II).

**Finds.** The Shelf's belongings, which are someone's. Taking one is theft; returning one is a debt owed to you.

**Hooks.** Return something to its owner. Trace the tapping.

### The three plots

*Household holdings in the lower Measures · three households · danger II · lore*

**At a glance.** Three households on the last dry ground of the lower basin, with a debris obstruction backing water toward all three.

**The Reed Yard.** Hesta and Corren Marr's reed-drying lean-to, a sleeping room on a low stone sill, and bundled matting across a fenced yard; damp reaches the lowest reeds first.

**The Three Steps.** Widow Elsweth Pell and her son Jory: three masonry steps to a cramped dwelling, a detached shed with bedding, and a heavy provision chest set lower than the water will stay. Elsweth cannot manage the muddy approach without help.

**The Bent Fence.** Devren and Alis Hobb and Alis's father Noll: patch beds, a patched fence that could block the shared approach if it loosens, and the neighbourhood's borrowed carrying frame.

**Dangers.** The rising water (II, V for the provision chest). **Finds.** Reed bundles; the provision chest's contents, if it is raised in time; the carrying frame, if borrowed honestly.

**Hooks.** Clear the obstruction, and decide whose ground floods first (`../cutwarden-adventure-pack.md`).
