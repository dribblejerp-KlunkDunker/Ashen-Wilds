A low basin off the Bell Road's quarry branch: drowned field walls, broken drainage works, occupied banks, and routes that change with the water.

## The basin

*Open country · a basin about twenty miles across, two days by bank and boat, reached from the Bell Road's quarry branch past Rook's Stair · held by its households, with the Silt Readers and the Cutwardens selling knowledge and work · danger II, V in the water · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A drowned country of measured fields: submerged boundary walls, broken drainage, occupied banks and roofs, and routes that change with the water and the banks. There is no basin-wide master lever. Its question is who is entitled to stay dry when protecting one bank sends water toward another.

**History.** The basin was the Crown's great drainage scheme, *the King's Measures* (new): low wet ground walled into measured fields by royal surveyors two centuries ago, drained through a system of cuts, mouths and brick culverts, and let to tenants by the measure. After S.0 nobody kept the sluices. The water came back a field at a time, and the people stayed on what was highest: banks, foundations, old survey posts, roofs. The **Silt Readers** began when an old crossing mark survived while the bank under it moved, and they started dating their observations.

**How it looks.** Horizontal distance. Dark water to the horizon broken by pale posts, drowned hedges, the tops of walls in straight lines, roofs with people on them, and ash collected in the lee corners. From a height at low water the whole old field system shows through the surface in a pattern of perfect rectangles, like a ledger ruled across the land (new).

**Route web (from the lore).** The Bell Road quarry approach; the **Dry Table**; **Milebank**, joined to **the Peg Houses**; **the Three Mouths**, joined to **the Long Cut**; **the Lower Measures**; **Far Bank Gate**.

**Who is here.**

**The Silt Readers,** twelve people at the Dry Table. *A depth mark without a date is a lie. A reading is not a promise.* **Ulla Renn** keeps the sheets; **Neris Kett** makes the field observations and records her failures; **Dain Orrel** repairs poles and escorts crossings. Their contradiction is unequal timeliness: paying clients get the freshest readings.

**The Cutwardens,** who do drainage work for hire under **Hadrin Mott**, with **Sella Vey** who will not certify a route she has not observed.

**The Low Rack Four,** a salvage partnership below the narrowed channel: **Mara Holt**, **Osric Dane**, **Fenna Wale**, **Tollen Pike**.

**The plot households: Hesta and Corren Marr** at the Reed Yard; **Widow Elsweth Pell and her son Jory** at the Three Steps; **Devren and Alis Hobb and Alis's father Noll** at the Bent Fence.

**Bank keepers** at Milebank and the Peg Houses: **Berra Tull**, **Orenna Sedge**.

**What hunts it. Reed-jaws** in the deep pockets: the lore names them and leaves them undescribed. The atlas proposes a long ridge-backed ambush animal of the reeds, seen as a head-and-ridge shape that could be floating timber, which takes from the landings (new description). Harrow eels in the flooded recesses. Greymaw on the dry approaches.

**Old magic.** Almost none, by the lore's own ruling: a shift is not a magical relocation, and nobody detects weight by magic. The oldest survey posts were staked by Stakeby's hands in the old way, and they are the ones that never lean.

**Mystery.** Tapping east of the Return Shelf: drift against submerged stone explains some of it; the rest is untraced. The Readers record its direction, timing and limits.

## The upper Measures

### The Dry Table

*The Silt Readers' house · an old weighing house on a raised foundation · twelve people ·* *danger I · lore*

The King's weighing house, where the measured fields' harvests were weighed for rent. The mechanism is gone; its long stone table survives, and at night it has to be cleared for bedding, because dry floor is never unlimited. **Layout:** the raised foundation and its steps; the screened entry where Neris leaves wet gear; the long room with the **stone table**; the oilcloth-covered **record cupboard**; the rack of banded poles; the hearth, where Dain keeps pole joints away from the records; the upper shutter where Ulla works. Outside, two boards: **immediate warnings** (free) and **services and debts** (larger). The **correction board**, public, where every withdrawn recommendation is posted with its date and reason. **Wonder:** the archive itself, thirty years of dated readings in one hand after another, the only true map of a country that will not hold still.

### Milebank and the Red Stitch

*A bank-road village · several homes on a raised road a mile long · danger II, V at the seam ·* *lore*

A narrow raised road carrying a row of occupied homes, their short doors facing the crest, woodpiles and rain vessels kept out of the shared passage. The foundations of the old houses lie under the water outside the bank. The **Red Stitch** is a long repair seam of darker packed material held by salvaged boards, its work area marked with red-brown cloth. **Berra Tull**, in a shortened patched coat and brown kerchief, keeps its inspection history and watches where visitors step before asking their names. Established households can buy their way out of bank labour with materials; newcomers are expected to go into the water. **Layout:** the south end and its waiting ground; the row of homes; the Stitch, midway; the north end, where the bank joins the Peg Houses. **Danger:** the Stitch if it fails (V).

### The Peg Houses and the Crooked Pair

*A post village · homes on surviving high foundations round old survey posts · danger II · lore*

Stone rooms and timber additions on the highest foundations, joined by short local walks, with flat scrap roofs and drying screens making low ragged silhouettes. The **Crooked Pair** are two survey posts once read together; one now leans, so the local observation book and the Readers' sheet disagree, and neither side is lying. **Orenna Sedge**, broad-handed in an oversized grey coat, wants the notation taught locally so residents can keep their own records. **Layout:** the walks between foundations; the Crooked Pair in open water between the houses; the local book's cupboard; the drying screens. **Wonder (new):** the oldest survey posts carry the King's surveyors' marks, a crown over a measuring rod, and the dates of the first draining, two hundred years ago.

## The lower Measures

### The Three Mouths and the King's Sluice

*Small site and deep place · three drainage openings below broken masonry, and the brick culverts behind them · held by nobody · danger II, V inside · the Mouths are lore; the culverts behind them are (new)*

Three drainage openings meet below broken masonry. One is partly useful, one holds debris, and one has been narrowed by successive local repairs. Pale water stains and old tool sockets show the present arrangement was not the original one. A household's recent patch protects its own room while worsening a neighbour's route. **Behind the Mouths (new): the King's Sluice.** The Crown's drainage culverts run under the whole basin: tall brick vaults built to carry the measured fields' water to the river, now flooded to the crown in most places and half-full at low water. Their inside walls carry the surveyors' marks every hundred paces. At low water a boat can go in for a mile under the basin, through vault after vault, past sluice gates rusted open, to a **pumping hall** where a great treadwheel stands in black water. The tapping heard east of the Return Shelf may come from here: drift knocking on the vaults, or something else. **Danger:** the water rising while you are inside (V); harrow eels in the vaults (II).

### The Long Cut and the Four Steps

*A drainage channel · straight for miles · danger II, III · lore*

A straight drainage channel that promises a simple journey. Its maintenance walk survives only in sections, reeds hide the gaps, and collapsed retaining walls interrupt the banks. The **Four Steps** are exposed masonry used as a local reading station, if their stability is checked; they are not a zero level for the whole basin. Reed-jaws in the deep pockets, eels in the recesses, Greymaw waiting on the dry approaches. **Signs:** disturbed reeds, a slide, scraps above the waterline.

### The Lower Measures and the Drowned Orchard

*Open water and fields · danger II · lore*

A field of submerged boundary walls, ridge tops and old planting lines. At low water, from a bank, an orchard's ordered stumps show through the surface: the **Drowned Orchard**. A disputed masonry socket in it might restore an older reference. **Wonder (new):** a few of the orchard's trees are not stumps. Their tops still stand above the water, alive, and in spring they flower over the flood, white blossom on black water, the only fruit trees in the basin.

### Far Bank Gate and the Return Shelf

*A gate and a waiting place · danger II · lore*

| Two gate piers rise above a drowned estate approach; the doors are gone. The Return Shelf, a covered platform, holds recovered belongings waiting to be claimed: a shoe, a tool, a strap, part of a carrying frame. Travellers wait here for escorts, compare notices, and leave messages naming their next destination. East of the shelf, people hear tapping. The three plots Household holdings in the lower Measures · lore The Reed Yard, Hesta and Corren Marr's reed-drying lean-to, a sleeping room on a low stone sill and bundled matting across a fenced yard; damp reaches the lowest reeds first. The Three Steps, Widow Elsweth Pell and her son Jory: three masonry steps to a cramped dwelling, a detached shed with bedding and a heavy provision chest set lower than the water will stay. The Bent Fence, Devren and Alis Hobb and Noll: patch beds, a patched fence that could block the shared approach if it loosens, and the neighbourhood's borrowed carrying frame. A debris obstruction is backing water toward all three. |
