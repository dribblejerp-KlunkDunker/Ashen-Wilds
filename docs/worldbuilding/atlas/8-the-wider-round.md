## The Round as a whole

*The world of the Slate Sea · nine lands round one sea · lore*

**At a glance.** *The Round* was the old one-season trading circuit of the Slate Sea, made possible when Osbert III's slag-iron fleet learned to winter at sea (S.-82 to S.-44). Under the Dim it is the road of the voyage in *The Long Account*: from Wexhaven to the Sable Shore, the courts of Vauclere and Sephar, and the White Deeps. As a working figure, Wexhaven to the Sable Shore is about a week under sail in fair weather.

| Land | Where | In S.30 |
|---|---|---|
| Orlen (the Smother) | North shore | Ash. The fen holding on, the Citadels empty. |
| The Scald and the Hoar | North of the highlands | Venting ground; herders walking south without herds |
| The Skerries | Islands west of Orlen | The world's lamp oil, behind shut harbours |
| The Weald | East, behind the Long Marrow | A third of it dead and standing, waiting to burn |
| The Slate Sea | The middle | Ice further south each bad year; drifting king's ships |
| The Sable Shore | South-west shore | Harbour chains, quarantine rocks, a market in Orlen's debts |
| Vauclere | Inland, behind the Sable Shore | Rye on the wine terraces; a boy called Osbert the Fifth |
| Sephar | Far south-east, on the great river | Low floods, a doctrine of debt, a guest who says he is a king |
| The White Deeps | The Saltbacks, between Vauclere and Sephar | Dark ore under the salt, and a wall across it |

**The Dim.** What fell as ash on Orlen arrived everywhere else as a weaker sun, red at noon, and summers that end early. Near Orlen a thin *grey rain* fell from S.0 to about S.4. Further off no ash fell and the harvests failed anyway. The sea cooled, and in a bad winter pack ice reaches the roads off Port Sable. Thirty poor harvests changed who rules: every realm on the Round is now governed by whoever holds the stored grain. Nobody outside Orlen knows the cause, and each land has drawn its own conclusion: an accident of greed (the quays), a judgment (Vauclere), a theft from the sun (Sephar), the sea turning (the Skerries), a fire inside the mountains (the Weald).

**What crossed the sea.** Five things left Orlen after S.0. **The fleet**, scattered by a gale off the Lazaret Rocks in S.3 and come ashore in pieces. **The cough**, which the quays treat as catching. **Goblins**, in bilges, as rats do; no ork has ever been seen south of the sea. **Stoves**, now treasure, since no slag-iron has left Orlen since the Citadels fell. **Lime**: *lime the dead or meet them* became law on every coast within ten years.

**How the world sees Orlen.** The charts hatch it grey and write nothing inside. The quays believe nobody lives north of Wexhaven, and anyone who does is *ashbound*.

**Money.** Salt in the fen; silver (the mark) on the quays and in Vauclere; lamp oil everywhere a ship lands, counted in *nights*; lime by the barrel in every port; grain everywhere, as power more than coin; slag-iron plate in the south, not counted but inherited, hidden and killed for; ash-paper at the Exchange, a hundredth of face in a good week.

**Tongues.** Orlen speech and Quay speech are cousins. **Quay-talk** is the pidgin of every sailor. Vauclere's court tongue and Sepharine are learned. The Hoarfolk share no words with anyone south of the Scald. **Hewers' hand**, the miners' signing, went south with refugee hewers, and the Greys now use it as a language the quays cannot overhear.

**Calendars.** S.30 is Year 411 of the Compact, the 21st of Ysabeau, and L.30 in Sephar. Only Orlen and Sephar start their count at the wound.

## The Slate Sea

*Open sea · the middle of the Round · held by nobody; the least governed region of all ·* *danger III in summer, V in winter · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The Round's road. Grey, cold and wide. Nobody sails north on purpose except the plate-reavers in the thaw, and nobody sails in winter without a slag-iron galley stove. A hull that still carries one is worth more than its cargo.

**How it looks.** Long grey swells under a red noon sun. In winter the pack ice comes south in plates and floes, further each bad year. Drifting wreckage, and now and then a sail under royal colours where no royal ship should be.

**Places on the sea (new).**

1. **The Narrows of Sable**, where the coast pinches in towards Port Sable and the Lazaret Rocks, and every ship on the Round passes within sight of the chain.
2. **The Ice Line**, the edge of the winter pack, drawn on new charts in pencil because it moves.
3. **The Royal Roads**, an anchorage in the open sea marked by a ring of old slag-iron buoys from Osbert III's day, where the Unpaid gather to divide what they take.

**Who is here. The Unpaid**: king's ships that never reached a harbour that would take them. Their crews turned to taking *the King's toll* from coasters, under royal colours, and their children crew them now. They have a claim on Orlen's receipts as good as anyone's. Coasters, reavers, Skerry oil-boats.

**The Dead Calm at sea.** The water goes flat as a floor and sound carries for miles. Sailors wrap the ship's bell and wait. Any voice aboard can be heard by any ship in sight.

**The Follower** (beast; never reasoned with). A very large cold-water scavenging fish, six or seven metres long. It trails any hull with dying aboard, because it has learned that ships drop their dead. It bumps the hull, and in a calm it takes a hand at the rail. The counter: do not give the sea your dead; lime or burn them ashore. Sailors insist it is one fish. Nobody knows.

**Wonder and mystery.** Sea-smoke rising off the water on a cold dawn, so a ship seems to sail through a burning field. The Royal Roads' buoys, still ringing faintly in a swell, thirty years after anyone tended them.

**Dangers.** Ice (V in winter). The Unpaid (IV). The Follower (IV). Gales.

**Hooks.** A king's ship that demands the King's toll. A body aboard and a long way to land.

## The Scald and the Hoar

### The Scald

*Venting moor · north of the Citadels · held by nobody · danger V · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** North of the Citadels the moor vents and nothing lies on it. Snow melts as it lands. The ground steams, cracks and stinks, and the air in its hollows is bad enough to kill a walker who sits down to rest.

**How it looks.** A brown and yellow plain under the Dim, crusted with sulphur, broken by fumaroles that glow at night. Old cairns of the Citadel prospectors cross it, many fallen. From the scarp the Scald at night is a field of small red lights to the horizon.

**Layout of a crossing (new).** 1. **The Scarp Foot**, below Kell Tor and the Slagcrown. 2. **The Cairn Line**, the old prospectors' track, safe where the cairns still stand. 3. **The Sinks**, hollows of still, bad air, which take anyone who goes down into them for shelter. 4. **The Glass Field**, where the ground has run and cooled into sheets of black glass that cut boots. 5. **The Cold Edge**, where the venting stops and the snow begins, and the Hoar beyond.

**Old magic. Cold light** is the only safe light on the Scald, for the same reason the hewers made it: a flame in the Sinks kills.

**Wonder and mystery.** The night field of red vents. A Sink at dawn, with frost round its rim and nothing living in it, and bodies of birds at the bottom.

**Dangers.** Bad air (V). The ground (IV). The cold beyond (IV).

### The Hoar and the Hoarfolk

*Ice and herd country · past the Scald to the north · held by the Hoarfolk · danger IV · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Tundra and ice, the country of the **Hoarfolk**: reindeer herders and seal hunters with their own tongue. Their herds died between S.1 and S.5 on lichen that had taken up the ash. A people who measured wealth in animals now own none. Some walked south across the Scald; carriers on the upper Bell Road call them *ice-walkers*. They may explain the smoke seen at Kell Tor in S.17. They may not.

**How it looks.** White distance under a red sun, low hills, frozen lakes. Hoarfolk camps of hide tents set round the bones of their herds, which they do not bury. Further north, the sea ice.

**Who is here.** The Hoarfolk, who share no words with anyone south of the Scald. What is known of them comes through the ice-walkers' signs and through carriers who have traded salt for skins.

**The Bald Hills.** North of the Slagcrown. In the Hoarfolk's grandmothers' time, long before S.0, snow never lay on them. That supports the Slagcrown families who say the venting was coming whether anyone dug or not, and proves only that those hills were warm.

**The rings of the Bald Hills.** Rings of stones on warm ground, which the Hoarfolk say they did not raise. The plain reading: frost sorts stones into rings without help. **Layout (new):** a hilltop of bare warm earth, eleven rings from a stride across to a hundred strides, nested and overlapping, and at the centre of the largest a flat stone that is never frosted. The Hoarfolk leave seal fat on it in the dark of the year. They will not say why, and the atlas does not.

**Wonder and mystery.** Green moss on the Bald Hills in midwinter, the only green in the north. The rings seen from above at dusk, when frost forms everywhere except inside the stones.

**Dangers.** Cold (V). Hunger. Hoar-bears at the ice edge (IV).

**Hooks.** An ice-walker on the upper Bell Road who keeps drawing rings in the snow.

## The Skerries

*Island group · forty-odd inhabited islands west of Orlen · held by the boat-owners under the Boat-Thing · danger II ashore, IV at a dark beacon · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Whaling and sealing isles that live on whales, seals, cod and rendered oil. They have no king: boat-owners meet once a year at **the Boat-Thing**, and its rulings are the only law. Since S.0 every lamp on the Round burns Skerry oil or goes dark, and the islands are richer than they have ever been. **Their harbours shut in S.2**, a year before the quays chained theirs. They take no refugees. They buy labour instead, as **oil-bonds**: seven years at the try-pots for passage and keep, with keep charged against the bond, so many bonds signed in the Long Dark are still running.

**How it looks.** Low green-black islands with no trees, ringed by surf. Stone harbours with booms across them. Every island has a smoking **try-works** where blubber is rendered in iron pots, a beacon on its highest point, and boats drawn up and guarded at night.

**Places (new where marked).**

1. **The Thing-isle**: a bare island with a ring of turf benches where the Boat-Thing sits each midsummer, and the law-rock from which rulings are spoken.
2. **A try-works**: pots, flensing slip, oil-stores, the bond-workers' longhouse. The smell carries a mile.
3. **The Fire Chain**: each island keeps a beacon. A dark beacon means sickness ashore, and no boat lands until it is lit again. Some have been dark for years.
4. **A dark island (new)** : a beacon unlit for nine years, a harbour with boats rotting at the boom, and smoke seen there anyway.
5. **The Sunk Stair**, on the outermost Skerry.

**The Sunk Stair.** In a sea cave on the outermost island, ledges like steps go down under the tideline. The plain reading: wave-cut benches. **Layout (new):** a cave mouth reached only at the lowest spring tides; a long sloping hall; the ledges descending into green water, each one a little too regular; and, at the lowest tide of the year, one more ledge showing than the year before. Skerry boat-law says the sea takes a share of every catch, and fishers leave a fish on the top ledge going out.

**Who is here.** Boat-owners and their crews; **boat-wives**, who keep the **wind-knot**; bond-workers, many of them Orlen's; flensers.

**Old magic.** The wind-knot: a knotted cord that holds one breath of wind until untied. The Skerries have no priests and want none.

**Wonder and mystery. The grey whales**: flensers say some whales come ashore with grey in their lungs, and in those years the islands burn the carcass and render nothing. Nobody has cut one open in front of a witness who wrote it down. A whole try-works burning a whale on the beach at night, and the whole island standing round it.

**Dangers.** Shut harbours (the boom, and the men behind it). Bond debt that never ends. A dark beacon's island (IV). Hoar-bears in pack-ice winters (IV).

**Finds.** Oil, the only light. Whalebone, sealskin, salt cod. Wind-knots.

**Hooks.** Buying out one oil-bond, and learning what the keep has cost. A dark island that is still burning something.

## The Weald

*Great forest · east of the Long Marrow, eight days deep on foot · held by the free steadings under the Moot of Posts · danger II in the living wood, IV on the dead edge · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Forest steadings of timber, pitch and charcoal, with no lord. Free steadings answer to **the Moot of Posts**, and each keeps a **name-post** carved with its dead. The ash killed the western third, and its trunks still stand: **the Standing Dead**, the largest pile of dry fuel on the Round, which has not burned yet.

**How it looks.** From the Marrow Passes the Weald is a wall of grey to the horizon, then a hard line, then green. In the Standing Dead the trunks are grey and barkless with nothing growing under them, silent, the light coming straight down. In the living wood, oak and pine, smoke from charcoal clamps, and every clearing has its name-post: a carved oak trunk, its faces covered in names, oldest at the bottom.

**Layout of the Weald (new where marked).**

1. **The dead edge**: the boundary between the Standing Dead and the living wood, with steadings strung along it.
2. **The Swathe**: each dead-edge steading cuts its firebreak every year. A steading that skips its Swathe endangers everyone downwind, and its neighbours burn it out before the forest can.
3. **A steading**: longhouse, pitch kilns, charcoal clamps, a palisade, and the name-post in the yard.
4. **The Moot-ground (new)** : a clearing where every steading has set up a copy of its post, so the Moot meets surrounded by every name in the Weald. It is the largest carved place on the Round, a forest of posts, and it is running out of room.
5. **The Deer Yards**: hollows where whole herds died together in the first winters and were never limed. They still bloom.
6. **The edge posts (new)** , on the far eastern side, where the Weald trades with the Grass.

**Who is here.** Steaders, charcoal-burners and pitch-boilers. The **dead-edge wolf** (beast): an ordinary starved wolf that follows a coughing traveller for days and waits. Keep the sick in the middle and the pace steady. Nothing about it is unknown. It is a wolf.

**Faith.** The name-posts: the dead stay while their names are carved, and a rotted post is a second death. The Smothering made more names than there is oak to carve.

**Law. The Brand-wind**, a dry east wind in autumn. When it blows, Weald law forbids any open flame outdoors, a traveller's torch included.

**Old magic.** Little that is spoken. The Weald fears the Long Marrow is burning inside, and watches the west for a fire that has not come.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Moot-ground in fog, every post a person. The silence of the Standing Dead, where no bird sings and footsteps carry a mile. The Deer Yards glowing faintly on a wet night.

**Dangers.** Fire (V, if it ever comes). The dead-edge wolf (III). Walking bloom in the Deer Yards (IV). A steading that thinks you lit a flame in a Brand-wind (III).

**Finds.** Timber, pitch, charcoal. News of the Grass.

**Hooks.** Cutting a steading's Swathe before the Brand-wind, with no fire allowed. A post with a name on it that the steading says it never carved.

## The Sable Shore and Port Sable

### The Sable Shore

*Merchant coast · eleven walled ports on the south-west shore · held by the Compact of Quays · danger II inside the walls, III in the Limewalks, IV in a Bloom Quarter · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Eleven walled ports that bought Orlen's slag-iron for two centuries. Each is ruled by its counting-houses. Together they are **the Compact of Quays** (sworn S.-381, which is how the quays count their years). The chief of them is **Port Sable**. Their faith is **the Tally**: you owe only what you contracted, a soul's account closes at death, and dying in debt is the one sin. No promise was made to Orlen, so none was broken.

**History. The Chain Years (S.3 to S.6).** When the first king's ships arrived with coughing passengers, the ports raised their harbour chains, and ships starved at anchor in sight of the bread ovens. Three king's ships starved off Port Sable in S.4. Since S.6 the chains come down for southern hulls only, and any northern hull lies forty days at the Lazaret Rocks.

**The eleven ports (new).** The Shore runs about 300 miles from west to east, a day and a half under sail end to end, with the ports 25 to 35 miles apart and Port Sable in the middle, where the coast pinches into the Narrows. Each port has one seat in the Compact. West to east:

1. **Gannet Quay** (150 miles west of Port Sable). A cliff harbour under the seabird ledges, living on eggs, feathers and salt fish. The poorest seat in the Compact. Quay talk says its harbourmaster lets northern hulls water there at night, for a fee.
2. **Kestle Head** (120 miles west). A fortified headland, the Compact's watch-station on the western approach. Its tower lights mark the coast, and the Compact's chain-smiths work there.
3. **Corvane** (85 miles west). The oil port: rendering yards for fish and seal oil, and the lamp-oil trade that once went north. It smells of it for a mile out to sea.
4. **Lisk** (55 miles west). Salt-pans and lime kilns, worked almost entirely by the Greys. It has its own Limewalks, smaller and worse than Port Sable's.
5. **Pennick** (25 miles west). The Compact's shipyard, where the hulls that sail the Round are built and caulked. Every hull on the Shore has a Pennick mark on its keel.
6. **Port Sable.** The chief of the eleven (below).
7. **Wendlemouth** (30 miles east). A river port at the mouth of the Wendle, Vauclere's river. The grain barges come down from the terraces here, and the road from Port Sable to Saint-Aumere joins the river here. Because grain rules the Round, Wendlemouth holds the second voice in the Compact.
8. **Marlstow** (60 miles east). The Shore's other Bloom Quarter: the whole lower town, walled off and given to the corpse-caps (danger IV). The port still trades from its upper town and does not speak of the lower one.
9. **Saltinge** (90 miles east). Tidal marshes and salt-pans: the salt that cures the Shore's fish.
10. **Brineholt** (120 miles east). A fortress harbour where the plate-reavers fit out each thaw and sail north under licence. Its taverns are the only place on the Shore where anyone talks about Orlen as a place rather than a debt.
11. **Seaverne** (150 miles east). The youngest seat, admitted to the Compact long after the oath, and still quarrelling over its vote. Its market sells passage beyond the Round, to anyone who can pay.

### Port Sable

*Walled port city · the greatest city on the Round · held by its counting-houses · lore, with new layout marked*

**How it looks.** A city of pale stone on a hook of harbour, rising in walled terraces to the counting-houses on the crown. A great iron chain lies across the harbour mouth between two towers. Outside the landward wall, the smoke and white dust of the Limewalks. Off

the coast, three bare islands with hulks moored round them.

**Layout, zone by zone (new).**

1. **The Chain Towers** at the harbour mouth, with the chain's winding-house between them.
2. **The Roads**, the outer anchorage, where ships wait for the chain.
3. **The Quays**: warehouses, cranes, lime-sheds, oil-stores. Hold-goblins in the drains.
4. **The Exchange**: a great colonnaded hall where everything on the Round is priced. Orlen's royal receipts, charters and deeds trade here as **ash-paper** for about a hundredth of face. **Deeps Company** shares are the best-performing paper on the floor.
5. **The Counting-houses** on the crown of the city, each a fortified house with its own strongroom and its own notaries.
6. **The Bloom Quarter**: one of the two districts on the Shore walled off and given up to the corpse-caps, which fruit in days in southern warmth.
7. **The Limewalks**, outside the landward wall.

**Who is here.** Factors and notaries. **Lucan Daw**, factor of Daw and Sturry, who wants the receipts at a hundredth. **The Collectors**, the houses' agents who reach the fen with certified southern copies. **Plate-reavers**, licensed to sail north each thaw for stoves; one Greystair plate buys a house here. **The Greys**: Orlen's refugees and their children, with no guild rights, living in the Limewalks and doing the work the quays will not: salt-pans, lime kilns, dead-carts. Their faith is still the Keeping. Their language among themselves is hewers' hand.

**The Limewalks.** A shanty town of lime-washed huts along the kilns, white with dust, where the dead-carts go out at dawn. The quays read the lime price as a physician reads a pulse: when it rises, a port is dying faster.

**Old magic. The bound word**, kept by the Tally's notaries: it seals a promise so that both parties feel it break. The Tally licenses it and taxes it. Every great contract on the Exchange floor is bound.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Exchange at the opening bell, a thousand voices pricing the end of a kingdom. The chain rising out of the water at dusk, streaming weed.

**Dangers.** The law (III, for a northerner). The Bloom Quarter (IV). Hold-goblins (II).

**Finds.** Anything, for silver. Passage anywhere. Grain, for a price.

**Hooks.** Selling the receipts at a hundredth: enough grain for the fen for one winter, and the land claims with it. The Limewalks' dead-cart crews stop work.

### The ten other ports of the Compact

*Ten walled ports along about 300 miles of the Sable Shore, west and east of Port Sable · each held by its counting-houses, one seat each in the Compact · (new) 2026-11-19*

Every port keeps a chain across its harbour (raised for northern hulls since S.6), lime-sheds for the dead, and a Tally notary. Every port has hold-goblins in its drains. Distances are along the Shore from Port Sable.

#### Gannet Quay

*Cliff harbour · 150 miles west, the Shore's last port · about 900 people · danger II*

**At a glance.** A harbour cut into the foot of a seabird cliff, where a hundred thousand gannets nest above the roofs and the town lives on eggs, feathers and salt fish: the poorest seat in the Compact.
**How it looks.** White cliffs gone whiter with birds; a harbour wall like a hook; houses stacked up the cliff on ledges, joined by ladders; nets everywhere.
**Layout.** 1. The harbour and its short chain. 2. The egging ladders up the cliff. 3. The fish-flakes, racks of salt cod. 4. The counting-house, the smallest on the Shore. 5. The night quay (a secret), a ledge round the headland.
**Who is here.** **Harbourmaster Ruan Pellow** (new), who quay talk says lets northern hulls water at the night quay, for a fee. Eggers on ropes. Greys who came west because Gannet Quay asks fewer questions.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** The cliff at dawn when every bird lifts at once. **Dangers.** The cliffs (IV), the harbourmaster's men (II).
**Finds.** Salt fish, eggs, down. Fresh water at the night quay, for northern hulls that cannot lie forty days.
**Hooks.** The night quay: a way round the Forty, and the Compact would hang Ruan for it.

#### Kestle Head

*Fortified headland · 120 miles west · the Compact's watch-station · about 1,200 people · danger II*

**At a glance.** A walled town on a headland with a light-tower at its point, where the Compact's chain-smiths forge the harbour chains for every port on the Shore.
**How it looks.** A fortress of grey stone on a rock above the sea, smoke from its forges, the great tower's fire visible thirty miles out.
**Layout.** 1. The light-tower. 2. The chain-forge, longest building on the Shore. 3. The Compact's armoury. 4. The garrison yard. 5. The fishers' quay below the wall.
**Who is here.** **Chainmaster Odile Brask** (new), who has never seen a slag-iron link and wants one to test. Smiths, soldiers of the Compact, lightkeepers.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** A harbour chain half-forged, each link the size of a man, laid out the length of the forge. **Dangers.** The garrison (III for a northerner).
**Finds.** Chain, iron fittings, the Compact's charts of the western approach. A slag-iron link would buy anything here.
**Hooks.** Bring Odile a slag-iron link and learn how every chain on the Shore can be broken.

#### Corvane

*Oil port · 85 miles west · about 2,000 people · danger II*

**At a glance.** The oil port: rendering yards for fish and seal oil, and the lamp-oil trade that once went north, smelling of it for a mile out to sea.
**How it looks.** Black try-pot sheds along the shore, a harbour full of oil-barges, every wall stained amber.
**Layout.** 1. The try-yards. 2. The barrel quay. 3. The oil exchange. 4. The rendering pits behind the town. 5. The chandlers' street, where lamp oil is counted in *nights*.
**Who is here.** **Factor Helis Stroud** (new) of the oil exchange, who buys Skerry oil and sells it on at three times; Skerry boat-masters; flensers.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** Corvane at night, every window lit with cheap oil: the brightest town on the Round. **Dangers.** Fire (V in the try-yards).
**Finds.** Lamp oil by the barrel. Seal-skin, whalebone. Tallow.
**Hooks.** Fuel for the voyage, at a price; or an oil-barge to borrow.

#### Lisk

*Salt and lime port · 55 miles west · about 1,500 people, half of them Greys · danger II, III in the Limewalks*

**At a glance.** A port of salt-pans and lime kilns worked almost entirely by the Greys, with its own Limewalks, smaller and worse than Port Sable's.
**How it looks.** White everything: salt-pans in square ranks along the shore, kilns smoking behind the town, huts of lime-washed wattle along the kilns.
**Layout.** 1. The pans. 2. The kilns. 3. The Lisk Limewalks. 4. The dead-cart yard. 5. The counting-house on the only hill.
**Who is here.** The Greys, who speak hewers' hand among themselves and still keep the Keeping. **Mother Annis Quill** (new), an old Grey who keeps a list of every northerner who died in Lisk.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** The Greys singing in hewers' hand, silently, at a burial. **Dangers.** The kilns, the dead-carts (II).
**Finds.** Salt. Lime. Annis's list, which may hold a name someone in the fen is waiting for.
**Hooks.** Carry Annis's list north. The lime price, which the quays read as a pulse.

#### Pennick

*Shipyard port · 25 miles west · about 2,500 people · danger I*

**At a glance.** The Compact's shipyard, where the hulls that sail the Round are built and caulked; every hull on the Shore has a Pennick mark on its keel.
**How it looks.** Slipways in rows, hulls in every stage of building like the skeletons of whales, the smell of pitch and sawn oak.
**Layout.** 1. The slipways. 2. The timber ponds. 3. The ropewalk, a third of a mile long. 4. The pitch-houses. 5. The shipwrights' guildhall.
**Who is here.** Shipwrights. **Master Shipwright Joran Tey** (new), who would like to meet Wat Hebble and has heard of the *Small Mercy*.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** A launch: a new hull sliding into the sea while the whole town shouts. **Dangers.** Few.
**Finds.** Ship's timber, rope, pitch, caulking, canvas. Repairs, for silver.
**Hooks.** Mend the *Small Mercy* properly, at a price Wat will hate.

#### Wendlemouth

*River port · 30 miles east · about 3,000 people · danger I*

**At a glance.** A river port at the mouth of the Wendle, Vauclere's river, where the grain barges come down from the terraces; because grain rules the Round, it holds the second voice in the Compact.
**How it looks.** A broad estuary crowded with barges, granaries along both banks, a bridge of seven arches, the road to Saint-Aumere leaving inland along the river.
**Layout.** 1. The barge basin. 2. The granaries. 3. The seven-arch bridge. 4. The river road. 5. The grain exchange, rival to Port Sable's.
**Who is here.** **Granary-Reeve Bastien Holm** (new), who sets the price of bread for half the Round. Bargees from Vauclere.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** The granaries' doors open at harvest: the smell of bread before it is baked. **Dangers.** The price of grain.
**Finds.** Grain, the only coin that matters. Passage up the river to Saint-Aumere.
**Hooks.** Grain for the fen, if the receipts can be made to mean anything here.

#### Marlstow

*Port with a Bloom Quarter · 60 miles east · about 1,800 people in the upper town · danger II, IV in the lower town*

**At a glance.** The Shore's other Bloom Quarter: the whole lower town, walled off and given to the corpse-caps, which fruit in days in southern warmth. The port still trades from its upper town and does not speak of the lower one.
**How it looks.** An upper town of good houses on a ridge; below it, behind a wall with no gate, a whole quarter of streets gone teal at night, the glow seen from the sea.
**Layout.** 1. The upper town and its quay. 2. The wall. 3. The lower town, given up. 4. The lime-house, where the wall's watch keeps its barrels. 5. The fishermen's steps down to the lower harbour (a way in).
**Who is here.** **Wallwarden Pieter Lusk** (new), who keeps the wall and wants the lower town burned. Walking blooms in the lower town.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** The lower town at night, teal in every window. **Dangers.** The lower town (IV): still air, spores, blooms.
**Finds.** In the lower town, everything its people left when the wall went up: a whole quarter's goods.
**Hooks.** Burn the lower town for Pieter, or go in for what was left.

#### Saltinge

*Salt-marsh port · 90 miles east · about 1,000 people · danger II*

**At a glance.** Tidal marshes and salt-pans: the salt that cures the Shore's fish, raked by people who walk on stilts across the flats.
**How it looks.** Flat grey marsh to the horizon, pans glittering, stilt-walkers moving across it like herons, a small town on the only gravel bank.
**Layout.** 1. The gravel bank and the town. 2. The pans. 3. The tide channels. 4. The salt-sheds. 5. The heron-hide, a lookout over the flats.
**Who is here.** Salt-rakers on stilts. Ditch-monitors in the warm channels in summer.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** The flats at sunset, the pans like a field of mirrors. **Dangers.** The tide (IV), ditch-monitors (III).
**Finds.** Salt, cheap. Stilts.
**Hooks.** Salt is the fen's money: a cargo of it is a winter.

#### Brineholt

*Fortress harbour · 120 miles east · about 1,400 people · danger II, III on the reavers' quay*

**At a glance.** A fortress harbour where the plate-reavers fit out each thaw and sail north under licence; its taverns are the only place on the Shore where anyone talks about Orlen as a place rather than a debt.
**How it looks.** A walled harbour with a castle on a rock, reaver ships with northern stove-plates nailed to their sterns as trophies.
**Layout.** 1. The reavers' quay. 2. The licence-house. 3. The taverns of Plate Street. 4. The castle. 5. The stove market, where Greystair plates are sold.
**Who is here.** Plate-reavers. **Corvin Hale**'s crew in the off-season. **Licenser Tobias Wren** (new), who sells the licences and buys the stories.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** The stove market: Greystair plates in rows, each one a house. **Dangers.** Reavers (III).
**Finds.** Stove plates (to buy, never cheap). Charts of Orlen's coast drawn by reavers. News from the north.
**Hooks.** Sell a story of the north in Plate Street and hear one back.

#### Seaverne

*The youngest port · 150 miles east, the Shore's last port east · about 1,100 people · danger II*

**At a glance.** The youngest seat, admitted to the Compact long after the oath and still quarrelling over its vote; its market sells passage beyond the Round, to anyone who can pay.
**How it looks.** A new town of brick on an old fishing harbour, the only port on the Shore without a full harbour chain, its quays crowded with ships flying flags from beyond the Round.
**Layout.** 1. The passage market. 2. The half-chain. 3. The brick quarter. 4. The old fishing harbour. 5. The Compact house, where Seaverne argues its vote.
**Who is here.** **Passage-broker Ilse Danner** (new), who will sell you a berth to the warm current and green land in the west, or say she can.
**Old magic.** None. **Wonder.** Ships from beyond the Round, painted strangely, sailing in with things nobody on the Shore has a word for. **Dangers.** Brokers (II).
**Finds.** Passage beyond the Round. Goods from far coasts.
**Hooks.** The western ocean: a few whaling boats came back after years away, talking of a warm current and green land.

## The Lazaret Rocks

*Quarantine islands · three bare islands off Port Sable, with old hulks moored round them as wards · held by the Compact's keepers, barely · danger IV, V in a sealed hulk · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance. The Forty**: since S.6 any northern hull must lie forty days here before the chain comes down for it. A company that waits loses a third of the summer. It can buy a clean bill with a stove plate or with receipts, or run the chain at night and be outlaws on the Sable Shore from then on. The keepers posted here are seldom relieved, and some hulks have not been opened in years.

**History.** The gale that scattered the royal fleet in the spring of S.3 blew off these rocks. A Port Sable factor will swear the King drowned here that spring. Two of the seven ships that reached the chain were sent here, and the hulks moored as wards are mostly king's ships.

**How it looks.** Three humps of black rock in a grey sea, white with birds and lime. On the largest, a stone keeper's house and a lime kiln that never goes out. Round all three, a ring of hulks moored bow to stern, masts cut down, hatches nailed shut, some with painted crosses on their sides.

**Layout, island by island (new).**

1. **Keeper's Rock**: the keeper's house, the kiln, the signal mast that talks to the Chain Towers, and the anchorage where waiting ships lie.
2. **The Ring**: the moored hulks, walked deck to deck along gangplanks, each one numbered in lime. Some are open and used as stores. Some are sealed.
3. **Bone Rock**: where the dead of the Forty are limed and buried in the shingle, row on row.
4. **The Outer Rock**: bare and unvisited, where a sealed hulk broke its moorings years ago and lies wedged on the reef.

**Who is here. Bryde Hallam**, keeper, who wants relief. She was posted for one year, eleven years ago. A handful of keepers under her. Crews of waiting ships, and whoever on them is coughing. **Hulk-congers** in the sealed hulks, each holding its one dark space. **Walking bloom** on the hulks and on Bone Rock, where in southern warmth the crop is heavier.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Ring at night, lanterns on the waiting ships and none at all on the sealed hulks. The question of the King's ship, which nobody on the Rocks will discuss.

**Dangers.** Bloom (IV). Congers in the sealed hulks (V). The keepers, who have orders about anyone who leaves early (III).

**Finds.** A clean bill, at a price. What the sealed hulks hold, at a worse one.

**Hooks.** Bryde Hallam's relief. Hulk Number Nine, which has been sealed since S.3 and whose papers are missing from the keeper's book.

## Vauclere and Saint-Aumere

### Vauclere

*Inland kingdom · river valleys, terraces and cloth towns behind the Sable Shore · held by Queen Ysabeau · danger II, III in a Hunger year · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** A kingdom of wine, cloth and grain, ruled by Queen **Ysabeau** (crowned S.10) from **Saint-Aumere**. The vines failed in S.2, **the Sour Years**, and rye grows badly on the wine terraces now. Vauclere has put down three peasant risings, which it numbers as the First, Second and Third Hunger. It says the Smothering was a judgment on Orlen's lords, which a rightful king in exile may yet lift, and it keeps an heir to prove it.

**How it looks.** Terraced valleys climbing from slow rivers, the terraces brown with rye where vines once stood, the old wine presses idle. Cloth towns with tall half-timbered halls. Gibbets at the crossroads from the last Hunger.

### Saint-Aumere

*Royal city and court · a hill city above a river · held by the Crown of Vauclere · lore, with new layout marked*

**Layout, zone by zone (new).**

1. **The river town**, cloth halls and granaries under guard.
2. **The Queen's Granary**, the largest building in the city after the palace, because whoever holds the grain rules.
3. **The palace**, on the hill.
4. **The Orlen Court (new)** : a wing of the palace given to Osbert's household, with Orlen's banners, a throne nobody else sits in, and a chancery that issues paper under his seal.
5. **The Priory of the Keeping in Exile**, where Orlen's registers are kept.
6. **The treasury vaults** under the palace, where the treasury ships' cargo went.
7. **The guest-house**, where a company may winter, and owe for it.

**Who is here. Queen Ysabeau**, who wants the receipts honoured in paper and a writ carried north. **Osbert, called the Fifth**, nineteen, said to be Aldric IV's grandson, born in exile; nobody has shown the marriage that would make him so. **Prior Lambert Strand** of the Keeping in Exile, who wants silence about a blessing his uncle gave in S.-1.

**The Keeping in Exile.** Most of the senior clergy who sailed in S.3 landed here with the archive ships. They hold Orlen's registers: thirty years of who owed what to whom, kept by the people who left. Vauclere's chancery appoints priests to Orlen's parishes and reasserts the old assessments under Osbert's seal; the priests who reach the fen claiming southern appointment come from here, on this proposal.

**The treasury ships.** They made Vauclere too. Ysabeau's father took them in for their cargo. Nobody at court will say what remains of it.

**Wonder and mystery.** The Priory's register hall: shelves of Orlen's parish books, with every name the fen ever wrote down, a whole kingdom filed in a foreign city. A boy on a throne for a country he has never seen.

**Dangers.** The court (III). The countryside in a Hunger (III). The levies, which Vauclere raises among the hewers for the White Deeps.

**Hooks.** Osbert's seal on every receipt: payment in full, upon restoration. What the Prior's uncle blessed in S.-1.

## Sephar

*River empire · the far south-east, where the sea narrows and a great river comes down to it* *· held by the Throne and the temples · danger II in the cities, III on the canals at dawn · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** Canals, mud-brick cities and the faith of **the Unconquered Day**: light is lent, and every harvest repays it. To Sephar the Dim was not weather. Its sun was lessened in the sight of everyone, and the river has flooded low every year since S.1. The doctrine now taught is **the Debt of Light**: the sun was robbed, the robber is the north, and the debt will be collected. In S.9 the **Embassy of Lamps**, six ships, sailed north to demand restitution. None came back, and nobody on any coast knows what became of them. Sephar counts its years from the wound: S.30 is L.30, the thirtieth Year of the Lessened Day.

**How it looks.** A brown river a mile wide between green strips, and beyond them desert. Cities of mud brick, whitewashed, with stepped temples whose roofs carry bronze sun-discs. Canals everywhere, low and stinking because the flood has failed. Every temple roof has a smoked-glass sighting-house on it.

**Places (new where marked).**

1. **The temple at Qesh**: its register of noon light, measured through smoked glass on every clear day since S.1, shows slow brightening since S.24.
2. **The temple at Marrah**: its register shows none. Each accuses the other of heresy, and nobody has ever carried one temple's glass to the other.
3. **The Garden of Hours**: a walled garden of sundials and shadow-courts at the Throne's seat, where every hour of the lessened day is marked in stone and watered at the Throne's expense. **The Guest** lives here as surety for the Debt.
4. **The oldest temple**: its foundations are set on older courses nobody can date. The plain reading: masons reuse what is there. **Layout (new):** a sanctuary on a mound; under it a crypt where the temple's own brick meets grey fitted stone of a different hand; and a passage in that stone, walled up by Sepharine masons with a sun-disc pressed into the mortar.
5. **The canals at dawn**, where the ditch-monitors are.

**Who is here.** Register-priests, who keep **kindle**: it lights tinder, wet or dry, with a crack like a snapped board. Collectors of the Debt, sent north, who do not return. **The Guest**, an old northerner who says he is Aldric IV. A Port Sable factor will swear the King drowned off the Lazaret Rocks in S.3 and that this is his chaplain. He knows what a king would know. So would a chaplain. He wants to be believed. The **ditch-monitor** (beast): a two-metre river lizard that has fed on famine dead for thirty years and lost its fear of the living. It is slow until the sun warms it; move at dawn. Whether the Dim's cold mornings are driving it into houses is not known.

**Old magic and law.** Sephar forbids **cold light** as light that nobody repaid.

**Wonder and mystery.** Noon on a temple roof, priests round the smoked glass, measuring a red sun. The Garden of Hours at dusk, every dial's shadow lengthening together.

**Dangers.** Ditch-monitors (III). Temple law, for a northerner who carries cold light (III).

**Hooks.** The Guest offers to write *I owe this* and sign it, which is worth nothing at the Exchange and is the only thing Gytha asked for. Carrying Qesh's glass to Marrah: half the disagreement turns out to be the instruments, and the other half remains.

## The Saltbacks, the White Deeps and Halsing

*Mountain range, salt mine and mine town · the Saltback mountains between Vauclere and Sephar; white galleries six hundred years deep · held by the Deeps Company · danger II in Halsing, III in the galleries, V at the wall · lore, with new layout marked*

**At a glance.** The oldest rock-salt mines on the Round, first cut about S.-600, under the mine town of **Halsing**. In S.27 a sump crew broke through the salt floor into dark, heavy, greasy stone that, fired, holds heat for hours. Assayers argue whether it is slag-iron or a poorer cousin. The **Deeps Company** was chartered at Port Sable in S.29, with shares in all three southern realms, the first thing the three have agreed on in thirty years. Nobody in the south knows how to work the ore, so the Company bonds the grandchildren of Orlen's Hewers' Lodges. They know exactly what they are looking at. In the autumn of S.30 the seam runs into close-fitted stone. The story ends here.

**How it looks.** Grey mountains with white scars where the salt is quarried, and Halsing in a high valley below: a town of stone and salt-block houses, chapels, bunkhouses, carting yards, and the Company's new pumping house steaming beside the old pit-heads. Below ground, galleries of white salt so large a church would fit in them, the walls glittering in lamplight, the floors worn smooth by six centuries of feet.

**Layout, zone by zone (new).**

1. **Halsing town**: the Company's offices, the carters' and bakers' lanes, the chapels, and the **bunkhouses** where the bonded hewers and their families live.
2. **The Old Pit-heads**: winding gear and stair-heads, six hundred years of them.
3. **The White Galleries**: the great salt halls, level below level, each level's last gallery left uncut as **the landlord's share**. Nobody remembers why.
4. **The Chapel Gallery (new)** : a hall carved entirely in salt by the cutters, altar, saints and all, lit by a hundred lamps.
5. **The Sump**, where the salt floor was broken, and the new ore workings below it.
6. **The pumps**, the Company's engines that keep the seam dry. Break them on a Dead Calm night and the ore is under water for years.
7. **The Wall**: close-jointed stone across the seam. A warm draught. Lamps that lean toward the crack. Here cold light gutters, which nowhere else it does.

**Who is here. Ines Corrant**, steward of the Deeps Company, who wants heat for forty thousand people in the valley towns before another winter. She is not in debt and she is not proud. She has counted. **Edith Brode**, spokeswoman of the **Low Lodge**,

granddaughter of Ancel Brode, who wants the right of refusal that no southern law grants. The Lodge is unlawful. Bonded hewers, salt-cutters, carters, bakers.

**What lives here: nothing.** Salt mines never held rats, bats or beetles, and no goblin has been seen in Halsing's drains. The Company cites this as proof the deep is empty. The hewers say nothing came up at the Slagcrown either, until the wall was broken.

**The wall and the shut doors.** If the wall is the same work as the Vaults, the makers were not only an older people of Orlen. If it is natural jointing (cooling rock joints itself into blocks, and the sea cliffs at one of the quays show it in daylight), perhaps the first wall was too. Witnesses disagree about the steps beyond the crack exactly as the Slagcrown's did: too shallow, too tall, frightened people in lamplight. The atlas answers none of it.

**Wonder and mystery.** The White Galleries under a hundred lamps, white to every horizon. The Chapel Gallery's saints, carved in salt, weeping brine in the warm draught.

**Dangers.** Collapse (III). The Company's law (III). The wall (V, and unknown).

**Hooks.** Fire-setting is planned to begin at the thaw of S.31. It took eleven days to break the wall at the Slagcrown, and the Company's engineers have planned for the same.

## Past the edges

*Off the chart · four directions, each a rumour of somewhere better · lore*

**West of the Skerries: the open ocean.** A few whaling boats came back after years away, talking of a warm current and green land. Most did not come back.

**East of the Weald: the Grass.** Horse-herding peoples who trade at the Weald's edge posts. Their herds are drifting west, so the winters are bad there too.

**South of Sephar: the Red Country.** Desert and caravan cities, a year's journey by camel. Grain is said to be cheap there. No ship of the Round has gone to find out.

**North of the Hoar.** Nothing is known.

These stay off the chart, and the atlas leaves them as rumours.
