Everything under the Highland scarp: the miners' galleries, the listeners' city, the road between the Citadels, and the bottom of the Black Seam, where the Older Work begins and the atlas stops.

## How the deep is built

The hills under the scarp are hollow for miles. Three things made the hollows, and each looks different.

| Who cut it | How it looks | How deep | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Citadels' crews | Square-cut drifts and stopes, timbered or bare, tally numbers on the walls, rails for ore tubs | Levels one to eleven under each Citadel | Every Citadel's shafts |
| The Hewers' Lodges | Galleries and halls far wider than any timber could hold, their lintels and spans cut with Holding and cold-light marks, steps worn smooth by centuries of boots | Between the levels, and joining the Citadels | The Low Galleries, the Hewers' Road, the old galleries at Rook's Stair |
| The Older Work | Fitted stone, steps the wrong height for a human stride, carving that cannot be copied the same way twice, warm air breathing | Below the eleventh level | The bottom of the Black Seam, and (some say) behind the walled galleries |

**Three rules for every deep place.**

1. **Sound is the danger.** Underground, everything carries further and everything listens. A Dead Calm falls in the deepest stretches and does not lift.
2. **The marks hold until they don't.** Where a Holding mark is worn or broken, the roof comes down, sometimes years later, all at once.
3. **The shut door stays shut.** The atlas describes the Older Work's edge and what the four survivors of the survey would say. It never describes what is beyond it. The listeners call it *the Hush*; the Citadel miners said *the Tenant*; fen folk say *below*.

**The levels.** Each Citadel numbered its levels downward from the pithead. The Slagcrown's went to eleven, the deepest. Kell Tor's went nearly as far, and its records say its lowest levels met walled galleries already old when the Citadel was young.

## The Low Galleries

*Deep place and city · the hewers' old galleries under the hills between Greystair and the Slagcrown, several miles of them on three levels · held by the listeners, about two hundred people (new number) · danger I among the listeners, IV in the troll drift · the listeners, the Quiet, Aud, the hand-cant, the leather doors, the troll pair and the route to the lower gate are lore; the city's form is (new)*

**At a glance.** A people who live without sound, in the oldest galleries the Hewers' Lodges ever cut. They keep the Quiet: no bells, no drums, a hand-cant instead of speech, wrapped tools, doors hung on leather. They will guide a stranger through to the Slagcrown's lower gate, but only in silence, and if the silence breaks they vanish into the side drifts.

**History.** When the Citadels emptied, some stayed under the hills: miners' families who would not leave their dead, Keeping penitents who believed the survivors owed a debt, and others who came later and did not say why. They took the hewers' galleries because their Holding marks still held and no flame was needed in them: the cold-light marks gave light. They made the Quiet because they believed the ringing of S.0 and S.1 woke or called what is below. They tell Hob's Height as the story of people who stopped ringing and lived.

**How it looks.** Galleries wider than a church, their roofs carried on spans that should not stand, their lintels cut with faint marks that glow blue when a listener whispers to them. No flame anywhere. Doorways hung with heavy leather that swings shut without a sound. People moving in soft wrapped shoes, hands talking in the blue light like weaving. A hall where forty people eat together and nobody speaks, and every face turns at a dropped spoon.

**Layout.**

1. **The Greystair mouth,** a hewers' adit in the hillside near Greystair's breathing hollow, its entrance hung with three leather curtains one behind another.
2. **The Listening Post,** a small chamber where a listener always sits, hand on the stone, feeling for footsteps. Visitors wait here to be met.
3. **The Hanging Doors,** a gallery of leather-hung doorways into the family rooms.
4. **The Hall of Forty,** the common hall, its span the widest Holding in the galleries, where the listeners eat and meet in silence.
5. **The Cant Hall,** where children learn the hand-cant before they learn to speak, and where the cant's thousand signs are cut into the walls as pictures of hands.
6. **The Lamp Gardens (new),** galleries where pale plants grow under cold light: white cress, blind roots, a grey vine with small sweet berries, the listeners' food.
7. **The Penitents' Chapel,** a chapel with no bell, its altar a block of uncut stone. The listeners name their dead here in hand-cant, which the fen's priests say does not count and the listeners say counts more.
8. **The Swearing Stone,** a pillar left uncut in the middle of a gallery, where outsiders swear the listeners' terms: keep the Quiet on the road home, and wrap any bell you keep, for as long as you live.
9. **The cisterns,** still black water fed by seep, where the only sound in the galleries is the drip.
10. **The long drift,** the deepest stretch on the way to the Slagcrown, where a Dead Calm lies and does not lift. **The troll pair** lives here, blind, hearing everything at more than half again the usual distance. The listeners cross it walking slowly with their shields raised, because walking while blocking makes no sound.
11. **The Slagcrown's lower gate,** at the far end, a small door into the Citadel that avoids the open courts.

**Who is here. The listeners,** about two hundred. **Aud** leads them, so far as anyone does: when asked what the orks fled, she signs *below*, then *we do not know*. Children who have never heard a raised voice. The troll pair in the long drift.

**Old magic. Holding** keeps every hall standing. **Cold light** is their only light: the word must be spoken aloud, so listeners whisper it, the quietest sound a person can make, and teach it to almost no one from outside.

**Wonder and mystery.** A city of silence. The blue light coming and going along the galleries as people whisper to the lintels. The Lamp Gardens. What they keep the Quiet against: *the Hush*, which they avoid even naming.

**Dangers.** Breaking the Quiet, which ends the guiding (the danger is being left alone down here). The troll pair (IV). The Dead Calm in the long drift.

**Finds.** Safe passage to the Slagcrown. Lamp-garden food. The hand-cant. Their terms, sworn.

**Hooks.** The novel's chapter 26, where Merrick's silence keeps them alive. In The Long Account, the listeners are who the company asks about the north.

## The Hewers' Road

*Deep place · a road under the scarp about sixty miles long, joining the five Citadels, with halls, stations and branches along it · held in stretches: listeners in the middle, orks at the Dunmarrow end, nobody at the Kell Tor end · danger III to V · (new)*

**At a glance.** The Hewers' Lodges' greatest work and their best-kept secret: a road under the mountains from Hob's Height in the west to Kell Tor in the east, cut over two centuries so the Lodges could move hewers, tools and the injured between the Citadels without the Slag-Lords' gates or tolls. Its halls are held up by Holding alone, and its way is marked with cold light. Most of Orlen never knew it existed.

**History.** The Deep Charter gave the Citadels everything below the turf, but the Lodges were sworn fellowships across all five, and they cut their own road between their levels. The lords tolerated it because it moved men to wherever a seam needed them. Each Citadel's Lodge kept its stretch and its station, and the road's lintels carry the marks of every Lodge that cut them. After the Breaking the Lodges used it to bring their people out from under the Citadels when the gates were shut. The listeners came to live in its middle reaches. At the Dunmarrow end the orks found it.

**How it looks.** A road wide enough for two carts, cut into black rock, rising and falling gently over sixty miles, with a channel for water along one side. Every lintel and every span carries a mark. When one is woken, the marks ahead answer one after another, faintly, a line of blue lights going away down the road into the hill like a road of stars, and then going out. The halls along it are vast and plain, with the Lodges' signs cut over their doors: a pick, a lamp, a pair of hands.

**Layout (west to east).**

1. **The Hob's Height station,** under the Bell-Spire's foot, beside the walled galleries.
2. **The Long Descent,** a stair road down three hundred steps, each one worn to a hollow.
3. **The Lamp Hall,** where the road's travellers drew their lamps, a hall of a thousand niches.
4. **The Greystair branch,** climbing to Greystair's breathing hollow, which is the road breathing.
5. **The Low Galleries,** the listeners' city, where the road widens into the old hewers' halls (see above).
6. **The Slagcrown station,** at the drift-head under the main shaft, with a side road down toward the Eleventh Gallery.
7. **The Hall of the Lodges,** the road's great hall, midway, where the five Lodges met once a year to elect their spokesmen and hear the year's dead read out. Its span is the largest Holding ever cut, and its roof is lost in the dark above. On its walls, the names of every hewer who died in the five Citadels for two hundred years.
8. **The Dunmarrow branch,** climbing to the deep forges under Dunmarrow. Ork ground: the packs come down this way in winter for the warmth.
9. **The Drowned Mile,** east of Dunmarrow, where the road dips under the tarn country and floods to the roof for a mile. Only a swimmer with a held breath or a boat could pass, and nobody has.
10. **The Kell Tor station,** beyond the Drowned Mile, under the sealed Citadel. Nobody has reached it since S.1.

**Who is here.** The listeners in the middle reaches. Ork packs from Dunmarrow in the east. Goblins in the side drifts. Quarry-trolls in the high halls, which are dry and warm.

**Old magic. Holding** and **cold light**, more than anywhere else in Orlen. The road would fall in a season without the marks.

**Wonder and mystery.** The road of stars. The Hall of the Lodges and its roll of the dead. What is at the Kell Tor station, past the Drowned Mile.

**Dangers.** Collapse where a mark has failed (V). The Drowned Mile (V). Orks (III, IV). Trolls in the halls (IV). Getting lost in the side drifts.

**Finds.** A way between the Citadels that avoids the surface. The roll of the dead. The marks.

**Hooks.** A route for the novel if Part Three needs one. In The Long Account, the road is how anyone in the north moves unseen.

## The Eleventh Gallery and the Black Seam drift

*Deep place · the Slagcrown's lowest level and the drift that follows the Black Seam down and east to the breach · held by nobody · danger V · lore, with new details marked*

**At a glance.** The bottom of the Slagcrown. In the Eleventh Gallery a deep crew under Ebba Cray broke into ore so dark and heavy the first lumps were taken for a joke. The drift that followed it down and east ended at a wall of fitted stone, and in the spring of S.0 the wall was broken.

**How it looks.** Low black galleries, hot and wet, the walls greasy with slag-iron ore that holds heat for hours. Rails for the ore tubs. Pumps that stopped. The drift narrows as it goes down, its roof carried on timber, then on stone cut by hewers, then on nothing anyone cut. The air moves: warm out of the dark, then still, then warm again. The lamp flames lean toward the bottom.

**Layout.**

1. **The Slagcrown station** on the Hewers' Road, and the side road down.
2. **The drift-head** and the **Weight Gate**, half built across the drift, its tools on the scaffold, three accounts of its purpose: *against the heat*, *against the Tenant*, *against what comes up the drift*.
3. **The side passage walled from below.** Below the Weight Gate, rough stone piled across a side passage from the far side, the Vault side: claw-scored rubble, no mortar. Orks block their openings with stone. That would mean they shut something behind them, or shut themselves out.
4. **The Eleventh Gallery,** the lowest level the Slagcrown cut: the crews' last shift's tools, the pump house with its rag-and-chain pumps seized, the fire-setting hearths black with soot.
5. **The Black Seam drift,** following the seam down and east, richer with every pace.
6. **The fire-setting chamber,** where the crews heated the wall for eleven days and cracked it with water: the floor ankle-deep in burned wood and split stone.
7. **The breach.** The fallen section of the wall, and the fitted stone on either side of it, blocks so close a knife will not go between them, the ore running up to and under it.
8. **The steps,** beyond the first crack, worn in the middle as steps wear, the wrong height for a human stride. Some say too shallow. Some say too tall.

**What the four said.** Six went through with Ebba Cray on the fourteenth day, with lamps and line. Four came back. They described stairs going down, galleries wider than any the Slagcrown had cut, and more fitted stone. They would not describe anything else, then or later. Ebba Cray did not come back. Marget Sayle was one of the four.

**Old magic.** The six crafts' marks will not take on the fitted stone. The hewers tried.

**Wonder and mystery.** The carving, worn almost smooth, that nobody can afterwards draw alike: lines like water, or only tool marks. One knock, far down, in a Dead Calm, once. The breath.

**Dangers.** Heat and bad air (V). The Dead Calm at the mouth, which does not lift. The noise of any work done here, and what it brings up the drift.

**Hooks.** The novel's chapters 28 and 29. The Long Account's last act, at the second wall in the White Deeps, mirrors this one.

## The edge of the Vaults, and the walled galleries

*The atlas's boundary · lore*

**The Deep-Seam Vaults** are everything beyond the breach. The lore's rule holds: the stonework there was not made by people, and who made it is never said. The atlas records only the survivors' words above and the evidence below. It gives no layout, no inhabitants and no explanation, and every future entry keeps it that way unless you rule otherwise.

**The evidence, all of it with a possible plain cause.** Ork dens from the Fold to the Slagcrown all face downhill, away from the Citadels. The wall piled from the Vault side. The tally boards in hand-cant. The breath, which could be a vent's cycle or the rock's heat. The knock, which cooling rock makes. The animals coming south in silence.

**The walled galleries.** Long before the Breaking, the hewers walled up galleries at **Hob's Height** and at **Kell Tor** and never reopened them. Older hewers said their grandparents had said the deepest levels *belonged to someone*. Kell Tor kept the records of every walled gallery in its deepest levels, and then shut its gate. The walls are hewers' work, mortared, and cut with a mark no other Lodge used: a hand held flat, palm out (new).

**The names.** The listeners say *the Hush* and avoid it. The Citadel miners said *the Tenant*. Fen folk say *below*. In the south, the salt-cutters of Halsing leave the last gallery of every level uncut and call it *the landlord's share*, and nobody remembers why.
