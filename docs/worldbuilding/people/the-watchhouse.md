# People: the Watchhouse and the Causeway Watch

Everyone named at the Wayfarer's Watchhouse, in its yard and texts, and in the Causeway Watch that held the tower
a mile east of its door. Ages and states are as of the winter of S.30 unless the entry says otherwise.

Each entry gives what the lore says first. The last line of each entry names its sources and lists what was
**new** on 2026-11-19, when the owner accepted all proposals and asked for every character to have a full entry
(ruling O10 in `CANON.md`). Loot under **Carries** uses the item names in `items-and-texts.md` where one fits.

Full entries that already exist elsewhere are not repeated here: Edda Wray, the eight background survivors and
the band are in `godot-people.md`; Gise Marrable is in `godot-people.md` section 4.

## The refuge

### Margery Reeve
*Keeper of the warning book · the Watchhouse · alive · 60*

**Who.** She wrote passes at the toll house before the Watch pulled back, and has kept the warning book since it
was begun in S.12. When the band took the house she saved the book by sitting on it in the dark. She holds
everyone to its habit: say what happened, say what you don't know, write down the dead. She knows the book has
been revised before, and by whom, and has never written that down. She speaks the ward over salt across a door or
a drain, learned from Grisel the hedge-priest: the first of the novel's three workings, and it costs her warmth
she cannot spare.
**Looks.** Small and upright, ink to the second knuckle of her right hand, a shawl pinned with a Watch buckle. Her
eyes go to your hands before your face, to see whether you are carrying paper.
**Wants.** Every torn page back. Everything written once, and true.
**Fears.** That the book will outlive the habit of keeping it honestly.
**Voice.** Exact. Says the words aloud as she writes them. *"Did you see it, or did somebody tell you? Both are worth
writing. They're not the same."*
**Carries.** The warning book itself (never given up). A horn of oak-gall ink, two goose quills, a twist of salt for
the ward, and a loose page she has not yet decided whether to bind in.
**Find her.** At the hall table by the one window, or at the Outside Fire while the house is held by others.
**Hooks.** Bring back a torn page: each one restores a bestiary entry and a name. Ask her who revised the admittance
rolls; she will answer only if you have already brought the fair copies from Saint Ebbe's.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §5; the novel's Bible (Cast). New: looks, fears, carries, find, the hook on the rolls.*

### Gil Rudd
*Door-warden · the Watchhouse door · alive · 62*

**Who.** A Watchman who lost a leg below the knee in S.14 and was sent back to the refuge, where he has kept the
door sixteen years. He was on it in S.19, when Corwen Dole's people were sent to the Outside Fire, and he said
the word. He calls the Watch Deserter a deserter to their face. While the house was held against them he sat at
the Outside Fire a month without saying what it was like.
**Looks.** A wooden leg strapped at the knee with harness leather, a Watch coat gone green at the seams, a bench at
the door worn to the shape of him.
**Wants.** The door kept. **Fears.** Being the one who has to say the word again.
**Voice.** Hard and short. *"Thirty beds, forty people, one door. You decide, then. Go on."*
**Carries.** The door's bar-pin on a thong round his neck. A Watch short sword he cannot run with. A tally of every
person admitted since S.14, notched on the doorpost, which no one else can read.
**Find him.** On the bench inside the 1.35 m doorway, every hour he is awake.
**Hooks.** The fair copies from Saint Ebbe's will show what he wrote in S.19. When the house is retaken the argument
about Lew Ashby begins again, and Gil decides it unless someone else will.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §5; the Bible. New: looks, fears, carries, the doorpost tally.*

### Joan Tallis
*Keeper of the stores · the Watchhouse · alive · 50*

**Who.** She writes every measure in and out. When the band came she hid the house's salt (the band's tally says
*Salt none, they hid it*) and will not say where until the house is retaken. Her count still includes the Cold
Passing salt that Edda gave away; she will find out. Not cruel, only exact: when salt runs out in deep winter,
exactness is how people die fairly.
**Looks.** Square-built, sleeves pinned back, a notched tally stick through her belt and a second one behind her ear.
**Wants.** Every measure accounted for. **Fears.** A winter where the count is right and still not enough.
**Voice.** Notches her stick as she talks. *"Every measure's somebody. Tell me who you want to go without."*
**Carries.** The stores tally; the key to the dry cellar; a Salt Ration she keeps back for the first child who
comes to the door in deep winter.
**Find her.** In the tally room, or at the Outside Fire counting what the turned-away have.
**Hooks.** Find where she hid the salt (she will tell you once the house is retaken, and it is more than she let on).
Tell her about the Cold Passing salt before Edda does, or after.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §5; the Bible. New: looks, fears, carries, the held-back ration.*

### Ottor Blewitt
*Fire-keeper · the Watchhouse · alive · 40*

**Who.** He banks the hearth, keeps the last coal and rations the fuel. Every dusk he carried a coal from the inside
hearth to the Outside Fire, so that the turned-away and the admitted burned the same fire. He was ten and inside
Saint Leof's at Crake Ford in the first winter; his mother and two sisters are among its fifty-one dead, and he
knows the hedge-priest's face. Kind to everyone but the one person he will not forgive.
**Looks.** Burn scars on both forearms, eyebrows long gone, a clay coal-pot slung on a chain.
**Wants.** The last coal kept. **Fears.** A night when he has to choose which fire goes out.
**Voice.** Quiet and practical; talks to the fire. *"Fire's not safety. Fire's fuel. Bring me fuel and I'll keep
you safe."*
**Carries.** The coal-pot (a live coal, always). A flint and steel, a bundle of dry tinder in oilcloth, and a
Seasoned Timber stick he will not burn because his sister carved her name in it.
**Find him.** At the hearth, and at the Outside Fire every dusk.
**Hooks.** The hedge-priest's door. Bring him peat from Brede at Moss Hythe and he becomes your ally in every
argument about the door that is not about the priest.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §2 (Hedge-priest) and §5; the Bible. New: looks, fears, carries.*

### Cress
*The rag-girl · the Watchhouse · alive · 10*

**Who.** She boils the refuge's rags: every clean rag a survivor carries passed through her hands. Her mother set
out a day ahead of her last winter and never arrived, and Cress asks every newcomer whether they saw a woman with
red thread at her cuff. Merewick's register buries such a woman beside a sluice; nothing says it is the same
woman, and Cress has never heard of Merewick. In the novel the walking bloom on the water path has red thread at
her cuff, and it is never confirmed.
**Looks.** Red hands, hair cut short with shears against lice, a boiling-stick taller than she is.
**Wants.** Her mother. **Fears.** Being told, and being told wrong.
**Voice.** Blunt, as children are. *"That rag's mine. I boiled it. Bring it back when you're done bleeding on it."*
**Carries.** Clean Rags, always four. A twist of red thread she pulled from her mother's spare shift, to match.
**Find her.** At the copper in the yard, or at the Outside Fire with the coughers, who are kind to her.
**Hooks.** The red thread. Anyone who goes to Merewick can read the sluice burial in the register and decide what
to tell her.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §5; the Bible; the novel's Part One beats. New: looks, fears, carries.*

### Lew Ashby
*Thatcher with the grey cough · the Watchhouse lean-to · dies in the novel's chapter 3 · 35*

**Who.** A thatcher at the cough's first stage. The refuge would not let him sleep inside and would not send him
away, so he slept in the lean-to and chopped wood for Ottor. When everyone was outside he still kept three paces
from the fire. When the house is retaken, the argument starts again with him. In the novel he dies in chapter 3.
**Looks.** Thin and long-armed, a reed-cutter's hook on his belt, a cloth over his mouth that he washes daily.
**Wants.** The axe back by morning. **Fears.** Being the reason the door shuts on someone else.
**Voice.** *"I don't need in. I need the axe back by morning, that's all."*
**Carries.** The refuge's woodaxe, on loan. A thatching needle and a coil of tarred twine. Everything he owns is in a
bundle a pace from where he sleeps, so nobody has to touch him to take it.
**Find him.** In the lean-to by the woodpile; in the game, at the Outside Fire's edge.
**Hooks.** The door argument. A thatcher can roof the Watchhouse's broken corner if anyone will let him inside to do it.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §5; the Bible; Part One beats. New: looks, fears, carries, the roof hook.*

### Winna and her two children
*Owner of the cooking pot · the Watchhouse · Winna's fate unsettled; the children alive in the refuge*

**Who.** The slag-iron cooking pot in the Watchhouse is hers: a string on its handle reads *This pot is Winna's and
her children's, boiled for this house since before the Breaking*. It holds its heat for hours. Tamsin Gale
probably borrowed it the night the tower fell and took it to the Long Stable to cook, and it ended in the ogre's
bone heap. The children's claim to it is unsettled; the refuge cooks in it.
**Looks.** The children (a girl of about eight and a boy of five) wear the same grey wool, cut from one blanket.
**Wants.** The children want the pot to be theirs in front of everyone, once.
**Carries.** The Cast-Iron Cooking Pot, if it is returned to them. The string with their mother's words on it.
**Find them.** In the hall, near the hearth, where Ottor lets them sit.
**Hooks.** Bring the pot back from the bone heap and give it to the children rather than the stores: Joan will count
it as theirs, and the refuge will remember who did.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`; `act-one-the-outskirts.md` (notes on the pot). New: the children's ages and looks,
the hook's outcome. Winna's own fate stays unsettled.*

### Osric Brayne
*The waystation's last Crown warden · gone south in S.3 · fate unknown*

**Who.** He rode south with the waystation's books in S.3, and someone cut THE CROWN DID NOT under the lintel after
him. The oldest residents swear he left a sheet behind a hatch on his last night: the families he admitted and the
families he turned away, the same names on both.
**Looks.** Remembered as a tall man in a blue Crown coat with a ledger satchel on each shoulder.
**Carries (what he left).** The sheet behind the hatch, if it exists. A Crown seal-matrix is said to have gone south
with him.
**Find him.** Not in Orlen. His books may be among the Keeping in Exile's papers at Saint-Aumere.
**Hooks.** Find the hatch. Find his books in Vauclere in Act II of The Long Account, where the Watchhouse's first
admittance rolls would be worth more than salt to anyone who can read them.

*Sources: `act-one-the-outskirts.md`; the atlas Blackfen tab. New: looks, the seal-matrix, the books at Saint-Aumere.*

## The Causeway Watch

### Wenlock Darby
*Captain of the Causeway tower · dead about S.20*

**Who.** He read the last orders in the tower yard in S.3, and kept the Watch on the road without pay afterwards. He
died about S.20, and Serjeant Agnes Kemble took the tower.
**Carries (what is left).** His captain's baldric hangs in the tower's guardroom, if the orks have left it. The order
he read, copied into the muster book's first leaf.
**Hooks.** The Watch Deserter joined under him. His grave is in the tower's yard, under the ork pack's midden.

*Sources: `the-kingdom-and-the-breaking.md`; `godot-people.md` §2. New: what is left of him.*

### Agnes Kemble
*Serjeant of the Causeway tower · fate unknown since the autumn of S.29*

**Who.** She took the tower after Darby's death in S.20 and held it until the orks came over the wall in a cinder-squall
in the autumn of S.29. The roster's standing order was hers: *Fall back to the Watchhouse. Bring the book.* She is
not among the four dead in the muster book, and nobody saw what became of her. In the novel, Pim says Kemble gave
the order; it is never confirmed, and her fate stays unknown.
**Looks.** Remembered as grey-haired at forty, a scarred chin, a bill she carried like a walking stick.
**Wants (in the novel).** Absent.
**Carries (if found).** The tower's key-ring. Nobody has found it.
**Hooks.** Her fate is a door the owner keeps shut: rumours of a grey-haired woman with a bill on the Salt Trace, at
the Drowned Lantern, at Brackenreach, none confirmed.

*Sources: `the-kingdom-and-the-breaking.md`; `godot-people.md` §2; the Bible; N9 and N10. New: looks, the rumours.*

### Wystan Prowse
*Serjeant of the Causeway Watch · fate unsettled*

**Who.** He counted the Watchhouse stores after the tower fell; his count is on a leaf in `items-and-texts.md`. His
fate is unsettled.
**Carries (what is left).** His count, signed. A Watch serjeant's whistle was found with it.
**Hooks.** Joan Tallis has his count and trusts it more than her own; if he is alive, he knows where the Watch's last
salt went.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`. New: the whistle, the hook.*

### Pim Haddow
*Watchman · alive at the start of the winter; dies in the novel's chapter 21*

**Who.** He shared the Watch Deserter's watch for six years and was last seen on the wall with a torch. He lived,
brought the muster book to the Watchhouse about nine weeks before Rafe, wrote its last entry (four dead, a date, and
*Deserted: none*) and went on east. In the novel he is in the lookout he cut at the White Trees, alive and dying of a
wound gone bad, and says Kemble gave the order.
**Looks.** Young, freckled under the grime, a split ear from a Greymaw.
**Wants.** Unknown until chapter 21.
**Carries.** A Watch arming sword notched at the forte; the knife he cut the lookout steps with; a Watch torch, burned
to the grip.
**Find him.** In the novel, the lookout in the White Trees. In the game, east of the Fen Gate once it is broken.
**Hooks.** Why he left the survivor out of the book: to save them or to save himself.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §2; the Bible; Part Two beats; N3, N9, N11, N17. New: looks, carries.*

### Stott, Wace, Pinder and Lamb
*Watchmen · dead in the tower, autumn S.29*

**Who.** The four dead in the muster book's last entry, in Pim's hand. Stott was the Watch Deserter's friend: in the
novel Rafe says his name with a boy's hands on his shoulders.
**Carries (what is left).** Their names on the muster book's line for the dead. Their gear in the tower's guardroom,
picked over by the pack: a padded coif, a cracked targe, Stott's whittling knife.
**Hooks.** Burying them means breaking the tower's pack first.

*Sources: the novel's Part One beats; N11, N14. New: what is left in the guardroom.*

### Tamsin Gale
*Watch · dead the night the tower fell*

**Who.** She had the first watch at the gap the night the tower fell. She came west off the Causeway hungry,
probably borrowed Winna's pot, and went out to cook at the Long Stable. Her kettle hat, scratched *T. GALE*, is in
the ogre's bone heap. The dead list says *T. Gale, Watch, at the tower or after. Not found.*
**Carries (what is left).** The Kettle Hat. Winna's pot. A scorched stone ring where she cooked.
**Hooks.** A Watch Deserter who puts on Gale's hat is wearing a comrade's helmet, and the Watchhouse asks for it back.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`; `act-one-the-outskirts.md`. Nothing new.*

### Joss Sefton
*Carter · missing since the week the tower fell*

**Who.** He took the Watchhouse cart east the same week the tower fell and did not come back.
**Carries (if found).** The Watchhouse cart, its load of rope and lime for the tower, and his oxen's bells.
**Hooks.** The Causeway Carter may find his cart on the way to the Cold Passing.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §2. New: the load.*

### Maud Kersey, Patience and Plod
*The Causeway Carter's wife, dead of the grey cough in S.21; the carter's oxen, left yoked at the Cold Passing*

**Who.** The Causeway Carter background lost their wife, Maud Kersey, in S.21. The oxen Patience and Plod were left
yoked at the Cold Passing the night the orks took the tower, when the carter had to run. Oxen do not last a winter
yoked in the open; the carter goes anyway.
**Carries (what is left).** The two-ox cart at the Cold Passing; the oxen's yoke and bells; Maud's comb in the cart's
locker.
**Hooks.** The cart at the passing: save it with a wheel, and Act II has its handcart or better.

*Sources: `godot-people.md` §2. New: the comb.*

## Named on the Watchhouse's texts

### Ebb Callow
*Cinder Factors carter · dead at the Causeway gap*

**Who.** The broadaxe in the yard came off his cart. His waybill is still in the plunder pile: *Carried by E. Callow,
carter, Watchhouse to Brackenreach.* The dead list says *E. Callow, carter, at the Causeway gap.*
**Carries (what is left).** The broadaxe, the waybill, a flask of lamp oil and a rag.
**Hooks.** The Cinder Factors pay on arrival, never before: his load was never paid for, and his house in Brackenreach
is owed.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`; `act-one-the-outskirts.md`. New: the debt hook.*

### Hildy Coyle, Odo Starke and the third
*Travellers · dead at the Spore-Choked Cart in the Spore Hollow, autumn S.29*

**Who.** Three travellers came up from the south with a cart, an ox and the grey in all their coughs, bound for the
hospice at Saint Orra's Steps. They came by the Watchhouse because the Corpseway, the road the sick are carried
along to the Steps, starts at its parish end. The ox died at the Hollow's edge. They left a note under a fresh torch and waited.
**Hildy Coyle**, a fen healer, left a note on her own body: *Fire, not a blade.* **Odo Starke**, a peat-cutter of the
Nine Stakes, sewed a name-strip in his cuff: *If I am silent, lime me.* The third would not say. No one lit the torch.
Whether they came to the Watchhouse door first, nobody knows. Odo's village does not know he is dead (see the Nine
Stakes in the atlas).
**Carries (what is left).** The cart, its pitch torch, Hildy's needles and thread, Odo's name-strip and his cutting
spade. The third left nothing with a name on it.
**Hooks.** Burn the cart with the torch they left. Carry Odo's name-strip to his brother Dowsett at the Nine Stakes.
Find out who the third was.

*Sources: `act-one-the-outskirts.md`; `items-and-texts.md`; the atlas (the Nine Stakes). New: the cart's contents.
Their route (up from the south fen to the Watchhouse, then the Corpseway west to the Steps) was ruled in O11.*

### Grisel
*Hedge-priest "of no chapel" · whereabouts unknown*

**Who.** The hedge-priest who taught Margery Reeve the ward, spoken over salt across a door or a drain, and who left a
scrap of it sewn in a gambeson.
**Looks.** Nobody at the Watchhouse agrees; Margery says *a woman with a voice like a man's*, Gil says the opposite.
**Carries (if found).** A bundle of ward-scraps, and salt.
**Hooks.** Grisel is not the background Hedge-priest (who is never named). Finding Grisel is finding the only living
teacher of the ward in the fen.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`; the atlas; the Bible (N4). New: the disagreement about who Grisel was, the scraps.*

### Lettice and her mother
*A child, written to · both unknown*

**Who.** Lettice is the child an unsent letter is written to; her mother wrote it and did not send it.
**Carries (what is left).** The letter.
**Hooks.** Nobody at the Watchhouse knows a Lettice. Margery copies the letter into the book in case one comes.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`. New: the hook.*

### The Fallow household, Widow Pruett and Abel the drover
*Households on the Causeway parish register leaf*

**Who.** Names on the flood-damaged Causeway parish register leaf: households admitted to the waystation in S.3, later
struck through as *departed*, and written on an older sheet as *refused*. Widow Pruett had two children.
**Hooks.** The leaf is the reason the fair copies at Saint Ebbe's matter.

*Sources: `items-and-texts.md`; `acts-two-and-three.md` (II.l). Nothing new.*
