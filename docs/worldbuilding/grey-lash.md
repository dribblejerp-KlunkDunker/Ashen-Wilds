# The Grey Lash: Faction, Leader, and Members

Structured consolidation of the faction introduction and expanded character profiles; not a verbatim archive. Proposed mobile contractors on the Bell Road, with a stop at Halwen House. See [relationship web](grey-lash-relationship-web.md). Not implemented AI or guaranteed neutrality.

## Identity

Small hauling/recovery company moving trapped timber, carts, injured travelers, bodies, and heavy salvage. Neither peacekeepers nor balance enforcers. Defining principle: preserve options and an exit. May rescue cheaply, perform paid work reliably, help enemies, refuse expanded tasks, abandon costly customers, or act through guilt and affection. No secret fixed allegiance. Reliability has future value, but is not an absolute guarantee.

Grey cloth marks their ropes and gear, producing the company name. People sometimes read recovered loads as theirs; they do not always correct that. At Halwen they bring a repaired handcart, ropes, tackle, wedges, slings, and tools. Stop by an old loading platform near the carriage drive, outside occupied rooms. Cart faces downhill. Potential work: dining-room recovery, water inspections, Remnant cargo, Sere’s frame/evacuation, salvage transport.

## Marda Cale: bargain-maker

Broad shoulders, weather-darkened skin, pale burn below lip, short wool coat clear of knees, separate inner pockets for payments, tallies, messages. Notices what must move to fit a cart through a doorway. Previously freight load attendant checking bindings, goods, damage. Employers praised avoided losses and blamed her when refusal upset a customer. Left with two outstanding wages and a purchased share in a damaged cart. Seeks bounded responsibility after having responsibility without authority.

Asks about replacement rope, waiting food, heavier-than-described loads, whether wagon recovery includes cargo, and responsibility for trapped people. Knows when insisting on answers loses work; colleagues trust compromises they may not see.

Secret aspiration: covered yard, secure storage, repair bench, ability to choose jobs. Wants somewhere affordable to remain while defending freedom to leave.

Flooded-yard incident: withdrew as a platform failed while hired workers continued freeing goods. Warned them but did not ensure comprehension. One died. Marda recalls warning; Vey recalls her walking while shouting; Tern recalls overload. None cancels the others.

Moral boundary: will not knowingly sell a person as cargo, yet may surrender essential goods to protect crew. Her reassurance sometimes conceals a privately retained withdrawal option. Prefers honest inconvenience to grand payment promises. Breaking point: choice between costly staying and plainly cruel departure cannot be disguised.

Voice: We can get the frame to the court. Beyond that, I need to see the slope. Anger replaces names with tasks: the customer has changed the load.

## Tern Roke: rigger

Tall, stooped, heavy forearms, stiff middle finger. Examines damaged rope closely and dislikes interruption during assessment; silence is not permission. Learned at a stone yard, stayed after scarce replacements forced worn gear into use, once judged a fitting might last one more lift. It failed; another worker died.

Marda’s usual respect for refusal sustains loyalty. Wants an apprentice but calls it another competent pair of hands. Teaches interested people, then resents departure or inattention.

Equipment represents past labor, repairs, foregone meals, and future livelihood. Will cut rope to save someone but may refuse abandoning tackle merely to improve a customer’s odds. Can be meticulous about immediate lift safety while ignoring the wider cost of delaying an evacuation.

Brik connection: asks where a sling hurts and changes it instead of demanding endurance. Dema connection: respects stopped unsafe work but challenges her when she denies workers rest. Quote: You know the wall needs a brace. Why don’t you know he needs sleep?

Could leave if Marda conceals changed loads or commits his skill without consent. Can tolerate a hard decision, not unauthorized promises in his name.

## Vey Sorn: road hand

Narrow face, uneven hair, sleeves pulled over hands while listening. Walks approaches, checks cart, speaks to overlooked people. Sometimes gathers information; sometimes avoids an unpleasant task. Prepares routes, clears people/animals, watches crew, secures possessions, helps frightened customers, notices omitted dependents. Can deter an isolated thief, not substitute for a trained escort. Prefers retreat when outmatched.

Joined after Grey Lash recovered her former party’s damaged cart. Traded labor for carrying her pack and stayed into next job without a formal invitation. Uncertainty about member versus indebted attendant drives demands for a voice.

Wants reputation for bringing people through, not just goods back. Makes private promises without consulting crew. Kindness includes desire to be needed: responsive to direct pleas, less attentive to quiet people whose needs complicate an existing commitment. Not impartial.

Sere corrects her for discussing transport as though elder absent. Their respect can grow through naming destination and uncertainty. Perrin’s unseen work draws sympathy; she may offer food and misread his refusal as obedience rather than risk awareness.

May decisively abandon a customer if convinced Marda or Tern faces immediate death, then struggle with earlier unconditional reassurance.

## Crew balance

Marda finds viable work; Tern performs it; threatened by unauthorized commitment or late refusal. Tern values Vey’s observations; she sees care in silence; threatened by promised equipment and competing personal need. Vey owes her place partly to Marda but prevents pure transaction; conflict when treated as attendant or binding others through private promises. No formal majority rule: argument and yielding depend on belief everyone remains afterward.

Core fracture: Vey promises Sere help; Marda agrees to inspect; Tern prepares frame. Each thinks a different commitment exists. Oren’s urgent paid recovery exposes that old ambiguity.

## Faction relations and decisions

Garden Hands offer provisions, knowledge, work; Dema respects Tern, but a better job may take crew away. Remnant pays best and pushes exclusivity; Elsin tracks supplies, Tern rejects unsafe commands. Kin need expensive help with poor payment: fittings, information, later obligations; Hesh wants definite limits, Vey may promise early. Sabeth may pay for discreet carriage; betrayal costs reputation but is possible.

They usually perform defined manageable paid jobs. Stop for expansion and renegotiate. Prioritize exit if fighting blocks it. Better offers test what finished means. Nonpayment may be outweighed by affection or future value. Powerful enemies encourage withdrawal/concealment. Personal promises can fracture crew. Tendencies, not automatic decision rules.

One-rig encounter: Sere’s move competes with Remnant cargo trapped by shifted masonry. Both cannot safely finish in daylight. Frame, inspected cargo, offer, Hesh’s observation, and Vey’s promise all visible before deadline. Switching does not automatically kill Kin; it leaves them exposed and distrustful.

## Preserved closing scene beats

Oren asks whose side Marda supports. Yours until beam down; Hands’ if doorway needed; Kin’s if Sere can get through. Vey insists they can; Marda says she was not asking her.

After a crew argument, Marda checks exit, Tern repairs a nearly sound strap, Vey sets three bowls. Tern asks departure; Marda says not tonight. Vey turns a bowl toward house. Marda asks whom it feeds; Vey names someone hungry. There are plenty, Marda says. Vey answers that she knows this one.
