# Dunmere: The First Arrivals

## Document status

It does not canonize all character details or claim implemented content. The wider Dunmere collapse and Tavin backstory are separate conversation material, not archived in full here. No existing documents, assets, or checkpoints are modified.

Continuity notes: eleven people arrive from three households; Oren Vey is missing and is not included in that count. His name does not establish an identity link to other characters named Oren. ## The arrivals

The first arrivals were three neighbouring households from a cluster of holdings called Alder Fold, beyond Tavin’s road station. Eleven people reached Dunmere that evening.

They knew one another well enough to share a cart, quarrel over what belonged on it, and notice who had quietly brought more than their share. They were not yet calling themselves refugees.

They asked for one night inside the walls.

## The Vey household

### Garran Vey — the father who wanted to go back

Garran walked beside the cart with one hand on its frame, steadying it whenever the repaired wheel struck a rut.

He kept looking north.

His missing son, Oren, was twenty. That morning, Garran had told him to follow the tracks only as far as the old charcoal path. When Oren did not return, Garran searched until the neighbouring households insisted they could not wait any longer.

At the gate, he described the missing sheep before mentioning his son. The animals were an explanation he could finish.

He was a tenant smallholder, accustomed to repairing what he could and asking for help only after exhausting the alternatives. Once admitted, he offered to mend the inn-yard fence in exchange for shelter.

Then he asked to borrow a lantern.

He genuinely intended to do both.

### Mera Vey — the woman with the hens

Mera carried six hens in a wicker cage lined with an old apron.

The birds were unsettled, scratching and striking the wicker whenever someone passed too close. Her wrists had gone numb around the handle, but she would not put the cage on the cart. Something might fall against it.

She had packed seed, a cooking pot, spare stockings, and a small cloth purse containing the household’s payments and obligations. The papers might matter if they needed to prove where they belonged.

She had left her wedding blanket on the bed.

It was too bulky. They were coming back.

At the inn yard, Mera checked her younger children, inspected the cart wheel, and asked where she could obtain hot water. Only when someone took the cage from her did her hands begin to shake.

She and Garran did not argue about Oren in public.

When Garran borrowed the lantern, she said:

“Bring someone with you.”

### Bess Vey — fifteen

Bess drove the sheep with a peeled hazel switch.

She was angry with the animals, angry with her father for looking back, and ashamed whenever she caught herself wishing they would forget Oren long enough to get through the gate.

Before leaving, she had gone into her brother’s sleeping space and taken his better pair of boots. He had left them behind to keep them out of the mud.

She wrapped them beneath her spare clothes.

She meant to give them back.

Inside Dunmere, Bess counted the sheep repeatedly. When a boy from the inn asked whether she knew how to count past seven, she struck his hand with the switch.

Mera made her apologize.

Bess did, without looking at him.

### Tollen Vey — eight

Tollen understood that Oren was missing. He did not understand why adults kept saying “when he comes back” while behaving as though something terrible had already happened.

He had been allowed to bring one thing.

He chose a wooden horse his brother had carved badly enough that it would not stand.

That night, he asked whether Oren would know they had gone to Dunmere.

Mera said they had left word.

Later, he asked who would feed the dog.

Nobody had brought it.

## The Renn household

### Hedd Renn — the man who repaired the wheel

Hedd was a widower who lived with his daughter and her husband in the next holding.

The cart belonged to him. So did the split wheel, which he had wanted to replace since the previous autumn.

He repaired it beside the road with a cut length of green wood while the others waited. Every time Garran looked north, Hedd found another instruction to give him.

Hold this. Lift that. Fetch the cord.

Keeping Garran occupied was deliberate. Hedd did not know how to comfort him.

At the gate, Hedd did most of the talking about accommodation. He knew the innkeeper from market days and hoped familiarity would secure them a place without making them sound desperate.

He called it “putting up for the night.”

He repeated that phrase even after their bedding was unloaded.

### Dena Renn — his daughter

Dena was twenty-seven and several months pregnant.

People kept telling her to ride on the cart. She refused until the last stretch because sitting among the bundles made her sick and every jolt hurt her back.

She had brought clean cloth, a small knife, dried beans, and a bundle of herbs she used for ordinary household remedies. She was not a healer, although people sometimes treated her as one because she was less frightened of blood than they were.

Dena noticed Mera’s shaking hands.

She did not ask whether Mera was all right.

She asked her to hold a cloth while she poured water over it. It gave them both something to do.

### Jory Renn — the husband who stayed too long

Jory reached them shortly before dusk.

He had remained behind to hide their better tools beneath the floor of an outbuilding and turn the livestock loose rather than leave it penned.

Dena had begged him not to.

When he caught up, she checked him for wounds and then refused to speak to him until they reached Dunmere.

He wanted to tell the others what he had seen on the road: men moving between trees, too distant to identify clearly, stopping when he stopped.

Once inside the walls, the account sounded less certain.

He worried that if he described it badly, they would dismiss him. If he described it too confidently, they would ask why he had not tried to help Oren.

He spent much of that first night examining the repaired wheel.

## The Senn household

### Rusk Senn — the reluctant arrival

Rusk had argued against leaving.

He kept two goats, cut fuel, and did seasonal work wherever neighbours could pay him. His holding contained little anyone would bother stealing.

That was his argument.

His wife replied that strangers would not know that until they had entered.

At Dunmere, Rusk was embarrassed by how relieved he felt when the gate closed behind them. He covered it by complaining about the smell of the livestock yard and the charge for fodder.

He had brought an old crossbow wrapped in sacking.

The gate watch inspected it. One limb had a visible split.

Rusk said it still shot.

The watchman handed it back without agreeing.

### Elva Senn — the person who made them leave

Elva had gone to the Veys’ holding after hearing about Oren.

She helped search the nearest fields. Then she returned home and began packing before Rusk had decided there was anything to pack for.

She was not unusually brave or perceptive. She simply refused to spend another night waiting for someone to knock.

Her private fear was that she had made them leave too soon.

She had covered the banked hearth, secured the door, and left bread wrapped in cloth for their return. All evening she pictured rain finding its way through the shutter she had meant to mend.

Inside the inn yard, she asked whether anyone had spare sewing thread.

She repaired a tear in Mera’s sleeve while they waited for water.

### Nella Senn — twelve

Nella had never slept in Dunmere.

She knew the market in daylight: ribbons, ironwork, hot food she was seldom allowed to buy. She had imagined staying overnight would mean an upstairs room at the inn.

Instead, she slept beside sheep on borrowed straw.

She complained once.

Then she saw Bess sitting with Oren’s boots in her lap and stopped.

### Fen Senn — four

Fen slept through the gate inspection against his father’s shoulder.

When he woke, he wanted his own cup.

Elva offered another. He refused it, then cried because he was thirsty.

The crying carried across the yard until Mera found a small wooden cup among her bundles.

Fen accepted it.

It had belonged to Oren when he was little.

## Their first night together

The innkeeper gave them the covered side of the yard and charged less than he could have. He also told them they would have to move if paying carriers arrived.

Hedd said that was understood.

Elva and Dena spread bedding. Bess tethered the sheep. Jory found somewhere to hang wet coats.

Garran borrowed the lantern.

For a short while, with everyone occupied, the yard almost resembled preparations for an ordinary journey.

Then Tollen asked whether they should keep supper warm for Oren.

No one answered immediately.

Mera set a portion aside.

Later, when the others were lying down, she ate it herself, standing beside the cart.

Garran was still at the gate.
