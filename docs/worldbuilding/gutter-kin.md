# The Gutter Kin

## Document status

Related documents: [Member profiles](gutter-kin-members.md), [Occupation factions](halwen-house-occupation-factions.md), [Garden Hands](garden-hands.md), [The Last Gardens](the-last-gardens.md), and [Private chapel](halwen-house-private-chapel.md). The separate detailed Halwen Remnant expansion is not archived by this file. Revised 2026-11-05 for owner decision O1 (only humans are people): the Gutter Kin are a human household of road waifs. The Gutter Kin live in the part of Halwen House its former owners would have considered an outbuilding.

They have made six sleeping places beneath a glasshouse workbench. Rainwater is caught in salvaged vessels. Cloth hangs inside the broken frames to interrupt sightlines from the house.

From the road, the range looks abandoned.

Closer, you notice that the debris has been moved. Certain gaps remain clear. Loose metal has been placed where a careless foot will disturb it.

The Kin’s first defense is **access**: small, light and practised since childhood, they can enter spaces other people cannot, move between obstacles without exposing themselves, and make a pursuer choose between continuing and keeping their footing.

They are not harmless refugees. They have injured people, stolen necessities, and learned to take advantage of someone already in trouble.

They are also six individuals with different ideas about how long they should remain.

Everything below is proposed lore expanding their occupation of the glasshouse range.

## 1. What makes them kin

The six are not related by blood.

They are road waifs. Most of them were children when they found one another under the Roofed Mile, the avalanche gallery on the Bell Road, before the animal that holds it now moved in. They lived by taking what wasn't watched from the carts and walkers who passed beneath. Sere was already grown, and taught them what was worth taking.

When the gallery was lost, they became a larger scavenging household that broke apart along the northern roads. Some died. Some left. Others were driven off after disputes over food and responsibility.

Nobody was born kin. The present group calls itself kin because its members chose one another, share shelter, and are expected to retrieve one another when something goes wrong.

Others call them *gutter*, and do not mean it kindly. To the Remnant they are squatters and thieves. The Kin took the word and kept it, the way they keep most things nobody else is watching.

That expectation is already under strain.

Hesh refuses to leave their injured elder behind.

Several others believe his refusal is turning the glasshouses into a place where all six will die.

### Why they chose Halwen

The range offered things they understood:

- A standing rear wall.
- Narrow routes beneath benches.
- Useful material overlooked by other scavengers.
- Several exits.
- Food close enough to steal.
- A house too damaged for its occupants to watch every approach.

The claimant’s arrival has made that last advantage less reliable.

## 2. The six occupants

### Hesh Split-Lip — the finder

Hesh chooses routes, approaches outsiders, and decides when a theft is worth attempting.

His facial wound makes some sounds difficult. Estate listeners hear a split mouth and gutter speech, and mistake both for limited understanding.

He sometimes lets them.

Hesh is good at remembering what a person actually did: whether they put down a weapon, reached for a companion, or moved closer while pretending to retreat.

He is less good at recognizing when loyalty has become coercion.

#### What he wants

A safe departure with all six alive and enough equipment to establish another shelter.

#### What makes him dangerous

Hesh will create a distraction using someone who does not understand the risk.

He might encourage a stranger to approach the hall while the Kin move behind it. If guards become violent, he will not necessarily interrupt the escape to help.

> “You wanted the front door. We went another way.”

### Sere — the injured elder

Sere is not a mystic or keeper of the world’s secret history.

She knows how to sort metal, straighten certain fittings, preserve a useful edge, and recognize when an apparently intact object will fail under load.

A fall injured her hip before the Kin reached the estate. She can work seated and move short distances with support. She cannot manage their usual escape route.

Her sleeping place is the driest.

Nobody mentions this without starting an argument.

#### What she wants

Sere says they should leave her tools and take her out.

She has not accepted that the tools might be the only things they can carry.

#### Her fault

She treats the younger members’ fear as evidence that they have not endured enough.

They have hauled her, fed her, and given her their dry cloth.

She still speaks to them as though they are considering departure out of selfishness.

### Kett — the wall scout

Kett watches the front block through gaps in the glasshouse wall.

He knows when the pack animals are fed, which retainer carries water, and which windows have recently been cleared.

He does not know every watch rotation. His information becomes outdated when people change their behavior.

Kett is the strongest advocate for leaving.

He has already hidden a small bundle outside the perimeter.

#### What he wants

Permission to go without being treated as a deserter.

#### His fault

He is quietly withholding the full condition of one escape route.

A section has collapsed since he last used it. He still describes it as passable because admitting otherwise would strengthen Hesh’s argument for waiting.

### Brik — the carrier

Brik moves heavy objects and helps Sere between sleeping places.

He is broader than the others, though still small beside the Remnant’s well-fed retainers. None of the Kin grew to full size on what the road gave them.

The others tend to assume that carrying is the work he is suited for.

He has begun noticing how often their escape plans depend on his strength while their food shares do not.

#### What he wants

To leave without carrying both Sere and everything everyone else refuses to abandon.

#### His fault

Brik has taken payment from another member of the Kin to move a private bundle during departure.

He has not told Hesh how much this will slow him.

### Iss — the cutter

Iss works leather, strips bindings, and dismantles objects whose useful parts are hidden beneath other material.

She knows which harness straps hold weight and which merely keep something tidy.

She took the injured Garden worker’s boots.

She does not deny it.

> “He could not walk. We could.”

The boots did not fit her. She cut them into usable pieces.

#### What she wants

An exchange arrangement that provides food and leather without requiring repeated raids.

#### Her fault

Iss considers a bargain finished when the agreed objects change hands.

She does not automatically accept the unspoken expectations settled people attach to generosity, rescue, or hospitality.

She may keep the exact bargain and still feel free to rob the same person later.

### Moth — the nest-maker

Moth maintains the sleeping area, patches containers, and catches drips before they soak the bedding.

The name comes from the scraps of cloth she wears tied over her shoulders, not from any supernatural association.

She understands the glasshouses as a place that can still be improved.

That makes her the least willing to leave without another shelter identified.

#### What she wants

A roof she does not have to remake every morning.

#### Her fault

She conceals useful material inside the shelter rather than letting it be traded.

Hesh may promise a fitting to an outsider and discover that Moth has built it into something she refuses to dismantle.

## 3. Their ground

The Gutter Kin do not control every inch of the glasshouse range.

They hold three connected spaces.

### The sleeping bench

A heavy masonry-backed bench shelters six low sleeping places.

The front is screened with layered cloth. The rear wall is checked every morning for new damp.

No one sleeps directly against the outside opening.

Sere occupies the inner end. Brik sleeps nearest the exit because he is expected to move her.

He resents that too.

### The sorting bed

One former planting bed contains their useful material:

- Short metal fittings.
- Leather strips.
- Cloth.
- Containers.
- Nails separated by condition.
- Objects not yet dismantled.

It looks like rubbish until someone watches them work.

A visitor taking a corroded fitting may be stealing the part they need to repair a water vessel.

### The maintenance trench

The trench allows movement behind several benches without standing upright.

Some covers are sound. Others are not.

The Kin know the difference because they tested them.

A newcomer can identify danger through displaced covers, warning scraps, fresh scuffs, and the absence of debris along a route.

The trench does not lead beneath the whole estate.

## 4. How they defend it

Their defenses are meant to discourage pursuit, reveal entry, and create time to escape.

Loose fittings make noise. Cloth blocks observation. Debris narrows approaches.

They have also placed concealed hazards that can injure an intruder.

The encounter should provide clues before those hazards become consequential. A deliberately cleared foothold beside a suspiciously covered gap is information, not decoration.

### How they fight

The Kin prefer several people distracting one opponent rather than one of them accepting a fair fight.

They use cover, thrown objects, sudden movement, and retreat.

They will take an exposed hand, ankle, pack, or tool rather than remain in reach of a weapon.

None of them can afford a long fight. A childhood of short rations left them frail, and their wounds are slow to stop bleeding.

If driven into a corner, they become much more dangerous.

Sere’s inability to retreat is the reason Hesh cannot simply rely on dispersal.

## 5. What they eat—and what theft costs

The glasshouses do not feed them.

They obtain food through scavenging, opportunistic hunting, theft, and occasional exchange.

They cannot indefinitely survive on scraps conveniently appearing outside occupied rooms.

Every meal creates pressure to leave cover.

### The stolen boots

The injured Garden worker is not one of Dema’s four-person core watch. He was part of an earlier salvage visit.

His injury cost the Gardens labor.

Losing his boots cost him the ability to work outside comfortably even after the wounds began healing.

The Kin’s gain was real.

So was the damage.

Returning a few strips of leather would not undo it.

That history should remain relevant if the player tries to arrange reconciliation.

## 6. Their customs

### Leave the opening clear

Inside a shelter, nobody sleeps across another person’s route out.

Putting belongings in an exit is treated as threatening behavior.

This is one reason they distrust people who stand in doorways while conducting friendly conversations.

### Put the goods down

For an exchange, each party places the goods within sight and steps back.

Touching someone else’s offered object before agreement can end the encounter.

Holding it out of reach is worse.

### Retrieve what can still answer

The household expects members to attempt retrieval when a missing companion can still signal or respond.

That is not an unconditional rescue oath.

It is an argument they repeatedly revisit: how much danger, how many attempts, and who decides that no response will come.

Sere’s injury has pushed that custom beyond anything they agreed in advance.

## 7. What they think of the house’s people

### The Garden Hands

They understand why the workers want the range cleared.

They also see people with a settlement below claiming every useful building above it.

Hesh finds the distinction between *recovering estate material* and *stealing from the house* unconvincing.

He is less willing to discuss the difference between taking an abandoned fitting and stripping an injured person.

### The Halwen Remnant

The banner means little to them.

The guards, animals, supplies, and attempts to control entrances mean a great deal.

Kett thinks the Remnant can be robbed.

Sere thinks people who expect obedience will eventually insist on it.

Hesh knows both may be right.

### Sabeth Rill

They have seen her move upstairs.

Iss once left a damaged cup where Sabeth would find it.

The cup returned with food inside.

Neither side considers this trust.

It is a small arrangement that would become dangerous if the Remnant discovered it.

## 8. The injured elder problem

Their departure requires a route suitable for carrying Sere.

The narrow breach used by the others is insufficient.

The outer chapel court offers a better passage, but crossing it exposes them to observation from the house.

This creates a concrete negotiation:

- A period when the court is left unguarded.
- Permission to move under escort.
- A temporary halt in work near the crossing.
- A carrying frame and help with the steep section.
- Another shelter identified before departure.

A successful agreement should genuinely get them out.

It should not secretly end with an unavoidable ambush merely to prove that the world is cruel.

The cruelty already exists in what people may demand for allowing passage.

## 9. Their first internal crisis: Kett’s bundle

Moth finds Kett’s hidden departure bundle.

It contains food, a sound container, and one of Sere’s small tools.

Kett says he took only his share.

Sere says the tool belongs to the household.

Brik asks whether Kett intends to leave him carrying everything again.

Hesh orders the bundle brought inside.

Now the group must decide whether kinship means obligation freely maintained or permission to prevent departure.

The player might witness this before being trusted.

They should not receive an automatic invitation to judge it.

## 10. What the player can change

The Gutter Kin support several lasting outcomes:

- A negotiated departure with Sere and some recovered goods.
- A limited salvage arrangement under which they remain temporarily.
- A split household, with some leaving and others staying.
- Forced displacement that makes the glasshouses available but sends survivors elsewhere.
- A violent clearance with injuries, deaths, and recoverable evidence of who lived there.
- A move to another viable shelter, without turning the Kin into obedient followers.

They may honor an agreement without becoming friendly.

They may become friendly without agreeing never to steal again.

Their behavior should respond to specific treatment, known events, and available choices—not an all-seeing faction reputation.

## The first conversation

A voice comes from behind a hanging sheet.

“Put down.”

The player lowers the offered food onto a stone.

A small hand draws it out of sight.

For several moments, nothing happens.

Then Hesh steps into the gap.

Behind him, someone is arguing quietly. Another voice coughs.

He looks at the player’s hands, then toward the house.

“You want us gone?”

Before the player answers, he adds:

“Where?”
