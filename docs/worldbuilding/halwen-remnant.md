# The Halwen Remnant

Structured consolidation of the expanded faction and five members from the conversation, not a verbatim prose archive. Proposed occupation phase; see [occupation overview](halwen-house-occupation-factions.md), [house](halwen-house.md), and [chapel](halwen-house-private-chapel.md). Not implemented AI or finalized balance.

## Identity and employer

Five people try to make a ruined estate function again. Three are trained fighters. Their primary task is recruiting labor and acknowledgment, not merely conquering rooms. Their belief is service: survival comes from belonging to someone. Employment, protection, food, and hierarchy are inseparable in their thinking.

Their proposed employer is Lady Maudren Halwen of a collateral family branch. Her documents establish a connection but do not conclusively settle ownership. She can fund five people, not an army. She wants productive land, Bell Road access, and dependents. Orders prohibit destroying improvements without necessarily protecting the people who made them.

## Commission and private priorities

Formal commission: secure property, protect inhabitants, inventory assets, prepare restoration. Private letter: secure water, inventory occupied structures and land, recruit useful workers, assess support for a larger household, avoid unrecoverable expenditure. It neither explicitly commands nor forbids forced labor or eviction. Oren keeps it separate from public instructions.

Reinforcement is not automatic. Reports need a messenger, a southern decision, supplies, and a successful journey. Honest failure could end funding; inflated success could bring unsupportable dependents.

## Five retainers

### Serjeant Oren Vale

Born after the Retreat. His father served the southern household, which fed Oren during a deadly winter. Patronage worked for him; he believes he is extending it. Maintains equipment and consistent rations, rejects casual theft, and requires accounts for takings. His distinction between theft and authorized seizure permits bureaucratic cruelty. He pressures dependents rather than simply fighting the resistant person. Quote: If you claim the right to answer for them, you may explain what your answer costs.

He respects Dema’s skill and offers maintenance authority beneath him, failing to understand why recognition as his employee may be rejected. He expects Sabeth’s report but does not know her hiding room. He can offer private correction for withheld information; public exposure of instructions looks like betrayal.

### Rauth Kell

Trained door fighter with spear, heavy knife, battered shield. Experienced in warehouses, quays, and confined approaches. Wants an early public demonstration: seize a gate or useful object and hurt someone sufficiently to establish a boundary. Opposes wasteful indiscriminate killing because it destroys labor and drives people away. Wants a permanent room and secure meals in the restored estate. His own security is to be built by making refusal dangerous for others.

### Lenna Marr

Third trained fighter, short bow on the road and spear within the grounds. Favors early force through occupied work areas and guarded access rather than a spectacular beating. Watches approaches and animals. Will not readily abandon civilian crew during withdrawal; she has seen drivers and workers sacrificed to preserve fighters. This limit does not make her uniformly merciful to outsiders. Could accept supervised Kin departure to avoid fighting in broken glass.

### Elsin Thale

Inventory keeper, correspondent, provision accountant; neither priest nor lawyer. Precise quantities, loaded descriptions: gardener-occupied space may become service accommodation withheld from household use. Wants a recognized office over stores and wages rather than attendant status. Advancement makes her reluctant to abandon harmful accounting. Distinguishes documents, ownership claims, and permissions, but may overextend all three.

### Perrin Holt

Civilian drover/general servant: animals, water, straps, cooking, and work described as minor that occupies most of the day. Continued employment keeps his younger sister housed in the south. He knows little inheritance law and much about dismissal. Vessa has noticed he walks alone. Noll may speak with him; shared vulnerability is not automatic allegiance. Later proposed private repair bargain with Iss can expose him to discipline.

## Life and leverage

Hall and sound dining-room corner hold bedding, cord boundary, inventory table, and locked supply/correspondence chest. The trapped supporting timber remains; they sleep away from it. Banner is visible, not their most useful possession. Watches, sleep, animal care, and errands require tradeoffs; there is no spare patrol. Copies of claim documents elsewhere mean chest theft does not erase the claim.

Grain, cloth, oil, tools, and southern purchase contacts are genuine offers. Selective distribution rewards acknowledgment. Household roll records names, dependents, skills, issued goods, expected duties. Some welcome recognition; others fear gifts becoming advances against service. A blanket can later become an obligation.

Dangerous clause: reasonable duties necessary to preserve the estate. Oren wants flexibility, gardeners want specificity. Whoever controls interpretation controls future labor. Water inspection points are a priority because access can be made conditional without poisoning or destruction.

## Faith and relationships

Oren and Rauth touch chapel lintels; Elsin values witnessed continuity. No priest is among the five. Covenant binds protection, but Oren disputes whether people rejecting household authority qualify as dependents. Gardens argues obligations preceded renewed submission. Ritual alone cannot settle it.

Gutter Kin initially classified as intruders. Rauth favors expulsion; Lenna may prefer supervised departure; Elsin demands fittings back. None knows routes or numbers automatically. Sabeth can expose conflicting duties; Perrin may sympathize without risking his sister’s place.

## Broken-yoke encounter

A pack animal injures a leg on paving, reducing transport. Perrin asks to borrow a Gardens cart. Its owner demands terms ensuring the loan creates no claimant obligation. Elsin disputes wording; Rauth suggests seizure. Oren must show whether protection constrains him when inconvenient. Options include bounded loan, alternative hauling, repair, or seizure. This affects credibility as well as cargo.

## Withdrawal fracture

If restoration fails, Oren may be ordered to recover value and return. Rauth wants assets to justify the mission. Elsin wants records blaming local resistance. Lenna insists everyone returns. Perrin fears dismissal. Departure may be peaceful, stripping, hostage-taking, or internal fracture, but needs visible causes and chances to intervene.

Possible outcomes: restricted front-block household; predatory service estate; dependent partner recognizing gardeners; failed mission whose members seek local places; divided command. Reciprocal limits, practical cooperation, and witnesses matter more than a document alone.

## Closing scene beat

Perrin serves the four others; Oren notices Dema waiting outside and asks if she has eaten. She refuses a bowl. Oren reads refusal of hospitality. Dema has counted who would see her accept it.
