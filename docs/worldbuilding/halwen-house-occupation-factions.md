# Halwen House: Occupation Factions

## Document status

Halwen House should be **divided**, not occupied by one large enemy camp.

Its main entrance, service wing, and glasshouse range each support a different group. The chapel sits between their routes. Sabeth Rill remains upstairs, suddenly caught between the people who commissioned her reconnaissance and the people whose home she is helping them assess.

This is a proposed next stage for the location: the house was sparsely occupied when first explored; now a returning claimant’s advance party has forced everyone else to establish boundaries.

## The occupation at a glance

Keep the numbers small. This is a damaged country house, not a fortress with room for an army.

| Group | Position | Immediate strength | What it controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Garden Hands | Kitchen, pantry, lower service yard | Four rotating residents, only two experienced fighters | Working access to the Gardens and parts of the water system |
| The Halwen Remnant | Entrance hall, dining room, approach court | Five retainers, including three trained fighters | The main entrance, carriage drive, and conspicuous authority |
| The Gutter Kin | Glasshouse range and adjoining maintenance trench | Six road waifs, including an injured elder | Narrow approaches, concealed movement, and stolen fittings |

The upper floor remains mostly unclaimed because of structural damage.

The chapel has no permanent garrison. Everyone wants something from it.

## 1. The Garden Hands

### “We are taking back what we kept alive.”

The Garden Hands are a work party drawn from the Last Gardens, not a separate military order.

Until recently, residents came uphill, recovered material, and returned before dark. The claimant’s arrival changed that. Leaving the house unattended now means allowing someone else to lock them out of its tools, plans, and water access.

Four people maintain a rotating presence in the service wing.

They sleep in the pantry, cook beneath the kitchen chimney, and keep the service-yard entrance clear.

### Their leader: Dema Rusk

Dema is a mason’s laborer who spent years repairing garden walls and drainage channels.

She knows how to shore a doorway and which rubble can be moved without bringing more down.

She does not know how to command a battle.

When frightened, she becomes rigid about work assignments because assigning a task feels better than admitting she has no plan.

Her weapon is a short spear recovered from a roadside casualty. She has practiced with it. She has never killed an armored person.

> “They call it abandoned. Ask them who cleared the drains.”

### What they want

- Continued access to the water channels.
- Recognition that the Gardens may recover necessary repair materials.
- No armed inspection of the seed room.
- No new obligations imposed merely because the estate has become useful again.

They do not initially demand the whole house.

That makes them easier to bargain with—and easier to push back one doorway at a time.

### Their cruelty

The Hands have ordered that no food be carried from the Gardens to the claimant’s party.

Dema extends that rule to anyone working for them.

A hungry local hired to carry luggage can therefore lose access to the common pot.

She calls this refusing to feed an occupation.

The carrier calls it punishment for finding work.

### What they offer the player

Tools, local knowledge, help moving heavy material, and access through the service wing.

They cannot promise that helping them grants permanent membership or a bed at the Gardens.

## 2. The Halwen Remnant

### “Possession has been resumed.”

The Remnant is an advance household sent by the southern claimant paying Sabeth.

They have not arrived to restore the entire estate. Their instructions are to establish a defensible presence, identify productive assets, and obtain acknowledgment from the people already living on the land.

Five people arrive with two pack animals.

Their banner is small enough to fit inside a saddlebag.

They hang it above the main entrance anyway.

### Their leader: Serjeant Oren Vale

Oren was born after the Retreat. He has never seen Halwen House before.

His father served a surviving branch of the family in the south. Oren inherited that employment, a repaired mail shirt, and a conviction that reliable service is the only protection people like him possess.

He is not pretending to believe the claim.

That makes him harder to intimidate with accounts of the family’s desertion.

> “I was not here when they left. I am here now.”

He will acknowledge what the gardeners maintained.

He will not acknowledge that maintaining it made it theirs.

### Their foothold

The party occupies the entrance hall and a sound corner of the dining room.

They have not removed the timber trapped beneath the fallen beam. They have placed their bedding well away from it.

They establish:

- A watch at the carriage approach.
- A cord across the hall beyond which visitors require permission.
- An inventory table.
- A locked chest containing supplies and correspondence.

The chest is useful, but taking it does not erase the claim. Copies exist elsewhere.

### Their leverage

They brought clean cloth, grain, lamp oil, and some proper repair tools.

They distribute these selectively.

A household that acknowledges the claimant’s authority may receive help before one that refuses.

Oren describes this as allocating limited resources to cooperative dependents.

### Their cruelty

They intend to secure the water inspection points before negotiating land tenure.

If allowed to occupy the service wing, they can make access to a shared necessity conditional on obedience.

They do not have to poison anything or burn a house.

A locked door and an armed man beside it would be enough.

### Their weakness

Five people cannot hold the whole estate, repair it, patrol the road, and escort supplies.

They need local labor.

Oren has orders to obtain compliance without ruining what he has come to claim. His two other fighters favor a faster demonstration of force.

## 3. The Gutter Kin

### The people beneath the broken glass

The Gutter Kin are a small household of road waifs occupying the glasshouse range: people who grew up sheltering under the Roofed Mile, lived by taking what wasn’t watched, and became kin to each other.

Others call them *gutter*, and mean it. To the Remnant they are squatters and thieves. Oren’s retainers say they carry the cough; nobody has looked.

They use the maintenance trench, gaps beneath the planting beds, and a breach in the outer wall. They do not possess a secret tunnel network reaching every room.

Their narrow routes become apparent through observation.

They have been stealing fittings, food scraps, cloth, and anything that can be made into a container.

They have also moved several useful objects away from places where rain was destroying them.

Neither activity makes them harmless.

### Their leader: Hesh Split-Lip

Hesh is a practical scavenger with an old facial wound that makes his speech difficult for strangers to follow.

He understands more of the Remnant’s formal southern speech than he lets them see.

He prefers exchanges involving objects placed on the ground. He does not trust a bargain conducted while one participant is holding the goods over his head.

> “Put down. Then talk.”

### What they want

Dry sleeping space, food, and a route out that cannot be watched from the house.

Their injured elder cannot manage the exposed climb beyond the glasshouse wall without help.

Hesh has delayed leaving because he will not abandon her.

Several of the younger Kin think this will get them trapped.

### Their violence

They have set concealed hazards around their sleeping area: sharpened fragments, unstable covers, and lines tied to loose metal.

They raid isolated workers rather than challenge armed groups.

A Garden Hand was badly cut after pursuing a stolen tool into the trench. The Kin took his boots while he struggled to climb out.

He survived because another worker came back for him.

### What they know

They have seen Sabeth enter the upstairs room.

They know the service passage gives access to a damaged water channel.

They also know the claimant’s pack animals are poorly watched before dawn.

Their knowledge is specific and earned. They do not know what is written in sealed correspondence or what happened thirty years ago.

### What they offer the player

A concealed approach to the glasshouses, recovered fittings, and information about recent movement.

Helping them leave would genuinely remove one source of danger.

It would also free space that another faction could occupy.

## 4. The chapel between them

The chapel matters because it connects territory with legitimacy.

The Garden Hands want the household covenant read before anyone recognizes the returning claim.

Oren wants a witnessed gathering there at which the residents acknowledge his employer’s authority.

The Gutter Kin want the outer court left unguarded long enough to move their injured elder.

Sabeth wants everyone kept away from the family passage.

None of those objectives requires a supernatural event.

### The first confrontation

Oren orders the chapel cleared for a household assembly.

Dema refuses unless the whole covenant is read aloud.

While they argue in the court, one of Oren’s retainers finds a fresh track of rag-bound feet beside the chapel wall.

He assumes Dema has been allowing the Kin through.

She has not.

Now a political dispute threatens to become a search of the service wing.

The player can investigate the track, interrupt the search, help arrange the reading, or exploit the distraction.

## 5. Sabeth’s position

The Remnant’s arrival should not automatically reveal Sabeth.

Her commission instructed her to observe and report. Oren expected to meet her, but does not know which room she uses.

She can approach him and become his guide.

She can approach the Gardens and admit what she has done.

Or she can continue hiding while her notes become increasingly dangerous to possess.

If the player has already met her, she asks one concrete favor:

> “Do not let him search the upper rooms until I have taken my things.”

Some of those things are personal.

Some describe exactly which cottages are occupied and where their water comes from.

## 6. Changes the player can actually achieve

The occupation should allow durable local outcomes, not require exterminating every faction.

- Negotiate shared inspection of the water system, preventing either the Hands or the Remnant from monopolizing it.
- Escort the Gutter Kin out, recovering stolen goods through agreement or accepting that some losses remain unresolved.
- Secure a written arrangement that limits the Remnant to the front block.
- Help the Hands defend the service entrance without turning them into rulers of the estate.
- Expose a retainer’s unauthorized seizure, creating a real split within Oren’s party.
- Clear a neutral route through the chapel for travelers.

Violence remains possible.

But killing Oren does not repair the cistern, make the Gardens welcoming, or prove the claimant has abandoned the property.

## The first sight of the occupied house

A visitor approaching Halwen House now sees three signs of life.

A banner hangs over the front entrance.

Cooking smoke rises from the service wing.

Beyond the broken glasshouses, a thin figure pulls a sheet of canvas out of sight.

Then someone inside the hall calls:

> “Keep to the drive.”

From behind the kitchen wall, another voice answers:

> “You needn’t.”
