# Hesh Challenges Oren

Full proposed dialogue scene preserved from the conversation. Set at the loading platform during the [goods interception](remnant-goods-interception.md). Represents one possible negotiated outcome, not mandatory behavior or implemented quest dialogue.

Rauth lifted the bundle from the cart.

Something metal knocked inside it.

Moth reached up. He moved it away from her hand.

“Set it down,” Hesh said.

Rauth looked toward Oren.

The serjeant stood beside Elsin’s table, one hand resting on the inventory sheet.

“Those pieces are disputed.”

“They were disputed inside,” Hesh said. “You looked. She wrote. We brought them out.”

“Inspection does not establish ownership.”

Hesh turned his head slightly, bringing Oren onto the side from which speaking hurt less.

“Then what did it establish?”

Elsin answered. “What was being removed.”

“And now you know.”

“Yes.”

“So put it back.”

Oren stepped away from the table.

“The occupants may leave. The disputed material remains until the account is settled.”

For a moment, Hesh said nothing.

Behind him, Brik shifted his grip on the frame. Sere was watching from beneath the blanket.

“Who settles it?” Hesh asked.

“The household.”

“Yours.”

“The claimant’s.”

“Your people.”

“There will be an account.”

Hesh glanced at the bundle.

“Will you bring it to us?”

Oren hesitated.

“You may petition for its release.”

“Come back here.”

“If necessary.”

“To your door.”

“To the estate.”

Hesh looked toward the glasshouses. The cloth screens were down. Moth’s sleeping place was visible between the broken uprights.

“You waited.”

Oren’s expression tightened.

“We are addressing the matter now.”

“You saw it before we packed it. You saw it before she took down the cloth.”

He pointed toward Moth.

“You wait until we have nowhere to put it back.”

Rauth lowered the bundle a little.

Not enough.

“The fitting was removed from an estate installation,” Elsin said.

Moth spoke without looking at her.

“It was lying in the trench.”

“It remained estate material.”

“It was broken.”

“That does not—”

“Sere fixed it. I made it catch the water.”

Oren raised a hand.

“No one is denying that you used it.”

Hesh looked back at him.

“You want the work left on it.”

“I want property belonging to this house retained.”

“When it was broken, you left it.”

“I was not here.”

“You are here for it now.”

Rauth gave a short, impatient breath.

“You have been allowed your bedding, your food, your personal effects—”

“Put it down,” Hesh said again.

Rauth’s fingers tightened on the strap.

Lenna moved closer.

“Keep the cart approach clear.”

“I am clear.”

“Then stop making everyone stand in it.”

Oren looked at her, then at Rauth.

“On the table.”

Rauth carried the bundle to Elsin’s table and set it down.

Moth followed two steps.

Hesh put out his hand, not touching her.

She stopped.

Elsin reached for a blank piece of paper.

“I can provide acknowledgment of retention.”

Iss laughed once.

Nobody joined her.

“What?” Elsin asked.

Iss pointed at the bundle.

“She needs that tonight.”

“The acknowledgment preserves the claim.”

“Will it keep her bed dry?”

Oren spoke before Elsin could answer.

“If the fitting is essential, we can discuss a substitute.”

Hesh looked at him.

“Now.”

“We are discussing it.”

“Show it.”

Oren turned toward Perrin, who stood beside the cart with a loose binding in his hands.

“What suitable vessels remain?”

Perrin glanced at Elsin.

“The spare basin. One jar.”

“The jar is for drinking water,” Elsin said.

“The basin?”

“It is listed for household use.”

“Bring it.”

Perrin set down the binding.

Moth’s voice came from behind Hesh.

“It needs the fitting.”

Oren looked at her.

“You have not seen the basin.”

“It catches from the cloth. It holds the cloth away from the wall. A bowl underneath is not the same.”

Tern, beside the frame, nodded once.

“She’s right.”

Oren’s eyes moved to him.

“Can you make something else?”

“Probably.”

“How long?”

“I need to see what you’re giving me.”

“And we need to leave,” Marda said.

She had remained beside the cart throughout. Now she took her hand from the wheel.

“You approved a load. We prepared that load. If it changes, we stop and agree again.”

“The six occupants are still permitted to depart.”

“Six people and the listed goods.”

“The list records—”

Marda shook her head.

“You read it to them as the outgoing load.”

Elsin’s pen hovered.

Oren looked at the paper.

Hesh watched his face.

“Read it,” he said.

Elsin glanced at Oren.

“Read what you wrote.”

“The pertinent entry permits personal bedding, working tools, food stores, and fittings necessary for—”

She stopped.

Moth took another step toward the table.

“For what?” Hesh asked.

Elsin finished.

“Necessary for establishing shelter at the agreed destination.”

The wind lifted the edge of the sheet. Oren put his palm on it.

For several breaths, the only sound was the cloth moving against Sere’s frame.

Then Oren said, “That wording was not intended to surrender every claim of the estate.”

“Keep your claim,” Hesh said.

“I cannot simply—”

“Write it. Keep it in your box. Tell whoever you tell.”

He pointed to the bundle.

“We take that.”

Rauth shifted his feet.

“And when you return for the rest?”

Hesh looked at him.

“We are trying to leave.”

“You took from the house.”

“Yes.”

The answer silenced Rauth for a moment.

Hesh turned back to Oren.

“We took things. We broke things. You named what you wanted returned. We put it there.”

He pointed to the separate pile beside Elsin’s chair.

“You do not have to call us good.”

“Nobody has asked—”

“You ask us to leave our things because you say you will be fair later.”

Hesh’s mouth had begun to pull painfully. He pressed two fingers against the scar, waited, and lowered his hand.

“Be fair now.”

Oren looked toward the glasshouses.

Then at the frame.

Sere was watching him without moving.

Finally, he said, “If the bundle leaves, you understand that the household does not recognize your ownership of its contents.”

Hesh frowned.

“Will someone stop the cart?”

“Not over this bundle.”

“Will someone take it at the gate?”

“No.”

“Will you send him after us?”

He indicated Rauth.

Oren’s jaw tightened.

“No one will pursue you to recover goods permitted under this agreement.”

Hesh looked at Marda.

She nodded.

“That says it.”

He turned back.

“Then write that too.”

Elsin began making room beneath the entry.

Oren spoke to Rauth.

“Return the bundle.”

Rauth did not move immediately.

“Serjeant.”

“Return it.”

Moth reached the table before he did.

She put both hands around the bundle and held it against her body.

Rauth stepped aside.

Oren waited until Hesh faced him again.

“This settlement depends on there being no further theft.”

“We heard you inside.”

“I want your acknowledgment.”

Hesh looked at the outgoing goods, the returned goods, and Elsin’s moving pen.

Then he nodded.

“No taking while we leave. As agreed.”

Oren accepted the answer.

“Marda. Proceed.”

Tern checked that Brik was ready.

Vey moved to her place.

As the frame began to lift, Oren said, more quietly, “You could have asked for the substitute.”

Hesh waited until Sere was clear of the table.

Then he looked back.

“We asked for our **bundle**.”

Moth was already walking beside the cart.
