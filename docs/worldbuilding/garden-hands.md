# The Garden Hands

## Document status

Project: Ashen Wilds. Preserved in full from the 2026-11-01 worldbuilding conversation. Proposed detailed faction profile for the occupation phase of [Halwen House](halwen-house.md). Related documents: [Occupation factions](halwen-house-occupation-factions.md), [The Last Gardens](the-last-gardens.md), and [Private chapel](halwen-house-private-chapel.md). These are narrative and encounter proposals, not implemented NPC schedules, AI, combat balance, or quest systems. Existing lore, assets, and development checkpoints are not modified. Revised 2026-11-05 for owner decision O1 (only humans are people). The original is in git history at commit fd60061.

The Garden Hands began with four people carrying tools uphill.

They were supposed to clear the service passage, recover usable fittings, and find where the estate’s water channels had failed. They took food for the day and expected to sleep at home.

Then the Halwen Remnant arrived.

Now the tools stay beside their bedding. The kitchen door is barred after dark. Someone watches the courtyard while another person works.

They still describe themselves as a **work** party.

Calling themselves soldiers would mean admitting how little preparation they have—and how much the Gardens has begun depending on them.

This is proposed faction lore, expanding their foothold in Halwen House without making them a separate army.

## 1. What they believe they are defending

The Hands do not initially claim every room, field, or surviving object at Halwen House.

Their claim is narrower:

> “You cannot shut us out of what keeps the Gardens alive.”

That means the service passages, water inspection points, garden records, and material needed for essential repairs.

Some members believe the house belongs to everyone who maintained its land.

Others would surrender most of it if the claimant guaranteed those practical rights.

That disagreement matters. An offer acceptable to one Hand may look like betrayal to another.

### What holds them together

Every member has someone sleeping below in the Gardens.

They are not defending an abstract homeland. They are defending particular people, meals, tools, and roofs.

The Remnant can threaten them without ever attacking their position: delay a supply carrier, recruit a relative, or offer one household a better arrangement.

## 2. Their four-person watch

Four people form the initial uphill party. Others occasionally carry supplies or relieve them, but the Gardens cannot sustain a permanent garrison.

Two members have experience fighting people. Dema is not one of them.

### Dema Rusk — the work leader

Dema knows masonry labor: moving stone, mixing repair material, shoring openings, and recognizing when a wall has begun to move.

She was chosen to lead the repair expedition because she could organize the job.

Nobody chose her to negotiate an occupation.

She continues writing tasks on a salvaged cupboard door:

- Clear passage.
- Brace pantry lintel.
- Inspect channel.
- Bring second water jar.

Below those, in a different hand:

- Watch front court.
- Keep spear inside.

Dema hates the second list.

#### What she does well

She divides difficult work into manageable tasks. She notices tired hands, worn rope, and loads too heavy for one person.

She will halt a repair rather than pretend it is safe.

#### Where she becomes dangerous

Dema treats withdrawal as something a person must justify to the group.

A Hand asking to go home may be told to finish one more task, then another. She knows that if one person leaves, the others must choose between abandoning work and spreading themselves thinner.

She has begun using that knowledge against them.

> “You can go when there’s someone to take your place.”

### Vessa Dorn — the courtyard fighter

Vessa once escorted small trading parties along the lower roads.

She carries a short spear and a broad knife. Her padded coat has been repaired with pieces of several other garments.

She is the most capable person in a sudden fight.

She also knows that capability has limits.

Vessa refuses heroic gestures: no chasing a thief into unknown ground, no standing in an open doorway to exchange threats, no warning strike that puts someone within reach.

#### Her obligation below

Her elderly father lives in the shared sleeping house. He needs help dressing and getting down from his platform.

While Vessa is uphill, another household does that work.

Its members have started mentioning the arrangement during disagreements over food.

#### Her fault

Vessa favors taking a hostage if the Remnant blocks the water passage.

She would prefer an unarmed servant to a fighter.

> “A man with a sword knows what he came for. Take someone he has to explain losing.”

Dema has not agreed.

Vessa has already noticed which member of the Remnant walks outside alone.

### Jerrit Salk — the night watch

Jerrit fought in two local feuds over livestock and a crossing.

He is not proud of either. He does not become reluctant when violence seems necessary.

He carries a wood axe, partly because it is useful and partly because he cannot afford to dedicate good iron to something used only for fighting.

Jerrit sleeps badly. During watch he counts sounds: loose shutter, dripping gutter, footstep, pause.

His reports are usually accurate.

His interpretations are less reliable.

#### His grievance

Years earlier, a creditor took his household’s draft animal. The household never recovered its ability to haul heavy loads.

He sees the Remnant’s inventory table as the beginning of the same process.

When Oren says he merely wants to establish what is present, Jerrit hears a list being made for removal.

#### His fault

He has begun setting aside seized goods without recording them.

He calls it a reserve.

A coil of rope taken from an unattended Remnant bundle now lies beneath his bedding. If discovered, it could turn an uneasy boundary dispute into a search.

### Noll Fen — the young repair hand

Noll is nineteen and good with wood.

He repairs handles, wedges doors, and makes braces from timber other people dismiss as useless.

He also carried luggage for the Remnant when they first arrived.

Dema’s food restriction caught him before he understood that accepting the job meant taking a side.

He joined the uphill party partly to recover his standing in the Gardens.

Nobody openly calls it punishment.

#### What he wants

A recognized sleeping place that does not depend on remaining in a household’s favor.

Bren’s proposed common guest platform interests him more than any argument about ancestral ownership.

#### His fault

Noll thinks useful service can make him indispensable.

He volunteers for work beyond his ability and conceals injuries rather than risk being sent down.

A cut beneath his glove has become swollen. He keeps using the hand.

## 3. Their foothold in the service wing

The Hands control a small, practical area rather than the whole rear of the estate.

### The kitchen

The kitchen is their working room.

Tools hang where everyone can see whether one is missing. Food is prepared over a modest hearth arrangement after the usable part of the chimney has been checked.

They avoid a conspicuous fire when weather permits.

Smoke tells the Remnant when someone is cooking. The smell tells them what.

### The pantry

The pantry holds bedding, food, and a covered water jar.

Its door can be barred, but it would be a poor place to withstand a determined attack. There is no second easy exit.

Vessa insists that nobody sleep there without boots within reach.

### The service yard

The yard provides access to the Gardens’ path.

The Hands have cleared enough rubble to move a loaded handcart through it. That improvement is one reason they cannot simply leave: the Remnant now wants to use the same route.

A length of cord marks where stored material must remain clear of the passage.

It is a work boundary, not a defensive barrier.

People keep treating it as one.

## 4. What sustains them

The Gardens supports the party, but not comfortably.

Food carried uphill comes out of the same household stores feeding children and older residents. Fuel carried to the house is fuel not burned below.

Dema is expected to justify the cost through useful work.

This creates pressure to keep repairing things even when the occupation has made repair dangerous.

### Their standing rules

- No food concealed from the common supply.
- No recovered tool removed without telling another Hand.
- No one goes upstairs alone.
- A watch must remain while the others work.
- Water access must not be surrendered in a private bargain.
- Nobody chases the Gutter Kin into the trench.

They have already broken several of these rules.

The faction’s character emerges from which breaches it forgives.

## 5. Their authority—and its limits

The Hands answer to the Gardens’ households.

There is no separate charter granting them power to negotiate land tenure, accept permanent duties, or declare war.

Dema can agree to move a stack of timber.

She cannot honestly promise that the whole settlement will acknowledge a claimant.

Oren knows this. He sometimes offers terms generous enough that refusing them makes her look unreasonable, but broad enough that accepting would exceed her authority.

### Disagreement with the Gardens

Sella wants inspection of the water system prioritized.

Nera wants the garden office protected.

Bren wants repair materials recovered and worries about the cost of a prolonged occupation.

Their priorities overlap without being identical.

A messenger can arrive carrying three requests that cannot all be completed that day.

Dema must choose whose disappointment to bring home.

## 6. The people they exclude

The Hands’ weakest moral position concerns people outside recognized households.

They accept casual labor when hauling is needed.

They do not necessarily share political authority, secure sleeping space, or the best portions with those workers.

Dema argues that established households supplied the tools and food.

Noll knows what that argument means from the other side.

If the player helps build the guest platform, some Hands welcome it. Others fear creating a new group of people whose needs they will be expected to defend.

Their resistance to dispossession does not automatically make them opponents of dependency.

## 7. Their relations inside the house

### The Halwen Remnant

The Hands need to prevent the Remnant from securing the service passage.

They cannot win an open fight reliably.

Their advantages are familiarity, access to the Gardens, and the fact that Oren needs the estate functioning more than he needs a damaged kitchen full of corpses.

That leverage weakens if his party obtains another source of labor.

### The Gutter Kin

The Hands want stolen tools returned and the glasshouses safe to work.

Jerrit wants the gutter brats driven out.

Dema would accept a departure agreement.

Vessa considers the Kin useful while they complicate the Remnant’s movements, but would never admit that openly to the injured worker whose boots they stole.

### Sabeth Rill

If discovered, Sabeth will divide the party.

Dema wants to know what she has reported.

Jerrit wants her notes seized.

Vessa wants to exchange her for concessions.

Noll recognizes someone accepting dangerous employment because other choices were worse.

None of them has the authority to imprison her indefinitely.

That may not stop them.

## 8. Their first internal crisis: the missing rope

Use Jerrit’s concealed rope as a small event with consequences.

A Remnant retainer identifies it while visiting the kitchen for a negotiated inspection.

He demands a search.

Dema does not know the rope is there.

Jerrit says it was recovered from an abandoned room.

Noll saw him bring it in from the front court.

Now the faction’s rules are tested by something more difficult than an outsider’s accusation.

Possible responses include returning the rope, admitting the taking, refusing the search, or offering compensation without conceding the retainer’s right to enter.

A successful resolution should preserve a particular boundary or prevent a particular fight.

It should not make everyone trust one another.

## 9. A player-facing task: shore the narrow door

The damaged pantry lintel needs bracing.

The required timber is available in the dining room—but lies within the Remnant’s occupied area.

The player can:

- Negotiate its release.
- Supply timber from elsewhere.
- Help recover a sound piece under disputed salvage terms.
- Steal it, with consequences only if evidence or witnesses connect the act to them.
- Persuade the Hands to abandon the pantry and establish a safer sleeping position.

This task reveals the faction through labor and decisions rather than a speech about its principles.

Once properly braced, the doorway remains safer.

That practical success gives the Hands something they can point to when the Gardens asks what their occupation has achieved.

## 10. Where they can end up

The Garden Hands need not always remain four armed workers in a ruined kitchen.

- They can return to ordinary repair work under a limited access agreement.
- They can become a permanent estate watch, gradually acquiring authority over the people below.
- They can fracture when one household accepts the claimant’s terms.
- They can lose the service wing but preserve essential access by negotiation.
- They can hold their ground while becoming harsher toward casual workers and suspected collaborators.

The darker development is not that they reveal themselves as secretly evil.

It is that defending a necessary function gives them power, and they begin deciding who deserves its benefits.

## The evening changeover

A replacement arrives from the Gardens carrying a food pot.

Before handing it over, she asks Dema whether the channel inspection is finished.

Dema says no.

“The brace?”

“Not yet.”

“What did you do?”

From the hall comes the sound of the Remnant moving furniture against a door.

Dema takes the pot.

“We stayed.”
