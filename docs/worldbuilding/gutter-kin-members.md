## The six beneath the workbench

The Gutter Kin should feel like people who know one another too well.

They recognize the sound of each other waking. They remember who received the larger portion three nights ago. They can work together without speaking—and use that familiarity to hurt one another precisely.

Hesh calls their bond **loyalty**.

The others are beginning to ask who gets to define it.

The following profiles are proposed additions to their existing faction lore. Their histories are local and personal; none possesses an authoritative explanation of the Vaults or the Smothering.

## Hesh Split-Lip — the leader who will not count a person as lost

Hesh has a narrow, underfed face, one ear torn at the rim in a fight under the Roofed Mile, and a pale seam pulling the left side of his mouth out of shape.

When he eats, he turns slightly away so spilled food is less visible.

When negotiating, he positions himself where he can see both the other person’s hands and the route behind them. He does not like sitting with his back against a wall unless there is another opening nearby.

### How he became their leader

Hesh did not defeat a rival or inherit command.

During the winter retreat along the northern roads, after the Roofed Mile was lost, he kept finding somewhere the group could stop: a dry culvert, a roofed stock pen, an abandoned charcoal shelter.

People began waiting for him to choose.

He became responsible because his decisions repeatedly worked.

Now the others still wait, even when they no longer agree.

### The wound in his mouth

A former companion cut him during an argument over food and departure.

Hesh had tried to stop that companion leaving with equipment the household still needed.

He tells outsiders that he was attacked.

That is true.

He usually leaves out the part where he stood across the exit.

His present refusal to let Kett leave threatens to repeat the same confrontation.

### How he speaks

His understanding exceeds what estate listeners assume of a gutter child grown up. His speech becomes more economical when his mouth hurts or he is speaking the careful, owning language of the house.

He should not always speak in fragments. Among the Kin, he can be patient, sarcastic, and exhaustingly thorough.

To a stranger:

> “Put it down. We can both see it there.”

To Kett:

> “You have described three ways out. You have not walked the last of them since the wall fell. Stop calling that a route.”

### What earns his respect

Someone who admits a limit before he has to discover it.

He would rather hear that a person cannot carry Sere than watch them abandon her halfway.

### What makes him turn

Taking someone from the shelter without agreement.

He may negotiate over tools, food, and stolen goods. A missing member makes him reckless.

### A private moment

When Sere sleeps, Hesh checks the proposed carrying route with a sack weighted roughly to her size.

He does this alone.

He does not want the others to see how often he has to put it down.

## Sere — the elder who fears becoming an object

Sere has thickened knuckles and unusually careful hands. She tests a piece of metal by turning it slowly, examining its wear before deciding whether to strike, bend, or discard it.

Her injured hip has changed everything except the way she gives instructions.

She still tells people where to put things as though she could get up and move them herself.

### Her place in the household

Sere was already a grown woman when the children found her under the Roofed Mile. She repaired equipment when the larger household was still together, and taught most of the others to sort what they took.

She remembers what certain possessions cost: the meal traded for a knife, the injury taken recovering a pot, the route lost while carrying too much.

She uses those memories to resist discarding objects.

Sometimes she is preserving something essential.

Sometimes she is making everyone carry her past.

### What she has not said aloud

She knows the youngest members have given up food for her.

She hears their arguments while pretending to sleep.

Admitting either would require thanking people she has been ordering about—or acknowledging that they might have a right to refuse.

Instead, she corrects their work.

### Her difficult kindness

Sere teaches by repairing someone’s mistake without destroying what remains useful.

She can spend an hour helping Moth make a leaking container hold.

Then she can humiliate Brik for dropping it.

Her patience is real. It is not evenly distributed.

### What she wants from a stranger

Not pity.

Ask her to identify a fitting or judge a damaged hinge, and she becomes engaged.

Offer to carry her without asking where she wants to go, and she becomes hostile.

> “You have found a way to move me. That is not the same as asking.”

### What could change her

Let her participate in planning the departure.

Hesh has begun protecting her from decisions about her own body. Being allowed to choose what is left behind could make her more willing to leave.

## Kett — the scout who has already begun departing

Kett is lean, restless, and meticulous about his feet.

He removes grit from his wrappings every evening. He checks routes in small sections rather than making one grand reconnaissance.

When others are speaking, he often looks toward the last sound outside.

This is not cowardice. Since he was a child under the Mile, he has been the first person expected to go and see. He has the softest feet and the sharpest eyes of the six.

### What he contributes

Kett can distinguish recent activity from old damage:

- A shutter closed since yesterday.
- A door dragged across fresh dust.
- An animal tether moved to a different post.
- A place where someone has begun standing regularly.

His reports are observations first. Trouble begins when he makes them sound more certain than they are.

### Why he wants to leave

He does not believe the house needs to agree before the Kin can go.

Every day spent waiting lets the Remnant learn more of the grounds.

He is right about that.

He is wrong to conceal the damaged escape route.

### His relationship with Hesh

Kett once followed Hesh without question.

Now Hesh’s caution sounds like an instruction to remain useful until someone else decides he has done enough.

He hates being called impatient by a leader who keeps giving him reasons to wait.

> “You say we leave together. You do not say when.”

### His private bundle

The food is partly his own saved portion.

The container was communal.

Sere’s tool was taken without permission.

He has arranged these distinctions carefully in his mind so he can call the whole bundle his.

### What earns his trust

An exit he can inspect.

Promises of safety mean little. A cleared path, an agreed meeting point, or a shelter he has seen matters.

## Brik — the carrier who wants to be asked

Brik has a heavier chest and shoulders than the others, though he is small for a grown man. One thumb was broken long ago and never straightened properly.

He adjusts loads with unusual care.

People mistake his deliberation for slowness.

He is usually calculating what will hurt least after the first few hundred steps.

### His place in the group

When something must be moved, everyone looks at him.

When the route is chosen, everyone looks at Hesh.

Brik has begun noticing the difference.

He wants a say in plans that depend on his body.

### His arrangement with Iss

Iss has promised him a good small knife if he carries her private leather bundle during departure.

He accepted.

The bundle is not large by itself. Combined with Sere, bedding, and shared equipment, it may be too much.

He keeps postponing the conversation.

### What he is good at besides carrying

Brik is the group’s most patient learner.

He remembers a repair after being shown once. He can also calm a frightened companion by giving them something specific to do.

Moth works more easily beside him than beside Sere.

### What makes him angry

Someone adding weight after a load has been agreed.

A seemingly minor act—hanging another pot from his pack—can provoke the strongest reaction the player has seen from him.

> “Take it off. Ask me again.”

### What might keep him with the Kin

A departure plan that distributes burdens honestly.

He does not necessarily want to leave them.

He wants remaining to stop meaning that he carries what they will not relinquish.

## Iss — the cutter who insists a debt must have an end

Iss keeps blades wrapped separately from the rest of her equipment.

She dislikes people handling them casually.

Her fingers are marked by old nicks. She tests unfamiliar cloth by pulling it across a knuckle, then against itself.

Where Moth sees something to keep whole, Iss sees usable pieces.

### The boots

Iss took the Garden worker’s boots while he was trapped and injured.

She did not cause the whole encounter, but she chose to profit from his helplessness.

She has already cut the boots apart.

A piece reinforces a water carrier. Another repairs Kett’s wrapping. Some remains in her private bundle.

That makes restitution awkward without making it impossible.

### Why she speaks so harshly about it

She expects the house to treat any admission from gutter thieves as permission to punish all six.

She therefore defends the taking more fiercely than she defends many things she actually believes.

If confronted privately, she may admit:

> “I could have left him the better one.”

That is not an apology.

It is the first place one might begin.

### Her idea of fairness

Iss prefers explicit exchanges because generosity has often become a demand later.

If someone gives food and then expects obedience, she considers that concealed bargaining.

She can recognize this clearly while ignoring obligations she has imposed on Brik.

### Her relationship with Hesh

Hesh negotiates on behalf of the household.

Iss wants to know what he has promised before anyone hands goods over.

She is the person most likely to interrupt him in front of outsiders.

### What could change her behavior

A bounded settlement: specific goods returned, specific work performed, a specific injury acknowledged.

She is more willing to accept a costly obligation with an end than an indefinite demand to prove remorse.

## Moth — the maker of a place worth staying

Moth collects flat pieces.

A shallow tray, a slate, a section of board: anything that keeps bedding off damp earth or makes a working surface.

Her corner is the least portable part of the shelter.

It is also where the others bring things they want fixed.

### Why the shelter matters

Moth has lived through too many departures in which repairs were abandoned just as they began working.

At Halwen, she has finally stopped one particular drip.

She checks the patch after every rain.

To Hesh, the glasshouses are a temporary refuge.

To Moth, leaving means dismantling weeks of effort without knowing whether the next shelter will be better.

### What she hides

She has built a useful metal fitting into the rain-catching arrangement.

Hesh believes it remains available for trade.

Moth knows removing it will make the shelter leak again.

She says nothing until someone comes to collect it.

### Her relationship with Sere

Moth wants Sere’s approval and resents how much she wants it.

She will endure criticism longer than the others, then refuse a small instruction with startling firmness.

Their worst arguments concern objects neither considers small.

### What she notices

Who repairs something after using it.

A visitor who closes a disturbed screen or returns a borrowed vessel clean may earn more goodwill than someone making grand offers.

> “It held before you came through.”

### What could persuade her to leave

Let her help assess and prepare the next shelter.

She needs evidence that departure is a move, not simply another loss.

## The relationships that hold—and strain

These six should not behave as six separate quest dispensers.

Their decisions pass through one another:

- Hesh trusts Kett’s eyes but increasingly distrusts his conclusions.
- Kett trusts Brik to come back for someone, which makes his own planned departure harder to admit.
- Brik likes Iss but knows her private bargain is unfair to the others.
- Iss defends Moth’s right to keep repaired objects, until she wants one dismantled.
- Moth protects Sere’s dry sleeping place while resenting its cost.
- Sere recognizes how much Hesh is carrying and still asks him for more.

A player who gains one member’s confidence has not gained the household’s.

A promise made to Hesh may be resisted by Iss. An escape accepted by Kett may be refused by Moth. Brik’s agreement to carry Sere may depend on equipment nobody has yet supplied.

## A scene that shows all six

Rain begins entering through a gap above the sleeping bench.

Moth climbs up to fix it.

Sere tells her the patch is facing the wrong way.

Brik steadies the bench.

Kett says the rain will hide movement and they should leave now.

Iss starts wrapping the tools.

Hesh asks where the carrying frame is.

Nobody answers.

For several seconds, all six look toward the unfinished pieces beside the trench.

Then Brik says:

> “Before anyone asks me, finish that.”
