# The third arch

Full proposed Roofed Mile story preserved from the conversation. Annet and Pell are not conclusively identified with similarly named regional characters. The creature remains unidentified. This is fiction, not implemented encounter logic or verified lime-exposure mechanics.

Annet saw the drag marks before she saw the roof.

They crossed the Bell Road from the downhill verge: two dark grooves through the ash, a broad smear between them. Whatever had been pulled uphill had caught against a stone and left a tuft of wool.

Pell stopped the handcart.

Above them, the avalanche gallery followed the curve of the slope. Its outer arches stood open to the valley. Between them, thick piers supported a roof buried beneath old falls of shale.

Out on the road, ash struck their faces hard enough to sting.

Under the gallery, the ground was almost clean.

“We go round,” Annet said.

Pell looked up the slope. Loose stone disappeared into grey weather.

“Round where?”

Behind the cart, little Renn pulled his scarf higher. He was seventeen and had been telling people twenty since they left Brackenreach.

They had six sacks of lime beneath the canvas. Two were payment for the journey. The other four belonged to households waiting on chimney repairs.

Annet moved closer to the entrance.

A sheep lay behind the third pier.

Its hindquarters were gone.

There were other shapes beyond it.

---

They waited in the mouth of the gallery.

Pell wanted to leave the cart outside and look ahead.

Annet pointed to the wheels. Ash was already filling the spaces between the spokes. If the weather worsened, they would have to dig the cart out before moving it.

Renn offered to climb onto the roof.

“No,” she said.

He had already put one boot against the wall.

She caught his sleeve.

The stone above his head was streaked with something greasy. A strip of hide hung from a crack beneath the roof.

He lowered his foot.

They listened.

Wind moved across the arches with a low, uneven whistle.

Farther inside, something knocked once against stone.

Pell lifted the cart handles.

“First arch only.”

---

The first recess held old dung and a split wooden bowl.

The second held a pack saddle.

The third held the sheep.

Annet crouched beside the saddle without touching it. One strap had been repaired with copper wire. The bags were missing, but the buckles remained.

A scavenger would have taken those.

She stood.

“Back.”

Pell had already lowered the cart.

“What?”

“Leave it. Back out.”

Then Renn said, “There’s a man.”

---

He was sitting beyond the fifth pier with his back against the inner wall.

One leg lay straight. The other ended beneath a dark bundle of cloth.

He had wrapped a belt around the thigh.

His lips moved when he saw them.

Annet could not hear him.

She took two steps forward.

He raised one hand, palm toward her.

Not beckoning.

Stopping.

Above him, in the narrow space where the roof met the hillside, a pale shape shifted.

Annet caught Renn by the coat and threw him backward.

The thing dropped where he had been standing.

---

It struck the paving on four limbs.

Its forelegs were too heavy for the rest of it, thick through the joints and nearly bare of hair. The hindquarters crouched low. Its head moved toward Renn without its body turning.

A length of sheep gut hung between its teeth.

Pell shoved the cart forward.

The iron-bound wheel hit the animal’s shoulder.

It snapped at the wheel, caught the wooden rim, and shook it hard enough to wrench the handles from Pell’s hands.

Annet dragged Renn behind the first pier.

“Up.”

He stared at her.

“Climb.”

There were footholds where stones had fallen from the outer face. She pushed him toward them.

Across the arch, Pell backed against the parapet.

The animal let go of the wheel.

It did not rush him.

It lowered its head and moved sideways until the cart no longer stood between them.

---

Annet had a hammer.

It was a good mason’s hammer, short-handled, with a narrow pick behind the face.

She struck the pier three times.

The animal turned toward the sound.

Pell climbed onto the parapet.

A cart’s width of open air lay behind him. Below that, the hillside dropped through scrub and broken rock.

“Stay there,” Annet said.

The animal came toward her.

She retreated around the pier, keeping stone between them. Its claws clicked once, then stopped.

She stopped too.

On the far side, something breathed through a wet nose.

Renn was above her now, clinging to a broken course of masonry.

He looked down at it.

Annet saw his face change.

Then the animal rose.

Its front claws reached the stone beside his boot.

---

Renn kicked.

One boot came off in its mouth.

He climbed higher, scraping his knees bloody, while Annet struck the exposed foreleg with the hammer.

She felt the blow through her wrist.

The animal dropped and turned on her.

She got behind the pier before its jaws closed.

Teeth struck stone.

A fragment cut her cheek.

“Pell!”

He was standing on the parapet, frozen.

“The cart!”

He looked at it.

“Turn it over!”

---

They had chosen the handcart because it was narrow enough for the upper road.

Pell tipped it across the passage.

The lime sacks slid beneath the canvas. One caught against the broken wheel. Another split when the frame hit the paving.

White powder spilled out.

The animal lunged around the pier.

Annet ran toward the overturned cart, ducked beneath the handles, and pulled the canvas after her.

It hit the other side.

A shaft broke.

The impact drove the cart against her shoulder and threw lime into the air.

She closed her eyes and kept her scarf tight over her mouth.

Something scrabbled against the wood.

Then it withdrew, coughing.

---

The man beyond the fifth pier was calling.

This time they heard him.

“Don’t come.”

Annet wiped her eyes with the inside of her sleeve.

The animal had backed into the deeper gallery. It rubbed its muzzle against the paving.

The man was trying to pull himself upright using a crack in the wall.

He looked past it toward them.

“Go.”

Pell had come down from the parapet. Renn hung above the entrance with one foot bare.

For a moment, nobody moved.

Then the man reached for the pack beside him.

A small earthen flask broke against the paving.

Lamp oil spread beneath his hand.

He struck his fire steel.

Once.

Twice.

The animal turned.

---

Annet got Renn down.

Pell tried to lift the cart. The axle had split.

They took two sacks each.

Annet could carry only one with her injured shoulder. Pell took the fifth across the back of his neck.

They left the torn sack beneath the canvas.

Inside the gallery, flame caught low along the wall.

The animal made a sound Annet had not heard from it before.

She did not look back until they reached the bend.

Smoke was coming through the arches.

No one followed.

---

They spent the night in a drainage cut beneath a slab of fallen road.

Pell gave Renn his spare foot wrapping.

Annet’s shoulder swelled until she could no longer pull her arm out of her coat.

At dawn, they heard something moving on the road above.

Slow steps.

A pause.

Something dragged over stone.

They stayed beneath the slab until the light strengthened.

---

Three days later, a carrier brought their warning to the Watchhouse.

He had written it on the back of a broken shutter board:

> ROOFED MILE
> **Occupied**
> DO NOT ENTER FOR SHELTER

Below it, in smaller letters:

> A man died beyond the fifth pier.
> We do not know his name.

Annet had insisted on the second part.

The lime reached Brackenreach. Four chimneys were repaired before the next hard frost.

Renn’s foot healed.

When people asked what the creature had been, Pell described its jaws. Renn described the way it climbed.

Annet asked whether anyone was missing a carrier with a copper repair on his saddle strap.
